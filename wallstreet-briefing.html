The Lead

Official close: S&P 500 +2.35%, Nasdaq +2.74%, Dow +2.15% — stocks rebound from last week’s rout; Nvidia sinks 4.3%

At the close, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 1,104.02 points, or 2.15%, to 52,389.75, the S&P 500 rose 2.35% to 7,549.94 — its best day since April 8 — and the Nasdaq Composite gained about 2.74% to 25,253.14, exiting correction territory (CNBC and Yahoo Finance closing data, via search; corroborated by Kiplinger: Dow +2.2% at 52,389, S&P 500 +2.4% at 7,549, Nasdaq +2.7% at 25,253). It was the first time since April 5 that all three benchmarks surged 2% or more in the same day, according to Dow Jones Market Data (Kiplinger). Stocks had sold off sharply last week on concerns that incoming Democratic leadership in Washington and several states will create headwinds for the data-center buildout (Kiplinger). Per Reuters, nine of the 11 major S&P 500 sector indexes gained more than 1%, led by industrials and consumer discretionary at about 4% each, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose about 7% after plunging more than 12% last week, with Nvidia its sole decliner (Reuters via MarketScreener).

Why it’s up: TipRanks says falling oil helped markets recover from last week’s sharp selloff — U.S. crude fell nearly 3% toward $88 a barrel after President Trump said further U.S. attacks on Iran were unlikely if peace talks progressed (citing Reuters). Morgan Stanley and UBS believe last week’s selloff went too far, questioning whether the new Congress will impose restrictions as severe as investors fear on AI data-center construction (TipRanks).

Chips lead, Nvidia the exception: AMD was up roughly 12%, Broadcom about 9.2% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 6.8% in TipRanks’ midday update, with the Nasdaq 100 up more than 3% (TipRanks). Nvidia fell as much as 8% after its board opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang, who admitted in a Nov. 8 memo to employees to an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence officials believe worked for China’s Ministry of State Security; the board said it had no reason to believe Nvidia’s technology or U.S. national security had been compromised (Yahoo Finance; TipRanks). Yahoo notes Nvidia is worth about $4.5 trillion and makes up nearly 7% of the S&P 500 (Yahoo Finance).

Context: Citi strategists led by Beata Manthey cut U.S. equities and global tech to neutral from overweight and raised European equities ex-UK to overweight; per Bloomberg (via TheStreet), concerns over tighter AI regulation pushed the S&P 500 to its biggest weekly loss since April 2025, with $2.3 trillion wiped off the Nasdaq 100 last Thursday alone (TheStreet).

Movers & Drivers

Up

QXO +17.5% on preliminary Q3

QXO rallied 17.5% after preliminary third-quarter sales rose 79% to about $4.9 billion; full results are due Nov. 12 (Benzinga, via search; TipRanks).

Up

AST SpaceMobile +15% on Rakuten Japan venture

AST SpaceMobile rose 15.1% after forming a joint venture with Rakuten Group to build a satellite-to-phone network in Japan, backed by up to ¥148 billion (about $1 billion) in government support; it reports third-quarter results after the close (Benzinga, via search; TipRanks).

Up

AMD +13.3%, Caterpillar +7.2%, Micron +6.3% at the close

AMD surged 13.3% after Oppenheimer’s Rick Schafer upgraded it to Outperform; Caterpillar, whose generators help power data centers, rose 7.2% to lead the Dow after plunging more than 10% last week; memory names Micron (+6.3%) and Sandisk (+6.2%) also rebounded (Kiplinger). Broadcom was up about 9.2% in TipRanks’ midday update (TipRanks).

Up

Constellation Energy +10%+, KKR +1.9% on earnings

Constellation Energy rose more than 10% after adjusted third-quarter profit beat expectations, and KKR advanced 1.9% after quarterly profit reached a record. By the close, Reuters reported Constellation up more than 10% and nine of 11 S&P 500 sectors up more than 1%, led by industrials and consumer discretionary at about 4% each; Nvidia touched its lowest intraday level since March 31 (Rallies.ai news report, late session, fetched ~3:05 PM ET).

Up

Space stocks rip: Rocket Lab +10.9%

Space stocks rallied for a second session after NASA named eight winners — including SpaceX, Blue Origin, Intuitive Machines, Firefly Aerospace and Voyager Technologies — for lunar delivery work worth up to $10 billion. Rocket Lab, not on NASA’s list, was on track for a two-day gain of nearly 30% ahead of its Q3 report after Monday’s close, per Yahoo Finance’s Jared Blikre (Yahoo Finance); its shares were quoted at $58.86, up 10.91% on the day (24/7 Wall St.).

Down

Hims & Hers −11.8% ahead of earnings

Hims & Hers was quoted at $22.94, down 11.79%, after the regular session, even after announcing an Eli Lilly GLP-1 deal days before results (24/7 Wall St.); short interest was around 23% (S&P Global Market Intelligence, via Yahoo Finance). See After-Hours Movers for the report.

Down

Nvidia −4.3% on board probe of CEO

Nvidia finished at the bottom of the Dow, sinking 4.3% after its board launched an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang, which it said Huang “fully supports”; Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn says there is “currently no confirmed indication that he will step down” (Kiplinger). It had fallen as much as 8% earlier; Yorkville’s Dan Ives expects Huang to continue as CEO “with no interruption in the near-term” (Yahoo Finance).

Down

Ionis −18.82% premarket

Ionis Pharmaceuticals tumbled before the bell after cutting full-year sales guidance for flagship drug Tryngolza by roughly 50% (TheStreet, 8:37 AM ET).

Down

Akamai −5.28% premarket

Akamai fell after proposing $2.2 billion of convertible senior notes, split evenly between notes due 2028 and 2031 (TheStreet, 8:37 AM ET).
