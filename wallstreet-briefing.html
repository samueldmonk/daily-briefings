The Lead

Black Friday close (1 PM ET): S&P 500 +1.60%, Nasdaq +2.3%, Dow +1.18% as battered AI stocks lead a holiday-week rebound

Official close. U.S. stocks finished sharply higher in the session that ended early at 1 p.m. Eastern: the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 591.99 points, or 1.18%, the S&P 500 gained 114.71 points, or 1.60%, and the Nasdaq Composite added 543.70 points, or 2.31%, as investors bought technology and chip stocks that had fallen sharply earlier in the month, on light volume (JBizNews, Nov 27, 1:29 PM ET). The AI stocks that drove November’s slide led the rebound; the Russell 2000 rose 1.71% and the VIX closed at 24.01, down from 28.29 on Wednesday (The Company Chronicle, Nov 27, 1:35 PM ET, Yahoo Finance data).

Chips led; breadth was wide. About four in five S&P 500 stocks rose, though technology led the way; the PHLX Semiconductor Index gained 4.7% while Nvidia lagged for much of the day, and the Nasdaq-100 rose 2.6% on the day and 6.8% for the week. The S&P 500 ended 6.7% below its Oct. 6 record close and is still on track for its worst November since 2008, with one session left in the month on Monday. Traders had nearly fully priced in a rate increase at the Federal Reserve’s Dec. 8–9 meeting, Reuters reported (StockAnalysis.com, Nov 27, 3:01 PM ET). AP’s closing story likewise has the S&P 500 up 1.6% Friday and 5.6% for the week — its biggest weekly gain since April 2025 — with the Dow up 591 points, or 1.2%, and the Nasdaq Composite up 2.3% (AP via BNN Bloomberg, updated 1:41 PM ET).

Inside AP’s full closing story. Microsoft rose 0.6% and Broadcom 3.8%; Anthropic, the first major frontier lab to go public, rose 11% to $91.88 — above its $83 IPO price but below the $115 high of its first trading day last week. Amazon gained 4% as holiday shopping kicked off, while retailers were mixed: Target fell 0.1% and Macy’s rose 1.2%. Brent crude fell 1.4% to $90.42 as of Friday afternoon, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.94% from 4.85% late Wednesday (AP, Damian J. Troise, via Northern Virginia Daily, Nov 27).

AP’s tally: best week in more than a year. The Associated Press confirms the closes — S&P 500 +114.71 points (+1.6%), Dow +591.99 (+1.2%), Nasdaq Composite +543.70 (+2.3%), Russell 2000 +46.24 (+1.7%) — and says the S&P 500’s 5.6% weekly gain was its biggest since April 2025. For the week the Dow rose 5.5%, the Nasdaq 6.7% and the Russell 2000 6.5%; for the year the S&P 500 is up 6.6%, the Dow 5.6%, the Nasdaq 3.8% and the Russell 2000 10.8%. Stocks rallied Monday, swung sharply Tuesday and Wednesday, and markets were closed Thursday for Thanksgiving (AP, “How major US stock indexes fared Friday 11/27/2026”).

A big week, a still-red month. For the holiday-shortened week the S&P 500 gained 5.60%, the Dow 5.46% and the Nasdaq Composite 6.68% (JBizNews). Measured against the end of October, the S&P 500 is still down 3.7% for November, the Nasdaq 5.1% and the Dow 1.9%; the S&P sits 6.7% below its Oct. 6 peak (The Company Chronicle). Stocks return to a full session Monday, Nov. 30, with next week’s jobs report in focus (JBizNews).

Wednesday, Nov. 25 — the latest close. Stocks recovered most of their steep early losses as a pullback in AI stocks eased, AP reports. The S&P 500 fell 0.3% after being down as much as 3.2% earlier in the day; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 164 points, or 0.3%; and the Nasdaq composite fell 0.6%. The market pared its losses even as a report raised more worries about high inflation and as President Donald Trump plans to escalate the U.S. trade war with major trading partners. AI stocks retreated for a second day following Monday’s relief rally over the surprise reinstatement of Jensen Huang as Nvidia’s CEO, and Treasury yields fell (AP via ABC News).

The comeback. “Dow wipes out a 1,100-point plunge as AI stocks slide again,” ran one closing headline (NewsCubby headline).
