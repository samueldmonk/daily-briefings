The Lead

At the close, Mon Oct. 19: Nasdaq +1.11%, S&P 500 +0.41%, Dow −0.07% as tech rebounds — and WTI settles 2.22% higher on Houthi attacks

The close (4:00 PM ET). The S&P 500 gained 0.41% to finish at 7,690.73, the Nasdaq Composite added 1.11% to 26,430.89, and the Dow slipped 36.38 points, or 0.07%, to 51,966.03, as traders moved back into tech after last week’s AI-related rout (CNBC). Among the Nasdaq’s big winners, Amazon rose 4.95%, Palantir 4.36%, Tesla 3.74% and Micron 2.95%, while Meta (−0.54%) and Apple (−0.14%) missed the rebound; WTI futures rose more than 2% on Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure and gold fell 0.47% (TheStreet, 4:19 PM ET). November WTI settled up 2.22% at $103.66 a barrel while Brent slipped 0.28% to $109.55, and the 10-year Treasury yield rose more than 4 basis points to 5.146% (CNBC). Earnings in focus next: Netflix and SpaceX report after Tuesday’s close, with Tesla, Meta, IBM and Texas Instruments after Wednesday’s (TheStreet).

AP closing table (published 4:20 PM ET). AP confirms the S&P 500 rose 31.29 points to 7,690.73, the Dow fell 36.38 points to 51,966.03 and the Nasdaq Composite rose 290.85 points to 26,430.89; the Russell 2000 added 6.93 points, or 0.3%, to 2,728.30. AP said the majority of U.S. stocks outside of AI and tech fell, the Dow swung between losses and gains before closing lower, European indexes fell more than 1% after weekend protests that could force governments to increase spending, and yields climbed in bond markets worldwide. For the year, the S&P 500 is up 12.3%, the Dow 8.1%, the Nasdaq 13.7% and the Russell 2000 9.9% (AP via KRMG).

Breadth check (TechStock², 12:47 PM ET). The headline gain is narrower than it looks: at 12:47 PM ET the S&P 500 was up 0.59% while the equal-weight S&P 500 fund (RSP) was down 0.28%, and only 2,097 of priced U.S. listings (42.4%) were advancing, down from 48.1% near 10 a.m. Just two sectors were higher — technology (+1.80%) and consumer discretionary (+1.67%) — while utilities fell 1.67%, real estate 1.20% and financials 0.69%. The Nasdaq Composite had been up as much as 1.81% before easing to +1.38%; the VIX was 18.03 (−9.80%) and the 10-year yield 5.148%, five basis points above Friday. Nvidia rose 1.9%, Amazon 5.2% and Marvell 6.8% (TechStock², citing Yahoo Finance and TradingView feeds). These are intraday readings, not closes.

Oil reverses higher (TheStreet, 3:42 PM ET). After retreating for hours following the U.S. lifting of sanctions on Russian fuel, oil prices were up around 2.36% for the day after Houthi forces announced responsibility for six attacks on Saudi Arabian oil and airport targets over the past two days; WTI futures were trading around $103.73 (TheStreet).

Early afternoon (Benzinga, 12:58 PM ET). U.S. stocks diverged at midday as a rebound in AI chip and infrastructure names lifted the Nasdaq 100 by 1.7% (up 511 points to 29,699) and the S&P 500 by 0.7% (up 51 points to 7,711), while the Dow slipped 6 points to 51,996, hovering at the flat line; the Russell 2000 rose 0.6% to 2,736. Intel gained 5.0%, Lam Research 5.4%, Micron about 4%, SanDisk nearly 7%, SK Hynix about 6% and Marvell 7.3%. Fed Chair Warsh declined to preview next week’s FOMC decision, saying recent hot inflation readings have not changed his assessment of the economy, with markets pricing roughly an 80% chance of an October hold; the 10-year Treasury yield rose about 6 basis points to 5.14%. These are intraday readings, not closes (Benzinga).

Friday’s close, Oct. 16. The S&P 500 fell 84.62 points (−1.09%) to 7,659.44, the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 175.28 points (−0.34%) to 52,002.41 and the Nasdaq Composite declined 572.99 points (−2.14%) to 26,140.03. AP said the S&P 500’s roughly 2% weekly drop wiped out its gains from the prior week, when it set an all-time high, and that the Nasdaq posted its first weekly loss in five weeks and its worst since June; slightly more than half of S&P 500 stocks fell, but all but seven of the 75 stocks in the index’s tech sector closed lower (AP via BNN Bloomberg).

Why. Chip companies were among the biggest decliners on mounting worries about how long the AI-buildout frenzy will last, AP said, with investors concerned that debt-financed AI spenders may not be able to keep borrowing as heavily with bond yields worldwide recently at multi-year highs (AP via BNN Bloomberg). Investopedia said the Nasdaq’s four-week winning streak ended with a 4.5% weekly slide, the S&P 500 closed 2% lower for the week — its fourth negative week in six — and the Dow rose 0.7% for a second straight weekly gain (Investopedia via Yahoo Finance).

The chip streak. Bloomberg’s closing wrap said chipmakers fell for an eighth straight day, the longest slide since 2008, and that the Nasdaq 100 — down 2.5% on the day — posted its worst week since the April 2025 tariff turmoil; CoreWeave, whose bonds recently hit levels that typically signal distress, sank 12% (Bloomberg via SWI swissinfo.ch). TheStreet’s 4:08 PM wrap said nearly two-thirds of U.S. issues declined, the S&P technology sector fell more than 3% while energy rose 2.52%, and the Russell 2000 lost 1.72% to 2,719.57 (TheStreet); AP’s closing table put the Russell 2000 at 2,721.37 (−45.90, −1.7%), down 3% for the week, so the small-cap level differs by source (AP via ABC News).

The Fed. Chair Kevin Warsh offered no fresh signal on rates while in Bangkok: “It’s a new chapter at the Fed, and we’re not in that business anymore,” he said. Investopedia cited CME FedWatch at a 17% chance of a hike at the late-October meeting and 89% for December (Investopedia via Yahoo Finance); AP, also citing CME Group data, put December hike odds at 74.3%, up from 50.2% a month ago (AP via BNN Bloomberg). The figures differ by source and time of reading. The Fed raised its key rate a quarter point to about 3.9% in September, its first increase in three years (AP).

Oil & bonds. Brent crude jumped 3.7% to settle at $109.86 a barrel, with no U.S.–Iran deal to end the war; the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to 5.09% from 5.10% late Thursday after topping 5.30% earlier this month (AP via BNN Bloomberg).
