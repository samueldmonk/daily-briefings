The Lead

Hot CPI deepens the tech slide: as of ~11:22 AM ET the S&P 500 is down 0.78% and the Nasdaq 1.11%, while the Dow holds near flat

NewLate-morning read (~11:22 AM ET; Yahoo Finance quote strip “U.S. markets close in 4h 38m”, Nasdaq and Dow quotes “As of 11:22:52 AM EDT”). The S&P 500 was at 7,744.46, down 60.99 points (−0.78%); the Dow at 51,650.59, down 19.51 (−0.04%); the Nasdaq Composite at 26,902.12, down 302.26 (−1.11%); and the Russell 2000 down 1.43%. The VIX was 15.95 (+4.46%), November WTI $95.33 (+1.26%), gold $4,106.80 (−1.69%) and bitcoin $81,789.21 (−1.86%). Yahoo reports semiconductor stocks continued to sell off, Bank of America stock sank as much as 9%, and Brent crude stayed above $105 a barrel as Houthis continued attacks on Saudi energy facilities. At 11:18 AM ET the 10-year Treasury yield was up 6 basis points at 5.27% and the 30-year up 2 basis points at 5.58%. Netflix shares, which lost early gains after the job-cut report, are down 25% since the start of 2026 (Yahoo Finance live). Also on today’s calendar: the Fed’s Beige Book. Earlier, New York Fed President John Williams said he sees one more rate hike as appropriate this year, per Bloomberg via Yahoo (Yahoo Finance live).

Where the pressure is. The selling in tech is concentrated in chips and AI infrastructure: the PHLX chip index has fallen more than 8% in five sessions and memory stocks extended their declines this morning, after Micron lost 3.6% Tuesday on reports of technology advances at Chinese memory maker CXMT; 24/7 Wall St. headlines today report CoreWeave down 10% as its bond yield touched 15%, Nebius down 6%, Applied Digital down 6%, Cipher Digital down 5% and IREN down 4% (24/7 Wall St.). Backdrop: analysts expect S&P 500 third-quarter earnings to rise more than 30%, which has pulled the index’s forward valuation below 19 times expected earnings, the lowest since 2023, but the rally is narrow — the median S&P 500 stock is up less than 5% this year (24/7 Wall St.).

Earlier read (Yahoo Finance quote strip, “U.S. markets close in 5h 17m”; Nasdaq quote “As of 10:43:21 AM EDT”). The S&P 500 was at 7,752.16, down 53.29 points (−0.68%); the Dow at 51,651.32, down 18.78 (−0.04%); the Nasdaq Composite at 26,965.28, down 239.10 (−0.88%); and the Russell 2000 down 1.36%. The 10-year yield was 5.26 (+0.05), the VIX 15.75 (+3.15%), November WTI $95.30 (+1.23%), gold $4,117.60 (−1.43%) and bitcoin $81,776.16 (−1.98%). Yahoo’s blog said tech stocks declined as the 10-year yield rose, and that stocks were attempting to recover from a global chip sell-off after South Korea’s KOSPI fell 12% over the past five days (Yahoo Finance live).

The inflation print (8:30 AM ET). The consumer price index rose 0.6% in September, in line with forecasts, but the annual rate reached 3.7% against the 3.6% economists polled by Dow Jones expected; core CPI rose 0.3% on the month and 2.6% on the year, above expectations of 0.2% and 2.5%. Treasury yields rose on concern the Fed could raise rates again this month: the 10-year climbed more than 6 basis points to 5.278% and the 30-year more than 2 basis points to 5.59%, per CNBC data cited by TheStreet. “Inflation is cooking with gas,” said Jamie Cox of Harris Financial Group; Allianz Investment Management’s Charlie Ripley said shelter, airfares and used cars pushed core above forecasts, with shelter alone adding roughly 0.15 percentage point to the core print (TheStreet). Roughly 78% of investors expect the Fed to hold at its upcoming meeting, per CME FedWatch as cited by Yahoo (Yahoo Finance live).

Opening bell (TheStreet, 9:38 AM ET). The S&P 500 fell 0.45%, the Dow slipped 0.04%, the Nasdaq lost 0.63% and the Russell 2000 gave up 0.40% as investors responded to the data (TheStreet).

Banks, round two. Bank of America’s third-quarter profit fell 17% to $6.93 billion, or $0.91 a share, versus the $1.10 analysts expected; excluding a $1.2 billion regulatory penalty it earned $8.1 billion, or $1.08 a share, and revenue rose 3% to $30 billion against roughly $30.6 billion expected. The stock sank 7%, per Yahoo’s live blog (Yahoo Finance; Yahoo Finance live). TheStreet reports federal regulators fined the bank over widespread failures in its safeguards against money laundering and other illicit financial activity. BlackRock beat estimates and Morgan Stanley also reported. “Investors appear increasingly focused on forward guidance rather than headline earnings beats,” said senior market analyst Daniela Hathorn (TheStreet).

Chips and oil. ASML posted record third-quarter sales and raised its full-year outlook on AI demand; investors await Taiwan Semiconductor earnings Thursday (Yahoo Finance live). Oil rose after Houthi forces attacked an administrative building of Saudi Aramco in Dhahran, causing a limited fire, and Iran’s Revolutionary Guards Navy said a container ship sank south of the Strait of Hormuz after striking a naval mine; at TheStreet’s 7:19 AM read WTI was up 1.41% at $95.47 and Brent up 0.79% at $106.50 (TheStreet).

Tuesday, Oct. 13 — the session in review. Markets finished broadly lower, with all four major U.S. indexes slipping, as JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo reported (TheStreet). Official closes, derived from Yahoo Finance’s quote strip (level less change) and reconciling to Monday’s closes: S&P 500 7,805.45 (−1.79, −0.02%), Nasdaq Composite 27,204.38 (−41.52, −0.15%), Dow 51,670.10 (−180.45, −0.35%) (Yahoo Finance).
