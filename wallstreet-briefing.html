The Lead

As of ~12:06 PM ET: S&P 500 −0.83%, Nasdaq −1.27%, Dow −0.63% after a hot October CPI

Yahoo Finance’s market strip, with U.S. markets closing in 3 hours 54 minutes (≈12:06 PM ET), showed the S&P 500 down 62.36 points, or 0.83%, the Dow down 328.67 points, or 0.63%, and the Nasdaq Composite down 321.64 points, or 1.27%; the Russell 2000 was off 1.32% and the VIX up 5.12% to 20.94 (Yahoo Finance). That is a touch weaker for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq than Yahoo’s ≈11:45 AM read (−0.80% and −1.15%), while the Dow pared slightly from −0.66% (Yahoo Finance). Stocks had opened lower — TheStreet’s 9:33 AM read had the S&P 500 −0.74%, the Dow −0.37% and the Nasdaq −1.05% (TheStreet) — a day after Monday’s broad rally.

Why it’s down: the Consumer Price Index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4% in October versus the 0.3% economists expected, cooler than September’s 0.6%; core CPI rose 0.3% versus 0.2% expected. The report carried no annual figures because last year’s government shutdown kept the Bureau of Labor Statistics from publishing full October 2025 data. Energy costs rose 0.7% (from 5.2% in September), housing 0.3% and food 0.5% (Yahoo Finance; TheStreet).

The Fed: traders now see an 84% chance of a 25-basis-point hike in December, and bets on a 50-basis-point move jumped from 0% on Monday to 15%, per Yahoo Finance’s 8:58 AM ET read. Governor Christopher Waller said in London before the data that he leans toward a quarter-point hike in December and that a 50-basis-point hike “should be considered” if core prices rose 0.3% or more — the pace October printed (Yahoo Finance; TheStreet/Reuters). Regan Capital’s Skyler Weinand said the CPI “sets the stage for another rate hike in December” (TheStreet).

Nvidia: shares were down 6.57% at $166.92 as of 12:06 PM ET (Yahoo Finance), deepening from a 5.41% morning drop in TheStreet’s 10:55 AM update and extending Monday’s 4.27% fall, after a shareholder lawsuit accused Nvidia and CEO Jensen Huang of misleading investors over Huang’s alleged affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive; The Information reported the relationship helped facilitate at least $7.5 billion of Nvidia chip shipments to Chinese firms, and President Trump said the FBI was looking into it (TheStreet; Yahoo Finance). Nvidia’s board has launched an investigation into the affair; in a Sunday statement it said it has “no reason to believe that either national security or Nvidia secrets were compromised.” Citi’s Atif Malik noted the company has said Huang will remain CEO while the investigation proceeds, and Yorkville Ives’ Dan Ives said Nvidia would be worth 25%–30% less if Huang were removed (Yahoo Finance Nvidia live blog).
