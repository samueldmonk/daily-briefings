The Lead

Midday, Mon Oct. 19 (as of 12:00 PM ET): S&P 500 +0.82%, Nasdaq +1.52%, Dow +0.17% as tech carries the rebound

New Midday (24/7 Wall St., 12:00 PM ET). The S&P 500 was up 0.82%, the Nasdaq Composite up 1.52% and the Dow up just 0.17%; 24/7 Wall St. said tech was “carrying the entire session,” with growth and technology stocks doing almost all of the heavy lifting, and that the index accelerated through midday after a tighter early range. These are intraday readings, not closes (24/7 Wall St.).

New Also this morning (24/7 Wall St. market cards). Europe closed broadly lower — FTSE 100 −1.43%, DAX −1.77%, CAC 40 −1.70%. U.S. homebuilder sentiment fell to 29 in October, its lowest since June 2012, with a record 43% of builders cutting prices. Sea Ltd. slid 5.3% to $88.08 by 10:00 AM ET, and Saudi Arabia is postponing the in-person “Davos in the Desert” event after Houthi attacks (FT, as relayed by 24/7 Wall St.) (24/7 Wall St.).

Late morning (TheStreet, 11:28 AM ET). The Invesco QQQ Trust, which tracks the Nasdaq-100, rose 1.90% to $723.27, rebounding after several sessions of sharp losses amid a tech sell-off; TheStreet’s blog headline now reads “S&P 500 climbs as tech rebound from sell-off.” At 11:14 AM, Bank of America CEO Brian Moynihan said the bank has added thousands of employees and strengthened reporting procedures after regulators hit it with a $1.2 billion penalty for failing to report more than $5 billion in suspicious transactions (TheStreet). No full index snapshot newer than ~10:16 AM ET could be verified for this edition; the live quotes above stream current levels.

Context (Quartz, citing CNBC). The Nasdaq fell 4.5% last week, ending a four-week winning streak; the S&P 500 suffered its steepest one-week loss since the end of June, and the information-technology sector lost more than 6%, its roughest stretch since April 2025 (Quartz).

Late-morning movers (TheStreet, 10:41 AM ET). Datadog jumped 5.48% after Bernstein upgraded it to Outperform from Market Perform and raised its price target to $350 from $237, and Western Digital rose 5.20% after Wall Street Zen upgraded it to Strong Buy ahead of its Oct. 22 results. On the downside, Mosaic fell 3.67% to a 52-week low, NRG Energy sank 3.63% and Ameriprise Financial lost 3.20% after BofA Securities downgraded it twice, from Buy to Underperform (TheStreet).

Bonds and oil this morning (CNBC TV18). The 10-year Treasury yield fell more than one basis point to 5.0854%, the 30-year slipped one basis point to 5.4242% and the 2-year was broadly unchanged at 4.7315%; Brent fell 1.22% to $108.52 by 10:02 GMT (6:02 AM ET) after settling Friday at its highest since May 19 (CNBC TV18 via TradingView).

Latest index read (as of ~10:16 AM ET). Stocks were mostly flat-to-higher as tech shares extended their bounce from last week’s declines but gave back part of the opening gain: the S&P 500 was up 29.30 points, or 0.38%, at 7,688.74; the Nasdaq Composite up 223.41, or 0.85%, at 26,363.44; and the Dow down 69.45, or 0.13%, at 51,932.96. The Nasdaq 100 was up 1.12% and the Russell 2000 up 0.23% (+6.28 to 2,727.65); the VIX fell 9.35% to 18.12. Intel (+4.81%), Amazon (+2.55%) and UnitedHealth (+1.61%) led the Dow, while Caterpillar (−1.32%), Sherwin-Williams (−1.38%) and Home Depot (−1.07%) weighed on it. Brent was quoted at $107.90 (−1.78%) and WTI at $100.48 (−0.92%) (market snapshot via The Sunday Guardian, published 10:16 AM ET). These are intraday readings, not closes; point changes and levels are consistent with Friday’s closes.

At the open (Reuters, published 9:35 AM ET). The S&P 500 and Nasdaq opened higher as AI-related stocks rebounded from last week’s sharp selloff, while a decline in oil prices provided additional support ahead of key earnings reports. The Dow fell 23.8 points, or 0.05%, to 51,978.58 at the open; the S&P 500 rose 43.3 points, or 0.57%, to 7,702.77; and the Nasdaq Composite rose 255.3 points, or 0.98%, to 26,395.29 (Reuters via Investing.com).

Fed watch (Yahoo Finance live blog). BofA economists still see an October rate hike on the table even though 85% of market participants expect policymakers to hold steady next week, per CME FedWatch. “Most of the FOMC was on board with two hikes this year. Why not do the second one in Oct, to retain the optionality for a third in Dec?” wrote BofA economist Aditya Bhave (Yahoo Finance).

Also at the open (TheStreet, 9:33 AM ET). “The US stock market is now open.” Stocks were mixed in early trading amid a rebound in technology and artificial-intelligence shares and a week filled with high-stakes corporate earnings reports: the S&P 500 gained 0.57%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.08% and the Nasdaq advanced 1.09%. “The key issue investors face this week is whether last week’s problems in the technology sector, especially in chips and data centers, will gain further traction,” said TheStreet Pro contributor James “Rev Shark” DePorre; “the reaction to earnings reports will determine where things are headed” (TheStreet). Also on TheStreet’s blog: Anthropic said Sunday it will commit 5% of its shares to a new public benefit fund, Reuters reported, and Elon Musk pledged to give one SpaceX share to every child in America next year (TheStreet).

Pre-open check (Benzinga, 8:23 AM ET). Benzinga’s futures table showed the Dow −0.30%, S&P 500 +0.39%, Nasdaq 100 +1.10% and Russell 2000 +0.31%. The 10-year Treasury yielded 5.07% and the 2-year 4.72%, and CME FedWatch priced a 14.9% chance of a hike after the October meeting. WTI crude was down 2.12% around $96.92 in the early New York session. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said September’s hotter inflation reading reflects an “energy shock” and argued prices will ease once the U.S. gets “to the other side of the Iran conflict” (Benzinga).

Earlier (TheStreet live blog, 8:53 AM ET). “Stock futures were mixed as tech stocks bounced back from last week’s sell-off,” TheStreet reported. Intel was up 4% premarket after JPMorgan upgraded it from Underweight to Neutral with an unchanged $85 price target, and CoreWeave rebounded 6.12% after Goldman Sachs upgraded it to Buy from Neutral and raised its price target to $139 from $106, following a sell-off of more than 25% last week. Cleveland-Cliffs surged 12.39% after beating third-quarter earnings and revenue estimates; Applied Digital gained 8.53% and Viavi Solutions 6.97%, while Mattel slipped 2.91%, Stellantis 2.52% and AutoZone 1.31% (TheStreet). Oil slipped from a five-month high after President Trump waived sanctions and tariffs on Russia: WTI fell 0.88% to $100.50 and Brent 1.47% to $108.20 in TheStreet’s 7:02 AM ET update; gold futures rose 0.20% to $4,189.60 and silver 0.79% to $57.76 in early trading. SpaceX edged down 0.45% to $145.92 premarket ahead of Tuesday’s earnings (TheStreet).

Earlier still (Reuters, 8:26 AM ET). Reuters reported S&P 500 E-minis up 0.47% and Nasdaq 100 E-minis up 1.05% while Dow E-minis slipped slightly, as investors were encouraged by a recovery in AI-related shares — including CoreWeave, Broadcom and Micron in premarket trading — along with falling crude futures and easing Treasury yields; the 10-year yield eased to around 5.08% (Reuters via The Sunday Guardian, Oct. 19, 8:26 AM ET). Brent fell after a U.S. decision to waive some sanctions on Russian oil exports, with Secretary of State Marco Rubio saying the administration would take up to 60 days to assess the economic implications; reports of a tanker striking a mine in the Strait of Hormuz kept supply risks in view (same source). An earlier Yahoo Finance futures strip, read with the open 2 hours 47 minutes away, showed S&P 500 futures +0.51%, Nasdaq futures +1.07%, Russell 2000 futures +0.46% and Dow futures −0.01%, with the VIX at 18.67 (−6.6%) (Yahoo Finance).

Earlier (as of 4 a.m. ET). U.S. stock futures were higher early Monday, recovering from last week’s Wall Street sell-off: as of 4 a.m. ET, Nasdaq futures were up 1.1%, Dow futures 0.1%, and S&P 500 and Russell 2000 futures 0.5% each, Stocktwits reported. CoreWeave and Nebius rebounded in early premarket trade amid reports that Nvidia is offering a credit lifeline to top neocloud survivors, and Micron, SK Hynix, Broadcom, Qualcomm and TSMC were up 2% to 5% (Stocktwits). Late Sunday, Benzinga put S&P 500 futures up 0.72%, Nasdaq 100 futures up 1.2% and Dow futures up 0.44%, amid trending calls for a “Black Monday” on the crash’s 39th anniversary that The Kobeissi Letter called “overdone” (Benzinga via AOL; Stocktwits). Futures readings differ by source and time; the regular session opens at 9:30 AM ET. Overseas, South Korea’s KOSPI rose 0.61% and Japan’s Nikkei 225 fell 0.88% (Benzinga via AOL).

This week. Nearly 15% of S&P 500 companies report, including SpaceX (Tuesday evening), Tesla and Meta (Wednesday); Netflix and United Airlines also report Tuesday (Stocktwits).

Friday’s close, Oct. 16. The S&P 500 fell 84.62 points (−1.09%) to 7,659.44, the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 175.28 points (−0.34%) to 52,002.41 and the Nasdaq Composite declined 572.99 points (−2.14%) to 26,140.03. AP said the S&P 500’s roughly 2% weekly drop wiped out its gains from the prior week, when it set an all-time high, and that the Nasdaq posted its first weekly loss in five weeks and its worst since June; slightly more than half of S&P 500 stocks fell, but all but seven of the 75 stocks in the index’s tech sector closed lower (AP via BNN Bloomberg).

Why. Chip companies were among the biggest decliners on mounting worries about how long the AI-buildout frenzy will last, AP said, with investors concerned that debt-financed AI spenders may not be able to keep borrowing as heavily with bond yields worldwide recently at multi-year highs (AP via BNN Bloomberg). Investopedia said the Nasdaq’s four-week winning streak ended with a 4.5% weekly slide, the S&P 500 closed 2% lower for the week — its fourth negative week in six — and the Dow rose 0.7% for a second straight weekly gain (Investopedia via Yahoo Finance).

The chip streak. Bloomberg’s closing wrap said chipmakers fell for an eighth straight day, the longest slide since 2008, and that the Nasdaq 100 — down 2.5% on the day — posted its worst week since the April 2025 tariff turmoil; CoreWeave, whose bonds recently hit levels that typically signal distress, sank 12% (Bloomberg via SWI swissinfo.ch). TheStreet’s 4:08 PM wrap said nearly two-thirds of U.S. issues declined, the S&P technology sector fell more than 3% while energy rose 2.52%, and the Russell 2000 lost 1.72% to 2,719.57 (TheStreet); AP’s closing table put the Russell 2000 at 2,721.37 (−45.90, −1.7%), down 3% for the week, so the small-cap level differs by source (AP via ABC News).

The Fed. Chair Kevin Warsh offered no fresh signal on rates while in Bangkok: “It’s a new chapter at the Fed, and we’re not in that business anymore,” he said. Investopedia cited CME FedWatch at a 17% chance of a hike at the late-October meeting and 89% for December (Investopedia via Yahoo Finance); AP, also citing CME Group data, put December hike odds at 74.3%, up from 50.2% a month ago (AP via BNN Bloomberg). The figures differ by source and time of reading. The Fed raised its key rate a quarter point to about 3.9% in September, its first increase in three years (AP).

Oil & bonds. Brent crude jumped 3.7% to settle at $109.86 a barrel, with no U.S.–Iran deal to end the war; the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to 5.09% from 5.10% late Thursday after topping 5.30% earlier this month (AP via BNN Bloomberg).

Watch · Banks

JPMorgan: $5 billion of equity in security-critical industries

JPMorgan Chase said it has invested $5 billion in equity in industries critical to U.S. economic and national security and has financed about $230 billion through its Security and Resiliency Initiative since launching it a year ago, Reuters reported; the bank had committed to financing $1.5 trillion over the next decade (TheStreet, 9:58 AM ET; TheStreet).

↓ Housing

Homebuyers canceling at near-record rates

Nationwide, 14.5% of homes fell out of contract in September, according to Redfin data — the highest share on record apart from the early months of the COVID-19 pandemic — a potential sign that higher mortgage rates are prompting buyers to rethink plans (Yahoo Finance).

↑ Premarket · Stocks in focus

OpenWorld +20.68%, Disc Medicine +17.89%, Red Cat +5.30%

OpenWorld surged on a non-binding term sheet with Meridian for a tokenized financing program of up to $1 billion; Disc Medicine rose ahead of a Monday webcast on topline results from its APOLLO Phase 3 trial of bitopertin; Red Cat climbed after announcing an offer for Austrian engine maker Steyr Motors. Elbit Systems slipped 0.95% despite a roughly $235 million rocket-artillery contract, and Steel Dynamics reports after the close (Benzinga, 8:23 AM ET; Benzinga).

Watch · AI / SpaceX

Anthropic’s 5% public-benefit fund; Musk’s SpaceX share pledge

Anthropic said it will commit 5% of its shares to a new public benefit fund, with an additional 2% at the board’s discretion for every 50% rise in its value above a yet-undisclosed baseline, as it plans one of the biggest IPOs in history (Reuters via TheStreet). SpaceX, down 0.45% to $145.92 premarket ahead of Tuesday’s earnings, saw Musk pledge one share to every child in America next year — worth more than $10 billion based on recent prices (TheStreet).

↑ Premarket · Earnings

Cleveland-Cliffs +12.39%

The steel producer surged premarket after beating third-quarter earnings and revenue estimates, per TheStreet’s 8:43 AM ET movers list; Applied Digital (+8.53%) and Viavi Solutions (+6.97%) also rose, while Mattel (−2.91%), Stellantis (−2.52%) and AutoZone (−1.31%) fell (TheStreet).

↑ Premarket · Upgrades

CoreWeave +6.12%, Intel +4%

Goldman Sachs upgraded CoreWeave to Buy from Neutral and lifted its target to $139 from $106 after a sell-off of more than 25% last week; JPMorgan upgraded Intel to Neutral from Underweight, keeping an $85 target (TheStreet, 8:53 AM ET; TheStreet).

↑ Premarket · Chips

Micron, SK Hynix, Broadcom, Qualcomm, TSMC up 2%–5%

Chipmakers bounced in early premarket trade Monday after last week’s slide; separately, Nvidia is reportedly asking Samsung, SK Hynix and Micron to use TSMC’s 3-nanometer process for the base die of its next-generation HBM chips (Stocktwits).

↑ Premarket · Neoclouds

CoreWeave, Nebius rebound

Both rebounded in early premarket trade amid reports Nvidia is offering a credit lifeline to top neocloud survivors; SEC filings show Nebius director Anne Grimme bought nearly $50K of shares last week (Stocktwits). Rocket Lab saw a 2% relief move premarket (Stocktwits).

Watch · Pre-open

Accenture weighs sale of China operations

Accenture is weighing a sale of its China operations that could fetch up to $1.5 billion and has hired JPMorgan to run a review; separately, new Pentagon rules banning Chinese-sourced rare-earth magnets from defense supply chains from Jan. 1, 2027 put Lockheed Martin and RTX sourcing in focus (Simply Wall St via Yahoo Finance).
