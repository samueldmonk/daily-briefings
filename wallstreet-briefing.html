The Lead

Into the open, ~9:30 AM ET: Nasdaq futures −1.4%, 10-yr back above 5% as Nvidia digests Huang’s exit

New Latest read. Yahoo Finance’s market strip at about 8:39 AM ET (51 minutes before the bell) showed Nasdaq futures down 1.37%, S&P 500 futures down 0.75%, Dow futures down 0.39% and Russell 2000 futures down 0.63%; the 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 bp to 5.05%, the VIX jumped 8.46% to 18.97, gold fell 2.15% and December WTI rose 0.86% to $87.27 (Yahoo Finance). Yahoo’s Ines Ferré reported NVDA sank as much as 9% Thursday morning while AMD rose more than 1% and Broadcom edged higher; CoreWeave fell 10% pre-market, and Intel (−2%), TSMC (−2%+), SanDisk (−5%) and Micron (−~4%) also declined (Yahoo Finance). No verified regular-session opening print was available when this edition was built (~9:40 AM ET); the live quotes above stream the current tape.

Breaking. “NVIDIA (NASDAQ: NVDA) Board of Directors announced today that Jensen Huang has resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer, and a member of the Board, effective immediately,” the board said. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while continuing as CFO, and the board is searching for a permanent CEO among internal and external candidates (Tom’s Hardware; CBS News, 8:57 AM ET). Nvidia announced the resignation at 8:48 AM ET; newly appointed Chair Harvey Jones said the board has full confidence in Kress and expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption, and Q3 results remain scheduled for Nov. 17 (Business Upturn).

The stock. NVDA was quoted at $154.68 pre-market at 8:55 AM ET, down $19.05 or 10.97% from Wednesday’s $173.73 close and about 5.8% below the previous 52-week low of $164.27 (Business Upturn).

Futures. CNBC’s live blog reported Nasdaq-100 futures down 1.2%, S&P 500 futures down 0.6% and Dow futures down 47 points, or 0.1%, with Broadcom and AMD up about 3% each pre-market after the resignation (CNBC; time of that read not stated). The last timestamped read, Yahoo Finance at ≈8:08 AM ET, had Nasdaq futures −1.05% and S&P futures −0.46% (prior edition). Futures are not the open — the first official read comes at 9:30 AM ET.

Data. Initial jobless claims rose 9,000 to 200,000 in the week ended Nov. 7, above the 195,000 Reuters poll; the prior week was revised to 191,000, the four-week average held at 195,250 (lowest since October 1969) and continuing claims fell 20,000 to 1.697 million (Reuters via Investing.com). Existing home sales are due at 10 AM ET (CNBC).

Wednesday’s close (for reference): Nasdaq Composite +1.60%, S&P 500 +1.31%, Dow +0.49% as CoreWeave revived the AI trade (desk record). Levels are in the Weekly Scorecard.

Down

Nvidia −10.97% pre-market as Huang resigns; Kress named interim CEO

$154.68 at 8:55 AM ET vs. a $173.73 close; the board announced the resignation at 8:48 AM ET (Business Upturn). Before the announcement, the stock had dropped more than 8% after hours Wednesday on the WSJ report that the board was preparing to oust Huang (Business Upturn, citing CBS News).

Down

Oppenheimer downgrades Nvidia and pulls its price target

Oppenheimer’s Rich Schafer downgraded NVDA and removed his price target “following reports (WSJ, NYT) of founding CEO Jensen Huang’s imminent departure,” calling a departure “a material, potentially thesis-changing event” and warning of a possible “brain drain” — a step Yahoo’s Brian Sozzi notes is usually reserved for sub-$10 stocks (Yahoo Finance).

Mixed

Chips split: AMD, Broadcom +3%; Intel, SanDisk slide

Advanced Micro Devices and Broadcom each gained over 3% premarket while Intel and SanDisk slid, and U.S. cloud-computing stocks largely fell (Dow Jones via MarketScreener, 4:58 AM ET). Yahoo also listed Micron among decliners (Yahoo Finance). Overseas, Samsung and SK Hynix — key Nvidia suppliers — declined; the Hang Seng fell 1.26% and the Nikkei 225 0.70% (TipRanks).

Down

Strategy to be removed from MSCI’s USA indices

Index provider MSCI is officially removing Strategy (MSTR), bitcoin’s biggest corporate bull, from its USA indices; bitcoin was down 1.49% on Yahoo’s ≈8:08 AM strip (Yahoo Finance). TipRanks reports MSTR falling on the decision (TipRanks, headline).

Down

Nebius down 10%+ premarket despite a Q3 beat

Nebius posted a loss of $0.13 a share vs. a consensus $0.63 loss; revenue surged 571% to $980.8 million vs. $907.2 million expected, and it guided fiscal 2027 revenue to $12–13 billion vs. $11.98 billion consensus — yet shares were down over 10% premarket. Quarterly capex surged 664% to $7.3 billion (Investing.com, 7:39 AM ET — Investing.com).

Up

Wednesday: Apple sets a record — $5.16 trillion valuation

Apple (+1.96%) notched a fresh record before the close, eclipsing $355.42 and lifting its valuation to $5.16 trillion, its highest ever; the shares are up 31.1% this year (TheStreet, 3:48 PM ET — TheStreet).

Up

Wednesday: CoreWeave +24.7% — best day since June 2025

Shares jumped 24.7%, their biggest one-day percentage gain since June 3, 2025, after Q3 showed a narrower-than-expected loss of $1.27 a share on revenue of $3.66 billion; CEO Michael Intrator said CoreWeave “will not use the unsecured debt market to fund” the business. The stock had been down nearly 32% year to date coming into the session (Kiplinger). 24/7 Wall St.’s 4:10 PM ET movers board had it at $60.51, +24.2% (24/7 Wall St.).

Up

Wednesday: Closing gainers: SanDisk +13.3%, Bloom +12.5%, Dell +11.9%, Arm +11.9%

CoreWeave led five double-digit gainers at the close as AI-infrastructure names “took the whole session”; on the downside GoPro fell 18.3% to $0.69, NetEase lost 4.4% and nothing else fell more than 4.4% (24/7 Wall St., 4:10 PM ET — 24/7 Wall St.).

Up

Wednesday: Nvidia +2.9% despite Baird downgrade

Wall Street bought the dip: Nvidia closed up 2.9% even after Baird cut it to Neutral and Morgan Stanley removed it from its Top Pick list while keeping Overweight. The stock was down 9.5% for the week coming into the session (Kiplinger).

Up

Wednesday: Costco +2.0% on $30 special dividend

Costco’s board approved a $30-a-share special dividend — roughly $13.3 billion — plus a $4 billion buyback; Mizuho’s David Bellinger said it is well above the $18–$20 he had forecast (Kiplinger).
