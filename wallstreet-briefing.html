The Lead

Split close: S&P 500 ~−0.5%, Nasdaq ~−1.1%, Dow ~+0.4% at the bell as tech selling swamps the oil-driven rally

Yahoo Finance’s market strip three minutes before the bell (“U.S. markets close in 3m”) showed the S&P 500 down 37.55 points, or 0.50%, the Nasdaq Composite down 283.27 points, or 1.11%, the Dow up 243.36 points, or 0.47%, and the Russell 2000 down 0.06% (Yahoo Finance). Yahoo’s close update, surfaced by search, put the finish at S&P 500 −0.51%, Nasdaq −1.07% and Dow +0.41% — consistent with the strip; official closing levels are not yet confirmed by a second source and are held out of the Scorecard (Yahoo Finance, via search).

It was a steady fade. Stocks opened higher — Dow +0.7%, S&P +0.5%, Nasdaq +0.7% — and the Nasdaq climbed as much as 1.3% before giving it all back; by mid-afternoon the S&P had slipped to session lows, with the decline “largely tech-driven.” The tech sector ETF (XLK) was down 2.4% as of 2:40 p.m. ET, dragged by hardware and chip stocks, while investors rotated into consumer cyclical, financial services and consumer defensive names — the reason the Dow stayed green (Yahoo Finance). The VIX rose 11.72% to 21.70 on the late strip.

The offset was oil. December WTI fell 7.84% to about $95.96 on Yahoo’s late strip and Brent traded below $105 after Axios reported President Trump wants to give diplomacy with Tehran another shot (Yahoo Finance); airlines rallied on cheaper fuel while energy lagged.

The backdrop is still fragile: the Nasdaq lost 5.2% in October and fell 2.86% on Friday alone after Intel’s outlook and Amazon’s report (Yahoo Finance; Reuters via Investing.com). Apple — down about 2% into the print — reports after the bell (consensus EPS $2 on revenue of $113.46 billion, per Bloomberg, cited by Yahoo), Tuesday brings the U.S. midterm elections, and Friday the October jobs report (Yahoo Finance).
