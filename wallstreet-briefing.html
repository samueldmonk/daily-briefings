The Lead

Opening bell, Monday Oct. 12 (as of ~9:37 AM ET): stocks open mixed — Dow +0.19%, S&P 500 +0.05%, Nasdaq −0.07% — ahead of big-bank earnings and September CPI

New Opening bell (TheStreet, 9:37 AM ET). “The US stock market is now open,” TheStreet wrote: stocks were mixed in early trading as the S&P 500 edged up 0.05%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 0.19% and the Nasdaq slipped 0.07%, while the Russell 2000 advanced 0.46%. TheStreet’s early oil update had WTI up 1.13% to $92.89 and Brent up 1.17% to $106 after the Houthis claimed a Saturday strike on Riyadh’s international airport that killed 12 and wounded more than 300, with a second attack Sunday killing another person; two tankers were also attacked over the weekend, one near Oman’s Sohar and one in the Strait of Hormuz. Microsoft rose 0.70% in early trading after unveiling its third-generation Maia 300 AI chip, which it plans to deploy in potentially more than 1 million units next year, per Reuters. Starbucks gained 1.34% and Chipotle fell 2.78% after a Starbucks spokesperson told CNBC “Starbucks is not buying Chipotle.” Capital.com’s Kyle Rodda said Wall Street “remains around record highs thanks to extraordinary (albeit highly concentrated) earnings growth” (TheStreet live blog, updated 10:03 AM ET). Separately, Robert E. Hall and Michael Woodford won the Nobel Prize in economics, per CNBC as cited by TheStreet.

Futures (Quartz, updated Oct. 12, ~7:35 AM ET). S&P 500 futures were up 0.1%, Dow futures added 53 points, or 0.1%, and Nasdaq 100 futures edged down 0.1%; U.S. bond markets are closed for Columbus Day, while stocks trade regular hours. Bank of America, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase, Morgan Stanley and Wells Fargo all report third-quarter results this week, with September CPI Wednesday and PPI Thursday; Fed Chair Kevin Warsh appears Thursday at the IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok, per the Wall Street Journal. CNBC, citing FactSet’s John Butters, says the S&P 500 is on track for 29.6% third-quarter earnings growth, which would be a fourth straight quarter above 25% (Quartz).

Reuters (Oct. 12, 8:31 AM ET read). “US stock indexes were on track for a muted open on Monday,” Reuters wrote: Dow E-minis rose 70 points, or 0.13%, S&P 500 E-minis gained 4.25 points, or 0.05%, and Nasdaq 100 E-minis fell 68.25 points, or 0.22%. Brent traded above $106 a barrel, its highest in about two weeks, after deadly strikes on Riyadh’s international airport. In premarket movers, IREN rose 3.5% on a reported multi-year Anthropic cloud deal, Salesforce gained 4% on BofA’s double upgrade to “buy,” and IonQ slipped 4% after agreeing an all-stock deal to buy privately held cybersecurity firm Elisity valued at up to $1.3 billion. “The big themes to watch in earnings season include how companies are deploying and profiting from AI,” said Bellwether Wealth’s Clark Bellin (Reuters via Central Oregon Daily).

investingLive (Oct. 12, published 8:23 AM ET). U.S. futures implied the Dow up 56 points and the S&P 500 up 2.21 points, with the Nasdaq 100 down 96 points — “no broad agreement across the major indices heading toward the opening.” WTI crude futures traded at $93.04, up $1.19 or 1.30%, reversing softer Asian-session trade, while gold fell 0.55% to $4,171.43. The dollar was higher against all seven majors. With the cash Treasury market closed, investingLive listed Friday’s closing yields — 2-year 4.7911%, 10-year 5.2421%, 30-year 5.6001% — and noted the 10-year reached 5.365% last week, its highest since 2002. The Houthis claimed strikes on Saudi airports and two Aramco facilities (the Aramco claims unverified), and Ukraine reported further Russian attacks on power infrastructure despite the announced energy ceasefire, which Moscow had not publicly confirmed (investingLive, Greg Michalowski).

AI vs. the rest. Yahoo Finance’s Jake Conley reports the S&P 500 is up roughly 13.5% this year, but an “old economy” index excluding AI-related names has gained just 5.5% since Jan. 1, slightly behind Europe’s STOXX 600 at about 5.6%. Reuters, via Yahoo’s live blog, said ICE gasoil futures fell 3% to $1,436 after Trump said Russia would immediately supply more than 300,000 metric tons of diesel, with another 500,000 tons in November (Yahoo Finance live).

Reuters (Oct. 12, 7:12 AM ET read). Dow E-minis rose 30 points, or 0.06%, S&P 500 E-minis gained 2.25 points, or 0.03%, and Nasdaq 100 E-minis fell 73 points, or 0.23%. Brent crude rose to its highest in about two weeks, above $106 a barrel, after deadly strikes on Riyadh’s international airport, a rise contained by Trump’s announcement that Ukraine and Russia had agreed to halt attacks on each other’s energy facilities; “Any hope that Trump’s deal with Putin might help bring down prices has been negated by the flare-up in the Houthi conflict,” said IG’s Chris Beauchamp. Reuters said higher energy prices are expected to push headline CPI up 3.7% annually (core 2.5%) — a tenth above the 3.6% in Friday’s Reuters poll cited below — and that markets price just a 19% chance of a rate hike this month, per CME FedWatch; S&P 500 third-quarter earnings are expected to jump 30.6%, per LSEG IBES (Reuters via AOL). Yahoo Finance’s 6 AM ET update noted the bull market turned four on Monday and put Brent at $103 and WTI at $91 at that earlier read; Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia’s main airport killed 12 over the weekend (Yahoo Finance).

Weekend news. President Trump declared an “energy ceasefire” between Russia and Ukraine and a diesel arrangement under which Russia would release an initial 300,000 tons of fuel; Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy quickly accused Moscow of breaking the ceasefire, and traders largely shrugged it off because the volume is below a single day of U.S. consumption, per the Journal. Brent crude was up roughly 1% in early trading and diesel futures dipped. Trump also said he was weighing whether the U.S. should join Saudi Arabia in operations against Iran-backed Houthi militants after a lethal strike on Riyadh’s main airport. Overseas, European indexes rose broadly, South Korea’s Kospi fell 3.65% and the Kosdaq 4.83%, and Japan was closed for a holiday (Quartz).

Week ahead (Reuters, Oct. 9). JPMorgan, Goldman Sachs, Citigroup and Wells Fargo report Tuesday, and Morgan Stanley and Bank of America on Wednesday, “rounding out reports from the six largest lenders,” Reuters wrote Friday; Johnson & Johnson, UnitedHealth and BlackRock also report. S&P 500 third-quarter earnings are expected to have jumped by more than 30%, per LSEG IBES, while the S&P 500 banks index is down 9% over the past month amid rising Treasury yields. Wednesday’s September CPI is expected to show a 3.6% annual rise (core 2.5%) in a Reuters poll — among the last key releases before the Fed’s Oct. 27–28 meeting, after it “hiked rates last month for the first time since 2023.” Reuters put the 10-year yield “last around 5.23%, after recently reaching its highest level in 24 years” (Reuters via Kitco, Oct. 9).

Data consensus. September CPI lands Wednesday at 8:30 AM ET, with headline expected +0.6% m/m (prior +0.4%) and 3.6% y/y (prior 3.4%), and core +0.2% m/m (prior +0.3%) and 2.5% y/y (prior 2.4%). Thursday’s 8:30 AM window brings PPI (+0.5% m/m expected), retail sales (+0.3% vs. +1.2% prior), initial jobless claims (195K expected) and the Philadelphia Fed index (26.5 vs. 37.8) all at once; Fed Chair Kevin Warsh speaks late Thursday, and September import/export prices follow Friday (investingLive, Greg Michalowski, Oct. 9).

JPMorgan bar. For Tuesday’s report, analysts expect JPMorgan’s third-quarter earnings to rise 16.4% to $5.90 a share on revenue up 8.8% to $51.3bn, and options imply a move of about 3.3% after results, CMC Markets’ Michael Kramer wrote Friday; the cash Treasury market is shut Monday for Columbus Day while equity markets and Treasury futures stay open (CMC Markets, Oct. 9).

Rate-hike odds. “Traders have reduced expectations the Fed will increase rates again at its October 27-28 meeting,” Reuters reported, though investors said data showing a hotter economy — including next week’s producer-price and retail-sales reports — “could revive hiking bets going forward” (Reuters via Kitco, Oct. 9).

Beyond the banks (Saxo). The Cleveland Fed’s nowcast puts September CPI at +0.5% m/m and +3.6% y/y, with core at +0.2% m/m and +2.4% y/y; a core print that soft “might just be enough for the Fed to wait until its December meeting to raise rates again,” Saxo’s Neil Wilson wrote. The KBW Bank Index is down around 13% since its August high. Chip bellwethers ASML (Wednesday) and TSMC also report, BP issues a Q3 trading update Tuesday with Brent “back above $100,” Fed Chair Kevin Warsh joins IMF chief Kristalina Georgieva for a fireside chat Friday at the IMF/World Bank meetings in Bangkok, and Tesla unveils its second-generation Roadster Thursday in Waco, Texas (Saxo, Oct. 9).

Friday, Oct. 9 — the session in review

Official close (4 PM ET). “The U.S. market is now closed. All four major indexes closed higher, while volatility declined modestly,” TheStreet wrote in its 4:00 PM ET market wrap: the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 423.37 points, or 0.83%, to 51,655.01; the Nasdaq Composite 172.83 points, or 0.64%, to 27,366.17; the S&P 500 46.15 points, or 0.59%, to 7,811.51; and the Russell 2000 0.52%. Real estate and health care were the dominant S&P 500 sectors, up 1.85% and 1.48%, while communication services fell 1.62%; earnings season “informally kicked off” with Delta’s disappointing third quarter, “clearing the way for bank earnings next week” (TheStreet). All three changes reconcile to the cent with Thursday’s closes; the S&P 500 and Nasdaq match Yahoo Finance’s closing strip, while Dow prints differ by cents across feeds (Yahoo 51,655.18; CNBC 51,654.95 per search listing). Yahoo said the S&P 500 and Nasdaq “remained on track for weekly gains after touching record highs earlier this week” (Yahoo Finance).

The week. Measured from the Oct. 2 closes in the Weekly Scorecard, the S&P 500 gained about 1.15% last week, the Dow about 0.93% and the Nasdaq about 0.65% — in line with CNBC’s weekly gains of roughly 1.2% for the S&P 500 and 0.9% for the Dow (per search listing). TheStreet’s 4:13 PM “Look Back” said stocks “had a good week, even despite the moves lower yesterday,” with “some tension in the tech portion of the market” and the Russell 2000 the only major index to decline on the week (TheStreet).
