The Lead

At the open (~9:35 AM ET): Dow −0.97%, S&P 500 −0.48%, Nasdaq −0.34% after Thursday’s tech rout

NewOpening bell. “Stocks were falling in early trading amid a sell-off in tech and AI stocks, rising geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and surging oil prices,” TheStreet reported at 9:35 AM ET: the S&P 500 slipped 0.48%, the Dow lost 0.97% and the Nasdaq Composite gave up 0.34%, while the Russell 2000 rose 0.11% (TheStreet). Yahoo Finance’s market strip at about 9:31 AM ET (“markets close in 6h 29m”) had the S&P 500 at 7,629.19 (−34.24, −0.45%), the Dow at 52,331.20 (−536.78, −1.02%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 25,943.48 (−65.38, −0.25%) — each change reconciling to Thursday’s close — with the VIX at 20.22 (+6.25%), December WTI at $101.76 (+2.30%), gold $4,046.00 (−0.35%) and Bitcoin $81,689 (+0.10%) (Yahoo Finance).

NewWhy it’s still about AI capex. Capital.com’s Kyle Rodda told TheStreet that the script has flipped: rather than hyperscalers spending too much, the worry is “that future capex could be lower than previously thought and the rate of growth in investment could be slowing down,” after Meta left its capex guidance unchanged and Tesla cut its full-year capex guidance by 20%. TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre: “When those leaders are the ones being sold, and the average stock is going up, a model built on the cap-weighted indexes tells you to get out right as money is moving into the rest of the market” (TheStreet).

NewBonds and the Fed. Thursday’s flight to bonds gave the 10-year Treasury yield its biggest one-day drop in more than two years and pushed it back below 5%; it was 4.965% (+0.012) on Friday morning. Fed funds futures now put the odds of a hike at next week’s meeting (Oct. 28) at about 5%, per Bloomberg data, down from 15% earlier this week and 37% at the end of September (Yahoo Finance).

NewOil and geopolitics. WTI jumped 1.56% to $101 a barrel and Brent 1.17% to $114.50 early Friday, with Brent at its highest in more than five months after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia. U.S. U.N. Ambassador Mike Waltz told the Financial Times the war with Iran could last another year; separately, a tanker carrying Russian diesel sailed for New York — the first such shipment since President Trump and President Putin agreed to release more fuel — and is due Nov. 10 (TheStreet).

Thursday’s close (for reference). The Nasdaq Composite fell 694.00 points, or 2.60%, to 26,008.86, its steepest drop since early June; the S&P 500 lost 93.74, or 1.21%, to 7,663.43; and the Dow rose 11.89, or 0.02%, to 52,867.98 (Yahoo Finance ^DJI, at close). The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 6.7%, Meta 7.0% and Tesla 3.1%, while Spotify (+11.1%), Blackstone (+8.4%) and Lockheed Martin (+7.0%) rallied; the 10-year yield closed at 4.953% (−0.193) and the 30-year at 5.29% (Reuters closing wrap and Yahoo closing strip; see Weekly Scorecard).

Last Night’s After-Hours Movers (Thu., Oct. 22)

Point-in-time readings from Thursday evening; see Movers above for this morning’s premarket moves. A fresh After-Hours block returns after Friday’s 4 PM close.

RingCentral (RNG) — up 15.2% after hours. Shares rose 15.2% after hours to $91.50, from a regular-session close down 3.64%, after third-quarter adjusted EPS of $1.33 topped the $1.28 consensus and revenue reached $676 million, up 5.9% year over year. RingCentral raised its 2026 EPS guidance to $5.07–$5.12 (consensus $5.03), though its full-year revenue forecast of $2.56–$2.57 billion fell short of the $2.64 billion expected (Investing.com, 4:35 PM ET).

Western Digital (WDC) — beat, then reversal. Shares initially rose about 4% after hours on the stronger-than-expected results and guidance (GuruFocus), then turned lower: Investing.com reported the stock fell 6.43% in the regular session to $354.19 and another 4.85% after hours to $337.01, as rising input costs and higher capital expenditures overshadowed the beat amid the broader AI-stock selloff (Investing.com). Yahoo Finance reported the AI selloff erased the initial gains (Yahoo Finance).

Western Digital (WDC) — results. Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of $4.38 billion, up 55% year over year; non-GAAP gross margin 58.1% and operating margin 49.1%; non-GAAP diluted EPS $4.64 (GAAP $4.63); free cash flow $1.38 billion. Fiscal Q2 guidance: revenue $5.1 billion ± $100 million (up 66%–72% y/y), non-GAAP gross margin 60%–61%, non-GAAP EPS $5.80 ± $0.20. Quarterly dividend raised 17% to $0.175. Conference call 4:30 PM ET (WD release, SEC 8-K). Pre-report consensus was about $4.08 EPS on $4.14 billion revenue (24/7 Wall St.).

Newmont (NEM) — results. Q3 2026: 1.3 million attributable gold ounces, on track for 5.3 million full-year guidance; AISC $1,454 per ounce, with full-year cost guidance improved to $925 CAS / $1,520 AISC; net income $2.5 billion, adjusted net income $2.3 billion or $2.20 per diluted share; adjusted EBITDA $4.0 billion; $0.26 quarterly dividend declared (Newmont release, SEC 8-K).
