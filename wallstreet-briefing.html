The Lead

New

Official close: Dow −561 points as insurers crater and Fed minutes turn hawkish; S&P 500 slips below its 200-day average

The close. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 561.25 points, or 1.11%, and the S&P 500 lost 0.23%, its fifth losing session in a row and its longest slide since June, CNBC said at 4:12 PM ET; the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.42% (StockTitan). The S&P 500 finished below its closely watched 200-day moving average (Motley Fool).

The Fed. Minutes of the October meeting showed almost all officials judged further rate rises would likely be appropriate. The Fed held its range at 3.75% to 4% in October; Lisa D. Cook and Beth M. Hammack preferred a quarter-point rise (Fed minutes, via StockTitan). Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC a half-point rise is probably needed at the December 8–9 meeting. The dollar hit a 19-month high (Motley Fool).

Insurers drive the Dow. Humana fell 13.04% and UnitedHealth 10.21% by the close, a second day of steep falls after a federal proposal to expand Medicare Advantage audits; Elevance fell 7.63% and Centene 7.06% (StockTitan).

Markets are closed. Figures are the official 4:00 PM ET close; after-hours moves are listed separately below.
