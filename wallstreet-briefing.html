The Lead

Official close (investingLive, 4:16 PM ET): Nasdaq +1.05%, S&P 500 +0.60%, Dow +0.69% — SpaceX relights the AI trade; after the bell (as of ~5:53 PM ET) IBM +6%, Meta up ~3%, Tesla slips ~2% on its call, Lam Research −6%

The close. The Nasdaq Composite rose 278.04 points, or 1.05%, to 26,702.86; the S&P 500 gained 45.96, or 0.60%, to 7,758.53; and the Dow added 361.22, or 0.69%, to 52,857.10. The Nasdaq 100 rose 1.13% while the Russell 2000 lagged (+0.16% per investingLive; Yahoo’s market strip shows +0.27%). Breadth was thin: 256 S&P 500 stocks advanced against 229 decliners (investingLive). Leaders included Super Micro (+9.55%), Strategy (+7.54%), Bloom Energy (+7.24%), Datadog (+6.50%), Dell (+5.27%) and CrowdStrike (+5.26%); AT&T (−6.45%), Celsius (−5.51%), Whirlpool (−5.35%) and Netflix (−5.31%) led decliners (investingLive).

What drove it. SpaceX reported Q3 revenue of $14.6 billion, up 161%, with its Super Intelligence unit (formerly the AI division) bringing in $7.56 billion, and guided Q1 2027 revenue to $33–$37 billion versus estimates near $23 billion; JPMorgan more than doubled its price target to $500. Its gain was its biggest one-day move since Aug. 7 (Benzinga). Elon Musk said SpaceX and Tesla have formed special committees to discuss a potential merger (Yahoo Finance).

Oil headline. Saudi Arabia’s state news agency reported an attack on an Aramco oil field in the Eastern Region this afternoon; Brent jumped more than $1.50 a barrel within minutes and was on track for its highest close since May 19, while stocks “barely flinched,” Yahoo’s Jared Blikre wrote (Yahoo Finance).

Meta after the bell. Meta reported Q3 revenue of $64.6 billion, up 26% and above the $63.37 billion estimate; operating income fell 37% to $12.99 billion, including a $9.89 billion legal charge; diluted EPS was $3.83. It guided Q4 revenue to $73–$76 billion and narrowed 2026 capex to $135–$145 billion (Meta release via StockTitan). It also plans a new Class C stock, with a one-time dividend of one Class C share per share held — effectively a 2-for-1 split. Shares were up 3.12% at $719.30 in after-hours trading at 4:21 PM ET (Benzinga).

Tesla after the bell. Q3 revenue was $28.05 billion, flat on the year and in line with the ~$28.06 billion consensus; adjusted EPS of $0.48 beat the $0.45 estimate. Gross margin rose to 19.6%, but operating income fell 56% to $718 million as AI and Optimus spending climbed; free cash flow was $1.53 billion. The earnings call was scheduled for 5:30 PM ET (Electrek). Shares rose more than 1% after hours (Investing.com’s 4:33 PM movers list had them up 2%) as automotive gross margin excluding regulatory credits improved to 18.1% from 15.4% a year earlier and 16.3% in Q2 (Investing.com, updated 4:45 PM ET).

Tesla’s call. The early after-hours gain faded: Stocktwits described the shares as flat at 5:13 PM ET, and by 5:53 PM ET Yahoo’s quote showed them at $378.57, down 2.04% from a $386.47 close (+3.41%). On the call Tesla cut its full-year capex guidance to $20 billion from $25 billion, which CFO Vaibhav Taneja said it plans to fund “primarily from our cash flows from operations”; free cash flow of $1.53 billion compared with an estimated −$4.92 billion. Musk said reaching an annualized rate of 1 million Optimus units would take “probably at least a year—maybe a little longer,” and on a SpaceX merger: “I think I said everything that should be said yesterday” (Yahoo Finance, 5:51 PM ET) (Stocktwits).

Lam Research after the bell. Shares slipped 6% after hours despite fiscal Q1 non-GAAP EPS of $2.20 on $8.22 billion revenue, ahead of the $2.18 and $8.13 billion estimates; Investing.com pointed to a wide Q2 guidance range ($8.2–$9.0 billion revenue, $2.18–$2.48 EPS) after a nearly 80% run in the stock (Investing.com).

IBM after the bell. Shares jumped 6% after a fiscal Q3 beat: adjusted EPS of $2.95 versus the $2.86 estimate on revenue of $17.1 billion versus $16.82 billion expected; management reaffirmed its full-year constant-currency revenue growth target of 4%–5% (Investing.com, 4:33 PM ET).

▼ NFLX

▼ WING

After-Hours Movers

Meta (META): up 3.12% to $719.30 in after-hours trading at 4:21 PM ET after revenue of $64.6 billion beat the $63.37 billion estimate; Q4 revenue guide $73–$76 billion; planned Class C share dividend works like a 2-for-1 split (Benzinga).

Tesla (TSLA): closed up 3.41% at $386.47, then turned lower after hours — $378.57, down 2.04%, at 5:53 PM ET during and after the earnings call (Yahoo Finance); it had been up more than 1% at 4:45 PM (Investing.com). Adjusted EPS $0.48 vs $0.44 expected; free cash flow about $1.5 billion vs a forecast outflow (investingLive); 2026 capex guide cut to $20 billion from $25 billion (Yahoo Finance).

Lam Research (LRCX): down 6% after hours despite a beat — non-GAAP EPS $2.20 vs $2.18 and revenue $8.22 billion vs $8.13 billion; non-GAAP gross margin 52.4%, operating margin 40.2% (Investing.com, 4:22 PM ET). Peers KLA and Applied Materials traded down in sympathy (Investing.com).

IBM (IBM): up 6% after hours — adjusted EPS $2.95 vs $2.86 est.; revenue $17.1 billion vs $16.82 billion; full-year constant-currency growth target of 4%–5% reaffirmed (Investing.com, 4:33 PM ET).

Kinder Morgan (KMI): up 3% on a Q3 beat — EPS $0.35 on $4.53 billion revenue — even as full-year 2026 EPS guidance of $1.36 trailed the $1.56 consensus (Investing.com).

Las Vegas Sands (LVS): down 4% after missing — Q3 EPS $0.54 vs $0.73 est.; revenue $3.22 billion vs $3.33 billion, citing lower VIP hold and a slower Macao mass-market recovery (Investing.com).

Texas Instruments (TXN): Q3 EPS $2.62 on $6.06 billion revenue; Q4 revenue guided to $5.8–$6.3 billion (AP, via investingLive). No after-hours move verified.
