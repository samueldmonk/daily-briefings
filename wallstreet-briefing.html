NewModerna trims losses. Moderna opened lower Friday but climbed into positive territory during the session as investors digested data from a late-stage trial of its personalized melanoma vaccine, rebounding from Thursday’s sharp sell-off (Yahoo Finance, Ines Ferré).

Early afternoon. Yahoo Finance’s market strip at about 12:51 PM ET (“U.S. markets close in 3h 9m”; ^DJI quote “as of 12:51:12 PM EDT”) had the S&P 500 at 7,647.16 (−16.27, −0.21%), the Dow at 52,367.92 (−500.06, −0.95%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,051.19 (+42.33, +0.16%) — each change reconciling to Thursday’s close — with the Russell 2000 at 2,776.43 (−0.27%), the VIX at 19.51 (+2.52%), the 10-year yield at 4.992% (+0.039), December WTI at $102.80 (+3.35%), gold at $4,017.00 (−1.07%) and Bitcoin at $81,661 (+0.60%). SpaceX was at $147.49 (+1.89%) and Tesla at $372.69 (−0.50%) as of 12:51 PM ET. The 10-year “held below the 5% threshold as investors flocked to bonds,” Yahoo reported (Yahoo Finance).

SK Hynix pulls Solidigm IPO. SK Hynix said Friday it has shelved plans to list Solidigm, its flash-memory unit, in the U.S., citing market conditions and share-price trends; Solidigm had aimed to raise $10 billion to $15 billion at a valuation of up to $150 billion, per industry estimates cited by the Wall Street Journal. SK Hynix priced its own U.S. shares at $149 in July — raising $26.5 billion, the biggest U.S. listing ever by a foreign company — and the stock, though rising Friday, still trades below that price, about a third below its September record (Yahoo Finance, Jared Blikre).

Around midday. Yahoo Finance’s market strip at about 12:05 PM ET (“U.S. markets close in 3h 55m”; ^DJI quote “as of 12:05:26 PM EDT”) had the S&P 500 at 7,652.91 (−10.52, −0.14%), the Dow at 52,359.82 (−508.16, −0.96%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,080.48 (+71.63, +0.28%) — each change reconciling to Thursday’s close — with the Russell 2000 at 2,774.70 (−0.33%), the VIX at 19.34 (+1.63%), the 10-year yield at 4.999% (+0.046), December WTI at $103.01 (+3.56%), gold at $4,016.60 (−1.08%) and Bitcoin at $81,598 (+0.52%). “US stocks were mixed on Friday as worries over the AI trade lingered,” Yahoo reported: Nvidia and AMD edged higher, SpaceX, Tesla and Meta stabilized after Thursday’s declines, and Brent hovered near $112 a barrel (Yahoo Finance).

Apple vs. Nvidia. Apple briefly topped Nvidia in market value on Friday — the fifth time it has taken the top spot back — with each worth about $5.1 trillion, on the day its $1,999 iPhone Duo hit stores; Nvidia has since moved back into the lead (Yahoo Finance, Jared Blikre). Apple’s first foldable went on sale worldwide with sales rationed, and Apple shares were down 0.22% in early-afternoon trading (TheStreet, citing Reuters, 12:14 PM ET).

Late morning. Yahoo Finance’s market strip at about 11:43 AM ET (“markets close in 4h 17m”; ^GSPC quote “as of 11:43:53 AM EDT”) had the S&P 500 at 7,646.58 (−16.85, −0.22%), the Dow at 52,313.78 (−554.20, −1.05%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,045.05 (+36.19, +0.14%) — each change reconciling to Thursday’s close — with the Russell 2000 at 2,776.40 (−0.27%), the VIX at 18.94 (−0.47%), the 10-year yield at 4.992% (+0.039), December WTI at $102.53 (+3.08%), gold at $4,009.80 (−1.25%) and Bitcoin at $81,366 (−0.27%). The S&P 500’s range so far is 7,612.07–7,660.15 (Yahoo Finance ^GSPC). That is a rebound from about 10:32 AM ET, when the Dow was off 1.39%, the S&P 500 0.50% and the Nasdaq 0.07% and the Russell 2000 1.02%. At the open, Yahoo noted the Nasdaq was on pace for a second straight weekly loss, the Dow for a third straight weekly gain and the S&P 500 near flat for the week (Yahoo Finance).

Consumers near a record low. The University of Michigan’s consumer sentiment index slipped to 48.0 in October from 48.1 in September — above the 46.3 economists expected (also the preliminary reading) but the second-lowest on record after May’s 44.8. One-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7% from 4.6%, and five-year expectations to 3.5% from 3.4%. “Sentiment is now 10% below a year ago and 15% less than February, just prior to the Iran conflict,” said survey director Joanne Hsu (TheStreet, citing Reuters, 11:06 AM ET).

At the open. “Stocks were falling in early trading amid a sell-off in tech and AI stocks, rising geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and surging oil prices,” TheStreet reported at 9:35 AM ET: the S&P 500 slipped 0.48%, the Dow lost 0.97% and the Nasdaq Composite gave up 0.34%, while the Russell 2000 rose 0.11% (TheStreet). Yahoo Finance’s market strip at about 9:31 AM ET (“markets close in 6h 29m”) had the S&P 500 at 7,629.19 (−34.24, −0.45%), the Dow at 52,331.20 (−536.78, −1.02%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 25,943.48 (−65.38, −0.25%) — each change reconciling to Thursday’s close — with the VIX at 20.22 (+6.25%), December WTI at $101.76 (+2.30%), gold $4,046.00 (−0.35%) and Bitcoin $81,689 (+0.10%) (Yahoo Finance).

Why it’s still about AI capex. Capital.com’s Kyle Rodda told TheStreet that the script has flipped: rather than hyperscalers spending too much, the worry is “that future capex could be lower than previously thought and the rate of growth in investment could be slowing down,” after Meta left its capex guidance unchanged and Tesla cut its full-year capex guidance by 20%. (Yahoo Finance notes Meta raised the low end of its capex range to $135–$145 billion from $130–$145 billion; Meta fell 7% Thursday — Yahoo Finance.) TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre: “When those leaders are the ones being sold, and the average stock is going up, a model built on the cap-weighted indexes tells you to get out right as money is moving into the rest of the market” (TheStreet).

Bonds and the Fed. Thursday’s flight to bonds gave the 10-year Treasury yield its biggest one-day drop in more than two years and pushed it back below 5%; it was 4.965% (+0.012) on Friday morning. Fed funds futures now put the odds of a hike at next week’s meeting (Oct. 28) at about 5%, per Bloomberg data, down from 15% earlier this week and 37% at the end of September (Yahoo Finance).

Oil and geopolitics. WTI jumped 1.56% to $101 a barrel and Brent 1.17% to $114.50 early Friday, with Brent at its highest in more than five months after Houthi attacks in Saudi Arabia. U.S. U.N. Ambassador Mike Waltz told the Financial Times the war with Iran could last another year; separately, a tanker carrying Russian diesel sailed for New York — the first such shipment since President Trump and President Putin agreed to release more fuel — and is due Nov. 10 (TheStreet).

Thursday’s close (for reference). The Nasdaq Composite fell 694.00 points, or 2.60%, to 26,008.86, its steepest drop since early June; the S&P 500 lost 93.74, or 1.21%, to 7,663.43; and the Dow rose 11.89, or 0.02%, to 52,867.98 (Yahoo Finance ^DJI, at close). The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell 6.7%, Meta 7.0% and Tesla 3.1%, while Spotify (+11.1%), Blackstone (+8.4%) and Lockheed Martin (+7.0%) rallied; the 10-year yield closed at 4.953% (−0.193) and the 30-year at 5.29% (Reuters closing wrap and Yahoo closing strip; see Weekly Scorecard).
