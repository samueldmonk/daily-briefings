The Lead

A good Friday, a bad week — and a Fed meeting on Wednesday

U.S. markets are closed for the weekend. Everything below is Friday 11 September’s official closes; the next session opens Monday 14 September.

Stocks opened higher on Friday and stayed there, snapping a four-session losing streak for all three major averages. It was not enough to save the week. The S&P 500 fell 0.8%, the Dow fell 1.6% and the Nasdaq fell 0.7% over the five sessions — Friday’s bounce trimmed the damage without undoing it.

The August CPI report was the week’s hinge. Headline inflation rose 0.4% on the month and 3.4% on the year, both in line with consensus, but core rose 0.3% against a 0.2% estimate. Traders read the print as clearing the way for a hike: Investrade’s closing recap puts the implied probability of a Fed rate rise next week at about 90%, up from roughly 70% before the release, and CNBC reports the same ~90% figure from CME FedWatch. The same recap elsewhere describes it as “better than 50%”, so treat the precise number as a range rather than a settled figure.

The other half of Friday’s story was Oracle. Its results and capital-spending plans lit up the AI-infrastructure supply chain beneath it — Dell and Hewlett Packard Enterprise each jumped roughly 11–12%, their largest single-session advances of the year — even as Oracle itself gave back almost all of a 10% pop.

Underneath it all, yields. The Treasury Yields Snapshot for 11 September puts the 10-year at 4.96% and the 2-year at 4.63%, after the 10-year touched its highest intraday level since October 2023 earlier in the week.

Movers & Drivers

NewRecord highAI infrastructure

Dell Technologies — a record close on Oracle’s spending plan

Dell posted its largest single-session advance of the year and a record high, with reads of the move running from “almost 11%” in the CNBC/Seeking Alpha movers wrap to +11.98% in a session recap that puts the market capitalisation above $360 billion. Two catalysts landed together: RBC Capital Markets initiated coverage at Outperform, citing AI infrastructure, AI PCs, storage, edge computing and PC-refresh demand; and Oracle reiterated on its earnings call a fiscal-2027 capital-expenditure plan of $90–$95 billion for AI data centres, naming Dell and HPE as principal recipients.

S&P leaderAI infrastructure

Hewlett Packard Enterprise — up about 11%

HPE led the S&P 500 on Friday on the same Oracle read-through, with one session report putting the close at $61.49. HPE had announced on 2 September that it was extending its collaboration with Oracle, deploying HPE Juniper Networking across Oracle’s AI data centres. Reads of the size of the move span +9.08% on an 11:50 AM board, “up nearly 11%” in the session review and “over 12%” in a third read — the session review is the later measurement.

M&AChart of the Day

ACV Auctions — +44.8% on a Copart bid

Copart agreed to acquire the digital dealer auction platform for $10.50 a share in cash, implying an equity value of about $1.9 billion — a premium of roughly 45%. Both boards approved unanimously; completion is targeted for the end of 2026 subject to customary conditions including antitrust review. Copart will fund it with cash on hand and expects the deal to be neutral to EPS in the first full year and accretive from FY2028. Session reads of ACVA’s move run from +40% to +45%; 44.8% is the regular-session figure.

EarningsCloud

Oracle — the catalyst that did not keep its own gain

Oracle’s Q1 revenue rose 30% year over year, with cloud revenue up 62% to $11.6 billion and infrastructure revenue surging 121% to $7.4 billion. Remaining performance obligations jumped $209 billion to $664 billion, including more than $30 billion in new AI cloud contracts, and the company delivered more than 300,000 GPUs to AI cloud customers during the quarter. The shares rose as much as 10.3% intraday and surrendered nearly all of it, finishing up about 0.3% — the gains went to the suppliers instead.

LaggardsHealth care

Health care and utilities were the drag

Health care and utilities were the two sectors that finished lower. The biggest Dow losers were UnitedHealth (−2.47%), Amgen (−1.34%) and Merck (−0.47%). MedTech stayed the weak spot — ResMed fell for a ninth straight day and Stryker also fell for a ninth, hitting 52-week lows after flagging manufacturing issues in its peripheral vascular business extending into the fourth quarter.

On the Radar

  • FOMC meets Tuesday and Wednesday, 15–16 September, with the decision at 2:00 PM ET on Wednesday and Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference at 2:30. Post-CPI pricing implies roughly a 90% chance of a rate rise — a hike, not a cut.
  • A crowded central-bank week beyond the Fed. The BCB announces Wednesday, the Bank of England on Thursday 17 September, and the Bank of Japan on Friday, where a hike is expected. The ECB raised rates this past week as expected, and Bundesbank’s Nagel said rates may have to rise further.
  • U.S. data: the September New York and Philadelphia Fed indices, August retail sales, industrial production and weekly jobless claims.
  • Oil is the inflation story now. U.S. diesel prices hit a record high as attacks along Middle East shipping routes stoked supply-disruption fears. The IEA cut its demand forecast and now expects world supply in 2026 to fall 5.7 million barrels a day, about 6%, up from a previously projected drop of around 4%.
  • Investors were already leaving. U.S. equity funds saw net sales of $32.27 billion in the week, the largest since $52.45 billion in the week to 17 December 2025, per LSEG Lipper data — with large-cap funds posting record weekly outflows of $40.44 billion.
