The Lead

Tech takes the lead by midday — Nasdaq +1.00%, S&P 500 +0.57% as of ~11:17 AM ET

Midday (posted 15:17 UTC, ~11:17 AM ET). U.S. indexes climbed together, led by the Nasdaq Composite up 1.00%; the S&P 500 advanced 0.57% and the Dow 0.16% (Phemex News, citing Binance market data) — the Dow turning higher after its mid-morning dip.

Early session detail (Reuters, 9:57 AM ET). The S&P 500 was up 0.16% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.38% while the Dow slipped 0.14%, weighed by a 3.4% drop in Sherwin-Williams after a quarterly profit miss; six of 11 S&P 500 sectors advanced, led by tech, with energy lagging. Chip stocks (.SOX) rose 1.8% for a third straight gain, Tesla climbed close to 3% and Meta fell 1.1% after upsizing its raise. Markets are pricing at least two more Fed hikes by mid-2027 after last month’s increase. Advancers led decliners 1.16-to-1 on the NYSE and 1.24-to-1 on the Nasdaq (Reuters via Kitco).

Mid-morning (~10:29 AM ET). The Dow was down 0.19% (−99.03 points), the S&P 500 up 0.04%, the Nasdaq Composite up 0.26% and the Russell 2000 up 0.21%, with the Nasdaq outperforming behind technology (XLK +0.72%); Boeing weighed on the Dow while UPS helped, and Coca-Cola was little changed after beating estimates (Investrade/Hammerstone, Mid-Morning Look). At the 9:31 AM open the S&P 500 was up 0.31%, the Dow 0.04%, the Nasdaq 0.48% and the Russell 2000 0.38% (TheStreet).

Data beat. The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index rose to 91.7 in October vs. 80.4 expected and 84.2 in September (revised from 81.9); September durable-goods orders rose 1.8% vs. 1.7% expected; new home sales ran at a 676,000 annual rate vs. 650,000 expected; and FHFA house prices rose 0.3% in August (Investrade).

AI capital raising. Meta raised its largest-ever equity fundraising to $97.5 billion, lifting the common-stock piece from $22.5 billion to $25 billion priced at $625.00 a share (Investrade), and Nvidia said it will invest $3 billion in Meta’s equity offering (Yahoo Finance). SpaceX rose 1.20% premarket to $148.66 after Anthropic said it would pay up to $120.5 billion for AI computing infrastructure through 2029, and GM jumped nearly 5% early on plans to move its listing from the NYSE to Nasdaq (TheStreet).

Fed, oil and yields. The FOMC begins its two-day meeting today; CME data priced only about 10% odds of a hike (Yahoo Finance). WTI was about $101.50 (−0.26) at mid-morning, the 10-year yield about 5.00%, the 30-year 5.306% and gold up $17 to $4,055.40 (Investrade); earlier, Yemen’s Houthis claimed a missile strike on Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery as Iran-related diplomacy continued (TheStreet, citing Al Jazeera/CBS).

Monday’s close. The S&P 500 gained 0.70% to 7,676.77, the Nasdaq Composite 0.82% to 26,169.38 and the Dow 0.28% to 52,361.58 (CappNotes; Motley Fool via Yahoo Finance; TradingKey), snapping a two-session slide as Anthropic publicly filed its S-1 to list on the Nasdaq under the ticker “ANT” (TradingKey). The filing showed $16.29 billion of first-half revenue and an $80.7 billion net loss; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index jumped 2.82% to 11,083.47 but is still down 12.2% for the month, and the Russell 2000 added 0.38% (CappNotes).

Breadth and sectors. Technology (+1.69%), industrials (+1.42%) and consumer discretionary (+1.03%) led, while communication services (−1.57%) lagged, using SPDR sector ETFs as proxies. NYSE advancers beat decliners by 1.36 to 1, but the Nasdaq still posted 349 new lows against 82 new highs on below-average volume; the VIX fell 5.45% to 18.39 (CappNotes).

AI capital needs stayed in focus. Meta fell 1.88% after launching a financing plan of about $95 billion — an underwritten offering of roughly $35 billion ($22.5 billion of Class A common stock and about $12.5 billion of mandatory convertible preferred depositary shares) plus an at-the-market program of about $60 billion that could start as early as the first quarter of 2027. SpaceX agreed a roughly $40 billion GPU lease financing with Apollo-managed entities to buy Nvidia’s next-generation Vera Rubin chips (TradingKey); its shares slipped 0.40% (CappNotes).

Brazil was the day’s shock. After Lula won the runoff with 51.79% of the vote, Brazil’s Ibovespa fell 13.88% — its worst day in six years, after a 30-minute circuit-breaker halt — the iShares MSCI Brazil ETF dropped 19.82%, and the real posted its biggest daily drop since 1999 (CappNotes).

Oil, bonds and gold. WTI settled about $101–$102 a barrel, down roughly 2% (−2.14% to $101.24 per TradingKey; −1.7% to $101.57 per CappNotes) on expectations of diplomatic progress in the Middle East. The 10-year yield was unchanged at 4.987%, while the 2-year rose 3.2 bp to 4.712% after a slightly soft $69 billion auction; gold settled at $4,038.40 for December delivery (CappNotes). Kalshi priced better than 90% odds of a hold at Wednesday’s FOMC, and CME FedWatch showed more than 70% odds of a December hike (CappNotes).

Earlier Monday. Stocks opened higher — the S&P 500 was up 0.33% at 9:32 AM ET (TheStreet) — and the S&P 500 was up 0.97% at 11:22 AM ET before settling back (TheStreet).

Friday’s final tally. The S&P 500 fell 39.75 points to 7,623.68, the Dow dropped 652.82 points to 52,215.16, and the Nasdaq Composite gave up 53.52 points to 25,955.34 (AP via Messenger-Inquirer). In percentage terms: Dow −1.23%, S&P 500 −0.52%, Nasdaq −0.21% (TradingKey). The S&P 500 added to Thursday’s decline and posted its second straight weekly loss; the Nasdaq slipped after giving up a modest gain, and nearly three of every four stocks in the S&P 500 fell (AP).

Last week, and this week. For the week the Dow finished up 0.41% while the S&P 500 fell 0.47% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.71%, with tech shares pressured after reports from Meta and Lam Research disappointed. The Fed decision Wednesday (2 p.m. ET statement, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference), Thursday’s core PCE and advance GDP data, and earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Intel, Caterpillar, Eli Lilly, Exxon and Chevron are the main catalysts. CME FedWatch showed an 88% probability of unchanged rates Wednesday, while futures pointed to roughly an 80% probability of a December hike; the Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023 (FXEmpire).
