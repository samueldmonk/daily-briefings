The Lead

At the close: Nasdaq +1.6%, S&P 500 +1.3%, Dow +0.5% as CoreWeave revives the AI trade

Official close. Stocks finished higher Wednesday while the bond market was closed for Veterans Day: the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.6% to 25,466, the S&P 500 added 1.3% to 7,619 and the Dow rose 0.5% to 52,486, with tech posting the biggest gains as Wall Street cheered CoreWeave’s earnings and even beaten-down Nvidia joined the rally (Kiplinger). The rebound followed Tuesday’s sell-off after hot October inflation data raised December rate-hike odds (Kiplinger). 24/7 Wall St.’s 4:00 PM ET closing card had the S&P 500 +1.29%, Dow +0.48% and Nasdaq +1.55%, saying the index built steadily through the afternoon and settled near its session highs, with growth far outpacing value (24/7 Wall St.) — its figures differ slightly from Kiplinger’s; final levels are in the Weekly Scorecard.

Final tally. TheStreet’s 4:19 PM ET market wrap — “Tech does the heavy lifting” — has the Nasdaq up 400.31 points (+1.60%), the S&P 500 up 98.45 (+1.31%), the Dow up 254.60 (+0.49%) and the Russell 2000 up 24.88 (+0.88%); all four changes reconcile with Tuesday’s closes (TheStreet). Levels are in the Weekly Scorecard.

Into the final half-hour, Yahoo Finance’s strip (U.S. markets closing in 37 minutes, ≈3:23 PM ET) had the S&P 500 up 101.72 points or 1.35%, the Dow up 246.36 or 0.47% and the Nasdaq up 424.24 or 1.69% — all reconciling with Tuesday’s closes — with the Russell 2000 +0.86% and the VIX down 10.33% to 17.45; Yahoo’s Nasdaq quote stamped 3:24:11 PM ET read +1.68% (Yahoo Finance).

Earlier, 24/7 Wall St.’s 12:00 PM ET midday snapshot had the S&P 500 up 1.32%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 0.10% and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.98%, with the S&P 500 “trading at 7,620 at midday” after adding to gains through the first half of trading — the Nasdaq “more than doubling” the S&P’s advance while the Dow is “essentially flat” (24/7 Wall St.). At 9:30 AM ET the same desk had the S&P 500 +0.78%, Dow −0.14% and Nasdaq +1.31% (24/7 Wall St.).

Before that, Yahoo Finance’s market strip, with U.S. markets closing in 5 hours 6 minutes (≈10:54 AM ET), showed the S&P 500 at 7,603.10, up 82.17 points or 1.09%; the Nasdaq Composite at 25,510.46, up 444.59 or 1.77%; and the Dow at 52,141.10, down 90.79 or 0.17% — all three reconcile exactly with Tuesday’s official closes. Yahoo’s Nasdaq quote stamped 10:55:05 AM ET read 25,512.72, +1.78%, and its Dow quote at 10:55:10 AM ET 52,137.76, −0.18%. At the 9:30 AM ET bell, Reuters had the Dow up 0.05% at 52,259.02, the S&P 500 up 0.97% at 7,593.95 and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.54% at 25,451.05 (Reuters via Investing.com); TheStreet’s 9:33 AM read had the S&P 500 +1.05%, Dow +0.16%, Nasdaq +1.64% and the Russell 2000 −0.68% (TheStreet). Before the bell Nasdaq futures had been up 1.9%, S&P 500 futures 0.9% and Dow futures 0.2% (Investopedia via Yahoo Finance).

Why it’s up: chip stocks surged after CoreWeave’s quarterly results eased worries about AI demand and financing. Nvidia rose for the first time since CEO Jensen Huang admitted to an affair with a woman suspected of being a Chinese spy — it was up 4.00% at $175.54 as of 10:55 AM ET — though Yahoo notes the stock is still down about 14% in November and has lost about $700 billion in market value; the bounce came despite a Baird downgrade to Neutral (Yahoo Finance). TheStreet says Nvidia’s two-day sell-off had erased more than $430 billion in market value (TheStreet). Investopedia said the market has been especially volatile in the week since Democrats’ decisive midterm victory, which sparked investor concerns that AI development could stall under new leadership in Washington and key statehouses (Investopedia).

Rates backdrop: the stock market is open for Veterans Day but the bond market is closed. On Tuesday the 10-year Treasury yield briefly spiked above 5% again as traders recalibrated bets on higher rates after the hot October CPI; Fed Governor Michelle Bowman said, “There is still work to do to bring inflation closer to 2%” (Yahoo Finance). It is a quiet day for earnings and economic data, with an MBA mortgage-rate update due (Yahoo Finance). No fresher time-stamped index read was available at ~3:05 PM ET (Yahoo’s strip was still cached at ≈2:28 PM). An untimed Benzinga afternoon snapshot had the S&P 500 up 1.42%, the Dow up 0.23% (121 points) and the Nasdaq 100 up 2.77%, with the Russell 2000 +0.79% and the VIX −8.4% to 17.82 (Benzinga via TradingView).

Movers & Drivers

Up

Apple sets a record — $5.16 trillion valuation

Apple (+1.96%) notched a fresh record before the close, eclipsing $355.42 and lifting its valuation to $5.16 trillion, its highest ever; the shares are up 31.1% this year (TheStreet, 3:48 PM ET — TheStreet).

Up

CoreWeave +24.7% — best day since June 2025

Shares jumped 24.7%, their biggest one-day percentage gain since June 3, 2025, after Q3 showed a narrower-than-expected loss of $1.27 a share on revenue of $3.66 billion; CEO Michael Intrator said CoreWeave “will not use the unsecured debt market to fund” the business. The stock had been down nearly 32% year to date coming into the session (Kiplinger). 24/7 Wall St.’s 4:10 PM ET movers board had it at $60.51, +24.2% (24/7 Wall St.).

Up

Up

Nvidia +2.9% despite Baird downgrade

Wall Street bought the dip: Nvidia closed up 2.9% even after Baird cut it to Neutral and Morgan Stanley removed it from its Top Pick list while keeping Overweight. The stock was down 9.5% for the week coming into the session (Kiplinger).

Up

Up

Bloom Energy jumps about 16% as power stocks rally

AI power-provider stocks rallied after CoreWeave’s results eased worries about how data-center build-outs get funded; Bloom Energy was one of the biggest winners, up about 16% by midday, per Yahoo’s Page Oamek. Its late-October Q3 showed record revenue of $1.24 billion (+138% y/y) and a third raise to 2026 revenue guidance, to $4.4–$4.6 billion, but operating cash flow of −$27.1 million and capex of $153.5 million (Yahoo Finance).

Quantum split: D-Wave up, Rigetti down

D-Wave missed on adjusted EPS (−$0.13 vs. −$0.10 expected) but posted revenue of $4.5 million, up 21% year over year, with bookings up 611%; Rigetti fell after an adjusted loss of $0.06 vs. −$0.05 expected and revenue of $1.4 million vs. roughly $3.5 million expected, plus worries about elevated capex (Yahoo Finance).

Anthropic debut could draw record retail buying — Vanda

Vanda Research estimates everyday investors could pour a record $250 million into Anthropic on its first trading day — more than double the $118 million of net retail buying on SpaceX’s first day — based on Hyperliquid derivatives tied to the unlisted shares, whose volume hit a record $270 million Tuesday; Vanda flags considerable uncertainty. Anthropic is targeting a $1.4 trillion valuation, below the $2 trillion previously discussed, with listing planned for next week (Yahoo Finance).
