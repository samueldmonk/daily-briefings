Top Story

The KEV list stops being theoretical: two of the seven September 2 flaws are dropping reverse shells

Unknown threat actors have been observed weaponizing CVE-2026-9586 (Sangoma Switchvox, SQL injection) and CVE-2026-82329 (JFrog Artifactory, improper authentication) to deploy reverse shells and mint admin tokens, then use those tokens for follow-on enumeration of users, groups, credential sets and federated access topologies.

Both CVEs are on the seven-flaw batch CISA added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 2, and both sit in the sub-group whose federal remediation date is September 5 — tomorrow. That moves this batch from a compliance deadline to an active incident: the token-minting step in particular means a successful Artifactory compromise does not end at the artifact repository, it becomes a map of the identity estate around it.

What this does not say. No source fetched this run names the actor, attributes the campaign, or gives a victim count, and none is asserted here.

Patch Priority

CVE-2026-83548 — SonicWall SMA1000, pre-auth SSRF, CVSS 10.0 — due September 5, 2026 (1 day left)

A pre-authentication server-side request forgery in the SMA1000 Appliance Work Place interface, scored 10.0 by SonicWall. Affects the 6210, 7210 and 8200v, including all supported hypervisor deployments; hotfixes were released September 1. It is the only item in that seven-CVE batch for which a vendor score was returned this run, and that score is the maximum; the batch is now demonstrably being exploited, and this deadline is the nearest. Patch it first.

This date is the same one used in the KEV section and the countdown below. No CISA directive is named on this page. So the due dates are stated as published by CISA and reported by the corroborating trackers, without asserting the mechanism behind them.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NewLiving off trusted software

The Faronics Deploy operator — a signed installer as the entire intrusion

Documented by Huntress and reported in early September. Phishing emails disguised as invoices, tax documents, financial records and event invitations prompt the victim to download and run a legitimate, signed Faronics Deploy installer dressed up as an Adobe document, a reader app or a plugin update. On install, the machine enrols itself into a Faronics deployment the attacker controls, handing them remote script execution at administrative privilege. From there the operator chains to ScreenConnect, so the remote-access traffic rides inside a workflow the defender already trusts.

Scale and outcome: more than 457 endpoints encountered Faronics-related lures over one month. Malicious activity dropped significantly from August 21, which Huntress reads as the vendor’s countermeasures working; Faronics has since added detection and blocking for fraudulent account registrations, including ones using impersonated organisations.

Breaches & Incidents

NewCollaboration

Microsoft Teams adds QR-code protection controls

Microsoft is preparing new QR-code protection controls for Teams, aimed at phishing and fraud in chats involving external users. A control, not an incident — included because QR lures in chat are one of the few phishing surfaces most mail gateways never see.

IoTPersistence

Operation CameraSwarm — 14,000+ Dahua cameras, one operator

At least 14,530 Dahua IP cameras compromised by a single operator over 35 days (17 June – 22 July 2026) along three concurrent paths: brute force on TCP 37777 (12,324 unique IPs), auth bypass via CVE-2021-33044 / CVE-2021-33045 (1,923 cameras), and a cloud-relay attack on 283 cameras behind NAT using only serial numbers and SDK credentials embedded in Dahua’s own applications. The backdoor account p2pwn / p2password survives an admin password change and, on most affected firmware, a factory reset. Concentrated in Ukraine and Russia.

macOSInfostealer

Fourteen fake macOS installers dropping a credential-stealing RAT

Mac users are being targeted with 14 fake application installers that present as familiar software and instead launch a credential-stealing remote-access trojan. No victim count is stated in any source fetched this run, so none is published.

Data I/O fully operational

All global systems were operational again by September 4 following its ransomware breach, with no reported customer, order or revenue losses.
