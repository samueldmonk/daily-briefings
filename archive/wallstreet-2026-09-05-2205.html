The Lead

A hot jobs number closed the week red and put a September rate hike back on the table

U.S. equity markets are closed — it is Saturday, and Monday, September 7 is the Labor Day holiday, with both stock and bond markets shut. The next session opens Tuesday, September 8. The figures below are Friday’s official close, re-verified this run.

Friday, September 4: the Dow fell 271.86 points, or 0.51%, to 53,414.25; the S&P 500 slid 0.38% to 7,718.60; the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.29% to 26,506.99. The trigger was the August employment report: nonfarm payrolls grew 162,000, far above the 53,000 expected by economists in CNBC’s Dow Jones survey (TheStreet cites a 55,000 consensus — both figures are printed here because the two sources differ). The unemployment rate was unchanged at 4.1%. Treasury yields rose and traders raised the odds that the Fed raises rates at this month’s meeting.

The Russell 2000 rose 0.25% — in TheStreet’s phrasing, “the one exception” among the major indexes.

On the Radar

  • NewFOMC: September 15–16, decision Wednesday, September 16 at 2:00 PM ET, press conference 2:30 PM ET. The Fed’s quiet period began Saturday, September 5 and runs through Thursday, September 17. Prior editions of this page declined to assert an FOMC date; it is sourced now.
  • Rate-path readings — reported separately, not chained. One return this run puts the probability of a 25 bp September hike at roughly 50%, down from about 63% a day earlier. A different return puts it at 57.5%, up from 35.4% a day earlier following Chair Kevin Warsh’s hawkish Jackson Hole remarks. The two cannot both be a current reading, and this desk does not resolve which is — they are snapshots from different moments, and this desk’s own log records a roughly 50% reading in late August after Warsh’s first major speech as Chair. Note also that Warsh is the Chair; Governor Chris Waller is a different person whose dovish comments produced a separate reading. The readings are printed side by side and never strung into one arrow-chain.
  • Norway’s $2.3 trillion sovereign wealth fund has proposed cutting government bonds in its benchmark from 70% to 50%, which a Reuters calculation puts at roughly $80 billion off about $215 billion of Treasuries. Any change is unlikely before well into 2027; the fund owns about 1.5% of every listed company globally.
