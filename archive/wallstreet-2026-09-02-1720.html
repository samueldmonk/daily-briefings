The Lead

Stocks snap a three-day skid, and Dell’s AI backlog is the reason the close looks the way it does

All three major U.S. indexes finished Wednesday, September 2 higher, ending a three-session losing streak. The S&P 500 rose 0.46%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 295.07 points, or 0.56%, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.45%. Those closes were re-confirmed against a fresh fetch this run, not carried forward.

The session had a single clear engine. Dell Technologies was the best-performing stock in the S&P 500, after booking a record $60.9 billion in AI-server orders and exiting the quarter with a record $95 billion backlog. Fiscal Q2 earnings before certain costs of $7.04 per share cleared a $4.92 estimate on $46.97 billion of revenue against $44.92 billion expected, up 58% year over year, and the company lifted its annual outlook. Sources disagree on the size of the move — one puts Dell up 13%, another “nearly 11%” — so the magnitude is printed both ways below and neither is adopted. Nvidia was the best performer in the Dow, up more than 3%.

The offsetting story was a cybersecurity name beating estimates and being sold anyway: Palo Alto Networks was the worst performer in the S&P 500, down about 11% despite quarterly results that topped Wall Street on what the company called a strong market for AI security. The 10-year Treasury yield touched an intraday high and then eased, which is what let the indexes turn; how far back that high reaches is disputed three ways and is set out in the rates section rather than settled here.

How this edition was built. Research for this run was fetched between 5:09 and 5:22 PM ET, roughly 80 minutes after the close. The index closes are official and each reconciles arithmetically against Tuesday’s verified closes (S&P 7,631.47; Dow 52,766.88; Nasdaq 26,099.77). Intraday figures carry the time their source published them. Where the live widgets above and this editorial disagree, the widgets are right.
