Top Story

Mike Malott knocks out Joaquin Buckley with a knee at UFC Edmonton

Mike Malott beat Joaquin Buckley by second-round KO (knee) in Saturday’s UFC Fight Night main event at Rogers Place in Edmonton. CBS Sports called it a Knockout of the Year contender: Malott “planted a perfect knee on the chin of Buckley,” sending his mouthpiece flying, and even after Buckley regained consciousness he could not get his feet under him. “I might be soft-spoken, but I’m f---ing hard-hitting,” Malott said afterward (CBS Sports).

CBS Sports said Malott has now won five straight and will likely assume Buckley’s No. 10 welterweight spot, and that Malott wants a top-10, or preferably top-five, opponent next; Buckley is winless in his last three fights (CBS Sports). In the co-main, Jasmine Jasudavicius upset Erin Blanchfield by unanimous decision (29-28, 29-28, 29-28) (CBS Sports; Cageside Press).

Bonuses: with 13 fights and only one going the distance, the UFC skipped Fight of the Night and paid four $100,000 Performance of the Night bonuses — to Malott, Marc-Andre Barriault, Mandel Nallo and heavyweight newcomer Joel Faglier; the remaining eight finishers each received $25,000 (Cageside Press).

Last week: Brendan Allen chokes out Christian Leroy Duncan in the fifth round at UFC Vegas 122

Brendan Allen beat Christian Leroy Duncan by submission (rear-naked choke) at 2:46 of round five in the Oct. 10 UFC Vegas 122 main event at the Meta APEX, extending his UFC win streak to four straight; Uncrowned lists him at 28-7 (Uncrowned via Yahoo Sports). LowKick MMA’s recap: Duncan stunned Allen with elbows and a spinning attack in a close first round, lost a point for an eye poke early in the second, and was controlled on the mat through the middle rounds before Allen took his back in the fifth and forced the tap (LowKick MMA). “I’m steady, defending my spot, doing what I need to do,” Allen said afterward. “Fight the champion, and if not, where’s [Dricus du Plessis]?” (Uncrowned).

The UFC paid four $100,000 Performance of the Night bonuses — to Allen, Andre Fili (head-kick KO of Kai Kamaka III), Otari Tanzilovi (KO of Malcolm Wellmaker) and Alice Pereira (TKO of Daria Zhelezniakova) — and no Fight of the Night. $25,000 finish bonuses went to Julius Walker, Francisco Prado, Niko Price and Allen Frye Jr.; Brendson Ribeiro’s upset guillotine of Felipe Franco did not qualify because he missed weight. Gerald Meerschaert announced his retirement after losing to Walker, and Price won what Heavy called his retirement fight (Heavy).

Prospect Watch

Prospect

Contender Series Week 9: four contracts, all first-round finishes

Dana White signed all four first-round finishers at the Meta APEX on Tuesday: Preston LaGrange (TKO, punches, over Nell Ariano), Salhahuddin “Sal” Everett (KO, punches, at 1:57 of round one over fellow unbeaten Ozzy Martin), Roque Conceição (submission, triangle choke, over Alexander Chavez) and Mateus Soares (TKO, punches, over Ryuho Miyaguchi). Everett — who joined the UFC through a third-party security agency before becoming a full-time runner for Dana White — moved to 5-0 and told White, “Hey boss, I am ready to be in the UFC” (Sherdog, Oct 7) . Sherdog headlined LaGrange’s KO as a win as a “+400 underdog.” Alivia Bierley beat the favored Summer Onley by unanimous decision (30-27, 29-28, 29-28) but did not get a contract (CBS Sports; Sherdog). Cageside Press counted 38 contracts through the first eight weeks against Season 5’s mark of 39 (Cageside Press, Oct 5); four more puts Season 10 at 42 by this desk’s count.

Around the Sport

Headline-level, same outlet: “UFC Edmonton Makes History With 12 Finishes, Tying UFC Serbia Record.”

It is what it is,” adding that he had not spoken to Strickland personally (Sherdog, Oct. 14).
  • Joaquin Buckley, now on three straight losses, responded to his knockout: “The fight didn’t go my way… I’m just going to get back to work,” he said in an Instagram video; Heavy expects him to stay top-15 but likely fight down the rankings next (Heavy, Oct. 18).
  • UFC 333 fight-week headlines on Sherdog: “Alexander Volkanovski predicts ‘wild’ finish of 20-0 Movsar Evloev,” “10 UFC wins, zero finishes: Movsar Evloev defends his fighting style” and “Merab Dvalishvili calls himself a ‘decision machine’ before Yan trilogy”; also “16-0 Farid Basharat fires back at Dana White over UFC exit comments” and “Muhammad Mokaev blames TKO & Paramount, not Dana White, for UFC exits” (headline-level) (Sherdog latest-news list).
  • Cageside Press reports Dana White addressed Farid Basharat’s release: “There’s Definitely a Reason” (headline-level, Oct. 18) (Cageside Press).
  • Undefeated bantamweight Farid Basharat (16-0, 7-0 in the UFC) has been removed from the UFC roster after his contract expired following a unanimous-decision win over John Garza at UFC 329 in July; the UFC is not offering him a new deal, MMA Fighting’s Guilherme Cruz reported. He had been ranked No. 13 at bantamweight and is now a free agent (Heavy).
  • Middleweight champion Sean Strickland says he may walk away over pay: “I gotta be honest unless things change with the UFC I’m kinda over it,” he wrote on X on Wednesday, adding that giving up his belt, “going to 205 and speed running my contract to fight Jake and make what these guys are making sounds pretty damn appealing.” Jake Paul invited him to contact Most Valuable Promotions once he is a free agent. Strickland, who regained the title by split decision over Khamzat Chimaev at UFC 328 in May, has yet to schedule a first defence; it is unclear how many fights remain on his contract (The National, Oct 9).
  • Wednesday’s Sherdog headlines: “Alexander Volkov predicts Alex Pereira losing streak following UFC 335,” “Shavkat Rakhmonov teases Michael Morales fight with cryptic 19-0 post,” “Alexander Volkanovski admits Movsar Evloev boos got in his head” and “Edson Barboza retired from MMA, but he’s not done competing” (Sherdog news list, Oct 7).
  • Oddsmakers are already looking ahead to new women’s flyweight champion Natália Silva’s first defense: Sherdog reports Silva opened as the underdog against two-time champion Valentina Shevchenko, who vacated while injured and is guaranteed a title shot when cleared; no bout is booked (Sherdog via Yahoo Sports, Oct 7).
  • A UFC promo video confirmed Alex Pereira (13-4) vs. Sergei Pavlovich (21-3) for the UFC 335 co-main on Dec. 12 at T-Mobile Arena; Pereira posted an AI-generated video captioned “See you soon,” and Pavlovich replied “Back in action soon.” Pavlovich is on a three-fight win streak, most recently knocking out Tallison Teixeira in 39 seconds at UFC Macau (Sherdog).
  • Former champion Khamzat Chimaev (15-1) offered Sean Strickland $1 million for a rematch — “Strickland I will pay you 1… Send me location and let’s fight” (X, Oct 5) — doubling Imavov’s $500,000 offer; Sherdog says Strickland’s defense against Imavov, first rumored for UFC 335 in December, “now looks set for 2027” (Sherdog, Oct 6).
  • No. 3-ranked middleweight Nassourdine Imavov offered Sean Strickland $500,000 of his own money if Strickland beats him in January — “If it’s really about money, I’ll put $500K of my own money on the line” — and called on the UFC to strip the champion, after Strickland confirmed money was the sticking point in talks; their linked Dec. 12 UFC 335 bout was not finalized. Imavov has won five straight; Strickland beat him by decision in January 2023 (MMA News, Oct 5).
  • Glover Teixeira, who cornered Raoni Barcelos, said tests after Barcelos suddenly lost consciousness during a wrestling exchange in his UFC Vegas 121 headliner against Raul Rosas Jr. on Sept. 26 found swelling in his brain — “a traumatic brain injury” that may have caused him to black out; Barcelos was ahead on the scorecards entering the final round and was taken to a Level 1 trauma center (Sherdog).
  • Payton Talbott called out Sean O’Malley after stopping Figueiredo, pitching a fight to decide the next bantamweight title challenger (CBS Sports).
  • Johnny Walker made his heavyweight debut with a first-round knee KO of Mick Parkin (Cageside Press).
  • Ismail Naurdiev handed Marvin Vettori a fifth straight loss (Cageside Press).
