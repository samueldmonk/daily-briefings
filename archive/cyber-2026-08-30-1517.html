McKesson tells the SEC it was breached. ShinyHunters says it took 284 million records and asked for $55 million.

What the company itself says. McKesson — a major U.S. healthcare company and pharmaceutical distributor supplying medicines, medical supplies, technology and services to providers and pharmacies — disclosed a cybersecurity incident in a Form 8-K filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, after CyberInsider first reported it. McKesson says it discovered the incident on August 25, 2026 and that the investigation remains in the early stages. In a separate notice to customers it confirmed the incident involved third-party applications and the unauthorised access and exfiltration of data. As of the filing date the company had not determined that the incident is material, or reasonably likely to have a material impact on its financial condition or results. It warns customers may see intermittent service degradation believed to be related to the attack, while saying it is not proactively disconnecting systems in its environment. That is a rounding of the same figure and not a second claim, so the precise form this page sourced first is kept and the rounded one is printed beside it.

Added at 2:11 PM — the 421 figure has competition, and the page now prints the spread instead of the number alone. This page has carried 421 CVEs as the size of August’s Patch Tuesday, and four independent write-ups fetched this run repeat it. But a fifth, a major vulnerability-management vendor’s own analysis, titles its post 398 CVEs, and a sixth tracker ranks 751. Nothing fetched reconciles the three, and the plausible explanation — that they count different things, Microsoft-issued versus republished third-party CVEs versus everything in the update — is an explanation this page can offer but cannot source. 421 stays, because it is the majority return and is described as the Security Update Guide total, but it is stated as one count among three rather than as the number. What every version agrees on is the part that matters operationally: exactly one flaw in the release was being exploited, CVE-2026-68820, the use-after-free in afd.sys used to reach SYSTEM — and the vendor putting the count at 398 adds that it was one of three zero-days, the other two disclosed but not exploited. A disputed total does not make a disputed zero-day.

What the company has not said — and this page does not fill in. What the attacker claims. ShinyHunters says it was behind the attack and that it got in by voice phishing — vishing — multiple McKesson employees — and at 11:05 AM that count stopped being vague: a report fetched this run puts it at two employees, which is the first specific number any edition has been able to attach to the intrusion — compromising their Okta single sign-on accounts and using those to reach the company's Salesforce and Snowflake environments. It claims it fully compromised the Salesforce environment including support cases, took a much larger set of patient-related data out of Snowflake, and exfiltrated about 1TB over four days, between August 21 and August 25. It says it contacted McKesson after finishing on August 25 and demanded $55,236,150 within 72 hours, and that the company neither responded nor negotiated.

The 284 million figure is a count of records, not of people — and the correction came from the attacker. Earlier reporting stated that information belonging to 284 million patients had been exposed. Both forms are printed here, because a count of rows and a count of people are different quantities, and the one the attacker actually stands behind is the count of rows. This page prints neither as a victim count.

The lure domain has a documented pattern behind it, and that part is actionable. Separately from the attacker, the reporting outlet says it learned the campaign used the domain mckesson[.]claims. ReliaQuest's post has since been deleted. If you do nothing else with this item, look for newly registered .claims domains bearing your own company's name.

Carried · from the 10:20 edition

PaperCut's emergency patch was bypassed. The second one shipped a day later.

PaperCut published an urgent advisory on August 27 saying it was investigating active exploitation of a flaw in PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF. On August 28 it assigned two identifiers to what turned out to be an exploit chain: CVE-2026-81578, a high-severity authentication bypass rated 8.8 in the web management interface, where “unauthenticated remote requests targeting administrative functions can trigger backend actions prior to the completion of access validation checks”; and CVE-2026-82078, a critical unsafe dynamic class-loading flaw rated 9.4 in PaperCut's database connection utilities, which loads driver classes from a configurable name without validating it against an allowlist.

Chained, they are pre-authentication remote code execution. In watchTowr head of threat intelligence Jake Knott's description: CVE-2026-81578 gets you past authentication, and from there you edit a configuration file to exploit CVE-2026-82078 and gain RCE.

Emergency patches now exist for NG/MF versions 24, 25 and 26 across Windows, Linux and macOS — and the current builds are Emergency Patch Release 2, published August 28 after PaperCut worked with watchTowr and Huntress researchers to close bypasses of the earlier patches. Administrators who applied the first patch are not finished.

The worst of it, first published in the 9:15 edition and re-verified against two independent reports in the 10:20 AM edition. watchTowr reports it found multiple patch bypasses plus an additional authentication bypass; one of those bypasses was closed in Emergency Patch Release 2, but the researchers state that new patch bypasses affecting the latest, fully patched version have been identified. Read that precisely: applying the current patch is necessary and, on this reporting, not provably sufficient — which is why PaperCut's own advice to take the application server off the internet still matters more than the version number.

Exploitation is also no longer abstract. Huntress reports detecting exploitation of the chain in two customer environments as of August 27; in one, the attacker ran base64-encoded whoami and ver commands to identify the account context and the operating system version. That is manual reconnaissance immediately after access — a useful thing to hunt for in your own command-line logging.

Still not stated by any source seen in any edition to date, and therefore still not printed: the PaperCut threat actor, the number of victims, whether ransomware is involved, or how many servers are exposed. Both were added to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on August 27, and the countdown below reads 0 days left for each. CVE-2023-49105 is an improper-authentication flaw in ownCloud Server 10.6.0 through 10.13.0 that lets an unauthenticated attacker access or modify another user’s files; reporting fetched this run ties its exploitation to the theft of nuclear research records from a Philippine research body. CVE-2026-8452 — Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway — federal deadline EXPIRED YESTERDAY (now overdue)

CISA added this to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 26 with a federal remediation date of Saturday, August 29. That is today: the countdown below reads 0 days left, and it does not get another edition. It is a pre-authentication heap memory overflow in the code that parses SAML single-sign-on messages in the appliance's AAA service, carrying a CVSS v4.0 base score of 8.8. Defenders have observed web shells and discovery activity on compromised appliances. If you run NetScaler with AAA or Gateway exposed, this is the item on this page most likely to be already overdue when you read it.

The exploitation is quantified — carried from the 8:46 edition, where it was first sourced. Telemetry cited by CISA-tracking researchers records 36 exploitation attempts over the past 12 days from 12 unique attacker IP addresses across Switzerland, Germany, Hong Kong, Japan, the Netherlands, Russia, Singapore, Türkiye, the United States and Vietnam. The observed post-exploitation behaviour is specific enough to hunt for: attackers dropped web shells named x.php and z.php and ran discovery commands including id and echo. If you cannot patch today, look for those filenames first.

A second deadline expires alongside it. CVE-2019-1068, a remote code execution flaw in Microsoft SQL Server that lets an attacker execute code in the context of the SQL Server Database Engine service account, was added to KEV in the same August 26 batch and carries the same August 29 due date. It ranks second here only because there is currently no public information on how it is being exploited in the wild — which is a reason to patch it, not a reason to deprioritise it.

An AI agent chained six flaws in a WordPress theme on a million sites, and wrote the exploit itself

What it is. A critical vulnerability chain in the Avada theme for WordPress lets an unauthenticated attacker execute arbitrary PHP code on the server. The six issues are tracked collectively as CVE-2026-18431 and carry a 9.8 critical severity score. Chained in a specific order they form a zero-click attack; the components are authorization, input-validation, trust-boundary and file-handling weaknesses. A successful attacker can plant malware, reach databases, redirect visitors or add rogue administrator accounts.

Why the prerequisites do not narrow it. Exploitation requires vulnerable versions of both the theme (Avada up to 7.16) and the Fusion Builder plugin (up to 3.16) to be active. That sounds like a filter and is not one: Wordfence researchers clarified that Fusion Builder is a required plugin for the Avada theme, so all sites running the theme are also running the plugin. Avada has more than 1 million sales. In the researchers’ own words, “any site that has the Avada theme installed is going to be exploitable.”

The part that is arguably the story. The chain was found by Argus, an internal agentic framework at Wordfence, which also developed proof-of-concept exploit code — the whole thing in about two hours. Argus reproduced the flaw on July 30; the vendor got full details on August 5, acknowledged on August 10, and ThemeFusion shipped Avada 7.16.1 and Fusion Builder 3.16.1. Wordfence is withholding complete technical details to give administrators time to update, and published only a six-step outline of the chain.

⚠ What is not established. No source seen this run states in-the-wild exploitation, a victim, or a public proof of concept outside Wordfence’s own; the flaw is not KEV-listed and carries no federal deadline. It is on this page for its severity and its reach, not for evidence of attacks. Independent investigation reported this run puts figures on an incident this page has carried since its mechanism was identified. NewAIAccount takeover

Anthropic is signing users out of Claude: infostealer malware on their own machines was hijacking live sessions to burn their usage.

Anthropic has begun warning Claude users that infostealer malware running on their PCs stole active Claude login sessions, and that an attacker used those sessions to sign in to their accounts and consume their usage. The company’s notice to affected users describes a bad actor using common infostealer malware to lift session tokens from people’s computers and then reach the accounts with them.

The tell was a billing symptom, not a security alert. Per the notice, if a user’s usage limits appeared to refill and then drain while they were not using Claude, that was the likely cause — which is worth dwelling on, because the observable signature of this compromise is a quota that behaves oddly, not a failed login or an unfamiliar device.

What Anthropic is doing about it. The company is signing affected users out of Claude, removing saved payment methods, and refunding charges it identifies as unauthorised.

The vector is the endpoint, and the company says so plainly. Infostealers of this kind typically arrive through downloads or malicious apps and take whatever is stored locally — browser passwords, login cookies and credentials belonging to other applications. Anthropic stresses there is no reason to believe the malware is related to Claude, was installed through Claude, or is related to anything the user did with Claude. ⚠ That framing matters for triage: a stolen session cookie is not a product vulnerability, and a user whose Claude session was taken should assume everything else in the same browser profile went with it.
