Top Story

SonicWall's SMA1000 has now had two exploited zero-day pairs seven weeks apart — and July's patching does not cover September's

SonicWall published advisory SNWLID-2026-0016 on September 1, 2026, confirming that its product security incident response team investigated a case indicating active exploitation of two flaws in SMA1000 Series secure mobile access appliances. CVE-2026-83548 carries a CVSS score of 10.0 and is a pre-authentication server-side request forgery in the SMA1000 Appliance Workplace interface. CVE-2026-83549 carries CVSS 7.8 and is an OS command injection in the Appliance Management Console that an authenticated attacker can use to run arbitrary OS commands. Affected are SMA1000 6210, 7210 and 8200v appliances running version 12.4.3-03453 or earlier and version 12.5.0-02835 or earlier.

That shape — SSRF in Workplace, command execution in the AMC — is the same one SonicWall disclosed seven weeks earlier under SNWLID-2026-0008, a genuinely different pair: CVE-2026-15409 (SSRF, CVSS 10) and CVE-2026-15410 (code injection in the AMC, CVSS 7.2), reported by Rapid7's MDR team and analysed by Tenable in July. Two exploited pairs, same product, same mechanism class, two months apart: an estate patched in July is not covered against the September pair.

Fixed versions for the September pair were not sourced this run. An earlier edition today carried 12.4.3-03526 and 12.5.0-02952, and the scope note that SSL-VPN and SMA100 are unaffected; both are repeated here only as carried, not re-fetched, items. Apply the vendor's own product notice rather than these figures.

Patch Priority

CVE-2026-64849 — MLflow SSRF — federal deadline expires TODAY

CVE-2026-64849 is a critical unauthenticated server-side request forgery in MLflow versions before 3.15.0, scored CVSS 9.3. Attackers with no credentials can reach internal cloud metadata services and retrieve live AWS, GCP and Azure credentials — a path to full cloud account takeover. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 19, 2026, with a remediation deadline of September 2, 2026. That is today: zero days left.

The SonicWall pair above is more severe and is also being exploited — but no federal clock for it was sourced this run, so it cannot outrank a deadline that expires at the end of today. Upgrade MLflow to 3.15.0 or later, then rotate any cloud credentials the affected hosts could have reached.

ShinyHunters

The group's McKesson intrusion ran through identity rather than software: voice phishing to compromise employee Okta single sign-on accounts, then a pivot into the company's cloud estate, reaching Salesforce and Snowflake. Exfiltration is reported to have run August 21 to 25, after which the group demanded more than $55 million — reported at $55.2M — on a 72-hour deadline. The same group told BleepingComputer it entered Panera Bread's systems through a Microsoft Entra single sign-on code, though that was a January 2026 incident and is not current news. The pattern to defend against is the same in both: the credential, not the CVE.

McKesson — 284M records claimed, $55.2M demanded

The healthcare distribution giant has confirmed a cyber incident involving exfiltration of certain data. ShinyHunters claims 284 million patient records, said to include names, contact details, Social Security numbers, diagnoses, medications and doctor–patient messages. Fresh this run: the $55.2M demand figure, the 72-hour deadline and the August 21–25 exfiltration window.

Actor namedOperations

Boston Scientific — a claimant, at last

The medical device maker's cyberattack disrupted global operations, blocking access to certain operating systems and business applications and affecting its ability to process and ship customer orders. This run resolves an open question from earlier editions: a pro-Russian group calling itself Server Killers has claimed responsibility. ShinyHunters has not claimed the attack and the company has not named them — the two healthcare incidents remain separate.

ExploitedSupply chain

JFrog Artifactory — auth bypass exploited days after disclosure

Threat actors are reported to be exploiting CVE-2026-82329, an authentication bypass in JFrog Artifactory that can yield administrative access, beginning within days of public disclosure. JFrog has not confirmed active exploitation. Its CTO has stated the flaw is improper authentication rather than remote code execution, and that it does not affect the JFrog SaaS platform — only self-hosted deployments.
