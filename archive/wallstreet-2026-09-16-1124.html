The Lead

Stocks climb into the Fed’s first hike decision in three years — as of the 9:32 a.m. ET open

Wall Street opened higher on Wednesday with the Federal Reserve set to announce its rate decision at 2 p.m. ET. At the opening bell TheStreet put the S&P 500 up 0.25%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 0.15% and the Nasdaq up 0.44% — but the Russell 2000 down 0.76%, the same small-cap weakness that left the index at a three-month closing low on Tuesday. Schwab attributed the early gain to falling oil after U.S. crude stockpiles reportedly climbed.

Futures markets put the odds of a quarter-point hike at 93% on the CME FedWatch Tool as of Schwab’s 9:13 a.m. ET market open note (TheStreet cited 92.5% at 8:50 a.m.). A hike would be the Fed’s first since 2023 and would move the target range from today’s 3.50%–3.75% to 3.75%–4.00%. Futures also imply 77% odds of a second hike before year-end, and CME trading projects three to five hikes by next September, with the highest odds at around four.

The morning’s data leaned hot: August retail sales rose 1.2% from July against an 0.8% consensus, with the control group up 1.4% and only building materials lower. Export and import prices rose 0.6% and 0.7%. TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre framed the risk plainly: “A hike paired with reassuring language could actually calm the bond market. A hike with hawkish or vague commentary could push yields higher and take stocks down with them.”

On the Radar

  • Today, 2 p.m. ET — FOMC decision, Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference. In June the average projection had the funds rate ending 2026 at 3.8% (one hike) and 2027 at 3.6%. June PCE estimates for 2026 were 3.6% headline / 3.3% core — Schwab expects both to rise.
  • Later today — an updated Atlanta Fed GDPNow estimate for third-quarter growth. The most recent reading was 4.4% annualised, against an average analyst estimate still below 3%.
  • Thursday, 17 September — August housing starts and building permits.
  • Friday, 18 September — Bank of Japan rate decision, August industrial production, and the Conference Board’s August leading indicators.
  • Context for the vote — July’s FOMC voted 9–3 to pause. Schwab notes today may be the last chance to hike at a regularly scheduled meeting until December, because October’s meeting falls just before the mid-term elections.
