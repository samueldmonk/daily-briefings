The Lead

Dow adds 509 points to end a losing week on a high note as crude cools and rate-hike bets firm into Wednesday

All three major averages finished Friday higher, ending a four-session losing streak. The Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 509.19 points, or 0.98%, the S&P 500 gained 0.86% and the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.96%. Closing levels are in the Weekly Scorecard below; four independent reads of the tape now agree on them and each reconciles to the cent against Thursday's close. Friday's bounce did not rescue the week, however — all three major averages still posted weekly declines, so the session is best read as a losing week ending on a high note rather than as a turn.

The driver was energy, not inflation. Iranian state media said Tehran will sit down with Gulf states in Oman next week to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, and the headline knocked the war premium out of crude: WTI settled down 2.4% at $100.05 and Brent down 2.8% at $104.61, after Brent had peaked near $108 and WTI above $104 on Thursday. Falling oil took the edge off the week's inflation anxiety even though both benchmarks still posted sharp weekly gains.

August CPI, out at 8:30 a.m. ET, was in line on the headline — +0.4% month over month and 3.4% year over year, both matching the Dow Jones consensus — but core CPI rose 0.3%, up from 0.2% in July and a tenth above forecast. Analysts read the print as still too hot to ignore ahead of the 15–16 September FOMC, with a decision due Wednesday from a target range of 3.50–3.75% under Chair Kevin Warsh.

The hike probability is printed as a spread, because the two best-attributed reads are fourteen points apart. A CME FedWatch read timed at 10:03 a.m. ET gave 85.6%; a futures read taken later in the session puts the probability at around 71%, up from earlier in the week. Two looser summaries bracket the same range — one at roughly 90%, from about 70% before Friday morning's CPI, another at above 65%. No single point estimate is asserted as the current number; what every read shares is the direction — a hike, not a cut, and firmer after the release.

Breadth backed the rally rather than a handful of megacaps: nine of the eleven S&P 500 sectors finished higher on a session review, with technology, industrials and communication services each up more than 1% and only health care and utilities lagging. An intraday read earlier in the day had ten of eleven green, with communication services up 1.9% — the session review is the later measurement and is the one this page asserts.

On the Radar

  • FOMC, 15–16 September. The decision lands Wednesday at 2:00 p.m. ET alongside a Summary of Economic Projections. After Friday's core CPI overshoot, futures are leaning toward a 25-basis-point hike from the 3.50–3.75% range. The probability is published as a spread: a 10:03 a.m. ET CME FedWatch read gave 85.6% and a later futures read gives around 71%, with looser summaries from above 65% to roughly 90%. No single number is asserted as current.
  • The Oman track on Hormuz. Tehran's talks with Gulf states are scheduled for next week. Crude's whole risk premium is riding on them; a breakdown would put the week's $108 Brent high back in play, and with it the energy pass-through that has been driving headline CPI.
  • The labour backdrop. August non-farm payrolls came in at 162,000 with unemployment at 4.1% — resilient enough that the Fed's hawks are not being restrained by the jobs side of the mandate.
  • The long end. A global bond selloff pushed the 10-year to the edge of 5% this week before Friday's small pullback. Duration, not equities, is where the inflation story is being expressed most cleanly.
