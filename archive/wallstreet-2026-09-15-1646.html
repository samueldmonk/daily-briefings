The Lead

Official close · Tuesday 15 September, 4:00 PM ET

Stocks closed lower for a second straight session as the 10-year Treasury yield reached its highest level since 2007 on the eve of the Fed decision

The settle is in, and it is the first official close this site has been able to publish today. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,585.73. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 328.09 points, or 0.63%, to 52,093.11. The Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.78% to 25,981.57. CNBC attributes the decline to traders looking ahead to the Federal Reserve’s policy decision this week and to Treasury yields surging to multiyear highs.

All three levels reconcile exactly against Monday’s verified closes, which is why they are published as levels rather than as percentages alone: 52,421.20 − 328.09 = 52,093.11; 7,619.98 − 34.25 = 7,585.73 (−0.4495%, i.e. −0.45%); 26,186.41 − 204.84 = 25,981.57 (−0.7822%, i.e. −0.78%). Every percentage matches to the basis point, and two independent readings of the same close agreed on all seven figures.

Both readings this page carries are negative and the second is worse than the first. At the opening bell (9:35 AM ET) TheStreet had the S&P 500 down 0.16%, the Dow down 0.49%, the Nasdaq down 0.26% and the Russell 2000 down 0.40%. By the close the S&P had lost about 2.8 times its opening decline and the Nasdaq about 3 times, while the Dow widened only about 1.3 times — so the deepening was not uniform, and the index that fell most in points widened least in proportion. Two snapshots are two points, not a path; no intraday course is asserted between them.

The bond market is the driver. The 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.041%, its highest level since 2007 (CNBC); TheStreet, in an article body stamped 1:55 PM ET, put the move at up as much as four basis points to 5.02%, above the 2023 peak. Oil is the second half of the story: WTI jumped 2.64% to $104.10 and Brent climbed 2.14% to $107.90 in early Tuesday trading, with Brent holding firmly above $100 after attacks on Saudi infrastructure and continued disruption around the Strait of Hormuz.

Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com, framed the setup: “Markets remain cautious ahead of Wednesday’s Federal Reserve decision, with the combination of $100-plus oil, elevated bond yields and renewed questions around the AI trade creating a difficult backdrop for risk assets.” On the decision itself: “A 25bp Fed hike is now almost fully priced, meaning the bigger catalyst will be whether Kevin Warsh presents it as a one-off recalibration or signals that persistent inflation requires further tightening.”

On the Radar

  • Wednesday 16 September, 2:00 PM ET — the FOMC decision, with Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference. A hike would be the Fed’s first since 2023. CME FedWatch put the odds of a quarter-point increase at 92.3% in the reading returned this run. Earlier editions today logged the same question at 91% and “better than 92%” from other same-day sources; those readings are named rather than averaged, and no trend is asserted across them.
  • The feedback loop Hathorn describes is the thing to watch, not the hike itself. Her argument, not this page’s: higher energy prices threaten growth but also make it harder for central banks to declare victory over inflation, and the dollar has benefited from the combination of higher yields, hike expectations and defensive demand while equities face the opposite pressure as borrowing costs and discount rates rise.
  • The AI-safety debate is now a named market factor. TheStreet reports that technology is adding a layer of uncertainty after prominent AI executives called for a slower pace of development on safety grounds, with semiconductor stocks bearing the brunt of Monday’s selling — “partly like a repricing of crowded positioning,” per Hathorn, “but it lands at a sensitive moment given existing concerns around AI capex, valuations and the growing use of debt to finance infrastructure.” Tuesday partly reversed that: Qualcomm, AMD and Coherent all rose.
  • Crypto legislation reached the Senate floor Tuesday. The CLARITY Act — formally the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act — came to a vote; Coinbase fell 4.59% ahead of it and Bitcoin was down 1.01% premarket. No result was returned in anything read this run.
  • The war is still in the oil price. A Defense Department inspector general report to Congress on Monday put the cost of the conflict with Iran at $33.4 billion and recorded the destruction of four F-15s and as many as 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi visits China Wednesday for talks.
