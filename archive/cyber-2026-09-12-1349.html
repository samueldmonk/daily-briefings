Top Story

IDScan confirms a breach behind 153 million stolen driver’s license scans

Identity-verification company IDScan has confirmed that an unauthorized third party accessed customer data held in its cloud platform, days after reporting linked the Louisiana firm to a dark-web database of more than 153 million US and Canadian driver’s license scans. The company says it learned on or around 1 September that data may have been accessed, and disclosed the incident in a 4 September security notice.

The chain of disclosure is itself part of the story. Journalist Brian Krebs reported on 1 September that a Russia-linked dark-web platform called Nexus was advertising access to the scans, and verified samples by searching for records belonging to himself and to others who consented. Alongside the licenses the database allegedly held 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents and 579,000 medical cards. IDScan’s own notification, published 4 September, was configured with a noindex directive instructing search engines not to list it — it was TechCrunch that spotted it.

IDScan says the exposed information can include full names and driver’s license or other government-issued identification numbers, and is offering free credit monitoring. It is cooperating with federal law enforcement; the FBI has confirmed it is investigating. Nexus has since been taken offline, though BleepingComputer notes the criminals likely retain the data, and multiple actors have since claimed to be selling the full database — claims BleepingComputer has not been able to verify.

Why it matters: IDScan’s platform is used by car rental companies, retailers, financial institutions, cannabis dispensaries, gun shops and hospitality businesses. The exposure is not of one company’s customers but of the identity documents those companies were required to check — and unlike a password, a driver’s license scan cannot be rotated.

Patch Priority

Act today — CVE-2026-20079, Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center, CVSS 10.0

CISA added this unauthenticated authentication-bypass flaw to the KEV catalog on 9 September and set a federal remediation deadline of 12 September 2026 — today. It allows a remote attacker with no credentials to execute scripts as root on the management plane of the firewall estate. Cisco Talos has confirmed exploitation by three separate clusters, including Qilin ransomware affiliates and an actor whose tooling overlaps with Sandworm. Hot fixes exist for release branches 7.0, 7.2, 7.4, 7.6, 7.7 and 10.0; Cisco lists affected branches as 6.4, 7.0, 7.1, 7.2, 7.3, 7.4, 7.6, 7.7 and 10.0 — so three affected branches (6.4, 7.1, 7.3) do not appear on the hot-fix list, though Cisco says a more comprehensive release covering additional vulnerabilities follows next week. Cloud-delivered FMC instances are not affected. Critical caveat: the hot fixes prevent future exploitation but do not remediate an existing compromise — patching is not sufficient; assume breach and hunt for the indicators below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ShinyHunters — the extortion brand behind two items on this page

ShinyHunters is named this week in both the Florida DAVID DMV breach, which it claimed before the state confirmed it, and the AdaptHealth attack that exposed 4.1 million people. Its Florida claim is instructive about how much weight to give a leak-site post: the group said it exploited a password reset flaw to reach multiple DAVID accounts — including one belonging to an FBI agent — then iterated through record IDs from 3 September, and claimed more than 200,000 driver records.

Florida’s own investigation reached a different conclusion: the intruder used compromised credentials belonging to a single Plant City Police Department user, improperly stored on that employee’s personal device. The state has not confirmed the 200,000 figure or disclosed any count of its own. Both accounts are published here because neither has been reconciled — but note which one is the victim’s and which is the attacker’s marketing.

Breaches & Incidents

NewGovernment

Florida confirms its DAVID driver database was breached

The Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles confirmed on 11 September that its DAVID driver database suffered a breach, which it learned of on 4 September. Access came via stolen credentials of one Plant City Police Department user, improperly stored on a personal device. FLHSMV says the breach was “quickly mitigated” and is not ongoing. ShinyHunters, which claimed the attack, described a different method and shared a DAVID record for Jeffrey Epstein as proof; it later said it had lost access. The state has notified the Florida Attorney General and is working with the Florida Digital Service and FDLE.

NewExploited

Cisco FMC flaws used by Qilin ransomware and a Sandworm-linked APT

Cisco Talos attributes post-compromise activity on Secure FMC devices to three clusters. UAT-11988 (high confidence, Qilin affiliates) harvested Active Directory and MySQL credentials, staged them in publicly readable files on the appliance, ran a Python SOCKS5 proxy and reverse SSH tunnel, then deployed ransomware. UAT-11823, overlapping with Sandworm, wrote a Netcat reverse shell into license.tmp and executed it as root via Cisco’s own package_info.pl, ultimately deploying a Cyclops Blink variant. UAT-12197 planted a JSP web shell and a cmd.jar file to query internal databases.

NewAvailability

September Windows Server updates break Remote Desktop Services

Microsoft’s 8 September cumulative updates — KB5122876 (Server 2019), KB5122882 (Server 2022) and KB5122871 (Server 2025) — are causing Remote Desktop Services failures. Servers run normally for a few hours, then connections begin to fail: existing sessions cannot disconnect or log off and new attempts hang before timing out, in the worst cases forcing hard reboots. Administrators report a suspected deadlock between RDP and the Local Session Manager. Microsoft says it is investigating. Rolling back restores RDP but also removes this month’s security fixes.

CarriedSupply chain

Trezor / ShipMonk — 81,000 customers

A further 67,000 US customers were added to an initial ~14,000 disclosed on 13 August, for a total of 81,000. Fulfilment partner ShipMonk notified Trezor on 10 August 2026. Exposed: names, emails, phones, shipping addresses and order numbers for orders placed November 2019 to August 2021. Trezor had repeatedly requested deletion of the data and received written confirmation it had been deleted; it had not.

CarriedPhishing

Trezor / Brevo — 347,000 targeted

A separate incident: 347,000 addresses were phished after a Brevo compromise, with 2,500 clicking through. The lure was a fake “critical security alert” sent from help@trezor.io claiming an STM32 microcontroller flaw could expose wallet seeds.

CarriedHealthcare

AdaptHealth — 4.1 million confirmed

4.1 million people confirmed exposed in a July attack attributed to ShinyHunters. Separately, a third-party vendor incident at Veradigm has a threat actor claiming 3.5 million patient records including Social Security numbers — a claimed figure, not a confirmed one, and the two are not added together here.

CarriedAviation

APIS leak — 220 million traveller records

More than 220 million passenger and crew records exposed in a Vietnam-linked leak, reached through a cloud path secured with default credentials. The data spans January 2017 to April 2026 and includes passport numbers and full itinerary detail.

CarriedRoot cause

Metabase CVE-2026-72898 — one bug under several breaches

An unauthenticated SQL injection (CVSS 10.0) reachable via the public POST /api/session/reset_password endpoint, yielding administrator and with it the stored credentials for every connected database. Exploited as a zero-day; Metabase disclosed the attack on its Cloud environment on 6 August 2026 and CISA added it to KEV on 11 August. For Mathspace, BleepingComputer states the data was taken after its Metabase reporting system was breached — platform named, CVE not. For ShipMonk, attribution to this CVE specifically rests on a single vendor analysis, not on ShipMonk or Trezor.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • Due today, 12 September — CVE-2026-20079 (Cisco Secure FMC), CVE-2026-19490 (Citrix NetScaler) and CVE-2025-25249 (Fortinet), all added 9 September. 0 days left.
  • Due 18 September — CVE-2026-85046 (Chromium V8 type confusion), added 4 September. 6 days left.
  • Due 23 September — CVE-2026-87491 (Chromium V8 out-of-bounds write), added 9 September. 11 days left.
  • The 9 September bulletin is now the cleanest proof yet that BOD 26-04 sets the clock per vulnerability. One alert, one add date, four CVEs — and two different deadlines eleven days apart: the Cisco, Citrix and Fortinet flaws were given three days, the Chromium flaw fourteen. The 4 September Chromium addition also got fourteen. So the interval tracks the individual vulnerability, not the batch: this is not the old BOD 22-01 uniform three-week window.
  • Seven added on 2 September — Sangoma Switchvox (CVE-2026-9586), Starlette (CVE-2026-48710), Kestra OSS (CVE-2026-49869), BerriAI LiteLLM (CVE-2026-59822), JFrog Artifactory (CVE-2026-82329) and two SonicWall SMA1000 flaws (CVE-2026-83548, CVE-2026-83549). Individual due dates for this batch were not confirmed this run and are therefore not stated.
  • Patching the Cisco flaw does not close it out. The hot fixes prevent future exploitation but do not remediate an existing compromise. Agencies and enterprises that were exposed before today should treat remediation and incident response as separate tasks.
