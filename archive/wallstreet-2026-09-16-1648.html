The Lead

Stocks close lower after the Fed’s first hike since July 2023 — Dow −631.21 to 51,461.90

The closing bell is in, and it supersedes every intraday read published earlier today. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 631.21 points, or 1.21%, to end at 51,461.90. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,551.81, and the Nasdaq Composite finished down 0.01% at 25,978.42 — effectively flat. Each of the three prints reconciles exactly against Tuesday’s verified closes (52,093.11 / 7,585.73 / 25,981.57), which is why levels are published this edition where earlier ones carried moves only.

The path mattered more than the destination. Stocks were higher going in — at the opening bell the S&P 500 was up 0.25%, the Dow 0.15% and the Nasdaq 0.44% (the Russell 2000 was the exception at −0.76%), and by 11:41 a.m. the S&P was up 0.43%. The Federal Reserve raised its target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% from 3.50%–3.75% at 2 p.m., and the selling arrived during the press conference that followed. By 3:06 p.m. ET TheStreet had the Dow at −1.07%, the S&P 500 at −0.45% and the Nasdaq at −0.06%, with the Russell 2000 improbably still green at +0.082%.

Breadth was worse than the headline indices. At that same 3:06 p.m. read, 60.8% (3,413) of all U.S. issues were declining against 36.3% (2,039) advancing. CNBC’s live-blog headline — “Dow falls 700 points” — describes the deeper late-afternoon trough, not the closing print of −631.21; both figures are real at different minutes and this page prints which is which rather than picking one silently.

Chair Kevin Warsh gave three reasons for the rise in U.S. bond yields: economic strength, competition for capital alongside a surge in capital expenditure, and geopolitics — while stressing the Fed’s independence. The Summary of Economic Projections points to one additional hike in 2026 before holding steady in 2027. Going in, CME FedWatch had the hike at 92.5%.
