The Lead

Wall Street inches higher as of 12:27 p.m. ET, waiting on Warsh

As of 12:27 p.m. ET the Dow Jones Industrial Average dipped below the flat line while the S&P 500 rose 0.2% and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.4%, per Yahoo Finance’s live coverage. Trading Economics independently puts the S&P 500 at +0.20% on the session — arithmetic consistent with Tuesday’s verified close of 7,585.73.

Everything hinges on 2:00 p.m. ET, when the FOMC releases its decision and updated economic projections, with Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference 30 minutes later. CME FedWatch puts the probability of moving the target range from 3.50%–3.75% to 3.75%–4.00% at 92.7% — which would be the first hike since July 2023. Yahoo’s morning write-up cited CME odds at 92%; both readings are printed rather than averaged.

Two things have been leaning against the tape all week. A bond sell-off pushed the 10-year Treasury yield to its highest level since 2007, and crude above $100 a barrel has kept the inflation outlook cloudy. Chipmakers were the offsetting bid.

No policy outcome is published on this page: the decision had not been announced at build time. Nothing here asserts what the Fed did.

Movers & Drivers

Chips · up

Intel +5% on SK Hynix memory talks

Reuters reported Wednesday that SK Hynix is in talks with Intel to manufacture its memory chips in the U.S. for the first time. Intel rose about 5%; SK Hynix’s Nasdaq-listed shares climbed about 2%. Options under consideration include leasing part of Intel’s planned Ohio fabrication facility or forming a joint venture alongside major cloud companies. No final decision has been reached on which memory types would be produced.

Semis · broad

The chip complex bucked the trend

Advanced Micro Devices and Nvidia also gained alongside the Intel headline, per Yahoo Finance. Some of the move was bargain buying after concerns over slowing AI development sparked broad chipmaking losses earlier in the week. Crypto · down

Bitcoin slips to the $75,000 level

Bitcoin dropped 2% to the $75,000 level after the Senate failed to pass a key procedural vote on the Clarity Act, the digital-asset regulatory framework. Yahoo’s overnight headline framed the same move as a 4% fall to $75,000; both figures are printed with their own framing rather than reconciled.

Energy · down

Crude retreats after two up sessions

WTI fell to around $104 a barrel Wednesday, retreating after two consecutive sessions of gains as signs of rising U.S. inventories weighed. Brent had risen past $108 on Tuesday, its highest in over four months, on Middle East supply curbs and fresh setbacks to Libyan output.
