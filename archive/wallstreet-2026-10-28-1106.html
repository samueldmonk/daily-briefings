The Lead

Fed day: choppy into late morning — Dow +0.13%, S&P 500 +0.12%, Nasdaq −0.06% — as of ~11:05 AM ET

New Late morning (~11:05 AM ET). Wall Street stayed choppy: Reuters reported that at 10:01 a.m. ET the Dow was up 0.14% and the S&P 500 up 0.09% while the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.03%, as weakness in megacaps such as Tesla (−1.9%) and Apple (−0.6%) offset chipmaker gains sparked by SK Hynix’s bumper results. Micron jumped 4.8% to its highest in over two weeks, SK Hynix’s US-listed shares gained 10.6% and the Philadelphia chip index rose 0.9%, on track for a fourth straight gain; Alphabet advanced 1.2% on a media report that OpenAI plans to buy Google’s AI chips, while SpaceX dropped 3.2%. Six of the 11 major S&P 500 sectors advanced, led by communication services, with real estate lagging. The quote board on the same page, read at about 11:05 AM ET, showed the Dow up 0.13%, the S&P 500 (US 500 cash) up 0.12% and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.06%, with WTI crude up 2.50% at $102.40 and the U.S. 10-year yield at 5.028% (Reuters via Investing.com).

Mid-morning (~10:35 AM ET). The opening tech-led bounce faded within the first hour: Investing.com’s quote board, read at about 10:35 AM ET, showed the Dow up 0.12% (US 30 cash), the S&P 500 up 0.08% (US 500 cash) and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.01%, with the VIX at 17.48, WTI crude up 2.07% to $101.97 and the 10-year yield at 4.995% (2-year 4.76%, 30-year 5.296%). Wendy’s was up 15.88% on the same board after Trian Fund Management tabled a formal, nonbinding $1.4 billion all-cash proposal to take the chain private at $7.35 a share — about a 20% premium to Tuesday’s close — backed by franchisee Flynn Group, BlueFive Capital and Sixth Street Partners (Investing.com).

At the open (9:30 AM ET). U.S. stocks opened mixed Wednesday: the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.43% and the S&P 500 gained 0.24% at the opening bell, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.03%, with tech-heavy indices leading early gains (Phemex News, 13:30 UTC). In Yahoo Finance’s last premarket read, with 23 minutes to the open (~9:07 AM ET), S&P 500 futures were up 0.18%, Nasdaq futures 0.30% and Dow futures 0.01%, with the 10-year yield at 4.999%, the VIX at 17.39 and December WTI crude up 2.21% to $102.11. Wendy’s rose 15% premarket after the Wall Street Journal reported Nelson Peltz’s Trian Partners offered $1.4 billion to buy the fast-food chain (Yahoo Finance).

Last premarket check (~8:36 AM ET). With 54 minutes to the open, Yahoo Finance’s futures strip showed S&P 500 futures +0.24% (+18.50), Nasdaq futures +0.39% and Dow futures +0.02% (+11 points) — Dow futures back to flat from about −0.2% earlier — with Russell 2000 futures +0.07%, the 10-year yield at 4.999%, the VIX at 17.42, December WTI crude +1.99% to $101.89 and gold −0.04%. On the Fed, Yahoo Finance’s Jennifer Schonberger writes that markets widely expect a hold even with hotter inflation, and former Fed vice chair Alan Blinder expects a “hawkish pause” (Yahoo Finance).

Strip check (~8:03 AM ET) and earnings movers. Yahoo Finance’s futures strip, with 1 hour 27 minutes to the open, showed S&P 500 futures +0.17%, Nasdaq futures +0.46% and Dow futures −0.19% (−102 points), with Russell 2000 futures −0.13%, the 10-year yield at 4.993%, the VIX at 17.44, December WTI crude +1.95% to $101.85 and gold −0.35%; interest-rate futures imply about a 75% chance of another Fed hike in December even as a hold is expected today (InvestorsHub via Yahoo Finance). In company news, “earnings are doing the heavy lifting before the bell”: Vertiv +16% and Enphase −17%, with NXP −7%, Robinhood −8%, SK Hynix +8% and SolarEdge −6%, as SPY traded up 0.2% at 7:02 a.m. ET (24/7 Wall St.).

Premarket (as of ~8 AM ET). Futures were still mixed in Yahoo Finance’s 7:58 a.m. ET update: Dow futures down 0.2%, S&P 500 futures up 0.1% and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.4%. Chip stocks kept gaining after SK Hynix reported quarterly operating profit up 632% year over year — about $62.7 billion on revenue up 328% to roughly $78.7 billion, an 80% operating margin — with its Seoul shares up 10%, Samsung up 5% and Micron up 2% (Yahoo Finance). TipRanks’ 5:30 a.m. read: Nasdaq-100 futures +0.30%, S&P 500 futures +0.10%, Dow futures −0.19%, with Brent +1.15% to $115.19 and WTI +1.70% to $101.60 (TipRanks).

Earlier (as of ~5 AM ET). U.S. stock futures were mixed ahead of what Yahoo Finance called “a critical day for markets”: Yahoo’s futures strip, with 4 hours 29 minutes to the open, showed S&P 500 futures up 0.08%, Nasdaq futures up 0.38% and Dow futures down 0.19% (−99 points), with Russell 2000 futures down 0.05%, the 10-year yield at 4.993%, the VIX at 17.58, December WTI crude up 1.54% to $101.44 and gold down 0.12% (Yahoo Finance). Stocktwits’ 5 a.m. ET read was similar — S&P 500 futures +0.05%, Nasdaq-100 futures +0.3%, Dow futures −0.2% (Stocktwits).

What’s driving it. Sentiment in the tech trade has markedly improved this week, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq posting back-to-back gains after Broadcom’s $63 billion AI-compute financing structure lifted chips Tuesday. The Fed decision is due at 2 p.m. ET and is expected to hold rates steady despite elevated inflation readings, with Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference watched for any hawkish bias; Microsoft and Alphabet report after the bell, and Microsoft’s report comes just after it offered buyouts to up to 60,000 employees (Yahoo Finance). Oil rose after Yemen’s Houthis said they attacked Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery with a ballistic missile — Brent futures +1.98% to $116.13 and WTI +2.21% to $102.11 at the time of Stocktwits’ writing — while SK Hynix’s record results and more than $23 billion of dividends and buybacks lifted chips; its Seoul shares rose more than 8%, lifting South Korea’s Kospi nearly 6% (Stocktwits). Before the open, GE Vernova and Stellantis report; Ford, T-Mobile US and eBay join Microsoft and Alphabet after the close (Stocktwits).

Tuesday’s official close (AP). The S&P 500 rose 0.6% (+45.83 points), the Nasdaq Composite 1.2% (+308.43) and the Russell 2000 1.2% (+32.45), while the Dow Jones Industrial Average edged up less than 0.1% (+15.99). The majority of stocks in the S&P 500 fell, but gains in several influential AI stocks made up for that: Broadcom climbed after announcing a financing deal that will give Anthropic access to its AI chips, and JetBlue and Royal Caribbean rose on quarterly results, offsetting drops in Boeing and Hilton. Treasury yields held relatively steady a day before the Fed’s rate decision (AP via KRMG). TheStreet’s wrap: “After weeks of greater breadth in the market, tech has found a new pep in its step” (TheStreet). Hammerstone noted energy (XLE) fell more than 2% as oil slipped, with REITs and consumer staples also lagging while tech, industrials, consumer discretionary and materials led; consumer confidence jumped to its highest level since June (Investrade Market Review). Levels are in the Weekly Scorecard.

Tuesday’s data beat. The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index rose to 91.7 in October vs. 80.4 expected and 84.2 in September (revised from 81.9); September durable-goods orders rose 1.8% vs. 1.7% expected; new home sales ran at a 676,000 annual rate vs. 650,000 expected; and FHFA house prices rose 0.3% in August (Investrade).

AI capital raising. Meta raised its largest-ever equity fundraising to $97.5 billion, lifting the common-stock piece from $22.5 billion to $25 billion priced at $625.00 a share (Investrade), and Nvidia said it will invest $3 billion in Meta’s equity offering (Yahoo Finance). SpaceX rose 1.20% premarket to $148.66 after Anthropic said it would pay up to $120.5 billion for AI computing infrastructure through 2029, and GM jumped nearly 5% early on plans to move its listing from the NYSE to Nasdaq (TheStreet).

Fed, oil and yields (Tuesday). The FOMC began its two-day meeting Tuesday; CME data priced only about 10% odds of a hike (Yahoo Finance). WTI was about $101.50 (−0.26) at mid-morning, the 10-year yield about 5.00%, the 30-year 5.306% and gold up $17 to $4,055.40 (Investrade); earlier, Yemen’s Houthis claimed a missile strike on Saudi Arabia’s Ras Tanura refinery as Iran-related diplomacy continued (TheStreet, citing Al Jazeera/CBS).

Last week, and this week. For the week the Dow finished up 0.41% while the S&P 500 fell 0.47% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.71%, with tech shares pressured after reports from Meta and Lam Research disappointed. The Fed decision Wednesday (2 p.m. ET statement, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference), Thursday’s core PCE and advance GDP data, and earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Intel, Caterpillar, Eli Lilly, Exxon and Chevron are the main catalysts. CME FedWatch showed an 88% probability of unchanged rates Wednesday, while futures pointed to roughly an 80% probability of a December hike; the Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023 (FXEmpire).
