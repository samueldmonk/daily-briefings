The Lead

As of ~9:36 AM ET: stocks rise at the opening bell as tech rebounds and oil retreats — S&P 500 +0.34%, Nasdaq +0.54%, Dow +0.19%

New Opening bell (TheStreet, 9:36 AM ET). “Stocks were rising at the opening bell as oil prices fell and artificial intelligence and technology shares rebounded following the previous day’s sell-off,” TheStreet reported. The S&P 500 gained 0.34%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average edged up 0.19%, the Nasdaq climbed 0.54% and the Russell 2000 advanced 0.03%. TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre said “a chaotic Thursday session may have set the stage for better market action,” pointing to President Trump’s promise not to attack Iran before the midterms and the Financial Times report that OpenAI’s annualized revenue run rate reached about $50 billion in September — “about $20 billion short” of the figure that circulated in late September (TheStreet).

New What’s next (TheStreet, 9:54 AM ET). Capital.com senior market analyst Daniela Hathorn said attention shifts to next week’s U.S. CPI report and the start of third-quarter earnings season, with Wednesday’s inflation figures “particularly important in determining whether the Fed can afford to pause following September’s rate hike,” and major U.S. banks reporting from Tuesday (TheStreet).

Market open (Schwab, Oct. 9). “Tech names led stocks higher in early trading Friday as jitters over OpenAI’s revenue faded and oil prices stabilized,” Schwab’s market-open update said. It listed SpaceX up more than 4% before the bell; T-Mobile, AT&T and Verizon all down at least 6%; tower owners American Tower and Crown Castle each up more than 7%; Humana up about 16%; Apple down more than 2%; Lumentum up more than 6% premarket after its CEO said optical components were sold out through 2029; and Freeport-McMoRan up more than 3% as gold rose, putting the metal on pace for its first weekly gain in three weeks. Investors are watching the University of Michigan’s preliminary sentiment report and remarks later from Kansas City Fed President Jeffrey Schmidt (Schwab).

Treasury hire (TheStreet, 9:15 AM ET). Judy Shelton, whose 2019 Fed nomination was blocked in the Senate in November 2020, has been appointed a counselor to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to advise on currency policy, “with a particular focus on evaluating financial conditions in China,” CNBC reported, per TheStreet (TheStreet).

Apple and the telecom debate (TheStreet, 8:40 AM ET; Invezz). Apple shares fell 2.23% ahead of the bell after Nikkei Asia reported the company told some suppliers to cut component orders for the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, with October orders cut by at least 15% from the levels originally requested; TheStreet’s live blog, updated at 9:00 AM ET, still headlined “Nasdaq futures rise as tech rebounds” (TheStreet). Invezz reported Nvidia up 1.6% premarket and Tesla and Amazon up about 1% each; it said Citi analyst Michael Rollins argued the telecom sell-off looked excessive, with a meaningful financial impact from SpaceX unlikely before at least 2029, and that Morgan Stanley analysts had not detected major supply-chain changes at Apple and kept their estimates broadly intact (Invezz).

Premarket movers (TheStreet, 8:22 AM ET). “Stock futures were climbing as oil prices eased and technology stocks rebounded,” TheStreet wrote at 8:27 AM. Its 8:22 AM movers list: Humana up 14.45%, Crown Castle up 7.60% and Applied Optoelectronics up 7.47%; T-Mobile down 7.49%, AT&T down 6.41%, Verizon down 5.58%, CVS Health down 3.23% and Apple down 2.34%. Delta was “falling nearly 4%” (TheStreet).

Pre-market (Yahoo Finance strip ~7:20 AM ET; TheStreet 7:48 AM ET). “US stock futures rose fractionally on Friday as oil prices fell and tech stocks rebounded,” Yahoo Finance reported. Its market strip (“U.S. markets open in 2h 10m”) had S&P 500 futures up 0.35%, Nasdaq futures up 0.74%, Dow futures up 0.08% and Russell 2000 futures up 0.29%, with the 10-year yield at 5.23 (−0.05), the VIX at 15.27 and November WTI at $90.67 (−0.90%) (Yahoo Finance live blog). “Futures are moving higher this morning, suggesting investors remain willing to buy into weakness,” Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn told TheStreet, which headlined its 7:48 AM update “Nasdaq futures rise as tech rebounds” (TheStreet). Market open is 9:30 AM ET; the live quotes above show the real-time picture.

Why tech is bouncing. Tech recovered after Thursday’s scare, when the Financial Times reported OpenAI’s annualized revenue topped out at $50 billion, $20 billion short of estimates; Bloomberg later reported OpenAI expects to reach or exceed $70 billion in annualized revenue by the end of the year, “helping quell worries that the AI boom is running out of steam,” Yahoo wrote (Yahoo Finance). Stocktwits, via Yahoo, said Nvidia, Micron and TSMC were up more than 1% in early premarket trading (4:38 AM ET article) (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance).

Oil and Iran. At 7:20 AM, West Texas Intermediate was down 0.64% at $90.90 and Brent down 1.08% at $103.20 after President Trump said the U.S. “will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections” on Nov. 3; Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran was reviewing a U.S. response to its proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, and China is set to resume fuel exports after its Golden Week pause (TheStreet). Hathorn cautioned that “continued attacks on shipping around the Strait of Hormuz mean the geopolitical premium is unlikely to disappear quickly.”

Fed. Fed Governor Christopher Waller on Thursday signaled that two additional rate hikes could be appropriate if data stay on their current trajectory, and St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said further tightening may be needed; CME FedWatch priced only a 19% chance of a hike at the October meeting, with 81% expecting a hold (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance).

Thursday, Oct. 8 — the session in review

Official close (4 PM ET). The S&P 500 fell 36.41 points, or 0.47%, to 7,765.36 and the Nasdaq Composite 345.35 points, or 1.25%, to 27,193.34, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 51.77 points, or 0.10%, to 51,231.64; the Russell 2000 slipped 0.03% (Yahoo Finance). “Tech was today’s biggest loser, keeping down a market that otherwise saw gains across most other sectors,” TheStreet wrote at 4:05 PM ET (TheStreet). Yahoo’s intraday detail: the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index “declined as much as 4% before trimming” losses; Arm Holdings, Intel and Marvell Technology “all declined more than 6%,” and Micron “fell more than 5%.” Bitcoin fell 2% to around $81,000, its lowest in roughly three weeks (Yahoo Finance).

Movers & Drivers

+7%+ pre

American Tower (AMT)

The tower owner jumped more than 7% before the open alongside Crown Castle, after SpaceX reached a deal to buy cellular spectrum licenses expected to pave the way for Starlink Mobile to become a major U.S. mobile carrier (Schwab, Oct. 9).

+6%+ pre

Lumentum (LITE)

Gained more than 6% in premarket trading after the CEO said the company had sold out of its optical components through 2029 (Schwab, Oct. 9); TheStreet reported CEO Michael Hurlston told Bloomberg Television capacity was “completely sold out” through early 2029 (TheStreet).

−2.90% to −4% pre

Delta Air Lines (DAL)

Fell 2.90% to $79.80 ahead of the bell (TheStreet, 7:37 AM), “nearly 4%” by TheStreet’s 7:48 AM update, after missing earnings estimates for the first time in two years and cutting its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $5.10–$5.60 from July’s $6.50–$7.50; free-cash-flow outlook cut to $2.5 billion from as much as $4 billion. CEO Ed Bastian said fares keep rising as Delta passes along much of a $6 billion jump in fuel costs, and Delta forecast a 20% rise in fourth-quarter revenue (TheStreet, citing CNBC). Adjusted Q3 EPS was $1.72 vs. $1.75 expected (CNBC, per search listing; CNBC premarket movers).

+14.45% pre

Humana (HUM)

Surged 14.45% after CMS restored a 4-star rating to Humana’s flagship Medicare Advantage contract (H5216), which covers more than 2 million members, up from 3.5 stars (TheStreet, 8:22 AM).

+7.60% pre

Crown Castle (CCI)

The communications-infrastructure REIT jumped 7.60% after SpaceX agreed to acquire a nationwide block of low-band wireless spectrum from Grain Management (TheStreet, 8:22 AM).

−3.23% pre

CVS Health (CVS)

Fell 3.23% after a setback in its Medicare Advantage star ratings from CMS threatened future bonus payments and profit margins (TheStreet, 8:22 AM).

+4.12% pre

SpaceX (SPCX)

Rose 4.12% to $167.18 premarket after agreeing to acquire Grain Management’s nationwide 800 MHz low-band spectrum in a deal valued at around $8 billion; Barclays’ Anthony Valentini initiated coverage with a bullish rating and a $254 price target. Stocktwits adds the FCC approved SpaceX’s application for a next-generation constellation of 15,000 satellites (TheStreet; Stocktwits via Yahoo).

−5.58% to −7.49% pre

AT&T (T), Verizon (VZ), T-Mobile (TMUS)

By 8:22 AM T-Mobile was down 7.49%, AT&T 6.41% and Verizon 5.58% after SpaceX announced the spectrum agreement (TheStreet). Earlier, the group continued Thursday’s after-hours selloff, falling about 6% in early premarket trade on the prospect of SpaceX as a new wireless competitor; AST SpaceMobile (ASTS) also stayed under pressure (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance, 4:38 AM ET). Citi’s Michael Rollins called the sell-off excessive, seeing a meaningful financial impact from SpaceX unlikely before at least 2029 (Invezz).

−2.23% to −2.34% pre

Apple (AAPL)

Down 2.23% in TheStreet’s 8:40 AM item, which cited Nikkei Asia’s report that October component orders were cut by at least 15%; down 2.34% at its 8:22 AM movers update (TheStreet), after more than 1.5% in early premarket trading after Nikkei reported Apple asked suppliers to cut iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max component orders by 15%–20% on soft initial demand and higher price points (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance).

+3% to +7.47% pre

Optical names (LITE, COHR, AAOI, POET)

Applied Optoelectronics was up 7.47% by 8:22 AM after Hurlston told Bloomberg Television Lumentum’s capacity was “completely sold out” through early 2029 (TheStreet). Earlier, Lumentum, Coherent, Applied Optoelectronics and POET Technologies rose 3%–5% early after Lumentum CEO Michael Hurlston reportedly said its optoelectronic components were effectively sold out through early 2029 (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance).

+2%+ pre

Rocket Lab (RKLB) & Archer (ACHR)

Each rose more than 2% early on Barclays calls: an Overweight initiation with an $85 target on Rocket Lab, and an upgrade of Archer to Overweight with the target raised to $8 from $4.50 (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance).

−6%+

Chipmakers (ARM, INTC, MRVL, MU) — Thursday

Arm Holdings, Intel and Marvell Technology each fell more than 6% and Micron more than 5% Thursday as the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index declined as much as 4% on the FT’s OpenAI revenue report (Yahoo Finance).
