Noche UFC · Desert Diamond Arena, Glendale, Arizona

Noche UFC just set the UFC’s biggest domestic Fight Night gate — and broke the building’s all-time record on the way

Saturday’s card did $4,436,470 in ticket sales in front of more than 16,928 people — the highest domestic gate for a UFC Fight Night, and enough to pass Drake’s 5 September 2023 show as the top-grossing event in Desert Diamond Arena’s history. It is the first single-day event to take that building record, which is the detail that makes the number remarkable: it beat a concert residency’s gross in one night.

This matters beyond the box office. Noche UFC is a Fight Night, not a numbered pay-per-view — the fourth annual edition, built around Mexican Independence Day, formally UFC Fight Night: Silva vs. Delgado. A non-PPV card clearing $4.4m says the Mexican Independence Day franchise now prices like a main event in its own right, independent of whose name is on the poster. Previous editions of this page reported that no gate or attendance figure had been published; both are now on the record.

The fight that headlined it — carried and re-confirmed

Jean Silva did not throw a strike for the first half of the opening round and was dropped by a right cross from Jose Miguel Delgado, who had taken the fight on short notice, at home in Glendale, after Yair Rodríguez withdrew injured. Silva woke up in the second. In the third he dropped Delgado along the fence, took the back, and sank a one-armed rear-naked choke at 2:57. It leaves him 7-1 inside the Octagon with six finishes and, in UFC.com’s framing, “making a case for a championship opportunity in the future.” One fight earlier, Alexa Grasso beat Manon Fiorot by unanimous decision to become, in UFC.com’s words, the “clubhouse leader” for the next women’s flyweight title shot — a chase that is live precisely because the belt is vacant.

Performance bonuses

$100,000 each, four awards. Performance of the Night: Jean Silva, for the main-event submission; and Sean King, who slammed Jessie Rosas for a knockout 36 seconds into his Octagon debut. Fight of the Night: Tommy McMillen and Marwan Rahiki, $100,000 each for fifteen minutes that nearly ended in the second round. Sherdog’s bonus report, fetched in full on an earlier run, enumerates these four and no others.

Around the Sport

  • Magomed Ankalaev is No. 1 in the official UFC light-heavyweight rankings and has nowhere left to rise. His most recent outing was a dominant main-event win over Bogdan Guskov at UFC Abu Dhabi on 25 July 2026, more than tripling Guskov’s striking output with two takedowns and three submission attempts before stopping him in the fifth round. With champion Carlos Ulberg out into 2027 after ACL surgery, the queue has nowhere to go: Ankalaev either waits or takes an interim fight that adds nothing and could cost everything. Kamaru Usman has floated Chimaev vs. Ankalaev for an interim light-heavyweight belt — that is Usman’s suggestion, not a UFC booking, and the promotion has not indicated an interim title will be created.
  • Note on rankings provenance: ESPN’s light-heavyweight list places Ankalaev at No. 2, behind Alex Pereira, on the October 2025 result between them. That is a contenders ranking and is reported as such; it does not bear on who holds the belt, which is settled in the Champions Board below.
  • Brandon Moreno ended a two-fight losing run in his first Noche UFC appearance — UFC.com calls it a “much-needed and emotional win” for the former flyweight champion.
  • Marwan Rahiki fell to 9-1, his first professional defeat, and still left with $100,000 for a fight he very nearly won.
  • Curtis Blaydes is back in the win column and likely back in the heavyweight top five, though UFC.com is blunt that the performance “left a lot to be desired.”
  • A scheduled newcomer bout between Ramiro Jimenez and Rodrigo Vera was cancelled when Vera was removed over visa issues.

Cross-check — the regressions cleared

ESPN’s “Current and all-time UFC champions” page, read this run, agrees with every men’s belt it returned: Aspinall at heavyweight, Ulberg at light heavyweight (UFC 327, 11 April 2026), Strickland at middleweight (UFC 328, 9 May 2026), Makhachev at welterweight, Gaethje at lightweight (Freedom 250, 14 June 2026) and Volkanovski at featherweight. That is a sharp reversal: the previous run recorded four regressed rows — Pereira at light heavyweight, Chimaev at middleweight, Topuria at lightweight and Shevchenko at women’s flyweight — the worst reading this site has logged. Three of those four are gone on this read.

Two caveats, so this is not mistaken for an all-clear. (1) The read returned only six divisions; bantamweight, flyweight and all three women’s divisions were not in what came back, so the women’s-flyweight row — regressed on five consecutive prior runs — is untested this run, not cleared. The vacancy is corroborated independently by the UFC 332 listing, which states Silva vs. Wang is for the vacant title. (2) The regression set has never been monotonic on this site — it got worse immediately after a run recorded it fixed. So this is logged as a good read, not a resolution, and every belt above remains re-derived from the most recent title-changing card rather than copied from any list.
