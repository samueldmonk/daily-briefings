The Lead

As of 1:30 PM ET: Dow up 0.82%, Nasdaq up ~0.5%, S&P 500 up ~0.4% as oil slides and yields ease

Wall Street is ending a volatile week on a firmer note. At 1:30 PM ET the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up about 420 points, or 0.82%, with the Nasdaq Composite up roughly 0.5% and the S&P 500 up about 0.4% — firmer than the 1:00 PM ET reading (Dow +0.72%, Nasdaq +0.46%, S&P 500 +0.39%). Around 12:15 PM ET Yahoo Finance’s quote strip had shown the Dow up 0.86%, the Nasdaq up 0.63% and the S&P 500 up 0.53%, with the Cboe Volatility Index down 3.51% at 15.12. Bloomberg said Treasury yields dropped from multi-decade highs, though a gauge of volatility was still set for its biggest weekly jump in over a year. CNBC said the Dow was still heading for a fourth straight losing week, down 0.1% week to date, while the S&P 500 was on track for a roughly 1% weekly advance and the Nasdaq was up nearly 2% for the week. Schwab noted early that the S&P 500 was on pace for a positive week despite the rally in yields. The lift came from oil: November WTI crude was down 2.80% at $91.96 at midday (Yahoo Finance), extending a 2.34% drop to $92.54 around the open (Schwab), after Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the Strait of Hormuz could reopen and nuclear talks restart within seven days if Washington meets conditions he said are already in the collapsed June “Islamabad MOU” (CNBC via TheStreet), and as Reuters reported U.S. and Iranian negotiators in New York exploring a phased path out of the war.

Bonds remain the market’s pressure point. The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.225% during Thursday’s session, its highest since 2007, and the 30-year reached 5.502%, its highest since 2004 (TheStreet); the 10-year stood at 5.17% heading into Friday’s open, below its overnight peak (Schwab). CME FedWatch put October rate-hike odds at 66% early Friday (Schwab). The University of Michigan’s final September consumer sentiment reading came in at 48.1, down 7% from August but above the 47.5 expected and the 47.8 preliminary reading (MarketScreener); year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.6%, the highest since June (CNN), and August durable goods orders were flat, up 0.3% excluding transportation (Schwab). On trade, U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer said Washington and Beijing reached agreements on a subset of goods that could trade on more favorable terms, with “a lot more details” due Monday (CNBC via TheStreet), as Chinese President Xi Jinping concludes his White House visit (Yahoo Finance).

Movers & Drivers

+8% mid-morning

Akamai Technologies (AKAM)

Surged 15.31% in premarket trading (TheStreet) and was up almost 17% early (Schwab), then pared the gain to 8% at $119 by around 10 AM ET (Investopedia) after announcing a seven-year, $11.6 billion cloud services commitment from Anthropic to support its CPU workloads on Akamai Cloud (TheStreet). The companies can expand the arrangement by another $9 billion, to roughly $20 billion, and Akamai issued Anthropic a warrant equal to up to about 5% of its shares outstanding; Piper Sandler raised its price target to $158 from $125 (Yahoo Finance). JPMorgan lifted its target to $167 from $158 but stayed neutral, noting Anthropic accounts for some 93% of the $14.4 billion in new deals Akamai has signed this year; Oppenheimer reiterated outperform with a $180 target (Investopedia). Reported premarket gains varied widely across outlets.

+10.3% pre-mkt

People Inc. (PPLI)

Jumped 10.27% premarket after The Wall Street Journal reported MGM Resorts is weighing a takeover bid for Barry Diller’s media company — days after People withdrew its own $48.30-a-share offer for the rest of MGM (TheStreet).

+5.1% pre-mkt

Equinix (EQIX)

Gained 5.12% premarket amid accelerating demand for enterprise AI infrastructure and recent bullish analyst price-target updates (TheStreet).

+5% early

Arm Holdings (ARM) & AMD

Chip and AI-infrastructure stocks moved mostly higher early, led by Arm, up 5%; AMD climbed 2% after Bank of America raised its price target, citing the importance of CPUs in the agentic era (Schwab).

−4.4% pre-mkt

Twilio (TWLO)

Lost 4.41% premarket after HSBC downgraded the stock to reduce from hold with a $211 price target (TheStreet).

−4.0% pre-mkt

Zscaler (ZS)

Fell 3.98% premarket after naming Ross Tackett chief revenue officer, effective Oct. 1 (TheStreet).

−2% early

Nike (NKE)

Fell 2% early after Bank of America downgraded it to underperform from neutral, seeing downside risk to earnings estimates and saying the turnaround is taking longer than expected (Schwab).

Lower pre-mkt

Costco (COST)

Slipped ahead of the open despite fiscal fourth-quarter earnings that topped estimates, as investors focused on a further slowdown in membership revenue growth (Schwab).
