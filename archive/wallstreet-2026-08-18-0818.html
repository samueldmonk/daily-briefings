The Lead

Futures lower before the bell as oil hits $91 and the long bond touches a 19-year high

Markets have not opened yet. This edition was compiled at roughly 8 AM ET on Tuesday, August 18, about ninety minutes before the New York opening bell; the figures below are futures and pre-market prices, and the last official closes are Monday's, set out in the Weekly Scorecard.

US stock futures fell on Tuesday as rising tensions between Washington and Tehran revived inflation concerns, with elevated oil prices adding pressure. In Yahoo Finance's 5 AM ET write-up, futures on the Dow Jones Industrial Average slid 0.2%, those on the S&P 500 declined 0.5%, and contracts for the Nasdaq-100 retreated 0.9%, leading the major indexes lower after a downbeat start to the trading week. Bloomberg's pre-market report, timed at 5:08 AM in New York, had the Nasdaq 100 contract down 1.1% and the S&P 500 contract down 0.5% — the same picture, a fraction wider on the tech side. A live read of Yahoo Finance's futures strip taken while this edition was being assembled showed S&P futures at 7,729.25 (−39.50, −0.51%), Dow futures at 53,492.00 (−52.00, −0.10%), Nasdaq futures at 29,762.75 (−333.25, −1.11%) and Russell 2000 futures at 3,055.30 (−9.60, −0.31%), with the VIX up 4.54% at 15.88.

Oil is the mechanism. Crude climbed to its highest level in more than two weeks after President Trump said he intends to inflict more economic pain on Iran and threatened to "bomb" Oman if it interferes with US plans for the Strait of Hormuz. Brent crude futures hit $91 a barrel and US benchmark West Texas Intermediate rose to $84, as the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to its lowest level since 1982. Trading Economics had Brent at $90.97, up 0.11% on the day; a separate account put the move at up 0.5% to $91.3 as the US–Iran ceasefire expired. Shipping data underlines the risk premium: only five commodity vessels transited the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday and none on Sunday, against 31 the previous weekend.

And rates are following it. Rising oil, an AI-driven borrowing spree and concerns over government borrowing have lifted bond yields globally. In the US the 10-year Treasury yield hit 4.72% and the 30-year advanced to 5.31% — a 19-year high. Those are the same levels CNBC reported at Monday's close, where the long bond rose more than four basis points to 5.311%, its highest since June 2007, and the 10-year gained more than two basis points to 4.724%. Rounded figures are published here rather than the decimal ones because the pre-market read is the newer of the two and is quoted to two figures.

Earnings are the offset. Reporting season has slowed but is still supporting equities, and Tuesday brings a small cluster of consumer names — Home Depot, Toll Brothers and Klarna among them. Home Depot reported at 6 AM ET and beat (see Movers below), which is the one clean piece of good news on the tape this morning.

Movers & Drivers

Up 2% pre-marketNew

Home Depot (HD)

Beat on both lines before the bell. Second-quarter fiscal 2026 sales of $47.9 billion, up 5.7% year over year, with comparable sales up 1.7% — more than the roughly 1% expected and the strongest comp since late 2022 — and US same-store sales up 1.3% against 0.9% expected. Diluted earnings came in at $4.79 per share. Outdoor categories including live goods, mulch, patio and grills drove the momentum. The company reiterated its 2026 forecast: total sales growth of 2.5% to 4.5% and comparable sales flat to up 2%. Bloomberg read the result as a sign that spending on improvement projects is holding up despite high borrowing and housing costs. Shares rose about 2% in pre-market trading. The earnings call is at 9 AM ET.

Index addNew

Reddit (RDDT)

Joins the S&P 500 effective before the opening of trading today, replacing AvalonBay Communities. S&P Dow Jones Indices announced the change on August 13 and Reddit shares jumped more than 11% on the news that Friday. At a roughly $29.5 billion market capitalisation, Reddit becomes the second major social media company in the index after Meta — and it enters with its stock down 31% on the year. AvalonBay is leaving because of its all-stock merger with Equity Residential, agreed in May, which forms a rental-housing company with a $69 billion enterprise value; the combined company, to be called Vivmark Residential, will remain in the index once the deal completes.

Up 0.49%

Crude oil (CL=F) — the driver

The September WTI contract was quoted at 84.15, up 0.41 or 0.49%, on Yahoo Finance's live commodities strip as this edition was assembled, with Brent at $91. This is the single input doing the most work on the tape this morning: it is what is reviving the inflation argument, what is pushing the long bond to a 19-year high, and what Bloomberg tied on Monday to speculation that the Federal Reserve could raise rates before the year is out. Gold, unusually, was not catching a bid alongside it — the December contract was quoted at 4,448.80, down 24.90 or 0.56%. Bitcoin was up 1.46% at $64,170.39.

No other individual US stock had a verified Tuesday pre-market percentage move in the sources fetched for this edition. Named pre-market movers carried in Monday's and Friday's CNBC round-ups — Alibaba, Intel, SanDisk, Western Digital, Micron, Applied Materials, Wayfair — belong to those sessions and are deliberately not restated here as today's moves.

On the Radar

  • Home Depot's earnings call at 9 AM ET — the print is out and it beat; the guidance was reiterated rather than raised, and the call is where management will be pressed on tariffs and the housing drag.
  • Toll Brothers and Klarna report among the remaining second-quarter names on deck today.
  • Reddit enters the S&P 500 at the open, replacing AvalonBay Communities — index funds must buy.
  • The Strait of Hormuz. Trump has threatened to bomb Oman, which has been mediating, if it interferes with US plans for the waterway. Transit volumes have collapsed — five vessels on Saturday, none on Sunday, against 31 the previous weekend — and that is what is holding the oil premium in place.
  • The long end of the curve. With the 30-year at a 19-year high and the stated mechanism being speculation the Fed could hike rather than cut before year-end, the bond market is now the thing setting the tone for equities rather than the other way round.
