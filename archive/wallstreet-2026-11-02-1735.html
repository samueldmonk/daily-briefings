The Lead

Closing bell: Dow +0.41%, S&P 500 −0.51%, Nasdaq −1.07% as tech sinks and oil plunges; Apple little changed after hours despite beat; Palantir, Fabrinet rise

By the closing bell the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 213.67 points, or 0.41%, to 51,983.98, while the S&P 500 shed 0.51% to 7,541.40 and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.07% to 25,164.32 (Kiplinger; Yahoo Finance, via search; TheStreet). All three indexes opened higher — Dow +0.7%, S&P +0.5%, Nasdaq +0.7% — and the Nasdaq climbed as much as 1.3% before the gains unwound in the second half of the session (Yahoo Finance).

The drag was the AI trade. Apple (−1.8%) and Nvidia (−1.3%) traded places atop the market-cap leaderboard amid another down day for chip stocks; Microsoft fell 2.8% and Meta 1.3% on lingering capex and return-on-AI worries, Marvell slid 12.7% and Lumentum 18.6% after their all-stock deal (Kiplinger). The tech sector ETF (XLK) was down 2.4% by 2:40 p.m. ET (Yahoo Finance). Materials, financials, industrials, consumer staples, real estate and consumer discretionary each rose more than 1%; IDEXX (+21.9%) led the S&P 500 and Sherwin-Williams (+6%) the Dow (Kiplinger).

The offset was oil. Front-month WTI fell 8% to $95.84 a barrel after Axios reported President Trump told Israel’s Benjamin Netanyahu he is open to diplomacy, and IRNA said Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei has permitted Iran’s government to engage in peace negotiations. Treasury yields were mixed: the 2-year rose 1.3 bp to 4.594% while the 10-year (−2.5 bp, 4.904%) and 30-year (−2.9 bp, 5.28%) slipped (Kiplinger).

After the bell, Apple reported fiscal Q4 revenue of $114.48 billion versus about $113.39 billion expected and EPS of $2.10 versus $1.99 (LSEG), with iPhone revenue of $57.58 billion against a $56.54 billion estimate; iPad sales missed (CNBC, via search). Early after-hours reads were mixed — TipRanks said shares slipped, while CNBC said they were little changed in extended trading (TipRanks; CNBC, via search); gross margin of 47.9% included about one point from tariff refunds, which also added $0.05 to EPS (TipRanks). Palantir, meanwhile, rose after hours after reporting third-quarter revenue up 96% to $2.31 billion and lifting its full-year revenue outlook to $8.53–$8.534 billion (TipRanks). Tuesday brings the U.S. midterm elections and Friday the October jobs report (Kiplinger).
