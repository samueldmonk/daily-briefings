Top Story

Michael Page wins on Saturday, is off the rankings by Monday, and a release is reportedly looming

UFC middleweight Michael “Venom” Page beat Nursulton Ruziboev by unanimous decision at UFC Paris on 5 September. He was initially eligible to be ranked following that win. Then, according to longtime MMA journalist John Morgan, the weekly UFC rankings email sent on Monday 7 September told voters that Page had been removed from the rankings and would not be eligible for this week's selection.

Page had been No. 15 in the UFC's media rankings and No. 8 in the recently formed Meta rankings, which the promotion adopted this summer. Reporting this run frames the removal as a strong signal that a release from the promotion is imminent. The release itself is reported as looming rather than announced, and is described that way here; nothing read this run is a promotion statement, and the ranking removal is the only action actually confirmed to have happened.

Rankings & Business

Rankings movement

  • Michael Page removed from the rankings on 7 September, having been No. 15 in the media rankings and No. 8 in the Meta rankings. See the Top Story.
  • The Meta rankings are new. The UFC adopted the Meta-branded ranking board this summer; it now runs alongside the long-standing media panel, and Page held a materially higher position on the newer board.

Business & broadcast

  • UFC Freedom 250 averaged 8.2 million viewers across the U.S. and Latin America on Paramount+, the streamer's biggest live exclusive event ever, and reached 17 million total viewers — unique people who watched for at least a minute. Including international audiences in Australia, China, India, South Korea, New Zealand and the U.K., TKO puts total global reach at 34 million.
  • UFC 324 drew a live average-minute audience of 4.96 million for its main card, the largest exclusive live event on Paramount+ at the time.
  • Paramount is in year one of a seven-year, $7.7 billion media-rights deal with the UFC, which is owned by TKO Group. Paramount says the arrangement is putting UFC events in front of an audience as much as 20 times larger than the pay-per-view average of the previous two years.
  • UFC 332 extends that reach to broadcast television, with the first numbered-event main card to air live and free on CBS.
