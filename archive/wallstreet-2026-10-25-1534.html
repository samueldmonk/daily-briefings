The Lead

Friday’s close (markets closed for the weekend): Dow −1.23%, S&P 500 −0.52%, Nasdaq −0.21% as Brent tops $116

The final tally. The S&P 500 fell 39.75 points to 7,623.68, the Dow dropped 652.82 points to 52,215.16, and the Nasdaq Composite gave up 53.52 points to 25,955.34 (AP via Messenger-Inquirer). In percentage terms: Dow −1.23%, S&P 500 −0.52%, Nasdaq −0.21% (TradingKey). The S&P 500 added to Thursday’s decline and posted its second straight weekly loss; the Nasdaq slipped after giving up a modest gain, and nearly three of every four stocks in the S&P 500 fell (AP).

Oil did the damage. Brent crude rose 3.1% to settle at $116.72 a barrel — up from less than $97 at the start of October and its highest level since June 2022 (AP). TradingKey said concerns about a renewed escalation of the U.S.-Iran conflict pushed WTI back above $100, and that a sell-off previously concentrated in tech spread to consumer and healthcare stocks (TradingKey).

AI names cushioned the fall. Micron rose 2.5%, Intel 1.7%, Microsoft 0.7% and AMD 1.7% (AP); the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index gained 0.44% to 10,779.46, with 17 of its 30 members higher (TradingKey). “Investors appear increasingly focused on the economics of AI, particularly the substantial upfront costs required to build AI infrastructure and the uncertainty around the timing of returns on those investments,” said Angelo Kourkafas, senior global strategist at Edward Jones (AP).

Bonds and the Fed. The 10-year Treasury yield edged up to 4.99% from 4.96% late Thursday, after Thursday’s sharp drop. Wall Street expects the Fed to hold rates steady at next week’s meeting but is leaning heavily toward another hike in December; the Fed raised its benchmark rate at its last meeting with U.S. inflation “stubbornly well above 3%” (AP).

Geopolitics and policy. Two senior Iranian security officials told Reuters Iran has drawn up plans to strike Gulf subsea communications cables if the U.S. and Israel attack again; TeleGeography’s Alan Mauldin said bandwidth routed through the Strait of Hormuz is less than 1% of the global total. Separately, the Wall Street Journal reported the administration plans to eliminate 50% tariffs on certain Canadian agricultural and forestry products, possibly offset by higher tariffs on other Canadian goods (TradingKey, citing Reuters and WSJ).

The week, and the week ahead. For the week the Dow finished up 0.41% while the S&P 500 fell 0.47% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.71%, with tech shares pressured after reports from Meta and Lam Research disappointed. The Fed decision Wednesday (2 p.m. ET statement, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference), Thursday’s core PCE and advance GDP data, and earnings from Alphabet, Microsoft, Amazon, Intel, Caterpillar, Eli Lilly, Exxon and Chevron are the main catalysts. CME FedWatch showed an 88% probability of unchanged rates Wednesday, while futures pointed to roughly an 80% probability of a December hike; the Fed raised rates in September for the first time since 2023 (FXEmpire).

How Friday unfolded. In Yahoo Finance market-strip readings from about 10:32 AM to 2:04 PM ET the Dow was down between 0.95% and 1.39%, while the Nasdaq swung between +0.28% and −0.07%, sitting at −0.05% at about 2:04 PM ET (desk record) — before closing 0.21% lower (AP).
