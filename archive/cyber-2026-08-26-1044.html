Top story

Boston Scientific’s 8-K: a cyber incident that has reached the ability to ship customer orders, worldwide

The filing itself is now available, and it is more specific than yesterday’s secondhand reports. In a Form 8-K filed with the SEC under Item 8.01 and fetched in full this run, Boston Scientific states that “On August 25, 2026, Boston Scientific Corporation identified a cybersecurity incident affecting certain of its information technology systems that has resulted in a global disruption to the Company’s operations.” The medical device maker is one of the largest in the world, and the disclosure is unusual in that it leads with operational impact rather than data exposure.

The operational language is the part that matters. The company says the incident “has caused, and is expected to continue to cause, disruptions and limitations of access to certain of the Company’s information systems and business applications that support aspects of the Company’s operations, including the ability to process and ship customer orders.” On restoration: “While the Company is working diligently to restore affected functions and systems access, the timeline for a full restoration is not yet known.” On response: it “activated its incident response protocols and began an investigation with the assistance of third-party cybersecurity experts to assess and to contain the threat.” And on materiality, the filing is explicit that the company “has not yet determined whether the incident is reasonably likely to have a material impact.” The 8-K is signed by Susan Thompson, Vice President, Chief Corporate Counsel and Assistant Secretary.

The market read it immediately. Boston Scientific stock fell 3.2% in premarket trading as of Investing.com’s wire published at 7:10 a.m. ET; later reads surfaced this run put the premarket decline at 3.5% to 4% and at 5.8%. ⚠ Those later figures carry no timestamp on the pages that returned them, so all three are published as successive snapshots of a moving premarket rather than reconciled into one number. That is the rare case where a security event and a market event are the same event, and both desks on this site are carrying it this morning.

What is still not known matters as much as what is. Nothing in the 8-K names a threat actor, a ransomware family, a CVE, an intrusion vector, or any patient-safety or device-integrity consequence, and the filing does not characterise the incident as a data breach or state that any data was exfiltrated. None of those things is asserted here. The company says its own investigation is ongoing and that “the full scope, nature and impacts, including operational and financial impacts, of the incident are not yet known.”

The defensive read-through, stated conservatively. Healthcare and medical-device suppliers sit upstream of hospital operations, so a disruption of this kind propagates as a third-party availability problem for customers before it becomes anything else. If Boston Scientific is in your supply chain, the useful work today is confirming your own contingency for delayed shipments and degraded vendor support — not speculating about the intrusion vector.

The wires have caught up, and they place it in a sequence. Accounts carried this run by The Star, New Hampshire’s Union Leader and Insurance Journal confirm the company detected the attack on August 25, activated incident response and engaged third-party cybersecurity experts — consistent with the filing — and set it against a run of incidents in the same sector, naming device makers Abbott Laboratories, Stryker and Medtronic, health insurer Clover Health, drugmaker Novo Nordisk and drug-delivery supplier West Pharmaceutical Services as having been hit recently. Fierce Biotech, surfaced this run, gives an overlapping but not identical list — Abbott, iRhythm, Medtronic and Stryker among medtechs, plus Amgen and Novo Nordisk among large pharma — adding iRhythm and Amgen to the names this page has seen. The two lists are printed as each outlet gives them and are not merged into one roster. Ireland’s Echo reports under the headline “Boston Scientific confirms it has been hit by cyberattack; Cork staff told to work from home” — the first specific operational consequence at a named site to surface on this page. ⚠ Those accounts are search summaries and headlines, not pages fetched in full, and they state only that the other companies were “recently hit” — no date, no attacker and no link between any of them is asserted here, and no common actor should be inferred.

Patch priority

Do this first — two federal deadlines inside 48 hours, both on flaws already under attack

CVE-2026-21962 — Oracle HTTP Server and Oracle WebLogic Server Proxy Plug-in — CVSS 10.0. An unauthenticated attacker with network access over HTTP can compromise the affected components, leading to unauthorized creation, deletion or modification of critical data, or complete access to all data those components can reach. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on August 24 citing evidence of active exploitation, with a remediation date of August 27 — that is tomorrow, on the tightest window the agency assigns. Affected versions are 12.2.1.4.0, 14.1.1.0.0 and 14.1.2.0.0; the fix has been available since Oracle’s January 20, 2026 critical patch update. If you run an internet-facing WebLogic proxy component or an Oracle HTTP Server front end, this is today’s work, not tomorrow’s.

And the flaw with no clock at all. GitLab CVE-2026-19478 (CVSS 9.4) came under active exploitation within days of disclosure, per watchTowr, which reproduced it within minutes of the advisory. It lets an unauthenticated attacker modify or delete publicly accessible GitLab projects and rewrite their data through a GraphQL directive — no credentials, no user interaction. It is not in KEV, so it carries no federal deadline and will not appear on the countdown below; that absence is precisely why it is named here. Affected: 18.2 before 18.11.11, 19.0 before 19.0.8, 19.1 before 19.1.6, 19.2 before 19.2.4. Fixed in 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8 and 18.11.11.

New on the board this run — Gitea CVE-2026-60004, due Friday. CISA added the critical Gitea code-injection flaw to KEV on Tuesday, August 25, confirming exploitation in the wild; Help Net Security reports that CISA has ordered US federal civilian agencies to remediate by August 28, 2026. The bug lets an attacker abuse Gitea’s diffpatch endpoint to install and execute a Git hook from repository-controlled content. In the maintainers’ own words: “An attacker with ordinary write access to a repository can execute arbitrary shell commands as the Gitea OS user … With open registration enabled, the attack can be performed by an unauthenticated visitor after registering a normal account and creating a repository.” The published advisory contained a PoC exploit. In the one documented compromise — written up by the victim, a self-described full-stack developer, on Habr — an automated scanner registered an account on an outdated instance with open registration and no email confirmation or CAPTCHA, wrote a “proof” of RCE into a Git branch, then pulled a shell loader and a crypto-miner dropper; the active part of the attack took about 11 seconds. Fixed in 1.27.1; 1.27.2 is the current release and is what admins are being told to move to. Depending on isolation, exposure may include Gitea’s main config file, application and process-environment secrets, database credentials and contents, and OAuth and integration credentials.

Overnight, one deadline lapsed. CVE-2026-68820 — the Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock use-after-free exploited as a zero-day by the North Korean Lazarus group — was due August 25 and is now one day past due. It has moved from the “due today” bucket to the overdue column below, which is where the majority of this board now sits.

Carried · 8:18 editionPhishing-as-a-serviceMirage2FA

Mirage2FA has been quietly eating Microsoft 365 sessions for two years

Thousands of companies have been affected by the Mirage2FA campaign from 2024 to 2026, according to ANY.RUN research reported by The Hacker News on August 25. It is a commercial phishing-as-a-service toolkit that targets Microsoft 365 accounts by abusing legitimate login flows and bypassing two-factor authentication — the headline count is 4,500 US and EU companies.

The number that should change your incident model is 48%. ANY.RUN assesses that 48% of targeted email addresses were potentially compromised. Because the toolkit steals passwords and session cookies, an attacker lands inside an already-authenticated Microsoft 365 session — and, with it, SSO-connected services. That is the part 2FA does not save you from: the second factor was satisfied, by the victim, on the attacker’s behalf. What follows is impersonation, fraud and onward compromise from a mailbox that colleagues and counterparties trust.

Geography: the United States accounts for 63.7% of the total, and most affected companies are US-based. This item was carried on this page as search synthesis for two days and has been promoted to a fetched primary this run — the standing note that it lacked one is now retired.

CarriedRansomwareMedusa

Medusa has passed 500 victims, and the federal advisory was rewritten for it

The FBI, CISA and the Department of Health and Human Services updated their joint #StopRansomware advisory on Medusa on August 18, 2026, using data from FBI investigations as of April 2026. The headline number moved sharply: Medusa developers and affiliates have now impacted more than 500 victims across critical infrastructure, against the more than 300 reported in the March 2025 version.

The mechanic worth planning around is the clock. Medusa runs double extortion with a 48-hour window — victims get two days to respond before the group begins contacting them directly and posts the stolen data with a countdown timer. That is an incident-response design constraint: the decision chain has to be exercised before the note arrives, not after it.

Attackers are switching Magento shoppers into other people’s accounts — and Adobe says it has not seen it

CVE-2026-71362 is an incorrect-authorization flaw in Adobe Commerce and Magento that can be leveraged to “gain elevated access to sensitive resources” without authentication. It is one of seven issues Adobe fixed in a security update, and the vendor advisory (APSB26-92) states Adobe is not aware of exploits in the wild for any of the fixed flaws. E-commerce security firm Sansec says otherwise: its Shield web application firewall is already blocking CVE-2026-71362 exploitation attempts. Both statements are published as made; this page does not adjudicate between the vendor and the WAF vendor.

The mechanic, from Sansec’s patch analysis. Exploiting it requires “no existing account, administrator privileges or user interaction.” The root cause is Magento improperly handling customer identity in an account session: “Sansec reviewed the patch and confirmed that the vulnerability lets attackers switch a customer session to another customer account. This gives them access to the victim’s account and private customer data.” The other six flaws in the same update are CVE-2026-48413 (8.7) and CVE-2026-48414 (7.7), stored XSS leading to arbitrary code execution; CVE-2026-48415 (7.6) and CVE-2026-48416 (7.5), incorrect authorization enabling a security-feature bypass; CVE-2026-48411 (6.5); and CVE-2026-48412 (2.7).

Carried · 9:16 editionCVSS 10.0Identity

Microsoft shipped a CVSS 10.0 Entra ID RCE — then corrected its own bulletin to say it was never exploited

Microsoft disclosed CVE-2026-69836 (CVSS 10.0), a remote code execution flaw in Entra ID, its cloud identity and access management service, formerly Azure Active Directory. In Microsoft’s words: “Deserialization of untrusted data in Microsoft Entra ID allows an unauthorized attacker to execute code over a network.” The bug class is the classic one — an application turning user-controlled data back into a live object without validating it.

The correction is the story. The security bulletin originally marked the “Exploited” field “Yes.” On August 21, 2026, after The Hacker News contacted the company, Microsoft changed that status to “No” and stated that “this vulnerability was not exploited in the wild.” A spokesperson added that the CVE was published “for greater transparency” and that “there are no additional actions customers need to take.” Microsoft says the flaw is fully mitigated on its side. It credits principal security engineer Robert Fitzpatrick with the discovery.

This item is carried precisely because the first version of it was wrong at the source. A maximum-severity identity flaw briefly labelled “exploited” by the vendor is exactly the kind of claim that propagates faster than its retraction — the corrected status is what this page publishes.

Carried · 8:18 editionAI securityNo CVE

A malicious webpage can poison the local AI model behind NVIDIA NemoClaw

Oasis Security has disclosed a weakness in NVIDIA NemoClaw that could let an attacker-controlled webpage take unauthenticated control of the local Ollama instance serving an AI agent — and plant hidden instructions inside the model itself. NemoClaw is NVIDIA’s open-source reference stack for running agents such as OpenClaw inside its OpenShell sandboxes; Ollama is one of its supported local inference backends.

Patch status is split by platform. Oasis Security’s head of research, Elad Luz, told The Hacker News that NemoClaw v0.0.35 fixed the issue on macOS and Linux. There is no fix on the Windows and WSL path, where v0.0.34 added a Windows installation that carries a warning instead. The findings were reported to NVIDIA’s PSIRT ahead of publication. The research carries no CVE identifier, and no exploitation had been reported as of August 25, 2026 — so it appears here and not in the Patch Priority box or the KEV countdowns.

Carried · 8:18 editionSupply chainClickFix

24 npm packages are being used as free phishing infrastructure

Researchers have disclosed a campaign using a cluster of 24 npm packages to host and redirect to ClickFix-style fake Cloudflare CAPTCHA pages. The twist is what the registry is being used for. “While the malware is simply a single HTML page inside the npm package, and while downloading it wouldn’t do harm, the threat actor’s use of npm isn’t to infect developers who install it, but to use the registry and its mirrors as a safe, validated storage for the malware,” said OX Security researchers Moshe Siman Tov Bustan and Vitalii Chepurko.

That reframes the detection problem: the payload is served from unpkg mirrors, which is to say from a domain your proxy almost certainly trusts and your developers legitimately need. Some of the packages are still available for download. The named cluster includes bgzxcuite2, prezdentkxheiw, egair0810, mnteckets, airdzticket, egypt0811, passport811 and @worrisome/reutil among others.

Carried · 8:18 editionAuthenticationDefensive

WhatsApp adds multiple passkeys per account — and a full password option

Meta announced on Tuesday a set of WhatsApp account-security features, headlined by support for multiple passkeys on a single account, so users with both iOS and Android devices can sign in with the phishing-resistant method on each. The company says more than 1 billion people use a passkey to log into WhatsApp. Passkey support arrived on Android in October 2023, on iOS in early 2024, and in Facebook logins in June 2025.

Alongside it, WhatsApp is adding a full password option as part of two-step verification — the stated aim being to prevent account takeover “even if they get hold of your one-time passcode” — and Android users will see more context on calls from people not in their contacts. Passkeys are managed under Settings > Account > Passkeys.

CarriedExploitedWordPress

Attackers are trying to log in as your WordPress administrator

Bad actors are attempting to exploit two severe unauthenticated authentication bypasses in the Xecurify miniOrange SAML 2.0 Single Sign On plugin that make it possible to sign in as any WordPress user, including administrators, per Patchstack. CVE-2026-15981 (CVSS 9.8) is an authentication bypass stemming from accepting malformed signatures as valid, fixed in 17.0.6 for the Standard edition; CVE-2026-61979 (CVSS 8.1) is an unauthenticated privilege escalation from signature-algorithm confusion, fixed in 17.0.5.

The root cause is a textbook one: mo_saml_validate_signature() performs a loose boolean check on the raw tri-state integer returned by PHP’s openssl_verify(), so the error return value of −1 evaluates as truthy and a failed signature check is treated as a successful one.

CarriedMalwareNovel tradecraft

E4del and PINHOLE read their orders out of FTP welcome banners

Researchers have flagged a campaign that uses FTP banners as dead drop resolvers to deliver two previously unreported remote access trojans, E4del and PINHOLE. An FTP banner is the welcome text a server sends the moment a client connects; the mechanism lets “malware stagers fetch commands directly from the protocol’s initial response,” SOCRadar said. Abusing legitimate services as dead drops is well established — doing it over FTP is, per the report, the first time this technique has been spotted in the wild.

It is also, as the report notes, less stealthy than web-based dead drops, because security controls are likely to flag FTP connections. That is the detection opportunity: ordinary endpoints have essentially no reason to speak FTP to the internet. No CVE, no CVSS and no KEV entry attaches — this is malware tradecraft, not a product flaw.

CarriedMalwareSEO poisoning

Weedhack is still being served to gamers through fake Minecraft clients

Several websites are still actively distributing a malware family known as Weedhack by masquerading as Minecraft clients. McAfee Labs says it detected and blocked more than 6,300 attempts to reach the malicious sites, and found lookalike pages copying the real projects down to branding, feature lists, FAQs, installation guides, developer credits and links to genuine GitHub repositories.

Notably, one of the sites was built using Lovable, an AI-powered website builder — a small detail with a large implication about how cheap a convincing lure page has become. Weedhack was first documented in June 2026, using SEO poisoning and YouTube to drive traffic; the chain is multi-stage and ends in JAR payloads. No CVE, no patch, no federal deadline.
