The Lead

The fourth straight decline settles on the tape as Treasury yields set 52-week highs at every maturity

Thursday’s close is now corroborated. The S&P 500 finished at 7,591.70, down 44.66 points or 0.58%; the Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 52,064.10, down 316.56 points or 0.60%. Both reconcile to the cent against Wednesday’s verified closes of 7,636.36 and 52,380.66, and the percentage moves match Yahoo Finance’s independent close report of −0.58% and −0.6%. The Nasdaq Composite fell 0.65%; no closing level for it has been corroborated by a second source, so none is printed here.

That correction matters: an earlier post-close read this afternoon put the S&P at 7,596.97 and the Dow at 52,082.88. Those figures were withheld at the time precisely because a second read disagreed, and the second read is the one that has now been confirmed. It is a fourth consecutive losing session for all three averages.

The pressure came from the bond market. TheStreet’s 3:48 PM ET Treasury check showed new 52-week highs across the entire curve: the 2-year at 4.567% (+14.0 bp), the 3-year at 4.666% (+14.5 bp), the 5-year at 4.747% (+13.4 bp), the 7-year at 4.845% (+12.6 bp), the 10-year at 4.954% (+11.4 bp), the 20-year at 5.39% (+10.2 bp) and the 30-year at 5.367% (+8.1 bp). August producer prices rose 0.4% month over month, matching the Dow Jones consensus, but followed an upwardly revised +0.1% July and pushed the annual rate to 5.4%, up from 4.8% and slightly above forecasts. Odds of a rate hike at next week’s Fed meeting sat near 70% on CME FedWatch.

Cooper Cos. (COO) — down nearly 14%

The medical device maker fell about 14% in early trading (having shown −18.1% premarket) after a disappointing strategic review, slashed financial guidance, a revenue miss and a wave of analyst downgrades. It was among TheStreet’s named midday losers.

DownCarried

Freeport-McMoRan (FCX) — down 7.3%

The miner sank as copper prices retreated from record highs, having been down 8.8% premarket. Southern Copper (SCCO) fell 7.1% premarket on the same move plus sector-wide profit-taking.

DownCarried

Intel (INTC) — down 5.7%

Profit-taking after a multi-session rally, amid a broader semiconductor selloff driven by rising 10-year yields and surging oil.

UpNew

Chemours (CC) — up 6.6% premarket

Chemours rose after it, DuPont and Corteva announced a $455 million settlement resolving long-standing PFAS “forever chemicals” contamination lawsuits in North Carolina.

UpNew

Fox Corp. (FOXA) — up 4.5% premarket

Investors responded to reassuring comments on the media group’s pending $22 billion acquisition of Roku. SS&C Technologies (SSNC) rose 5% premarket after UBS lifted its price target to $101 from $99 with a Buy rating.

UpCarried

Reddit (RDDT), Charter (CHTR), Elevance (ELV)

Reddit gained 4.7% on Piper Sandler data showing an 8% month-over-month increase in its August user base; Charter rose 4.5% on a technical rebound and management’s growth plans after Wednesday’s selloff; Elevance added 4.1% after saying it will reaffirm full-year 2026 earnings and benefit-expense guidance in upcoming investor meetings.

EarningsCarried

Oracle (ORCL) — Q1 fiscal 2027, reported after the bell

Oracle’s own June guidance called for total revenue growth of 27% to 29% and non-GAAP EPS of $1.72 to $1.76 in USD. The number to watch is conversion of the $638 billion remaining-performance-obligations backlog reported at the end of Q4 FY2026 — up 363% year over year and $85 billion in a single quarter — of which only about 12% is expected to convert within twelve months. Options pricing implied roughly a 5% to 15% move off a $161.75 premarket price. No post-bell price move is published here: no source read this run states one, and one search result returned a reaction paired with figures from an earlier fiscal quarter, which was refused.

On the Radar

  • August CPI, Friday. The Dow Jones consensus calls for a 0.4% monthly increase and 3.4% over twelve months, with core expected at 0.2%. TheStreet Pro’s James DePorre notes Fed Governor Waller said last week he would vote to hold at 0.2% core and support a hike if it comes in higher — a single tenth of a percentage point could decide next week’s meeting.
  • Existing home sales fell 2% in August to a 3.98 million seasonally adjusted annual rate, the slowest since June 2025 and down 1.2% year over year. Inventory of 1.62 million homes is a 4.9-month supply, the highest in more than a decade; the median price rose 1.6% to $429,100.
  • The war is the oil story. Multiple U.S. military aircraft were damaged in Iranian strikes overnight Tuesday into Wednesday at Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan — one A-10 lost a wing and roughly eight F-15s took light damage, with no U.S. deaths reported. President Trump warned Iran “not to get cute” over activity at the suspected nuclear site at Pickaxe Mountain, and said oil prices are unlikely to fall until after the midterms.
  • The “Trump Dividend.” At the Republican midterm convention in Dallas the president proposed sending every American adult $5,000 if Republicans hold the House and Senate. Reuters estimates the cost at about $1.35 trillion; it would require congressional approval, and Vice President Vance later suggested wealthier Americans might be excluded.
  • Oracle and Adobe results land overnight and will set the tone for software and AI-infrastructure names into Friday’s inflation print.
