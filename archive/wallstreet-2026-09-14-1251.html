The Lead

The AI trade is hit by the AI labs themselves — S&P 500 −0.39%, Nasdaq −1.04% as of 12:00 p.m. ET

Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei published an essay over the weekend urging the AI industry to slow the development of frontier models on safety grounds. OpenAI’s Sam Altman backed it — writing that the industry needs to “pace the frontier,” warning progress could go “very badly,” and telling Fortune that safety concerns make this an “ill-advised moment” to go public — and Elon Musk backed it too. The trigger behind both: Jacob Coxon, a researcher who worked at Anthropic and previously at OpenAI, resigned last week saying the two companies are “racing straight to self-improving superintelligence and gambling with our lives.”

Chips took the hit. At the open the S&P 500 fell 0.75%, the Dow slipped 0.28% and the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.17%, while the Russell 2000 gained 0.45% — a split that narrowed through the morning. By 12:00 p.m. ET the S&P was off 0.39% at 7,627.35, the Dow off 0.42% and the Nasdaq Composite off 1.04%, with 24/7 Wall St. noting the selling stayed concentrated in the highest-multiple technology names rather than spreading across the market.

President Donald Trump pushed back hard, posting on Truth Social that the only “guardrails” AI needs are a “STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT” and calling the episode a “SICK conspiracy going on against AI and Data Centers.” Bank of America Securities told clients it stays bullish on semiconductors, calling the frontier-lab controversy “noise relative to a secular market where AI-capex could surge 3x to $3tn+ by decade-end.”

Two other forces are pressing at once. Oil spiked after Houthi forces attacked Saudi Arabia — including a claimed strike on the King Khalid Air Base at Khamis Mushait — and Saudi Arabia shut the East-West pipeline it had been using to bypass the closed Strait of Hormuz; Oman then postponed its planned Hormuz talks with Iran without setting a new date. And the 10-year Treasury yield rose more than 2 basis points to around 5%, its highest since October 2023, two days before an FOMC decision markets now price as a hike.

On the Radar

  • FOMC decision Wednesday. Markets price an 88% chance of a 25bp hike (CME FedWatch via Schwab); Capital.com’s read via TheStreet is roughly 85%. Schwab’s Collin Martin: “We believe upside is limited, but we won’t be surprised if the 10-year yield does touch 5%” — adding that a hike may be needed to keep long-end yields from rising further “as credibility may be on the line.” Schwab also notes this week is the last regular-schedule chance to hike before late October, which narrowly precedes November’s midterms.
  • Consumer sentiment is cracking. The preliminary September University of Michigan Index of Consumer Sentiment fell to 47.8 — its second-lowest reading on record — against expectations of 51.5, down from 51.7 in August and 55.1 a year earlier. Year-ahead inflation expectations spiked to 4.6% from 4.0% last month and 3.4% in February. The final September reading is due 25 September.
  • The rest of the week (Schwab’s calendar): Wednesday — Lennar earnings, August retail sales, export and import prices, and the Fed decision. Thursday — August housing starts and building permits. Friday — Bank of Japan rate decision, industrial production, leading indicators. Monday and Tuesday carry no scheduled releases on that calendar.
  • Crypto legislation. The Senate is set to hold a key procedural vote Tuesday on the Clarity Act, which would establish a regulatory framework for cryptocurrencies and other digital assets; Coinbase and other crypto-linked names climbed early.
  • The IPO pipeline is the story inside the story. Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley — the two banks expected to lead OpenAI’s IPO — both fell Monday morning after Altman’s comments. Separately, Reuters reported Nvidia is in talks to invest in Anthropic’s IPO, and Business Insider reported Anthropic has chosen to list on Nasdaq. SpaceX rose more than 2% after Morgan Stanley reiterated an overweight rating, having slipped 2% to $148.16 premarket.
