The Lead

Stocks claw back the Fed-hike selloff — S&P 500 up about 1% as of 10:54 a.m. ET

The tape has turned. A day after the Federal Reserve's first rate increase since July 2023 sent the Dow down 631.21 points, TheStreet's live blog reported at 10:54 a.m. ET that the S&P 500 was "bouncing back today, up about one percent at last glance," with 259 of the index's 503 holdings advancing, "anchored by strength in cyclicals and tech." Trading Economics separately states the S&P 500 "rose to 7,596 points on September 17, 2026, gaining 0.59%" — a different intraday snapshot from a different moment. Both are printed here with their sources; neither is a close, and they are not averaged.

Before the open, Yahoo Finance had Dow futures up 0.7%, S&P futures up 0.8% and Nasdaq-100 futures up 1.1%. Yahoo Finance reported that analysts said Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish rhetoric helped restore the Fed's credibility on inflation. Matthew Miskin, co-chief investment strategist at Manulife John Hancock Investments, told Reuters the meeting "does make them look independent…it adds trust to the market" — while adding that the Fed "may have come off a little too hawkish in this meeting and we're just going to have to see how the economy can react in the next couple of months." Wednesday's move was a unanimous quarter-point hike to a 3.75%–4.00% target range, and the new projections show 16 of 18 officials expecting at least one further hike this year, per Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn. Warsh's line: "the plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long."

Falling crude is doing some of the work. WTI was off 1.70% to $100.70 and Brent down 2.07% to $103.60 as Saudi Arabia moved to restore its East-West pipeline and offered extra cargoes via ship-to-ship transfer off Oman's Sohar port. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.943%, backing off the 5% mark it touched on the decision.

REFUSED THIS RUN: TheStreet's 9:32 a.m. "Opening Bell" block reads "the S&P 500 climbed 1.19%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained 8.81%, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.01%." On the Radar

  • SEC opens a path for tokenized US stocks. NEW The Commission issued an order creating a regulatory pathway for certain trading venues to list and trade tokenized versions of publicly traded US stocks, effective immediately. The five-year "Innovation Exemption" gives platforms and liquidity providers conditional relief. Two conditions are already contested: token holders must retain the same rights as traditional equity holders, and companies must be able to object to having their securities represented as tokens. It lands two days after the Clarity Act failed to advance in the Senate.
  • Jobless claims came in low. Initial claims fell 10,000 to 196,000 for the week ended 12 September, the lowest since mid-July, per the Labor Department. Continuing claims were forecast at 1,780K against 1,774K prior.
  • The rest of Thursday's data slate. Housing starts (1.320M forecast vs 1.239M prior), building permits (1.400M forecast vs 1.433M prior) and the Philadelphia Fed manufacturing index, which ran at 47.4 a month ago. Consensus for the Philly Fed differs by source — Investing.com has 31.3, Yahoo Finance has 28.6 — so both are named rather than one picked. No notable earnings are scheduled.
  • Oil supply risk is easing, not resolved. US Energy Secretary Chris Wright said Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline should return to roughly half capacity within days and full operations in about six weeks. He put flows through the Strait of Hormuz at 18 million barrels earlier this week, with a seven-day average of 11 million barrels a day. President Trump said the US is "hopefully" nearing the end of its nearly seven-month war with Iran and that he had spoken with Tehran "directly."
  • Trade friction with Canada and the EU. Prime Minister Mark Carney welcomed the EU's proposal to make Canada an associate member; Trump called the prospect "laughable" and threatened heavy tariffs on the bloc if he deems it a hostile act.
  • SpaceX's next Starship test is set for 22 September. Shares rose 1.84% to $153.65 premarket after ARK's Cathie Wood argued Starship could generate $10 trillion in annual revenue by 2030; Elon Musk called the underlying 10,000-flights-a-year target "not impossible." SPCX debuted at $135 on 12 June and hit a record $225.64 on 16 June.
