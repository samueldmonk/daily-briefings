The Lead

The selloff deepens with the Fed hours away: Dow down 1.4–1.6% in late-morning trade as oil’s war premium builds — and Microsoft & Meta tonight

Late morning, as of roughly 11:15 AM ET, this run’s research window. What began as a modest tilt lower has broadened into a real Fed-day selloff: Yahoo Finance’s live blog headline now has the Dow down more than 800 points — about 1.6% — with the S&P 500 off 0.8% and the Nasdaq down about 1%, while 24/7 Wall St’s live index board read S&P −0.78%, Dow −1.43%, Nasdaq-100 −1.34% and Russell 2000 −1.07% on this run’s late-morning fetch (~11:15 AM ET). The slide has been steady all morning: the ~9:10 AM readings had the S&P up 0.12% with the Dow off about 230 points, by ~9:40 the Dow was down roughly 0.7–0.8%, and the selling has accelerated since (Yahoo Finance/24/7 Wall St, both fetched this run). Chips remain the sore spot — the iShares Semiconductor ETF entered the day off four straight sessions with chip stocks down nearly 7% for the week (24/7 Wall St) after investors in Asia again dumped Samsung and SK Hynix, handing South Korea’s Kospi a nearly 6% loss. It all follows Tuesday’s split close: Dow +537.45 points (+1.03%) to 52,747.53, S&P 500 +0.22% to 7,429.22, Nasdaq −0.22% to 24,876.91 (Yahoo Finance closing board/CNBC).

The war premium is back — and building. Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched what U.S. Central Command called an “attempted surprise attack” — ballistic missiles fired at U.S. forces, all intercepted, the first exchange since Friday’s pause in hostilities (CNN/Yahoo Finance) — and TheStreet’s Wednesday blog adds that Jordan’s military intercepted a fresh barrage of Iranian missiles Wednesday. President Trump told Fox News the U.S. “will hit Iran hard,” saying Iran “is going to get a beating,” and joint U.S.–Saudi strikes hit Iranian-backed forces in Iraq — killing at least 20, per CBS News reporting cited by TheStreet — after more than 30 drone attacks in three days by Iran-aligned groups (CNBC). Crude kept climbing through the morning: by ~9:40 AM, 24/7 Wall St had Brent up about 7% at roughly $89.94 and WTI up 6.9% near $84.69, after TheStreet’s early readings of $88.49 and $83.18 and CNBC’s overnight $89.61/$84.31 prints — snapping what Bloomberg called a three-day drop that wire coverage put at more than 14% for Brent. One offset in the mix: Oman has proposed a new regional deal over the Strait of Hormuz (TheStreet), and API data showed U.S. crude inventories down 3.3 million barrels last week. Gold futures were little changed, at $4,087.70 (−0.27%) early per TheStreet.

Into that tape drops the Federal Reserve decision at 2 PM ET, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s 2:30 press conference — the moment the central bank either holds at 3.50–3.75% or delivers its first rate hike in three years (Yahoo Finance/TheStreet). The odds are unusually live: Kalshi traders price a 77% chance the Fed stays put versus a 23% chance of a surprise quarter-point hike, while Citadel Securities is leaning the other way — expecting Warsh to raise rates and “reassert the Fed’s inflation-fighting credibility” (24/7 Wall St). Capital.com’s Kyle Rodda puts the market-implied hike probability near 31%, noting recent data lean toward a hold but Warsh’s hawkish bias and reluctance to give forward guidance keep a move on the table (TheStreet). The 10-year Treasury yield edged up to 4.63% early Wednesday (TradingEconomics) after three straight down sessions.

Earnings pile on top — and deals. CBIZ surged toward Grant Thornton Advisors’ $55-a-share offer after the firms announced a definitive all-cash deal at a $5 billion enterprise value, a roughly 54% premium (Grant Thornton/Accounting Today); a live-blog reading had the stock up nearly 16.6% at $54.45. Vertiv fell about 11% in premarket trade after Q2 revenue of $3.274 billion came in roughly 3% short, and SoFi beat on both lines yet was down 9.4% in late-morning trade (24/7 Wall St). Visa beat overnight — fiscal Q3 EPS of $3.32 on revenue up 14% to $11.63 billion — but slipped about 1.6% after hours on moderated full-year growth guidance and ~2,600 job cuts (Seeking Alpha/Benzinga/Yahoo Finance), while SK Hynix’s record quarter still missed elevated consensus, feeding the “AI peak” anxiety pressuring chips (SK hynix newsroom/Korea Times/TechTimes). CNBC’s premarket board had Ford up 6% on a beat-and-raise (a late-morning gainers board read it +7.6%), Generac +5.5%, Manhattan Associates +11% — against Procter & Gamble down about 3% on a revenue miss and CoStar off 15%. Starbucks and Chipotle report today; after the bell it’s the main event: Microsoft and Meta, a make-or-break moment for the AI trade after Alphabet’s capex guidance spooked markets last week, plus Qualcomm and Arm (Yahoo Finance). Apple and Amazon follow Thursday.

Movers & Drivers

Crude oil ~+7%

Oil built on its overnight surge all morning: 24/7 Wall St’s ~9:40 AM reading had Brent up about 7% at roughly $89.94 and WTI up 6.9% near $84.69, after TheStreet’s early $88.49/$83.18 prints and CNBC’s $89.61/$84.31 — the re-escalation trade after Iran’s intercepted missile attacks and Trump’s vow to “hit Iran hard.” Bloomberg framed it as oil surging after a three-day drop wire coverage put at >14% for Brent; Tuesday’s settles were $84.09 Brent / $79.26 WTI (CNBC). API says U.S. crude stocks fell 3.3M barrels last week (TheStreet).

CBIZ (CBZ) +~17% New

Grant Thornton Advisors will acquire CBIZ in a definitive all-cash deal: $55.00 per share, a $5 billion enterprise value and a ~54% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price, with the close expected in Q4 2026 and CBIZ shares to be delisted from the NYSE (Grant Thornton press release/Accounting Today/Investing.com). A live-blog reading had the stock up nearly 16.6% at $54.45, pinned just under the offer.

Vertiv (VRT) −~11% premarket New

The data-center power-and-cooling maker fell 10.9% in premarket trade to $240.10 after second-quarter revenue of $3.274 billion came in about 3.1% below expectations (live-blog movers reading this run) — another data point for the AI-infrastructure nerves that have dogged the tape since Alphabet’s capex guidance.

SoFi (SOFI) −9.4% New

Beat on both lines — EPS $0.12 vs $0.11 expected, revenue $1.2B vs $1.11B — and fell anyway, down 9.4% in late-morning trade, a sign investors wanted more than a clean beat; shares are down 42% year to date (24/7 Wall St).

Visa (V) −1.6% after hours

Beat and trimmed: fiscal Q3 EPS $3.32 vs $3.23 expected, revenue +14% to $11.63B vs $11.39B, processed transactions +10% to 71.7B and quarterly payments volume above $4 trillion for the first time — but shares slipped ~1.6% in late trading as Visa moderated full-year growth guidance and disclosed ~2,600 job cuts (~7% of staff) with $563M in related costs. CEO: consumer and business spending “remains resilient” (Seeking Alpha/Benzinga/Yahoo Finance).

SK Hynix / chips Kospi ~−6%

The memory bellwether’s Q2 was an all-time record — ₩79.32T revenue, ₩60.54T operating profit, up 257% and 557% year over year, with HBM4 mass production begun — yet it landed below the ~₩64T consensus, and the “record earnings, record low” reaction sent Seoul reeling again: the Kospi fell nearly 6% Wednesday as investors dumped Samsung and SK Hynix (SK hynix newsroom/Korea Times/TechTimes·KuCoin/Yahoo Finance). Stateside, the iShares Semiconductor ETF entered the day off four straight sessions with chip stocks down nearly 7% for the week, and SK Hynix’s U.S.-listed shares slipped again (24/7 Wall St).

Morning board F +6–7.6% PG −3%

CNBC’s premarket movers: Ford +6% on a Q2 beat and raised 2026 outlook; Generac +5.5% ($2.91 adj EPS vs $2.01 forecast); Manhattan Associates +11%; Deutsche Bank +2%; General Dynamics ~+1% — against Procter & Gamble −3% on a revenue miss and CoStar −15%. By late morning (~11:15 AM ET), 24/7 Wall St’s live boards showed earnings-day swings both ways: Garmin +16.2% and GE HealthCare +11.6% led gainers with Ford +7.6%, while Lennox (−18.8–19.6% across two readings) and Humana (−6.2–8.2%) led decliners — Humana on its Q2 report day, where consensus had been EPS of $7.00 on ~$40.6B revenue (Yahoo Finance/Benzinga previews). Board readings; causes not stated for Lennox in sources seen this run.

Microsoft & Meta tonight

Two Magnificent Seven reports after the bell stand as the AI trade’s other big catalyst — a make-or-break read after Alphabet’s capex guidance spooked markets last week (Yahoo Finance). Qualcomm and Arm also report tonight. The stakes: Bloomberg notes the Nasdaq 100 entered the day on the brink of a technical correction after the chip selloff (via Benzinga).

Fed-day tape

The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.63% early Wednesday, up 2 basis points (TradingEconomics), gold futures were little changed at $4,087.70 and silver rose 0.34% to $57.72 in early trading (TheStreet) — but the equity tape is anything but becalmed, with the Dow off more than 800 points in late-morning trade ahead of the 2 PM decision and a genuinely live hold-vs-hike debate (Kalshi 77/23; Citadel expects a hike).

Snowflake (SNOW) Street-high target

Wells Fargo reiterated its overweight rating and raised its price target to $500 from $320 — the highest on Wall Street. Analyst Ryan MacWilliams argues Snowflake will benefit from AI rather than be disrupted by it, with AI-powered tools and agents increasing demand for its technology: “The game has changed” (24/7 Wall St, as quoted by CNBC). The stock had earlier sold off on fears AI would hurt traditional SaaS before recovering.
