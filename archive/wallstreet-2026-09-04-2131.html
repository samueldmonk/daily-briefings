The Lead

A blowout jobs report turned into a down day — because it was a blowout

The U.S. economy added 162,000 jobs in August, decisively beating a consensus that sat near 55,000, with the unemployment rate unchanged at 4.1%. Stocks fell anyway. The Dow Jones Industrial Average closed at 53,414.25, down 271.86 points or 0.51%; the S&P 500 fell 0.38% to 7,718.60; the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.29% to 26,506.99.

The mechanism is the one traders call good-news-is-bad-news. A labour market this strong frees the Fed to concentrate on inflation, and the front end of the curve repriced immediately: the 2-year and 5-year Treasury yields both set fresh 52-week highs. “The August jobs report was much better than expected, focusing the Fed squarely on controlling inflation when they meet next in September,” said Bill Adams, chief U.S. economist at Fifth Third Commercial Bank, who added that next week’s CPI and PPI releases “have the power to decide whether the Fed hikes or holds.”

That leaves the week ending on a round trip. Rate-hike pricing for the September meeting ran near 63% before Fed Governor Chris Waller’s relatively dovish remarks, fell to roughly 50% after them — which is what powered Thursday’s rally — and post-payrolls readings now diverge. This desk’s standing file carries 58% on CME FedWatch from the previous edition; a source fetched this run puts it at 57%. Both are printed and neither is presented as settled — the 58% was not re-confirmed this run, and the 49.4% prior-day figure rests on CNBC alone.

  • Norway’s $2.3 trillion sovereign wealth fund has proposed cutting government bonds to 50% of its benchmark bond index from 70%, which would mean trimming close to $80 billion from about $215 billion of U.S. Treasuries. Any implementation is unlikely before well into 2027.
  • The Iran conflict continues to set the oil bid. The EU has formally joined the U.S.-led “Operation Economic Outcast” sanctions campaign and South Korea is weighing a military role in reopening the Strait of Hormuz, even as Vice President Vance rejected the word “war” and Iran fired at Kuwait on Thursday.
