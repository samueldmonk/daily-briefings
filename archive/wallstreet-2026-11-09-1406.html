The Lead

New

As of ~1:55 PM ET: S&P 500 +2.4%, Nasdaq +2.6%, Dow +2.2% — rally broadens into the final two hours as Nvidia lags

Yahoo Finance’s index strip, with U.S. markets due to close in 2 hours 6 minutes (about 1:54 p.m. ET), had the S&P 500 up 175.79 points, or 2.38%, the Dow up 1,138.66, or 2.22%, and the Nasdaq Composite up 645.05, or 2.62%; the Russell 2000 was up 3.05% and the VIX down nearly 21%. Nvidia was down 5.19% as of 1:55 p.m. ET (Yahoo Finance). All three indexes posted losses last week; Yahoo says the post-election week saw investors dump AI and chip stocks, sending the Nasdaq into correction territory before Friday’s partial recovery (Yahoo Finance).

Why it’s up: TipRanks says falling oil helped markets recover from last week’s sharp selloff — U.S. crude fell nearly 3% toward $88 a barrel after President Trump said further U.S. attacks on Iran were unlikely if peace talks progressed (citing Reuters). Morgan Stanley and UBS believe last week’s selloff went too far, questioning whether the new Congress will impose restrictions as severe as investors fear on AI data-center construction (TipRanks).

Chips lead, Nvidia the exception: AMD was up roughly 12%, Broadcom about 9.2% and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index 6.8% in TipRanks’ midday update, with the Nasdaq 100 up more than 3% (TipRanks). Nvidia fell as much as 8% after its board opened an independent investigation into CEO Jensen Huang, who admitted in a Nov. 8 memo to employees to an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence officials believe worked for China’s Ministry of State Security; the board said it had no reason to believe Nvidia’s technology or U.S. national security had been compromised (Yahoo Finance; TipRanks). Yahoo notes Nvidia is worth about $4.5 trillion and makes up nearly 7% of the S&P 500 (Yahoo Finance).

Context: Citi strategists led by Beata Manthey cut U.S. equities and global tech to neutral from overweight and raised European equities ex-UK to overweight; per Bloomberg (via TheStreet), concerns over tighter AI regulation pushed the S&P 500 to its biggest weekly loss since April 2025, with $2.3 trillion wiped off the Nasdaq 100 last Thursday alone (TheStreet).

Movers & Drivers

Up

QXO +17.5% on preliminary Q3

QXO rallied 17.5% after preliminary third-quarter sales rose 79% to about $4.9 billion; full results are due Nov. 12 (Benzinga, via search; TipRanks).

Up

AST SpaceMobile +15% on Rakuten Japan venture

AST SpaceMobile rose 15.1% after forming a joint venture with Rakuten Group to build a satellite-to-phone network in Japan, backed by up to ¥148 billion (about $1 billion) in government support; it reports third-quarter results after the close (Benzinga, via search; TipRanks).

Up

AMD ~+12%, Broadcom ~+9.2%, SOX +6.8%

Chipmakers powered the rebound despite Nvidia’s slide; Oppenheimer’s Rick Schafer had upgraded AMD to outperform with a $600 target before the open (TipRanks; TheStreet).

NewUp

Space stocks rip: Rocket Lab near +30% over two days

Space stocks rallied for a second session after NASA named eight winners — including SpaceX, Blue Origin, Intuitive Machines, Firefly Aerospace and Voyager Technologies — for lunar delivery work worth up to $10 billion. Rocket Lab, not on NASA’s list, was on track for a two-day gain of nearly 30% ahead of its Q3 report after Monday’s close, per Yahoo Finance’s Jared Blikre (Yahoo Finance).

NewDown

Hims & Hers −10% ahead of earnings

Hims & Hers fell 10% ahead of third-quarter results due after the bell, with short interest around 23% (S&P Global Market Intelligence); analysts expect adjusted EPS of $0.15 on revenue of $892.7 million (Yahoo Finance).

Down

Nvidia −5.19% on board probe of CEO

Down 5.19% at $176.96 as of 1:55 p.m. ET after falling as much as 8% earlier, before selling eased; Yorkville’s Dan Ives expects Huang to continue as CEO “with no interruption in the near-term” (Yahoo Finance).

Down

Ionis −18.82% premarket

Ionis Pharmaceuticals tumbled before the bell after cutting full-year sales guidance for flagship drug Tryngolza by roughly 50% (TheStreet, 8:37 AM ET).

Down

Akamai fell after proposing $2.2 billion of convertible senior notes, split evenly between notes due 2028 and 2031 (TheStreet, 8:37 AM ET).
