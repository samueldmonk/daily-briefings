The Lead

Stocks close lower across the board as the 60-day Iran clock runs out

U.S. stocks closed lower across the board on Monday, August 17. The S&P 500 fell 0.52% to 7,745.06, the Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 272.63 points, or 0.51%, to 53,459.78, and the Nasdaq Composite lost 0.32% to 26,644.91. The June 17 memorandum of understanding between Washington and Tehran hit its 60-day deadline with nothing signed. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran is going "fully offensive" and would use its military to break the U.S. naval blockade if talks fail. President Trump, for his part, told Fox News he is in no rush, and threatened to bomb Oman — which has been trying to mediate — if it gets in the way of negotiations.

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has essentially stopped: three vessels crossed on Sunday, against roughly 130 daily before the war began in February. Brent crude rose 0.7% to $89.13 at midday, a quiet day by recent standards, though Yahoo Finance reported the international benchmark touching $90 a barrel during the session — and Bloomberg's evening wrap says Brent settled near $91, the highest figure any source has attached to the session. The sharper move was in rates — the 30-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.31%, its highest level since June 2007, on concerns about growing national debt.

The evening framing. Bloomberg's close recap ties the day together the way the intraday reports could not: stocks declined on concern that elevated oil prices will fuel inflation amid dimming hopes for a deal to end the Iran war, and declines in most S&P 500 companies overshadowed an advance in chipmakers — the same narrow-breadth picture the equal-weight index and the advance/decline line described earlier. The oil move, on that reading, is what "spurred speculation that inflationary pressures could make the Federal Reserve raise rates before the year is over," which is the mechanism behind the long bond's move.

On the closing levels. This edition publishes Monday’s official closes because they now corroborate across independent reports and reconcile exactly against Friday’s verified levels: the S&P 500’s 40.70-point decline, the Dow’s 272.63-point drop and the Nasdaq’s 84.25-point fall each tie the Friday close to the Monday close and match the stated percentages. The earlier edition of this briefing withheld the levels while sources disagreed. One number was rejected again this run: a widely syndicated “Stock Market News for Aug 17” recap returned 7,785.76 / 53,732.41 / 26,729.16 — those are Friday’s closes, republished under a Monday headline. That reading is now settled rather than inferred: a separate weekly wrap describes the S&P 500 as ending the week up 0.4% at 7,785.76 for the period ending August 16 — i.e. the same figure, explicitly labelled as the week's final level, before Monday's session began. The same recap surfaced again this evening and was rejected for the fifth time. Independently, a fresh check of the Monday percentage moves returned −0.5% for the Dow, −0.5% for the S&P 500 and −0.3% for the Nasdaq Composite — matching the published levels to the decimal.

Movers & Drivers

Up 6.5%

SK Hynix (SKHY)

Rose 6.5% and added roughly $83 billion in market value, leading a memory complex that has been on a remarkable run. Micron Technology climbed about 5.9% and moved back above $1,000 a share. The memory trade absorbed a Morgan Stanley note on hyperscaler capital spending as a bullish signal rather than a warning. A second thread re-verified this run: Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told The Wall Street Journal the administration “is not in favor” of Apple resolving its memory supply problems by buying from Chinese suppliers Yangtze Memory Technologies and CXMT — a policy tailwind for the incumbents. A second account of Monday's memory-chip rally put the SK Hynix move at 5.6% rather than 6.5%; the figures are close and both are published rather than picking one silently.

Down 2.4%

Microsoft (MSFT)

The single biggest drag on the S&P 500, down 2.4%, following a high-profile Morgan Stanley note observing that hyperscalers are spending heavily upfront on AI data-center builds while the payoff may take years to materialise. Microsoft weighed on all three indexes more than any other stock through the morning session.

Down 3.3%

Meta Platforms (META)

Fell 3.3% ahead of Tuesday's opening arguments in a federal trial in Oakland, where 29 state attorneys general allege the company designed Facebook and Instagram to get children addicted.

Up 0.9%

Nvidia (NVDA)

Gained 0.9% on news it will provide up to $105 billion in credit support for a large AI data-center campus being built for OpenAI in Pike County, Ohio, and invest $1.5 billion in project developer SB Energy. SoftBank-backed SB Energy will build, own and operate the campus with OpenAI as primary tenant under a 20-year lease; the first 4.25 gigawatts arrive in 2028.

Up 1.5%

Intel (INTC)

Up about 1.5% after disclosure that chief executive Lip-Bu Tan purchased more than 105,000 shares last week at $95 apiece.

Up 5.7%

Astera Labs (ALAB)

Gained 5.7% after Northland upgraded the AI connectivity semiconductor maker to Outperform from Market Perform — one of the few non-memory names to finish materially higher on a session where most stocks fell.

Higher

HIVE Digital Technologies (HIVE)

Rose on a five-year, $350 million GPU cloud services deal that adds roughly $70 million in annualised revenue and accelerates the company's AI infrastructure business. The percentage move was not stated in the sources checked for this edition, so only the direction and the contract terms are published here.

NewHigher

Diana Shipping (DSX)

One of Monday's named gainers, rising after Diana criticised what it called Genco's high demands and withdrew its acquisition offer — an M&A break rather than an AI story, and one of the few non-technology names on the day's movers list. As with HIVE, no percentage figure appeared in the sources checked for this edition, so the direction and the deal news are all that is published here.

Also named among Monday's biggest movers without a percentage attached in the sources checked this edition: L3Harris (LHX), SPCX, CBRS and Etsy (ETSY). They are listed here rather than given cards, because a mover card without a verified move is not worth printing.

Sector Heat — live

Sector figures verified this edition: Energy (XLE) +1.39% led on higher crude, with Utilities (XLU) +0.61% and Industrials (XLI) +0.39% also higher, while Consumer Staples fell 1.46% — the weakest group — alongside real estate and other rate-sensitive defensives, in what one account describes as rotation out of consumer-sensitive sectors after weak retail sales. Breadth was materially worse than the headline indexes suggest: the Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF fell 0.58%, more than three times the cap-weighted decline, and decliners beat advancers by about 1.5-to-1 on the NYSE. Most stocks had a bad Monday — a handful of chipmakers covered widespread losses.

After-Hours Movers

Two hours after the closing bell, still no verified single-stock after-hours move for the August 17 session has surfaced in the sources checked for this edition — a fifth consecutive post-close check with the same result. The broad index proxies were close to unchanged on post-market screens — SPY down 0.03%, QQQ down 0.06%, DIA down 0.04% and IWM down 0.01% — though those screens carry no explicit timestamp, so read them as direction rather than a settled figure. Nothing further is published here rather than carrying an unsourced number or a prior-session snapshot. The extended-hours tape should get busier from Tuesday, when Home Depot opens the big-box retail earnings run.
