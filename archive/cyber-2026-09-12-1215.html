BlueMoon: an off-the-shelf espionage chain from a Chrome tab to SYSTEM

Proofpoint has named BlueMoon, an exploit kit used by at least four distinct threat clusters since late August 2026, the majority of them with a suspected China nexus. Every stage was unpatched when the kit first surfaced. A victim who clicks a link is served a page that runs the whole chain without further interaction.

The chain runs three deep. CVE-2026-85046 is a type-confusion flaw in Chromium’s V8 JavaScript engine that yields code execution in the renderer by abusing an optimization bug in V8’s JIT compiler. CVE-2026-87491 is a V8 sandbox escape that corrupts WebAssembly module metadata to overwrite compiled function bodies with attacker shellcode. CVE-2026-85880, a heap-based buffer overflow in Windows ALPC — described in one account as a Windows kernel local privilege-escalation flaw — finishes the job by lifting a low-privileged process to SYSTEM.

The first confirmed use came from TA412, also tracked as Violet Typhoon and APT31, on 28 August 2026; other groups adopted the same capability within days. TA412 used spearphishing lures posing as university interns and academic conference outreach against US NGOs, mining firms and commodity trading companies, installing a malicious browser extension disguised as “Google Gemini” that Proofpoint tracks as GemStone. A second China-linked group, UNK_LateNight, used it against multiple US aerospace companies; UNK_DoubleCheck targeted a manufacturing organization in Vietnam.

The window is closing but is not shut. The Chrome flaws were fixed in the Stable channel on 3 and 8 September 2026, and the Windows flaw on September Patch Tuesday — which means an unpatched browser and an unpatched host are each individually sufficient to break the chain. CVE-2026-85046 is already in CISA’s KEV catalog (added 4 September) and CVE-2026-85880 is due 22 September. One outlet frames the root cause as a Chrome patch gap — the interval between a public V8 fix and shipped browsers — rather than as three independent discoveries.

Patch Priority

Do this first — deadline today

Patch Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center against CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated auth bypass to root). CISA’s federal remediation deadline is 12 September 2026 — 0 days left, due today — and the same date applies to Citrix CVE-2026-19490 (CVSS 9.3) and Fortinet CVE-2025-25249 (CVSS 7.3). Talos has confirmed exploitation of the Cisco flaw by a Russian state actor and a ransomware operator, so treat any internet-reachable FMC as suspect and hunt before you assume a clean patch is sufficient.

Next in the queue: Check Point CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103 (both CVSS 9.8, unauthenticated RCE on VPN gateways). These carry no CISA deadline — they are not in the KEV catalog, because no exploitation has been confirmed — but the Dutch NCSC assesses attacks as imminent, and every comparable edge flaw on this page was patched only after someone was already inside.

Threat Actor Spotlight

CarriedState-alignedChina

TA412 / Violet Typhoon — first through the BlueMoon door

Also tracked as APT31, JungleBamboo and Tide Castle. Proofpoint records the group as the first confirmed user of BlueMoon on 28 August 2026, ahead of the several other China-linked actors that followed within days. Its lures impersonate university interns and academic conference organisers; its targets this campaign were US NGOs, mining firms and commodity traders; its payload is a browser extension masquerading as “Google Gemini”, tracked as GemStone. The tell is the pretext, not the payload.

NewExtortionFinancially motivated

FulcrumSec — the “Hardcoded Horrorshow”

FulcrumSec steals and extorts without ever encrypting anything — a model it calls “steal and squeeze.” Its signature is credentials left in client-side JavaScript. Against Novo Nordisk it pulled a GitHub personal access token and Azure container-registry credentials out of front-end bundles on two subdomains in March 2026, cloned hundreds of private repositories, and used the API tokens and service-account passwords committed inside them to move laterally. The extortion demand surfaced in June 2026: $25 million against roughly 1.3 TB claimed. Novo Nordisk refused to pay. The lesson is a build-pipeline one, not a firewall one — grep your shipped bundles before someone else does.

CarriedState-sponsoredRussia

Sandworm — via a Cyclops Blink variant

Talos attributes cluster UAT-11823 to activity ending in a variant of Cyclops Blink, malware previously attributed by the United States and the United Kingdom to the Russian APT Sandworm. The route in was CVE-2026-20079 chained with CVE-2026-20316, followed by a Netcat-based reverse shell and proxy tooling. Network-edge management appliances — not endpoints — were the target.

Breaches & Incidents

NewExploitation imminentEdge

Check Point VPN — a national CERT sounding the alarm before the attacks

The Dutch Nationaal Cyber Security Centrum warns that exploitation of CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103 is imminent. Both are rated CVSS 9.8 and both yield unauthenticated remote code execution: 85102 is improper validation of certificate data during VPN negotiation, affecting Security Gateway and Spark Firewall where site-to-site or remote-access VPN is configured; 85103 is a heap overflow in the certificate ASN.1 decoding path, reaching Security Management Server as well. Check Point’s own research team found them, says it has seen no exploitation and no public proof-of-concept, and began rolling out Live Patch on 9 September. The NCSC is not disputing that — it is saying the gap never lasts.

NewExtortionPharmaceutical

Novo Nordisk — $25m demanded, nothing encrypted

Fresh analysis this week retraces the FulcrumSec intrusion at Novo Nordisk. Initial access came from a GitHub personal access token embedded in client-side JavaScript in March 2026; roughly 1.3 TB was allegedly taken and a $25 million demand followed in June. Among the claimed haul: 4,750 source-code repositories, records on about 163,000 employees, and 11,500 pseudonymised clinical-trial patients. No ransomware was deployed at any point.

CarriedActively exploited

Cisco Secure FMC — Sandworm and Qilin, same appliance

Cisco Talos ties exploitation of two patched Secure Firewall Management Center flaws to three clusters (UAT-12197, UAT-11823, UAT-11988). UAT-11823 chained CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated auth bypass to root) with CVE-2026-20316, ending in a Cyclops Blink variant attributed to Russian APT Sandworm; UAT-11988 is assessed with high confidence as a ransomware operator matching Qilin tradecraft. The federal deadline for 20079 is today.

CarriedGovernment

Florida DHSMV — DAVID driver database

The Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles has confirmed a breach of its DAVID driver database. Attackers gained access using credentials belonging to a police department employee — a valid-credential intrusion rather than an exploited flaw.

CarriedIdentity

ShinyHunters and Helix — passkey-themed social engineering

Microsoft says actors linked to ShinyHunters, Helix and other extortion gangs are running passkey and single-sign-on-themed social engineering to compromise corporate Microsoft accounts. The lure borrows the language of a security upgrade.

CarriedSupply chain

JFrog Artifactory — Rust backdoor deployed

Threat actors are exploiting critical and high-severity flaws in JFrog Artifactory to bypass authentication and deploy a Rust backdoor on vulnerable self-hosted servers. CISA added Artifactory CVE-2026-82329 (improper authentication) to the KEV catalog on 2 September.

CarriedEducation

Mathspace — over 1 million people

Education platform Mathspace has disclosed a data breach affecting more than 1 million people.

CarriedPhishing

Trezor via Brevo — 347,000 emails

Trezor has confirmed that phishing against its customers earlier this week targeted 347,000 email addresses and affected the 2,500 users who clicked the embedded malicious link. The address list came from a breach at marketing provider Brevo.

CarriedRansomware

INC Ransom claimed responsibility in September and alleged it stole 5.7 terabytes of data; a Department of Health and Human Services investigation followed.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • Cisco CVE-2026-20079, Citrix CVE-2026-19490, Fortinet CVE-2025-25249 — due 12 September 2026 (0 days left — due today).
  • Adobe Commerce CVE-2026-75650 and N-able N-central CVE-2026-86218 — were due 11 September 2026 (1 day overdue).
  • MikroTik CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060 — added 10 September, due 13 September 2026 (1 day left). CISA marked 86060 as requiring forensic triage under BOD 26-04.
  • GitLab CVE-2026-85706 — added 11 September, due 14 September 2026 (2 days left).
  • Windows CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880 — due 22 September 2026 (10 days left). The 8 September bulletin of four therefore split its deadlines, a second proof — alongside the Chromium case — that BOD 26-04 assigns its window per CVE and not per batch.
  • Recent additions this month: seven on 2 September (Sangoma Switchvox CVE-2026-9586, Starlette CVE-2026-48710, Kestra CVE-2026-49869, LiteLLM CVE-2026-59822, JFrog Artifactory CVE-2026-82329, SonicWall CVE-2026-83548 and CVE-2026-83549); one on 4 September (Chromium V8 CVE-2026-85046); four on 8 September.
  • Check Point CVE-2026-85102 and CVE-2026-85103 are deliberately absent from this list. KEV entry requires evidence of exploitation, and none has been confirmed — so there is no federal deadline on two CVSS 9.8 unauthenticated-RCE flaws that a national CERT expects to be attacked imminently. The catalog is a record of what has already happened, not a queue of what is about to.
  • BOD 22-01’s flat three-week window no longer governs these entries — BOD 26-04 sets the remediation clock per vulnerability, which is why deadlines in the same bulletin land eleven days apart.

Also Notable

  • September’s Windows Server updates are breaking Remote Desktop Services. Admins report RDS failures on Windows Server 2019, 2022 and 2025 that prevent connections and in some cases require a hard reset.
  • The Patch Tuesday CVE count is genuinely disputed — and a fifth tracker has now widened the spread. BleepingComputer counts 966 flaws with 105 critical, broken out as 81 remote code execution, 20 elevation of privilege, 2 information disclosure and 1 security-feature bypass — the only tally read this run that itemises its critical subset, and those four categories sum to 104, one short of the 105 stated. That is not a typo worth glossing: the most granular count available does not close against itself, which is the plainest evidence that nobody has a clean number for this month. Tenable counts 964 (104 critical); other trackers say 973 (one breaking that out as 113 critical) and 974. All agree on two exploited Windows zero-days — CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880, both added to KEV the same day — and that this is Microsoft’s largest month ever, against 570 in July.
  • Anthropic says state-linked and criminal actors tried to misuse Claude — and published the cases. Its 10 September threat-intelligence report covers activity from December 2025 to August 2026 across seven harm areas, including cyber operations, influence operations, surveillance, fraud and illicit model distillation. Named threads: Iran-linked groups seeking naval targeting and surveillance dossiers, actors tied to Yemen’s Houthi movement asking for guided-rocket and missile-research help, and Chinese labs routing hundreds of thousands of queries to extract model capabilities. The report’s own framing is the part defenders should read — misuse has moved from prompt-level assistance to agentic execution and operational orchestration.
