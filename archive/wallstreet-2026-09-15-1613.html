The Lead

As of ~3:57 PM ET · Tuesday 15 September

Oil and the bond market pressed on a market that was lower at every reading returned, with the 10-year at a nineteen-year high

Research for this edition closed at ~3:57 PM ET, minutes before the bell, and no official settle was returned by anything read. What follows is therefore the session as of late afternoon, printed as a sequence rather than a single number. The Weekly Scorecard below still carries Monday’s closes, which is the only set of official levels available at the time of writing.

At the opening bell (9:35 AM ET) TheStreet had the S&P 500 down 0.16%, the Dow down 0.49%, the Nasdaq down 0.26% and the Russell 2000 down 0.40%. By 2:03 PM ET, the Associated Press had the S&P 500 down 0.4%, the Dow down 406 points, 0.8%, and the Nasdaq down 0.8% — a clear deepening on all three.

Two later readings arrived with no as-of time, which would normally disqualify them from being published as current. They are published because each reconciles exactly against Monday’s verified closes: the Dow down 421.43 points, 0.80%, at 51,999.77 (52,421.20 − 421.43 = 51,999.77) with the Nasdaq Composite down 196.16 points, 0.75%, at 25,990.25 (26,186.41 − 196.16 = 25,990.25); and separately the S&P 500 at 7,576, down 0.58% (7,619.98 − 43.98 = 7,576.00). Every percentage matches to the basis point. They came from different sources, so they are set out as two readings and not merged into one snapshot.

The driver is the bond market. The 10-year Treasury yield rose as much as four basis points to 5.02%, moving above its 2023 peak to its highest level since 2007; the AP put it at 5.01%, up from 4.97% late Monday, after it briefly touched 5.04% overnight. Darrell Cronk, president of Wells Fargo Investment Institute: “The result is a market that must work harder to generate earnings growth just as investors become less willing to pay premium valuations for that growth.” The AP notes the last time the 10-year sat consistently above 5% was around the turn of the millennium, and that the climb has accelerated since February, when the war with Iran sent oil much higher.

Oil is the second half of the same story. Brent climbed 3.5% to $109.35 by early Tuesday afternoon after several sharp swings in the morning — against $72 in early July, before the war began. Daniela Hathorn of Capital.com framed the setup: a 25bp hike is now almost fully priced, so “the bigger catalyst will be whether Kevin Warsh presents it as a one-off recalibration or signals that persistent inflation requires further tightening.” She called higher energy prices “an increasingly uncomfortable feedback loop” — a drag on growth that also makes it harder for central banks to declare victory on inflation.

On the Radar

  • Wednesday 16 September, 2:00 PM ET — the FOMC decision, with the Summary of Economic Projections and Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference. A hike would be the Fed’s first in three years. Odds do not resolve to one number and no trend is asserted across them: CME FedWatch 91% in Yahoo’s Tuesday reading, better than 92% at CNBC, and approximately 92% in the summary read this run — three same-day readings, printed rather than averaged. Traders are still betting on a slight chance the Fed holds off — and the AP notes that if it does, the market could swing, because investors may read it as the Fed being less committed to getting inflation lower.
  • The Dow is down more than 2% so far in September, on pace for its steepest September decline since 2008, as of Tuesday afternoon.
  • Crypto legislation is on the Senate floor today. The CLARITY Act — formally the Digital Asset Market Clarity Act, a proposed framework for cryptocurrencies and digital assets — came to a vote Tuesday. Coinbase fell 4.59% premarket; Bitcoin was down about 1% premarket and one afternoon reading put it down 3%. CMC head of research Alice Liu told CoinDesk that BTC open interest is down about 3.5% over seven days, the Coinbase premium has turned slightly negative, and spot BTC ETFs saw around $300 million of net outflows over five sessions before a $160 million inflow on Monday.
  • The war with Iran is now an explicit cost line. The Defense Department inspector general reported to Congress on Monday that the conflict has cost $33.4 billion, with four F-15s and as many as 30 MQ-9 Reaper drones destroyed, one F-35 and seven KC-135 tankers damaged, and hundreds of buildings damaged or destroyed at US bases across eight countries. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi visits China on Wednesday.
  • Musk hinted at a possible Tesla–SpaceX combination at the All-In Summit in Los Angeles on Monday, in remarks TheStreet relays from Investor’s Business Daily, citing the two companies’ collaboration on a semiconductor fab dubbed Terafab, Starlink terminals in the Cybercab robotaxi, and regular cross-purchasing. SPCX edged up 0.49% to $148.88 premarket; Tesla was slightly lower at $358.46.
  • Politics reached the tape indirectly. President Trump publicly attacked the Supreme Court on Tuesday after it declined to let a new Postal Service rule on mail-in ballots take effect for the November midterms.
