Top story

Noche UFC returns to Glendale tonight with a main event rebuilt on short notice — and a co-main that may matter more

The fourth annual Noche UFC lands at Desert Diamond Arena in Glendale, Arizona tonight, on Mexican Independence Day weekend. The headline booking is Jean Silva, the No. 6 featherweight, against Jose Miguel Delgado — a fight that exists in this shape because Yair Rodriguez withdrew injured and Delgado, an Arizonan, took it on short notice.

The books have priced it accordingly. Silva is a four-to-one favourite, quoted at −425 to Delgado’s +330 at DraftKings, with other books nearer −410 / +320; one read carries a quoted 77% win probability alongside the price. Short-notice replacements against ranked opposition are the single most reliably mispriced situation in the sport, in both directions.

The co-main may be the consequential fight. Manon Fiorot, ranked No. 2 at women’s flyweight, meets Alexa Grasso, No. 3 and a former champion — and the division’s belt is currently vacant, with Natalia Silva and Wang Cong contesting it at UFC 332 on 3 October. Whoever wins tonight walks directly into that conversation.

Prospect watch

Prospect

Salahdine Parnasse — 24-2, six straight stoppages

Twenty-eight years old, a former two-time KSW featherweight champion and one-time KSW lightweight champion (14-2 inside KSW, four defences of the lightweight belt) who signed with the UFC in late July 2026 having previously turned the promotion down — and was handed a main event on debut. He did not come through the Contender Series. He stopped Dan Hooker inside a round in Paris and has now won six in a row, all by stoppage.

Prospect

Jose Miguel Delgado — the short-notice test

An Arizonan stepping into a main event in his home state against a top-ten featherweight, after Yair Rodriguez withdrew injured. The books have him a four-to-one underdog. Tonight is the whole evaluation.

Prospect

Quillan Salkilld — 6-0 in the UFC

The Australian lightweight submitted No. 8-ranked Mateusz Gamrot by rear-naked choke in round one of his first UFC main event at UFC Vegas 120 in August, earning a Performance of the Night — his third $100,000 bonus of 2026, $300,000 on the year.

Around the sport

  • The women’s flyweight belt is vacant. Valentina Shevchenko vacated it while sidelined by injury for roughly a year; Natalia Silva and Wang Cong contest the vacant title at UFC 332 on 3 October. Four sources describe it as vacated rather than stripped — no promotional sanction is alleged.
  • Tonight’s co-main is effectively a title eliminator by circumstance. Fiorot at No. 2 against Grasso at No. 3, with the belt already off the board, is as close as a non-title fight gets to deciding the next challenger.
  • Curtis Blaydes vs. Waldo Cortes-Acosta sits in the heavyweight middle of the card — a division where Tom Aspinall holds the undisputed belt and Ciryl Gane the interim.
  • Brandon Moreno returns against Joseph Morales on a Mexican Independence Day weekend card, in a flyweight division whose title is contested a week later at UFC 331.
  • DWCS Season 10 continues Tuesday. Week 5, on 8 September, produced four contracts — Quentin Pasley, Isaac Moreno, Martin Kozák and Christian Natividad. Apollo Gomes won by unanimous decision and was not signed.

Rankings & business

Rankings movement. Jean Silva enters tonight at No. 6 at featherweight. At women’s flyweight, Manon Fiorot is No. 2 and Alexa Grasso No. 3 — the two highest-ranked available contenders meeting while the belt sits vacant. At featherweight, Movsar Evloev remains the No. 1 contender after beating Lerone Murphy at UFC London in March.

Business & broadcast. The Paramount/TKO agreement is a seven-year, $7.7 billion deal, now in its first year, reaching 16 million subscriber households; UFC content on Paramount+ has drawn 180 million hours since 1 January, with viewership running up to 20× the prior two years’ PPV average. UFC 324 drew 4.96 million views; Freedom 250 averaged 8.2 million, 34 million total globally and 17 million on Paramount+ across the US and LatAm. TKO Group reported Q1 revenue of $1.6 billion (+26%), net income of $250 million and adjusted EBITDA of $550 million (+32%). The partnership extends to Canada from 2027.
