Top Story

One answered video call, full kernel access — and Unisoc has not replied

SSD Secure Disclosure published an advisory today setting out the second stage of a two-stage exploit chain that ends in full Android kernel access on devices running Unisoc modem firmware. The first stage, disclosed in March 2026, achieved remote code execution in the same firmware through a malformed SIP video call; today's release supplies the privilege escalation that completes the chain. Executing it requires the attacker to control a private 4G cellular network and the victim to answer the incoming video call. The research is credited to an independent researcher working under the handle 0x50594d.

There is no fix. SSD says it tried to reach the chipset maker "through multiple channels (email and LinkedIn) but have not been able to receive any response," and the August 2026 Android Security Bulletin does not address the privilege-escalation flaw. Owners of affected handsets have no patch or documented mitigation available and are left waiting on a firmware update from their device manufacturer.

Patch Priority

Do this first
CVE-2026-68820 — Microsoft Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (afd.sys), CVSS 7.0. Federal remediation deadline Monday, August 25, 2026 — 8 days away. This is not a theoretical elevation-of-privilege bug: Check Point attributes it to Lazarus Group, which ran it as an unpatched zero-day against defence and aerospace targets before Microsoft fixed it in the August 11 Patch Tuesday release. In those intrusions the flaw carried the FudModule rootkit to SYSTEM privileges and switched off endpoint detection tooling. Patch Windows to the August release, and treat any host that was exposed to the Dream Job lure set as suspect rather than clean. Four other catalog entries below — Metabase, Cisco Secure Firewall, JetBrains TeamCity and Progress LoadMaster — are already past their due dates and should be closed out at the same time.

Threat Actor Spotlight

APTNew

Lazarus Group — Operation Dream Job, 2026 wave

Check Point has identified a new wave of North Korea's long-running Operation Dream Job campaign, active since early 2026 and aimed squarely at the global defence sector — aerospace, aviation and military technology firms in Europe and India, with targeting reported across France, Germany, Brazil and India, including organisations working on surveillance sensors, drones and robotics. Operators pose as recruiters, most often over LinkedIn or direct message, and walk targets from a fake job offer into downloading malicious files. The intrusion chain paired the afd.sys zero-day (CVE-2026-68820) with the FudModule rootkit for SYSTEM access and EDR shutdown, while the MISTPEN in-memory downloader routed command-and-control through Microsoft's Graph API and OneDrive so the traffic sat inside normal Microsoft flows.

Breaches & Incidents

BreachNew

Levi Strauss & Co. — corporate data exfiltrated after three employees were socially engineered

Levi Strauss reported on August 7 that an unauthorised third party reached its systems by social-engineering three employees into granting access to their devices — psychological manipulation rather than a software exploit. The company says "certain corporate information was accessed and exfiltrated," with no evidence to date of consumer data being compromised. Day-to-day operations were not disrupted and Levi Strauss does not expect a material impact on its operations or financial results; containment measures were applied, third-party cyber experts engaged, and the investigation is ongoing.

Data exposureNew

UK police data lapse exposed highly sensitive records

A watchdog report has found that a data-handling lapse at UK police exposed highly sensitive records. The finding surfaced in today's security news cycle; the watchdog's report is the source of record for the scope of the exposure.

Ransomware

DeadLock puts its command-and-control on a blockchain

Microsoft Threat Intelligence detailed on August 10 how the DeadLock crew stores its C2 configuration and leak-site content in Polygon smart contracts that cannot be seized. The victim-side app queries the contract for the current chat-proxy address, so infrastructure rotates without redelivering the payload. The group surfaced in July 2025, went quiet for roughly eleven months and accelerated through June 2026; more than 80 victims are listed, over half of them in Europe. Reproducing the architecture costs about a dollar in crypto.

Ransomware

INC accelerating through August via SonicWall SMA 1000 flaws

Resecurity tracks INC as the dominant ransomware actor since the start of August, leaning on vulnerabilities in SonicWall's SMA 1000 series appliances for access. Ransomware.Live counts 885 claimed victims to date. Two SMA 1000 CVEs — CVE-2026-15409 (CVSS 10.0, server-side request forgery) and CVE-2026-15410 (CVSS 7.2, code injection) — sit in the CISA catalog flagged for ransomware use.
