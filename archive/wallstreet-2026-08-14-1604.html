The Lead

Two weak consumer prints knock stocks off a record — as of ~3:45 PM ET

Wall Street gave back a little of Thursday's record close on Friday. In afternoon trading the S&P 500 was down about 0.2%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average off about 0.1% and the Nasdaq Composite down about 0.4%, with the S&P still on pace for its third consecutive weekly gain, according to Yahoo Finance's live blog and TheStreet.

Two consumer releases did the damage. US retail sales fell in July by the most in more than a year, per Census Bureau data released Friday — the first decline in nine months. Then the University of Michigan's preliminary August consumer sentiment reading came in at 51, down from 55.2 in July, as Americans grew more downbeat with inflation still front of mind.

Trading Economics' market summary framed it as macro headwinds outweighing the week's tech optimism: long-maturity Treasury yields swung higher on concern the Federal Reserve could be complacent about inflation despite this week's cooler price data, with Alphabet, Amazon, Broadcom and Oracle all turning lower while memory names extended their run. On the TE board at roughly 3:45 PM ET, the US500 CFD was at 7,780.97 (−0.23%), the US30 at 53,712.54 (−0.24%) and the US100 at 29,895.48 (−0.63%), with VIX at 14.44.

Friday's official closing levels had not been published at edition time; the Weekly Scorecard below carries the last verified official closes, from Thursday, August 13.

Movers & Drivers

NewUpIndex inclusion

Reddit (RDDT) +10.4%

S&P Dow Jones Indices said the company will join the S&P 500 effective prior to the opening bell on Tuesday, August 18, replacing AvalonBay Communities. Shares had jumped 12.6% in premarket trading on the news.

NewDownSemis

Broadcom (AVGO) −5.8%

The biggest jolt in the megacap complex. Trading Economics' component board showed Broadcom at 393.48, down 24.34 points or 5.83%, as of roughly 3:45 PM ET, with Alphabet, Amazon and Oracle also lower.

NewDownEarnings

Applied Materials (AMAT) −5%

The chip-equipment maker fell about 5% despite beating forecast estimates and guiding fourth-quarter revenue above Wall Street expectations. Trading Economics recorded earnings above expectations at $3.50 per share.

UpMemory

Extending a rally sparked by its 2026 Investor Day, where management delivered a bullish long-term financial outlook. JPMorgan upgraded the memory-chip maker to Overweight; shares had risen 3.8% before the bell.

The session's steepest decliner among named movers, after a disappointing second-quarter earnings report. Range Resources fell 7.3% on lower natural gas prices and regional supply gluts, and MDU Resources dropped 6.3% on second-quarter revenue below expectations.

Trading Economics showed Micron at 963.17, up 13.34 points or 1.40%, at roughly 3:45 PM ET, part of a memory-sector bid that TE attributed to bullish analyst and broker recommendations.

On the Radar

  • Reddit joins the S&P 500 before Tuesday's open. Effective prior to market open on August 18, replacing AvalonBay Communities — index funds tracking the benchmark will need to buy.
  • September Fed odds lean the wrong way for bulls. Markets see around a 40% chance of a 25 basis-point rate hike at the September meeting, per Trading Economics, with softer July inflation (3.4%, down from 3.5%) consolidating the view that current levels do not warrant one.
  • The long end is the pressure point. The 10-year yield is rebounding toward a 19-month high after easing from 4.75% two sessions before August 13 on softer inflation data — Trading Economics attributes Friday's move to concern the Fed could be complacent about inflation.
  • AI capital markets stay busy. Trading Economics notes that both OpenAI and Anthropic are aiming for IPOs this year, and that Anthropic moved to acquire Decart — part of what it describes as signals of more hyperscaler spending underpinning the AI-infrastructure bid.
