The Lead

As of ~12:55 PM ET: stocks pare losses — Dow −0.46%, S&P 500 and Nasdaq about −0.5% — as sanctions-relief reports pull oil off its highs

The major averages came off their lows around midday after CNN and Axios, citing White House officials, reported that President Trump is open to providing sanctions relief to Iran on nuclear matters — reports that sent crude well off its highs of the day. CNBC’s midday update had the Dow down 188 points, or 0.4%, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite each down 0.5%; as of 12:54 PM ET the Dow stood at 51,591.73, down 236.89 points (−0.46%). The 10-year yield was still trading above 5.2% and the 30-year above 5.5%, around multiyear highs (CNBC). Earlier in the session the losses were deeper, as described below.

Rising oil prices knocked the U.S. stock market further from its record high on Monday. As of 11:45 a.m. Eastern the S&P 500 was down 0.8%, giving back most of last week’s gains, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 372 points, or 0.7%, and the Nasdaq composite was 1% lower (AP). The slide deepened through the morning: at the 9:30 open TheStreet had the S&P 500 down 0.48%, the Dow down 0.71% and the Nasdaq down 0.55%, with the Russell 2000 up 0.07%, and at 10 a.m. AP had the S&P off 0.4% and the Dow down 274 points.

The pressure came from oil and bonds. The most actively traded Brent contract rose 2.8% to $100.19 a barrel by late morning, after climbing above $101 before the open (AP). President Trump on Saturday rejected Iran’s latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and told Axios on Sunday he expects more talks this week: “They want to make a deal, but it is not the deal that I want to make” (CNN; TheStreet). On Monday a U.S. official told CNN that Trump is willing to give Iran sanctions relief and release frozen funds for “concrete progress” on a nuclear deal, but that there will be no deal unless nuclear issues are addressed. The 10-year Treasury yield jumped to 5.26% from 5.17% late Friday — back to its 2007 level — and the 30-year yield leapt to 5.58% from 5.49%, back to where it was in 2004 (AP). AAA puts regular gasoline at nearly $4.48 a gallon, up from $3.13 a year ago (AP).

AI stocks were broadly weaker after OpenAI said it paused training of its most capable models until it is “confident” additional safeguards are in place (Schwab), a rare exception being Nvidia, which rose after adding a record $150 billion to its buyback. Airlines sank on fuel costs — American Airlines down 3.8% and United down 3.6% — and gold miners fell as gold dropped 3.9%, with Newmont down 4.4% (AP). Early Monday CME FedWatch put October rate-hike odds at 70% (Schwab). Abroad, European indexes were mixed while Seoul fell 2.7% and Shanghai 1.7% (AP).

Intraday readings are AP (Stan Choe, as of 11:45 AM and 10 AM ET), TheStreet (9:36 AM) and Schwab (9:13 AM); they will change by the close. Quartz reported the Dow down 377 points (0.7%) shortly before 10 AM. Friday’s official closes are in the Weekly Scorecard.
