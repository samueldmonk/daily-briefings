Top Story

Two perfect-score flaws, two federal clocks — one runs out today, the other already has

CISA’s deadline for CVE-2026-20079, the authentication-bypass flaw in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center carrying a CVSS score of 10.0, falls today, 12 September 2026. The same batch — added 9 September — also covers Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway (CVE-2026-19490, CVSS 9.3) and Fortinet FortiOS (CVE-2025-25249, CVSS 7.3). Cisco has updated its advisory to say it became aware of active exploitation in August 2026, and identified three clusters of post-compromise activity on FMC instances — tracked as UAT-12197, UAT-11823 and UAT-11988 — deploying web shells and malware.

Running behind it is a second maximum-severity flaw whose deadline has already passed. CVE-2026-75650 in Adobe Commerce and Magento Open Source also scores 10.0; CISA added it on 8 September with a remediation deadline of 11 September. Dutch e-commerce security firm Sansec, which named the bug StyleSmuggler, published an early advisory on 5 September warning that stores were already compromised — including deployments that had applied the July and August 2026 patches.

The Citrix flaw is drawing volume too: Previdian’s honeypots registered 56 exploitation attempts since 3 September, 36 of them on 8 September alone.

Patch Priority

Act today — deadline reached

Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center — CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0). The CISA KEV remediation deadline for Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies is 12 September 2026 — that is today. An unauthenticated remote attacker can bypass authentication via the web interface and execute script files to obtain root on the underlying OS. Cisco has confirmed active exploitation dating to August 2026. If you have not patched, treat any internet-reachable FMC as suspect and hunt for web shells. Immediately behind it: CVE-2026-75650 (Adobe Commerce/Magento, CVSS 10.0) was due 11 September and is now overdue.

Threat Actor Spotlight

RansomwareData leakNew

Rhysida — the Berlin leak

The Rhysida ransomware gang published roughly 6 terabytes of data stolen from Berlin’s state government on the dark web after the ransom went unpaid past its 4 September deadline; the leak was confirmed by 11 September. The demand was 30 bitcoin, worth close to €2 million at the time. Rhysida’s pattern is the textbook double-extortion play: exfiltrate first, encrypt second, then auction or dump when the clock runs out — which makes the deadline, not the encryption, the part defenders should plan around.

Breaches & Incidents

Third partyCourtsSSNsNew

Thomson Reuters C-Track — courts in 11 states, sealed records exposed

An unauthorized party had access to the C-Track court case management platform from March 2026 until West Publishing caught it on 30 June 2026 — roughly a three-month gap. Thomson Reuters disclosed the incident on 2 September 2026. Affected: courts in Alabama, Kentucky, Montana, Nevada, New Hampshire, North Dakota, Ohio, Pennsylvania, South Carolina, Tennessee and Wyoming, plus the U.S. Virgin Islands and three courts in Ontario, Canada. Exposed data can include names, Social Security numbers, driver’s licence numbers and medical and health-insurance information — and some files were confidential, redacted or sealed under court order. Twelve months of Experian IdentityWorks is on offer, with enrollment open until 31 December 2026.

Zero-dayE-commerceNew

StyleSmuggler — Rust backdoors in Magento stores

Exploitation of the Adobe Commerce / Magento zero-day began 4 September. The implant is written in Rust and first appeared disguised as [kworker/u:8:0]; a second version surfaced on 6 September masquerading as fc-cache. It hides its command-and-control traffic as NTP server replies, and beacons host information including agent ID, hostname and username, memory and disk usage, OS version, uptime, root access and implant version. The RCE works against Magento 2.4.7, 2.4.8 and 2.4.9. Adobe has since shipped a fix.

Credential theftAI toolingNew

Langflow and Rails turned into credential-probing infrastructure

VulnCheck reports attackers chaining two critical flaws: CVE-2026-0768 in Langflow (CVSS 9.8, unauthenticated RCE as root via an unsanitised code parameter passed to Python’s exec()) and CVE-2026-66066 in Ruby on Rails (CVSS 9.5, dubbed KindaRails2Shell, arbitrary file read leaking secret_key_base, the Rails master key, database passwords and cloud credentials). Detections went from more than 50 within a few hours on 30 August to 360 by Monday. The queries are unmistakably credential-shaped — LANGFLOW_SUPERUSER, OPENAI_API*, AWS_ACCESS*, AWS_SECRET*, plus reads of the Langflow secret key and checks on .ssh access.

Identity dataFBI

IDScan — 153 million driver’s licences

The identity-verification vendor learned on or around 1 September that data may have been accessed, and confirmed in a 4 September security notice that attackers reached customer data in its cloud platform. Brian Krebs reported the dark-web marketplace Nexus offering the U.S. and Canadian licence scans alongside 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents and 579,000 medical cards. The FBI is investigating and IDScan has been sued. No ransom demand and no intrusion vector have appeared in any source read.
