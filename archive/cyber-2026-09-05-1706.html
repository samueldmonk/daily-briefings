Top Story

NewUnit 42Agentic AI

An attacker pointed AI agents at an enterprise network and had root in under ten hours

Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 published an incident-response account on September 2 (updated September 3 and 4) of a human attacker who used frontier AI models and attack-specific agentic frameworks to run an intrusion end to end. Unit 42 writes that the operator “compressed weeks of methodical intrusion tradecraft (using more than 50 MITRE ATT&CK techniques) into less than 10 hours” — impact the firm puts at the scale of several coordinated red teams.

The chain, as Unit 42 describes it: a publicly accessible web service was breached to tunnel in; an automated recon agent mapped internal microservices; sub-agents combed enterprise code repositories for hard-coded tokens and service passwords; those tokens got the attacker into the secrets-management system and from there to root; CI/CD pipelines were hijacked to exfiltrate cloud access keys; and the victim’s own cloud AI endpoints were turned into post-compromise infrastructure. An attempt to plant backdoors in Terraform configurations was stopped by branch-protection controls. The agent was then directed to leave behind an 80-page technical audit of the organisation’s security posture.

What Unit 42 stresses is what was not involved: no zero-day and no elite tradecraft — the advantage was operational speed from an automated loop that monitored, evaluated, acted and re-planned in real time.

Date and framing check. Several outlets have described this as “agentic ransomware.” Unit 42’s own page carries a correction dated September 3, 5:25 a.m. PT clarifying that the incident “was an intrusion, and not a ransomware attack.” This desk follows the primary source. Ransom negotiations did take place — that is how Unit 42 learned which tooling was used — but the victim is not named and no ransom figure was reported.

Patch Priority

Do this first · SonicWall SMA1000

CVE-2026-83548 — unauthenticated server-side request forgery in the SMA1000 Appliance Work Place interface, CVSS 10.0 — is confirmed by SonicWall as exploited in the wild and can be chained with CVE-2026-83549 (CVSS 7.8, OS command injection in the Appliance Management Console) to reach unauthenticated remote code execution on the appliance. SonicWall disclosed both on September 1; CISA added both to the KEV catalog on September 2. These are internet-facing remote-access boxes, which is the worst place to carry a maximum-severity flaw.

This callout is red because the desk’s own rule reserves red for a max-severity flaw under active exploitation — which 83548 is — not because a deadline has passed. No CISA remediation due date for the September 2 batch was confirmed this run, and none is asserted here. The one federal deadline this edition can source is September 18 for the Chromium flaw below; the KEV section states it identically.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NewData extortion

ShinyHunters — and the “Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters” banner

ShinyHunters is a financially motivated data-theft and extortion crew rather than an encryption-first ransomware operation, and it is the group claiming the McKesson theft below. Profiles from SOCRadar and Huntress describe it operating jointly with Scattered Spider and LAPSUS$ under the Scattered LAPSUS$ Hunters banner via a public Telegram channel, with a rough division of labour: Scattered Spider supplies initial access, ShinyHunters handles theft and publication, LAPSUS$ members amplify the extortion.

Those profiles put the group among the most active data-extortion operations globally, with 40+ claimed breaches in 2026 as of July, worked through social engineering and SaaS-credential abuse rather than endpoint exploitation. Claimed 2026 victims listed include Canvas/Instructure (~275M student records), Carnival, ADT (~5.5M records), Charter, Kemper, McGraw-Hill, Rockstar Games, Telus and the European Commission. Current tradecraft described: renewed vishing against SSO platforms, SharePoint and OneDrive; an “AuraInspector” campaign against close to 400 organisations via Salesforce Experience Cloud misconfigurations; and abuse of stolen Anodot tokens to pull data out of Google BigQuery.

Victim counts and record totals in this card are the group’s own claims as recorded by threat-intelligence vendors, not confirmations from the named companies.

Breaches & Incidents

Healthcare distribution
NewExtortion

McKesson confirms a cyber incident; ShinyHunters claims 284 million patient records

McKesson disclosed unauthorised access to third-party applications and data theft, having discovered the incident on August 25, 2026. ShinyHunters says it reached the company’s Snowflake and Salesforce instances by voice-phishing multiple employees, removed roughly 1 TB between August 21 and 25, and then demanded $55,236,150 with a 72-hour clock. The group says McKesson did not respond or negotiate. The claimed 284 million records figure is the attackers’ and, as reporting notes, does not necessarily mean 284 million distinct patients; claimed fields include names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, patient IDs, Medicaid details, medical record numbers, medication and allergy data and physician information. McKesson says its investigation is early.

Court records
Sealed material

Thomson Reuters discloses the C-Track breach

Thomson Reuters disclosed on September 3 a breach of C-Track, the court case-management platform run by its subsidiaries. An unauthorised third party obtained C-Track files in March 2026; the company discovered the activity on June 30, 2026 — roughly four months later. A subset of records may contain names, Social Security numbers, driver’s licence numbers, dates of birth, medical information and health-insurance information. Affected individuals are offered 12 months of Experian IdentityWorks, enrolment open until December 31, 2026. Count discrepancy, printed not resolved: this run’s reporting says courts in “at least 12 US states” plus the U.S. Virgin Islands and Canada; this desk’s standing file records body text naming 11 states plus USVI and Ontario. No threat actor is named and it is not described as ransomware.

Unnamed enterprise
AI-assisted

The Unit 42 victim: source repos raided, secrets manager taken, AI endpoints hijacked

The organisation in the top story is not identified. What is documented is the damage pattern: hard-coded tokens harvested out of code repositories, the secrets-management system infiltrated for master administrative credentials, unauthorised CI/CD builds triggered, cloud access keys exfiltrated, and the company’s own AI model endpoints invoked with stolen keys — hiding orchestration traffic inside expected traffic and putting the compute bill on the victim. Unit 42’s stated defensive tells for agentic intrusions: structured Markdown passed between sessions, Python caches, paired asset folders, bursty API requests and rapid 401/200 state shifts.

Date check · not today’s news
June 2026

The Klue supply-chain breach keeps surfacing in September-scoped searches

A search for current breach news returned the Klue compromise — the market-research vendor whose OAuth tokens gave an extortion crew calling itself Icarus a route into close to 200 customers, among them LastPass, BeyondTrust, Jamf, HackerOne, Recorded Future, Snyk, Tanium and Huntress. TechCrunch dated the story to June 22 and June 25, 2026. It is real, it is significant, and it is not new this week; it is recorded here so the next edition does not import it as fresh. The credential used was reportedly issued in 2022 for a limited pilot and never decommissioned.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • September 4 — one addition: CVE-2026-85046, Google Chromium V8 type confusion. Reporting on the entry gives a remediation due date of September 18, 2026 for covered federal systems — 13 days left as of today. Sourcing note: the CISA alert page for September 4 returned empty on fetch this run, for the second consecutive edition. The September 18 date is therefore attributed to reporting on the entry, not read off CISA’s own page.
  • September 2 — seven additions: CVE-2026-9586 (Sangoma Switchvox, SQL injection), CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette, HTTP request/response smuggling), CVE-2026-49869 (Kestra OSS, OS command injection), CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM, improper authentication), CVE-2026-82329 (JFrog Artifactory, improper authentication), and CVE-2026-83548 + CVE-2026-83549 (SonicWall SMA1000). No due dates for this batch were confirmed this run, and none are asserted.
  • August 31 — two additions: CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078, both PaperCut. August 26 — six additions (CVEs not enumerated this run). August 11 — three additions: CVE-2026-20349 (Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD), CVE-2026-68820 (Windows use-after-free), CVE-2026-72898 (Metabase SQL injection).
  • How the clock works now. The flat three-week window of BOD 22-01 has been superseded by BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk” — a risk-based regime under which agencies must prioritise rapid remediation of KEV-listed CVEs on publicly exposed assets that grant total control post-exploitation, with the tightest cases carrying windows measured in days. This edition therefore does not compute any deadline arithmetically; it publishes only the one date reporting explicitly states.
