The Lead

A jobs blowout closes the market lower — and moves the Fed debate from cut to hike

Stocks finished Friday in the red. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 271.86 points, or 0.51%, to close at 53,414.25; the S&P 500 slid 0.38% to 7,718.60; and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.29% to 26,506.99. It was an orderly decline rather than a rout — but a decline driven, unusually, by good news.

Nonfarm payrolls grew 162,000 in August against a consensus that different desks put at 53,000 (CNBC/Dow Jones) or 55,000 (TheStreet). This page does not merge those figures. Either way the print was roughly triple what was expected, and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%.

The market read that as licence for the Fed to tighten. Per the CME FedWatch tool, the probability of a rate hike at the September 15–16 meeting climbed to 58%, up from 49.4% the previous day. Short-dated Treasuries sold off hardest: the 2-year and 5-year yields both set fresh 52-week highs.

“The August jobs report was much better than expected, focusing the Fed squarely on controlling inflation when they meet next in September,” said Bill Adams, chief U.S. economist at Fifth Third Commercial Bank. “The next Fed decision will be finely balanced: Next week’s releases of the CPI and PPI reports have the power to decide whether the Fed hikes or holds.”

One question from the open went unanswered. Northlight’s Chris Zaccarelli had said he would watch whether stocks shook off the hike scare and rallied into the long weekend, which would signal the AI build-out mattered more to investors than Fed policy. They did not.

The President’s ultimatum — quoted, not paraphrased

Reacting to the payrolls print, President Trump demanded the Fed cut rates in a Truth Social post: “LOWER THE RATE OR I’LL STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE HAVE A DEFICIT, which the U.S. Supreme Court, in its ridiculous and very costly Tariff decision, strongly acknowledged ‘the President’ has an absolute right to do. IT’S BETTER THAN TARIFFS! The Fed Board, with its great new leader, must get smart – BE PATRIOTS for a change.”

NOTE ON SOURCING: TheStreet’s headline renders the threat as aimed at countries running surpluses with the U.S.; its own body text and the quoted post both say deficit. This page prints the post verbatim and does not adopt the headline’s wording.

New

Norway’s wealth fund proposes an $80bn Treasury cut

Norges Bank Investment Management, manager of Norway’s $2.3 trillion sovereign fund, has recommended cutting government bonds to 50% of its benchmark bond index from 70%, with U.S. Treasuries taking the biggest reduction — roughly $80 billion of about $215 billion held at end-June, per Reuters calculations. Any cuts are unlikely before well into 2027. (TheStreet, 11:27 AM ET)

SpaceX (SPCX) −0.97%

Slipped to $148.52 in early trading after SpaceX — along with Blue Origin, Stoke Space and Starcloud — told French authorities Thursday it was “canceling their participation” in President Macron’s Paris space summit under White House pressure, per Politico. (TheStreet, 10:16 AM ET)

Volkswagen +6.1%

Europe’s biggest automaker jumped after approving its Future Plan 2030, cutting a further 50,000 positions — bringing the total to 100,000 — amid tariff pressure and Chinese competition. (CNBC via TheStreet, 7:29 AM ET)
