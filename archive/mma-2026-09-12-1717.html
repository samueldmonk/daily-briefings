Top Story

The refused reverse triangle was real — and the fix was not a better live blog but a different kind of page

For four editions this page has refused a set of Noche UFC prelim winners that search kept returning and that no live page it could fetch would confirm. One of them has now been confirmed, and the way it was confirmed is the story. Cageside Press publishes a separate recap article for each individual bout, and each one ends with a line reading “Official Result:”. The Gantt–Klose article, fetched in full this run, ends: “Official Result: Thomas Gantt def. Drakkar Klose by submission (reverse triangle), Round 3, 2:46.” That is character-for-character the finish, the round and the time this page was refusing last edition as a phantom.

The refusal was not wrong; the source list was. Every live-results page this site had been reaching — UFC.com's prelim and main-card pages, Sherdog's play-by-play, the Wrestling Observer's, FIGHTMAG's — is built to be filled in by a human as the night runs, and all of them are still blind. Five were fetched again this run and every one of them still lists thirteen bouts as matchups: Sherdog's thirteen “The Official Result” headings are all empty, the Observer's page is a bout list, FIGHTMAG's live blog was last modified at 6:14 UTC — before the card began — and UFC.com's main-card page is published-stamped 5:00 PM ET with all six main-card bouts written as previews. A per-fight recap, by contrast, is only written after the fight it recaps. It cannot be blank, because it would not exist. That is a structural property, not a matter of which outlet is faster, and it is the lesson of this edition: the answer to a blind live blog is not another live blog.

What that unlocked, and what it did not. Cageside Press's own site navigation — fetched as part of that article — carries four sibling headlines, each naming a winner: “Rafa García Turned Back by Zhu Rong”, “Sean King Wrecks Jessie Rosas in Violent Debut”, “Decision Win Over JJ Aldrich Has Regina Tarin Dancing” and the Gantt piece itself. Separately, MMA Mania and Yahoo both return the headline “Belgaroui Steamrolls Santos With Vicious TKO Stoppage.” That is five of the seven prelims, each with a winner named by a published headline or an official-result line rather than by a search summary. They are on the table below. Two prelims are still refused, and the reasons differ. No headline anywhere names a winner of Bahamondes–Salikhov: Bloody Elbow has a piece whose title ends “result” but names no one, and the page returned empty when fetched. And the only source that names a winner of Elliott–Chairez is an Athlon Sports page whose own title reads “AI Prediction, Results and Highlights” — a prediction engine, which is the precise thing this page has spent four editions refusing.

One number moved, and it is worth recording. A search this run returned Gantt's finish as “submission (triangle choke) at 2:31 of round 3”; a second search, and the fetched official-result line, both give 2:46 and reverse triangle. This page prints 2:46 because a fetched result field says so. The 2:31 is recorded here as refused. Two summaries of the same fight disagreeing by fifteen seconds is a useful reminder that a number is not evidence for being precise — which was the argument this page made against the phantoms, and it holds just as well now that one of them has turned out to be true.

Colour from the recap, all of it sourced. Gantt — a former ACC wrestling champion at NC State — took Klose down inside thirty seconds with a slam, controlled the fence for two rounds, then in the third took the back, threatened the triangle and finished while landing elbows. He had Daniel Cormier in his corner, on broadcast duty that night, after his own coach fell sick before the fight. He screamed in Klose's face afterwards and was escorted away by referee Marc Goddard, threw his mouthpiece into the crowd, then apologised for it in the interview and called Klose a man who “has beaten legends.”

Prospect Watch

NewProspect

Regina Tarin — 9-0, and through a ranked opponent at 21

The Mexico City flyweight arrived 8-0 off a February debut win and leaves the Noche UFC opener 9-0 with a unanimous-decision win over the division's No. 15. Beating a ranked veteran with a 22-fight record in a second UFC appearance is the kind of result that reorders a prospect's timeline.

NewProspect

Sean King III — 7-0 after 36 seconds

The New Orleans featherweight arrived 6-0 per UFC.com and leaves 7-0, having slammed Jessie Rosas (8-1) unconscious at 0:36 of the first round in a meeting of newcomers. He was a −200 favourite. Sourced in an earlier edition, not this one: he is 22 years old, and he was originally booked for Dana White's Contender Series before being moved onto this card on nine days' notice. A knockout that fast, on a card built for highlights, is the kind of debut-tier win that gets a fighter booked again quickly — though no next booking is announced in anything read.

NewProspect

Thomas "Tommy" Gantt — still unbeaten, and through the card's best veteran

The former ACC wrestling champion at NC State arrived 12-0 with 1 no contest and a −450 price, and submitted the lightweight veteran Drakkar Klose (16-3-1) — who fights out of Glendale itself — with a reverse triangle at 2:46 of round three. Two rounds of fence control, then the back, then elbows into the finish. Daniel Cormier cornered him, having been on broadcast duty that night, after Gantt's own coach fell ill before the fight. The naming varies by source and both are real: UFC.com's copy says Tommy Gantt; its own athlete link, Sherdog and Cageside Press say Thomas Gantt.

NewProspect

Yousri Belgaroui — a sixth straight win, on the card's widest prelim line

The middleweight, fighting out of Danbury, Connecticut by way of Amsterdam, took a five-fight win streak into a matchup with Djorden Santos (11-3) as a −900 favourite — the shortest price on the preliminary card — and stopped him by TKO in the first round, per a headline carried by both MMA Mania and Yahoo. The finishing sequence described in search results is not published here; the headline is what is sourced.

NewProspect

Zhu Rong — 28-6, and past the card's hottest Mexican lightweight

The Sichuan lightweight, listed by UFC.com as Rongzhu and by Cageside Press and Sherdog as Zhu Rong, came in a +145 underdog and beat Rafa García (19-4), who had been looking for his second win of 2026 in front of a Mexican-Independence-weekend crowd. The winner comes from a published headline; the scoreline does not, and is not asserted.

Refused This Run

Three retired, two still standing, one new

Retired, with the reasoning kept. Three winners this page refused across the last four editions — Gantt over Klose, Belgaroui over Santos, Zhu Rong over García — are now published, because each is carried by a page written after the fight: an “Official Result” line in Gantt's case, a winner-naming headline in the other two. That is a change in the evidence, not a change in the standard. It is worth being explicit that this page was refusing true statements for several hours, and that this is the expected cost of the rule rather than a failure of it: a standard that never withholds anything true is a standard that also never withholds anything false.

Still refused, for two different reasons. Ignacio Bahamondes def. Muslim Salikhov, unanimous decision, 30-27, 30-27, 29-28 — the scorecards have now been returned by two separate searches, but no page names a winner of that bout. Bloody Elbow has a piece titled “Ignacio Bahamondes vs Muslim Salikhov result” that names no one in its title and returned empty when fetched. Consistency is not corroboration when both instances are the same kind of source. And Édgar Cháirez def. Tim Elliott — or, in this run's search summary, the reverse, Elliott def. Cháirez by unanimous decision — is refused more firmly than before, because the page behind it identifies itself in its own title as an “AI Prediction”. Note that the direction flipped between editions; a source that can name either man is naming neither.

New this run, and refused: a fight time. One search returned Gantt's finish as “triangle choke at 2:31 of round 3”. The fetched official-result line says reverse triangle, 2:46. The 2:46 is published and the 2:31 is refused — the same test applied to a detail inside a result that is otherwise confirmed.

The wider rule, revised where the evidence revised it. What held: "def." carries no evidential weight, a set of winners in correct bout order is what a picks list looks like by construction, and favourites winning is not confirmation — every line refused above and below was the favourite, and so were the three now retired. What this run adds: specificity still is not evidence, but neither is it disqualifying. "2:46" was correct and "2:31" was not, and nothing about either sentence told you which. The thing that settled it was not the number's precision but the kind of page it sat on. The remaining Athlon Sports predictions stay unpublished for the reason they always have.

Any champions list seating Valentina Shevchenko at 125 pounds is stale and refused on sight, and Alex Pereira is not the light-heavyweight champion however often a cached page says so.
