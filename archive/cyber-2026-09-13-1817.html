Top Story

New this edition

“MikroTrick”: two RouterOS bugs that hand over an edge router with no password, and the clock runs out today

CERT Polska has published the fullest account yet of the two MikroTik RouterOS flaws CISA added to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 10 September, and the detail that matters is that they compose. CVE-2026-67277 (CVSS 8.8) is a missing-authentication flaw in the btest service that leaks kernel memory and can be driven to denial of service. CVE-2026-86060 (CVSS 9.2) allows an attacker to rewrite the trusted RouterOS policy mask and escalate privilege. Used together — a chain CERT Polska names MikroTrick — they give full control of the device without authentication on any router reachable over SSH. CERT Polska says it has observed unidentified threat actors doing exactly that.

The federal remediation date for both is 13 September 2026 — today — and CISA marked CVE-2026-86060 as requiring forensic triage under BOD 26-04, not merely a patch. That is the directive’s severest tier. For anyone running RouterOS at a network edge, the question this weekend is not whether the box is patched but whether it was already taken before the patch landed. One caveat printed rather than resolved: a vulnerability tracker read this run scores CVE-2026-86060 at 9.8 rather than 9.2. The CISA-derived figure is the one published.

Still running — carried and re-confirmed

BlueMoon, the exploit kit Proofpoint documented earlier this week, remains the other half of the week’s story. It chains CVE-2026-85046 (type confusion in Chrome’s V8 engine) → CVE-2026-87491 (V8 sandbox escape) → CVE-2026-85880 (heap-based buffer overflow in Windows ALPC) to go from a clicked link to SYSTEM on a fully updated machine. The China-linked TA412 / APT31 was first seen using it on 28 August 2026 against NGOs and commodity traders; within days UNK_LateNight (US aerospace), UNK_DoubleCheck (Vietnamese manufacturing) and UNK_QuietRacket (government and finance in Indonesia and Singapore) were running the identical chain. Google shipped Chrome Stable fixes on 3 and 8 September; the first was already exploited at release. Four unrelated crews on one chain inside a week implies a shared supplier rather than four independent discoveries.

Patch Priority

Deadline today — 0 days left

MikroTik RouterOS — CVE-2026-86060 and CVE-2026-67277. Added to the KEV catalog 10 September 2026, federal remediation date 13 September 2026 — today. CVE-2026-86060 (CVSS 9.2, policy-mask rewrite → privilege escalation) additionally carries a mandatory forensic triage obligation under BOD 26-04. Both are being exploited in the wild, and the combination yields unauthenticated device takeover where SSH remote access is enabled. Update RouterOS and restrict management-plane exposure; if the device was internet-facing before today, treat it as suspect rather than fixed. This is the same deadline given in the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Newunattributededge devices

The MikroTrick operators — identity unknown, access total

CERT Polska attributes the RouterOS exploitation only to unknown threat actors, and that vagueness is the point: the chain needs no credentials, no user interaction and no foothold — just an SSH-reachable router. Edge network gear sits outside most EDR coverage and logs sparsely, so the population of compromised devices is likely larger than the population of detected ones. Contrast with TA412/APT31 and the BlueMoon clusters above, which are named, tracked and phishing-dependent: those you can teach users to resist; this one you can only patch.

supply chain

Trezor — 347,000 addresses phished through its email vendor

Newsletter provider Brevo was breached on 9 September, an incident touching 120 Brevo accounts. The attacker sent mail that genuinely originated from Trezor’s own campaigns, subject-lined “Critical Security Alert: STM32 Entropy Vulnerability”, steering readers to a site built to harvest wallet seeds. 347,000 addresses were targeted, 2,500 clicked, and Trezor killed the domain within 20 minutes. Only the newsletter database was exposed; no Trezor system was compromised. Re-confirmed this run.

How these deadlines are set — and why one of them is three days

BOD 22-01 is revoked. CISA replaced it on 10 June 2026 with BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk”, which also revokes BOD 19-02. The old flat three-week KEV window is gone; in its place is a risk matrix keyed to four variables — whether the asset is publicly exposed, whether the CVE is in KEV, whether exploitation can be fully automated, and the technical impact achieved after exploitation. Deadlines are tiered at 3, 14 or 60 days. A vulnerability meeting all four high-risk criteria must be remediated in three calendar days — the most aggressive standing timeline in the history of federal cyber directives, and the reason the RouterOS pair added on 10 September came due on 13 September. Agencies must be operating to these timelines by 7 December 2026; the directive binds federal civilian agencies, not the private sector. Two consequences for reading this page: a same-week deadline is now normal and must not be “corrected” to three weeks, and CVEs in the same bulletin can carry different dates — take the stated date per CVE, never derive it. A corollary worth keeping: KEV entry requires evidence of exploitation, so a severe flaw nobody is exploiting carries no federal deadline at all. Absence from KEV is not a severity judgement.
