Top Story

A CVSS 10.0 pre-auth RCE in N-able N-central is being exploited — and federal agencies have until Friday

CVE-2026-86218 carries a CVSSv3 score of 10.0 and is described by CISA as a static code injection flaw (CWE-96) that “could allow for pre-authentication remote code execution.” CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on Tuesday 8 September, requiring Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies to apply the fix by 11 September 2026. It is patched in N-central 2026.3 Hotfix 4, released 5 September.

N-central is a remote monitoring and management platform — the console managed service providers use to reach every endpoint they administer. No authentication and no user interaction are required, so anyone with network access to an exposed N-central instance can run arbitrary commands, and the foothold is not limited to the platform itself. In its own “urgent” notice to customers, N-able said the flaw “has been observed being exploited in the wild,” that it is “actively investigating this matter and [has] taken additional steps to help protect customer environments,” and urged immediate application of the hotfix.

The KEV listing followed Huntress opening an investigation into the compromise of a customer’s fully patched N-central production environment on 4 September. Huntress has not been able to say which flaw was used: “Due to limited historical logging available directly on the appliance, we cannot definitively confirm which specific exploit the threat actor used to achieve their compromise, nor can we rule out the use of alternative vulnerabilities.”

Two other flaws are in the frame for that intrusion. CVE-2026-86206 and CVE-2026-86207 — found and reported by Rapid7’s Stephen Fewer — can be chained to let a remote unauthenticated attacker bypass authentication and create an attacker-controlled System Administrator account on the server. They were patched the same day in Hotfix 3. This is N-able’s fourth N-central hotfix in five weeks.

Patch Priority

Do this first

Apply N-central 2026.3 Hotfix 4 to every N-able N-central server, today. CVE-2026-86218 is a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated RCE, confirmed exploited in the wild by the vendor, KEV-listed on 8 September with a federal remediation deadline of 11 September 2026 ( ). The blast radius is the reason it outranks everything else: a compromised N-central reaches every downstream endpoint the MSP manages. Because Huntress saw a fully patched environment fall on 4 September, patching alone is not sufficient — hunt as well.

Immediately after it: Adobe Hotfix VULN-39341 for Adobe Commerce, Commerce B2B and Magento Open Source (StyleSmuggler, CVE-2026-75650, also CVSS 10.0, exploited from 4 September — three days before any patch existed).

On the deadline. The 11 September date for CVE-2026-86218 is stated by The Hacker News reporting on, and linking to, CISA’s 8 September KEV alert; CISA’s own pages again returned an empty body when fetched directly this run. No due date is asserted for the other three CVEs added the same day, including CVE-2026-75650 — the previous edition found the 11 September date attributed to both, and only the N-able attribution has been corroborated since. The old flat three-week BOD 22-01 arithmetic is no longer safe to apply, because BOD 26-04 sets risk-tiered remediation deadlines instead.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Active
ShinyHunters

An extortion brand that has moved from SaaS tenants to state records

ShinyHunters is an extortion crew that targets web applications and cloud SaaS environments for data theft; the name has been associated with numerous threat actors since 2018 and survives repeated arrests. Its recent method is voice phishing against Okta, Microsoft and Google single sign-on accounts — impersonating IT support to harvest credentials and MFA codes — plus device-code vishing for Microsoft tokens, then using hijacked SSO to reach Salesforce, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, SAP, Slack, Adobe, Atlassian, Zendesk and Dropbox. The Florida DMV claim below is a departure: a password-reset flaw rather than social engineering, against a government law-enforcement database. The group told BleepingComputer to expect more DMV breaches in the coming weeks.

Breaches & Incidents

NewGovernment

ShinyHunters claims 200,000+ records from Florida’s “DAVID” DMV database

The gang says it breached DAVID — the Driver and Vehicle Information Database operated by the Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles agency and used by law enforcement to look up drivers — and took over 200,000 records since the intrusion began on 3 September. The route in was a password-reset flaw that let them compromise multiple accounts, allegedly belonging to DMV employees and an FBI agent; they then iterated through record IDs and downloaded the HTML and images. As proof they released a screenshot of Jeffrey Epstein’s DAVID record, containing address, Social Security number, birth date, licence ID and registered vehicles. FLHSMV was added to the leak site on the night of 7 September. The attackers say they have since lost access and the flaw is being patched. FLHSMV has not confirmed a breach; BleepingComputer says it contacted the agency and the FBI and had no response at publication.

NewMSP

Huntress: a fully patched N-central environment fell on 4 September

Huntress opened an investigation after a customer’s fully patched N-central production environment was compromised on 4 September — the trigger for the vendor and CISA activity in the top story. Limited on-appliance logging means the specific exploit used cannot be confirmed, and alternative vulnerabilities cannot be ruled out. For anyone running N-central, that makes this a hunting exercise as well as a patching one.

CarriedIdentity data

FBI probes “Nexus” selling 153 million driver’s licence scans

A dark-web service that surfaced on a Russian cybercrime forum on 31 August offered scans of more than 153 million driver’s licences, 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents and 579,000 medical cards covering the U.S. and Canada. Its operators claimed the material came from an ongoing breach at an identity-verification provider; Krebs on Security names IDScan.net. The FBI’s New Orleans field office has opened a formal investigation. Nexus went offline shortly after Krebs published.

CarriedRansomware

Panzer: a new RaaS with 16 victims in 11 countries

A newly identified ransomware-as-a-service operation has claimed 16 victims across 11 countries since its leak site went live on 5 August 2026, spanning technology, manufacturing, government, agriculture, energy, education and retail — technology (4) and manufacturing (3) worst hit. Both data theft and encryption are involved.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • 8 September — four added. CVE-2026-75650 (Adobe Commerce/Magento), CVE-2026-81963 (Windows link following), CVE-2026-85880 (Windows heap overflow), CVE-2026-86218 (N-able N-central static code injection). CVE-2026-86218 is due 11 September 2026 ( ) — the one due date corroborated this run, via The Hacker News reporting on and linking to the CISA alert. No due date is asserted for the other three, and none is printed.
  • 4 September — one added. CVE-2026-85046, Google Chromium V8 type confusion. No due date verified this run.
  • 2 September — seven added. Covering Sangoma Switchvox, Kludex Starlette, Kestra OSS, BerriAI LiteLLM, JFrog Artifactory and SonicWall SMA1000 appliances. No due date verified this run.
  • Why only one countdown. Previous editions ran a three-week clock from the add date under BOD 22-01. BOD 26-04 has since replaced that flat window with remediation deadlines tiered by risk, so that arithmetic no longer holds — and CISA’s own pages returned empty bodies on direct fetch again this run. The single countdown above is printed because a date was read this run; the rest are not guessed. Operationally, treat both CVSS 10.0 flaws as due now.
  • Volume check. Twelve CVEs entered the catalogue in the last seven days — a heavy week by any measure.

Around the Industry

  • September Patch Tuesday has no single count. Tenable’s advisory says 964 CVEs, BleepingComputer 966, six other reads 973, and SecurityWeek 974 (calling it a record). All name the same two exploited zero-days for the same release; more than 110 of the fixes carry a critical rating, and nearly 90% fall into privilege escalation, remote code execution or information disclosure. The spread is printed as a spread; no single figure is presented as the number.
  • Wormable flaws in the same release. Twenty of the fixed vulnerabilities are considered wormable, among them CVE-2026-55007 (Exchange RCE), CVE-2026-69465 (SharePoint RCE), CVE-2026-65669 (SQL Server EoP), CVE-2026-69525 (RDS RCE) and CVE-2026-80097 (Authenticator EoP).
  • Headline-level items, carried only as far as their headlines go. Three stories surfaced on BleepingComputer’s front page this run that were not read in full, so only what each headline states is repeated here: the Trezor data-breach impact now reaches 81,000 customers; a Microsoft 365 phishing service called BigBear bypassed MFA at 258 organisations; and over 5,400 hacked sites are serving ClickFix payloads stored on the blockchain.
