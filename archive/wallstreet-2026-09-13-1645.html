The Lead

A streak broken on Friday, a rate decision on Wednesday

US markets are closed — it is Sunday. The most recent session is Friday 11 September, when all three major indexes rose and snapped four straight sessions of losses “as oil prices and Treasury yields paused their surge.” The S&P 500 climbed 0.86%, the Dow added 509.19 points, or 0.98%, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.96%. It was a rescue, not a rally: across the full week the S&P fell 0.8%, the Dow 1.6% and the Nasdaq 0.7%.

What waits on the other side of the weekend is the harder question. The FOMC meets 15–16 September, with the decision and a fresh dot plot due Wednesday at 2:00 p.m. ET — and the debate is not whether the Fed cuts but whether it hikes. August CPI came in hot on the core measure, +0.3% month-over-month against +0.2% expected, with headline inflation at 3.4% year-over-year. CME futures pricing has moved to roughly 80–90% for a 25 basis-point increase.

The inflation problem has an obvious source. Producer inflation accelerated in August as the Iran war drove up wholesale energy prices; crude touched $108 for the first time since May earlier in the month, and the 10-year Treasury yield reached its highest level since October 2023. Friday’s relief came precisely because both of those pressures paused.

On the Radar

  • Tuesday–Wednesday: the FOMC meets 15–16 September. Decision and Summary of Economic Projections — the dot plot — land Wednesday at 2:00 p.m. ET. Futures price roughly 80–90% odds of a 25 bp hike.
  • Tuesday: ADP weekly employment change and the Empire State manufacturing index.
  • Wednesday, before the Fed: August retail sales — the one that matters most, since roughly two-thirds of US GDP runs through consumer spending — plus export and import price indexes, business inventories and the NAHB housing market index.
  • Thursday: August housing starts and pending home sales.
  • Earnings: Trip.com and Lennar are the week’s named reports.
  • Context carried forward: August payrolls came in at +162,000, above expectations, with wage growth slowing to 3.1%. Oracle posted fiscal-Q1 adjusted EPS of $1.92 on record revenue of $19.35B, +30% year-over-year.
