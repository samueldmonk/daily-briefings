Top Story

The one Windows bug Microsoft admits is being exploited is a kernel driver every socket touches

CVE-2026-68820 is a use-after-free in the Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock — afd.sys, the kernel-mode driver that backs the Windows Sockets API. SecurityWeek reports it is the only bug in the August 2026 Patch Tuesday release that Microsoft confirms is being exploited in the wild, out of 421 vulnerabilities fixed, 62 of them marked critical. Threat actors have been using it to elevate privileges to SYSTEM.

CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 11 with a federal remediation due date of August 25. The catalog's required action directs agencies to apply vendor mitigations in accordance with BOD 26-04 (Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk) and CISA's Forensics Triage Requirements, or discontinue use of the product where mitigations are unavailable.

Practical read: this is not the flaw that gets an attacker in the door — it is the one that turns a phished user session into full system control. SecurityWeek notes that, based on historical tradecraft targeting afd.sys flaws, the CVE may have been exploited by nation-state actors.

Patch Priority

CVE-2026-20349 — Cisco Secure Firewall ASA and FTD — federal deadline is TODAY, August 14, 2026

CISA added the Cisco Secure Firewall Adaptive Security Appliance (ASA) and Secure Firewall Threat Defense (FTD) heap inspection vulnerability to the KEV catalog on August 11 with a federal remediation due date of August 14. CISA's description: the flaw could allow an unauthenticated, remote attacker to cause the device to reload unexpectedly, resulting in a denial-of-service condition.

The same August 14 deadline applies to CVE-2026-72898, the Metabase SQL injection that CISA says lets an unauthenticated remote attacker inject arbitrary SQL, obtain administrator access, change application configuration, steal stored database credentials and export data. Both are internet-facing classes of asset; treat them as today's work, not next week's.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ExtortionVishingMicrosoft 365

ShinyHunters

The extortion group is converting phone calls into cloud-tenant breaches. Questel confirmed on August 13 that a voice-phishing call gave attackers entry to a Sales SharePoint site inside its Microsoft 365 environment; ShinyHunters claims more than 21 million records and over 147GB of internal corporate files, and had listed Questel on August 1 alongside Alcon and Lumenis with an August 4 contact deadline. The same group is behind the RingCentral intrusion the company disclosed on July 28 and blamed on a sophisticated social engineering campaign: ShinyHunters claimed 623GB, then published a 280GB archive after RingCentral declined to pay.
