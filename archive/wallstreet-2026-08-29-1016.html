The Lead

A closed tape, a settled week — and a $50 billion buyout that fell apart on the way out of it

It is Saturday morning and U.S. equity markets are closed. Nothing on this page is an intraday read: the most recent session is Friday, August 28, and its official closes stand unchanged, re-verified a sixth time at 10:20 AM against a fresh search returning the same three figures — S&P 500 7,711.76, −0.25%; Nasdaq Composite 26,402.42, −0.52%; Dow Jones Industrial Average 53,559.99, −9.45 points, −0.02%.

The week still finished green. Friday's decline was shallow enough that the S&P 500 notched a winning week anyway, with the Nasdaq's fall driven by semiconductors — Nvidia and Intel were both named as drags. The session's frame was Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole address, in which he said, in his own words, that “while this summer's PCE and CPI readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved.”

The corporate story of the weekend is unchanged since the 8:19 edition and is repeated here in full because it is still the largest single-name item on the board — it is not new to this edition. The rate pricing turned over in the 9:40 AM edition, not this one; it is in On the Radar below. Stripe and Advent International have abandoned their pursuit of PayPal, an offer worth more than $50 billion. The bid stood at $60.50 per share, valuing PayPal at more than $53 billion; PayPal's board considered it too low and the company had not sent any formal reply. Block had been part of the original April approach but left the group before Stripe and Advent put the bid forward. A transaction on that scale would have ranked among the largest leveraged buyouts on record. PayPal fell as much as 16% in premarket trading on Friday — that is a premarket figure, explicitly not a closing move, and no Friday closing percentage for PYPL was stated by any source seen this run, so none is printed. Two reads of that move are on the record and neither is adopted: one report describes the fall as “as much as 16% in premarket trading”, another headlines it as a 15% plunge. Reporting seen this run adds that the board rejected the offer over concerns it undervalued the company, that the two sides had been negotiating a potential higher price, and that a future approach by Advent and Stripe remains possible should conditions shift.

Weekend standing: the next scheduled session is Monday, August 31. The live widgets on this page will show weekend/last-traded values until the tape reopens.

On the Radar

  • Friday, September 4 — the jobs report, and it now has a number attached. The Employment Situation is released by the Bureau of Labor Statistics at 8:30 AM ET. Expectations, sourced in the 8:46 AM edition and carried: nonfarm payrolls +58,000, the unemployment rate holding at 4.1%, and average hourly earnings +3.1% year over year. It is the first major read on the labour market since Warsh's Jackson Hole warning, and the week's largest scheduled risk.
  • Next week's other U.S. releases: ISM Manufacturing and ISM Services surveys, the JOLTS report and the ADP employment reading all land before Friday, alongside Eurozone flash CPI and a Bank of Canada rate decision. Monday, August 31 is quiet — no major U.S. economic report is scheduled — but the tape reopens that morning.
  • Earnings still to come: Broadcom, Dell Technologies, lululemon athletica, Palo Alto Networks and Zscaler all report next week. Three of the five are read directly against the AI-infrastructure trade that drove this week's tape.
  • Friday, September 11 — CPI. The next consumer price index release, and the last inflation print before the Fed meets.
  • Wednesday, September 16 — FOMC. The next Federal Reserve interest-rate decision lands at 2:00 PM ET. The pricing on this page changed in the 9:40 AM edition, not this one, and the earlier figure stays superseded. The prior editions printed roughly 65% odds the Fed holds in September. The source fetched for the 9:40 AM edition reports that traders on the prediction market Kalshi now put 48% odds on a 25 basis-point hike, and states plainly that this is a shift from a point when the odds of the central bank holding in September were nearly 70% — the change attributed to Warsh saying he is committed to fighting inflation. Both readings are printed and the direction of travel is the finding; the two are consecutive reads of the same question, not simultaneous books disagreeing. The above-70% odds of a hike by December is carried from the previous edition and was not restated by any source seen this run.
  • What the jobs number now decides. That same 9:40 AM source frames next week's payrolls release as the report that “should give some clarity on the path for monetary policy” after Warsh doubled down against inflation even with growing pockets of weakness in the U.S. economy — which is why the September pricing above moved on a speech rather than on data. No forecast of the Fed's decision is offered here.
  • The PayPal question. With Stripe and Advent gone and Block already out of the group, no bidder remains publicly attached to the company. No source seen this run states what PayPal's board does next, and this page does not speculate.
