The Lead

Stocks stay lower and the 10-year yield returns to 5% after the Fed’s first hike since 2023 (as of ~3:40 p.m. ET)

At 2:00 p.m. ET the Federal Open Market Committee raised the target range for the federal funds rate by a quarter percentage point to 3.75%–4.00% — the first increase since July 2023 and the first policy change of Chair Kevin Warsh’s tenure. Coverage read this run put the vote at 12–0; Kiplinger’s live blog described it as unanimous.

Stocks took the increase in stride at first and then turned lower through the press conference, and they had not come back by the final hour. The freshest read verified this run, stamped ~3:40 p.m. ET, had the Dow down 1.07%, the S&P 500 down 0.45% and the Nasdaq down 0.06% — the Dow lower still than the 0.9% decline Yahoo Finance’s live blog carried at 3:28 p.m. ET, with the Nasdaq back near the flat line. CNBC’s live blog had the Dow down 751 points (−1.5%) with financial-related shares leading the way lower, the S&P 500 down 0.5% and the Nasdaq down 0.4%, on a read that carried no timestamp in what was returned. All three reads are printed as reported and none is reconciled against the others; no post-decision index level was verified this run, so none is published.

The chair’s language did the damage. “The plain fact is that inflation is too high and has been for too long,” Warsh said. In his opening remarks: “We must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective, clearly and at sufficient speed,” and “today, the FOMC decided that this standard has not been satisfied.” He added that “this summer’s inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved,” while allowing that “productivity growth is strong and capital investment is robust, job gains have kept pace with the workforce, and the unemployment rate has changed little.”

The projections point to more. Officials forecast one additional hike in 2026, with the committee expecting a second increase later this year to 4.1%. Kiplinger’s 2:24 p.m. read of the dot plot saw two further hikes — one this year and one in 2027 — before the funds rate starts coming down, with a long-run range of 3.0%–4.0% and PCE inflation reaching 2.0% by 2029 (June: 2028); TheStreet’s 2:05 p.m. bulletin instead described one more hike in 2026 “before holding steady in 2027.” The two agree on 2026 and differ on 2027; both are printed and neither is reconciled.
