The Lead

A hot jobs number closed the week — and the next real test doesn’t come until Thursday

Markets are closed. It is Sunday, and U.S. equity and bond markets are shut again on Monday 7 September for Labor Day, with the next session on Tuesday 8 September. Everything below reflects Friday 4 September, the most recent completed session.

Stocks fell and Treasury yields rose after August nonfarm payrolls grew 162,000 — far above the 53,000 economists polled by Dow Jones had expected. The unemployment rate was 4.1%, and average hourly earnings rose 0.3% month-over-month to $37.75, up 3.1% year-over-year. The read pushed traders toward pricing a Fed hike rather than a cut this month.

Under the surface the week was close to flat: the S&P 500 rose 0.1%, the Nasdaq rose 0.4% and the Dow fell 0.3% across the five sessions. The bigger move was in energy, where Brent traded in the mid-$90s after renewed U.S.–Iran hostilities: Kuwait’s army reported missile and drone attacks from Iran on 3 September. No Friday settle is asserted — sources fetched this run put the level anywhere between roughly $95 and $96.28, and the conflict is set out under Rates, Bonds & Commodities below. Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has thinned even as volumes hold: Energy Secretary Chris Wright told CNBC that more than 17 million barrels transited Hormuz on Monday under U.S. naval protection, a wartime record, while commodity-vessel counts fell to six on Wednesday from eleven on Tuesday. No 10-day average is published: returns this run gave two different baselines for that same statistic, so it is withheld.

Movers & Drivers

DownCarried forward

Tesla −5.92%

Shares fell after the Cybercab launch event underwhelmed Wall Street. Elon Musk was not at the private Austin event, attendance was limited to a small group of shareholders and content creators under NDA, and Tesla — which usually livestreams — did not stream it. The stock was further pressured after NHTSA opened an “audit query” into whether Tesla correctly self-certified that the Cybercab is safe for public road use and meets federal safety standards. Volume hit 64.4 million shares, about 53% above its three-month average of 42.1 million.

On the Radar

  • Monday 7 September — Labor Day. U.S. stock and bond markets closed. Next session Tuesday 8 September.
  • Thursday 10 September — PPI, 8:30 AM ET. Deutsche Bank expects headline producer prices +0.2% and core PPI +0.3%.
  • Friday 11 September — CPI, 8:30 AM ET. The week’s main event for the rate path; officials may weight the CPI and PPI reports more heavily than the jobs report in shaping the mid-month decision.
  • Tuesday 15 – Wednesday 16 September — FOMC, decision Wednesday 2:00 PM ET. The pre-meeting quiet period began Saturday 5 September and runs through Thursday 17 September.
  • Earnings: GameStop Tuesday 8 September; Chewy Wednesday 9 September; Oracle, Macy’s and Adobe Thursday 10 September.
  • The oil bid is a diplomatic story, and the diplomacy went backwards. Iran’s foreign ministry spokesman says the U.S. must lift its naval blockade before Tehran will agree to fully reopen the Strait of Hormuz — “as long as the U.S. naval blockade continues, the necessary conditions for the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz do not exist.” Israel’s defence minister has separately threatened “crippling” attacks on Iranian infrastructure including energy facilities, and the two sides traded missile strikes this week for the first time since July. No forecast is offered here; this is the standing condition crude is trading against into Tuesday’s reopen.
  • The rate call is genuinely contested and this desk is not drawing an arrow. Returns this run put September hike odds at 58%, ~56% and 60% after Jackson Hole, with several outlets calling it “a coin flip.” Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s 28 August Jackson Hole remarks — that underlying inflation is not slowing — were read as hawkish and roughly doubled hike odds from a prior reading near 30%. Other desks disagree outright: Goldman Sachs has called a September hike “very unlikely.” One further reading surfaced this run and is printed with its date attached rather than as a current figure: CNBC reported CME FedWatch odds of 66.1% on Monday 31 August, the highest of any reading this desk has seen and already a week old. No single probability is published here.
