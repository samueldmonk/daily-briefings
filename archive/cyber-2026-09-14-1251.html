Top Story

Identity verification, breached

IDScan.net confirms a breach behind 153 million driver’s licence scans for sale on the dark web

The Louisiana-based identity-verification provider — which runs ID checks for car-rental companies, retailers and cannabis dispensaries — has confirmed that hackers accessed customer data stored on its cloud platform. IDScan.net says it received information on or around 1 September 2026 indicating data may have been accessed without authorisation, began securing its environment and brought in third-party forensic specialists.

The trail started with security journalist Brian Krebs, who was alerted on 31 August to a new identity-theft service called “Nexus” advertised on the Russian-language cybercrime forum Exploit. Its operator claimed a trove covering more than 170 million people: over 153 million driver’s licences, roughly 10 million other identification cards, about 3 million travel documents, at least 579,000 medical cards including cannabis dispensary cards, roughly 91,000 residence cards and 77,000 employment-authorisation records.

The FBI’s New Orleans field office has opened an investigation. The exposure is a third-party one: the organisations whose customers are in that data did not run the breached system.

Patch priority — deadline today

CVE-2026-84869 — ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVSS 9.9). Improper privilege management and missing authorisation that can let an attacker transfer and execute files through an active remote session without authorisation or host confirmation. Exploitation is tied to three unrelated incidents documented by Huntress in which threat actors abused ScreenConnect to distribute a malicious VBScript payload to newly connected systems. Huntress urges organisations to update to ScreenConnect version 26.6.5. ConnectWise says the condition is in the client; ScreenConnect servers are not impacted. Federal civilian agencies must remediate by 14 September 2026 — today.

Threat Actor Spotlight

China-linkedEspionageNew

UNC3569 — one click into Sogou, then GRAYRABBIT

Google Threat Intelligence ties UNC3569 to China and places it in the country’s hacker-for-hire scene; Google has tracked the group since 2021 and says it has targeted government, education, technology and finance, mostly across East and Southeast Asia. Gen Digital found the group exploiting CVE-2026-51990 in Tencent’s Sogou Input Method for Windows — a chain of three weaknesses (unvalidated command-line argument injection in the sgbiz: protocol handler, unrestricted URL navigation in a CEF-based webview, and a severely outdated, unsandboxed Chromium engine) collapsed into a single crafted link the victim only has to click once. The payload is GRAYRABBIT, a small backdoor Google describes as the group’s first step onto a machine: remote command shell, two-way file transfer, and additional modules loadable from the attacker’s server at any time. Tencent fixed the flaw in April 2026; the fix is in version 16.3.0.3498, which Gen says Tencent pushed to all users by automatic update on 21 April 2026.

Confirmed breach of the identity-verification provider’s cloud platform, detected on or around 1 September; the FBI’s New Orleans field office is investigating a dark-web service advertising the data.

Tencent Sogou → GRAYRABBIT

UNC3569 exploited CVE-2026-51990 in Sogou Input Method for Windows through a one-click sgbiz: link to deploy the GRAYRABBIT backdoor. Patched by Tencent in April 2026 (v16.3.0.3498).

Critical

JFrog Artifactory — chained to admin

Wiz reports attackers chaining CVE-2026-42016 and CVE-2026-42018 alongside CVE-2026-82329 (CVSS 9.8) to take administrator control of self-hosted servers between 15 August and 8 September 2026 — creating persistent administrator accounts, deploying malicious Groovy plugins for code execution and installing Rust-based backdoors for persistence.

Watch

Revolut — data handed to an impersonator

The fintech has disclosed that it shared data on an undisclosed number of customers with a threat actor impersonating a government agency. The requests came from a legitimate government-agency domain and were fulfilled as standard legal compliance.

Watch

MikroTik RouterOS — “MikroTrick”

CERT Polska observed unknown threat actors chaining two RouterOS flaws to seize control of devices without authentication, gaining administrative control and deploying backdoors. The agency dubbed the chain MikroTrick.

Watch

Microsoft — September updates break RDS

Microsoft has confirmed that the September 2026 security updates cause Remote Desktop Services failures on Windows Server systems — an availability problem sitting on top of a heavy patch month.

Ransomware Landscape

  • Qilin dominates the victim count. Bitdefender’s September threat debrief puts Qilin at roughly one in every five to six victims, growing from 250 victims to 1,358 and operating across more than 50 countries.
  • The Gentlemen is the fastest riser. First seen in August 2025, the group went from 35 victims in Q4 2025 to 182 in Q1 2026, making it the second most active.
  • Volume has plateaued at a high level. Black Kite’s 2026 ransomware report counts 7,551 victims, up 24.9%; industry analysis frames this as an elevated “new normal” rather than a spike.
  • Also circulating: a phishing operation abusing third-party email delivery infrastructure sent more than a million scam messages between 3 and 5 August 2026 by masquerading as chief executives, and is now pairing passkey-themed social engineering with attempts to breach cloud environments.
