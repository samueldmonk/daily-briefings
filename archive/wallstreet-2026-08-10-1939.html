The Lead

Rally stalls at the record line: stocks slip as oil climbs and CPI looms

Wall Street pulled back from all-time highs Monday. At the closing bell the S&P 500 eased 0.06% to 7,753.11 after hovering near record territory, the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.32% to 26,605.36, and the Dow finished 0.11% lower at 53,975.98 (Motley Fool). The Associated Press tallied the day the same way: all three benchmarks down 0.1–0.3% as the market slid just below its highs.

The culprit was crude. With no weekend progress toward a U.S.–Iran deal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, Brent for October delivery rose 1.04% to $84.42 and WTI for September gained 0.83% to $78.83 (CNBC), stoking inflation worries two days before Wednesday's consumer price report. Energy stocks outpaced every other sector, while technology and real estate fell the most (Motley Fool).

The pause follows a powerful week: the S&P 500 closed Friday at a record 7,757.64 (+0.62%) and posted its strongest week since April (CNBC) after the July jobs report showed the economy unexpectedly lost 23,000 jobs — against a consensus near +83,000 — with unemployment easing to 4.1% and May–June payrolls revised down by a combined 103,000 (CNBC, NBC News). Traders read the miss as raising the odds the Fed stays friendly; this week's CPI print is the next test of that bet.
