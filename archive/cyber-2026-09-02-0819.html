Top Story

Nutex Health confirms the theft; The Gentlemen claim it

U.S. hospital operator Nutex Health says attackers stole private or confidential patient, employee, provider, business and financial information in the cyberattack it disclosed last week. Nutex first reported the intrusion on August 24 under Item 8.01 of a Form 8-K — the catch-all “Other Events” category — and in an updated SEC filing submitted Monday said an unauthorized third party had threatened to publish the stolen information.

The Gentlemen ransomware-as-a-service operation added the company to its leak site on Monday and claimed responsibility. Naming a victim and threatening publication is the standard pressure tactic of a double-extortion attack, and the leak-site listing is a claim by the attacker, not a confirmation by Nutex of who was responsible. To date the company has not identified any material impact on its business operations or financial reporting systems; a purported class-action complaint has been filed against it in Texas.

Patch Priority

Citrix NetScaler ADC & Gateway — CVE-2026-8452. Deadline elapsed August 29; four days past due.

This is the single most urgent item on the page, and it is urgent precisely because its clock has already run out. CISA added CVE-2026-8452 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 26 and directed Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies to secure every vulnerable Citrix appliance by August 29, under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. That date is now four days behind us. watchTowr proved pre-auth root RCE on August 14 and in-the-wild exploitation began within days, with web shells dropped and discovery commands run. An elapsed deadline outranks a live one — the PaperCut pair below still has twelve days.

Threat Actor Spotlight

RaaSMost active, Q2 2026

The Gentlemen

Emerged in mid-2025 and rose to become the most active threat actor of the second quarter of 2026, outpacing established rivals as it scaled. Its leak site listed more than 200 victim organisations across over 50 countries and 20-plus industries — energy, government and healthcare services among them — as of early 2026, with more than 320 claimed victims by April. In the first half of 2026 its strongest activity was in Europe and the U.K., where it was credited with 144 ransomware attacks.

The operation advertises openly on criminal forums and offers affiliates a 90% cut. It runs double extortion — affiliates exfiltrate, and the data is published on a Tor leak site if the ransom goes unpaid — with negotiations conducted over Tox and Session. It is led by a Russian-speaking actor using the alias “hastalamuerte”, also tracked as LARVA-368, who previously ran an affiliate crew called ArmCorp inside the Qilin programme before splitting off over a payment dispute in July 2025.

New9.5M affectedHealthcare

Aesto Health

The Birmingham, Alabama health-technology firm — which handles secure data migration, EHR exchange and legacy data archiving for providers and practices — has notified HHS that 9,540,683 individuals are affected. The breach was discovered December 18, 2025 in portions of its AWS infrastructure, with exfiltration determined to have occurred between December 2 and December 18. Compromised fields include names, Social Security numbers, driver’s licence and other ID numbers, dates of birth, financial account numbers, medical and health-insurance information and taxpayer identification numbers.

What the company has not said is as notable as what it has: Aesto has not publicly identified the threat actor, has not explained how the AWS environment was compromised, and its notices do not state whether ransomware was deployed or whether extortion was attempted.

~22,000 Microsoft Exchange servers still exposed

Nearly 22,000 Exchange servers worldwide remain unpatched for CVE-2026-62911, with roughly 6,200 in the United States and 5,100 in Germany, plus several hundred each in the U.K., Russia, Canada, Austria and France. Public exploit code exists — Orange Tsai demonstrated the flaw at Pwn2Own Berlin 2026 as one link in a three-bug chain. As of publication no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation has been reported, which is the only reassuring sentence in this card and the one most likely to expire first.
