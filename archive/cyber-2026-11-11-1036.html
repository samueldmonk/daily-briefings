Top Story

Microsoft patches exploited Outlook zero-day in 1,093-CVE November release

Microsoft’s November 2026 Patch Tuesday (Nov. 10) addressed 1,093 CVEs — 63 rated Critical, 1,029 Important and one Moderate — including CVE-2026-124194, a Microsoft Outlook spoofing vulnerability that was exploited in the wild as a zero-day. It carries a CVSSv3 score of 6.5 and is rated Moderate; an attacker could exploit it by convincing a target to open a crafted Microsoft Office file. Tenable notes the advisory credits the discovery to a researcher who used Anthropic’s Claude (Tenable).

SOCRadar says a crafted message file makes Outlook connect to an attacker-controlled location without the usual external-content warning, leaking the user’s NTLMv2 hash; the fix ships through Office updates rather than the Windows cumulative update, so Office channels should move first. It reports CISA added the flaw to the KEV catalog on Nov. 10 with a Nov. 24 remediation deadline for federal agencies, and recommends blocking outbound SMB (TCP 445) to the internet and enforcing SMB/LDAP signing and EPA as general hardening (SOCRadar).

Other flaws to prioritize, per Tenable: Active Directory Domain Services RCE CVE-2026-118146 (CVSS 8.8, Critical, “Exploitation More Likely”), Exchange Server RCE CVE-2026-118207 (8.8, Critical, More Likely), Windows Schannel RCE CVE-2026-119952 (8.1, Critical, unauthenticated, More Likely) and SharePoint Server elevation of privilege CVE-2026-120002 (9.9, Important, Less Likely) (Tenable).

Threat Actor Spotlight

Nation-state · Iran

Shahid Kaveh (IRGC-affiliated cyber unit)

France’s cybersecurity agency ANSSI attributed the Oct. 12 attack on supermarket refrigeration systems to Shahid Kaveh, described as a “cyber unit” affiliated with Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Attackers remotely took control of systems in hundreds of stores — about 700 of France’s 10,000 supermarkets were affected — driving temperatures to 50°C (122°F) for about six hours; Intermarché reported losses of “tens of millions of euros” in a day. Investigators found Farsi use and IP addresses linked to devices in Iran. France summoned Iran’s ambassador and will seek EU sanctions; Paris prosecutors opened a judicial investigation and are seeking international arrest warrants for the group’s leader and two deputies. ANSSI tied earlier attacks, including on Charlie Hebdo in 2023 and the Paris Olympics organization in 2024, to another IRGC-linked group, Shahid Shoostari (AP via SecurityWeek).

Breaches & Incidents

Exploited

Attackers hit AhsayCBS backup servers, hide malware in PNG images

Huntress investigated compromises of AhsayCBS backup servers via CVE-2026-105133, an authentication bypass, and CVE-2026-105134, unauthenticated remote code execution with SYSTEM privileges; it found five webshell families, a second cryptocurrency miner and a host-bound Python implant, but could not confirm every payload or whether backup data was stolen. Ahsay released hotfix 10.3.4.45 for version 10 and 9.15.2.70 for versions 7 through 9; Huntress recommends rebuilding from trusted backups and rotating reachable credentials (Cyber Security News, citing Huntress, Nov. 11).

Zero-day

Chrome patches eighth exploited zero-day of 2026

Google fixed CVE-2026-125350, a high-severity use-after-free in the XML component with an exploit in the wild; update to 157.0.8103.50/.51 on Windows/macOS and 157.0.8103.50 on Linux (The Hacker News, desk record).

Cloud breach

Attacker abuses Qubole’s trusted AWS access to raid customer accounts

An attacker broke into AWS accounts belonging to multiple Qubole customers between Oct. 14 and Oct. 26 and stole stored secrets and data, Wiz said. The attacker used credentials for the AWS account Qubole’s platform uses to reach customers, assuming IAM roles whose trust policies still trusted Qubole’s account — most of them otherwise unused; how the credentials were obtained has not been disclosed. Qubole is a data-lake platform owned by Idera (The Hacker News, Nov. 9). Audit and remove IAM roles that trust third-party accounts you no longer use.

Ransomware

BYOD gang claims Texas HHSC breach of ~2 million residents’ records

The BYOD ransomware gang claims on its leak site to have stolen records belonging to about two million Texans from the Texas Health and Human Services Commission, including Medicaid, SNAP, TANF, disaster-assistance and banking records, and says a vulnerability in the agency’s system let it in. The gang has not released data samples, its leak site was down at the time of writing, and HHSC had not commented — the claim is unverified (Cybernews, Nov. 10).

Ransomware

Vanderbilt begins restoring systems after BYOD attack

A ransomware attack discovered Monday appears to have compromised large amounts of student and employee personal data. As of Tuesday morning the university said campus Wi-Fi, Okta, Outlook, Brightspace, Zoom and YES were safe to use again and users could resume using Vanderbilt devices, while others such as CampusPrint remain restricted; it is working with government agencies. BYOD claimed the attack on its leak blog and claims to hold more than one million Social Security numbers — not confirmed by the university (CyberScoop, Nov. 10).

Supply chain

Ethersphere’s bee-js and Swarm Desktop releases trojanized

Attackers used the project’s own release automation to publish malicious bee-js 13.3.0 and Swarm Desktop 0.56.2, which steal the Swarm node wallet key and its stored password and install a backdoor on Windows, macOS and Linux. Both releases were pulled and a clean Swarm Desktop update shipped, but that does not remove an installed backdoor or protect a key already sent; no theft of funds had been reported (The Hacker News, Nov. 9).

Exploited

Node-RED missing-auth flaw exploited to drop Linux bots and webshells

VulnCheck observed in-the-wild exploitation of CVE-2026-58115 on Oct. 30 and 15 more attacks from the same IP by Nov. 1, deploying a Linux bot and persistent webshells via a single HTTP request against Node-RED instances without adminAuth. Siemens disclosed the bug in August in SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices; version 4.3.4.1 and later contain the fix. VulnCheck: check whether Node-RED is reachable from outside your network today and review flows for unexpected exec nodes (SecurityWeek, Nov. 3).

Patch Day

SAP fixes four CVSS 10.0 flaws in NetWeaver and Web Dispatcher

SAP’s November Security Patch Day fixed four flaws attackers could exploit without logging in, all rated 10.0 by the vendor: memory-corruption bugs in the Internet Communication Manager (CVE-2026-76964) and Web Dispatcher (CVE-2026-89126) that could allow code execution, a SAML 2.0 service-provider flaw (CVE-2026-120017) that could let an attacker log in as any user on some SAML single-sign-on setups, and an SAP Gateway access-control flaw (CVE-2026-89103). None requires user action; SAP strongly recommends applying the notes as a priority (The Hacker News).

Zero-day dump

N-able takes hosted N-central offline after 30+ zero-days dropped

A researcher, Sean Bales, published more than 30 alleged N-central zero-days — dubbed “Able2Shell,” not yet assigned CVEs — with proof-of-concept code on GitHub on Sunday, claiming paths to remote code execution as ‘nable’ or root, admin-account creation and credential leaks. N-able took hosted N-central offline Monday, said a hotfix should roll out around midday Nov. 10, and advised keeping on-premises deployments offline until patched; it has no evidence of exploitation (SecurityWeek).

Exploited

CISA flags exploited Aspera Faspex, FreeSwitch, GPUStack flaws — all due Nov. 12

IBM Aspera Faspex CVE-2026-123218 (CVSS 9.8) is an authorization bypass letting authenticated users gain full admin rights; SignalWire FreeSwitch CVE-2026-116779 (9.8) is a code injection via inbound SIP MESSAGE chat handling. CISA also warned of exploitation of GPUStack auth bypass CVE-2026-102895 (9.8) and OpenSSH SSHole (SecurityWeek). eSecurity Planet confirms all four entries carry a Nov. 12 federal deadline under BOD 26-04, which also requires forensic triage; GPUStack before 2.2.0 can treat remote requests as trusted local traffic, letting attackers create admin API keys — and upgrading does not revoke keys already created, so review and rotate them (eSecurity Planet).

ICS / OT

France blames Iran for refrigeration sabotage

Last week a cyberattack on several French blood banks tried to raise storage temperatures but was thwarted, and supermarkets in Sweden, Belgium, Luxembourg, Ireland and Estonia reported fridge and freezer problems apparently caused by cyberattacks (AP via SecurityWeek). See Threat Actor Spotlight.

Exploited

QNAP: NAS devices breached via Plex2Shell

QNAP says some Plex Media Server installs on its NAS devices were compromised through four Plex flaws (CVE-2026-96652, -96654, -96655, -96656); update to Plex 1.43.3.10861, treat exposed devices as compromised and stop forwarding port 32400 if remote access isn’t needed (BleepingComputer, Nov. 6).

Crypto / supply chain

Web3Auth flaw used to drain Keplr wallets

An attacker exploited Web3Auth infrastructure to steal crypto from some Keplr wallets created with Google or Apple sign-in; Bitquery counted at least $6.01 million moved from more than 44,000 wallets into recovery wallets, and GoPlus estimates at least about $1 million was stolen (The Hacker News, Nov. 9).

Ransomware

Hawkmoth (Anubis) plants new Lindnor backdoor

Symantec says the Anubis RaaS operator it tracks as Hawkmoth planted a custom backdoor, Lindnor, on two Microsoft SQL Servers at a U.S. organization in October; the encryptor was blocked (GBHackers, citing Symantec).
