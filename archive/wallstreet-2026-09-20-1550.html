The Lead

US markets are shut for the weekend — the last print is Friday's, and it closed a hike week almost exactly flat

This is a Sunday edition: there is no live US session to report, so everything editorial below refers to the most recent close, Friday 18 September 2026. Re-verified independently this run against CNBC-sourced reporting: the S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50; the Dow shed 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64; the Nasdaq Composite added 0.39% to 26,522.55. All three percentages recompute from their own point changes.

The week around that close mattered more than the close itself. At its 16 September meeting the FOMC raised the federal funds target range by 0.25 percentage point to 3.75%–4.00%, its first hike in more than three years. Friday then ran into a very large options expiry: Citadel Securities data as of 16 September, cited by EBC Financial Group, put roughly $7 trillion of US equity options notional expiring that day, about 25% of total US options exposure and described as the second-largest expiration on record. Traders also spent the session navigating rising Treasury yields and elevated oil.

One arithmetic wrinkle is printed rather than smoothed: the Dow's implied Thursday close (51,682.64 + 95.40 = 51,778.04) sits 1.81 points below the 51,779.85 this desk recorded for Thursday on 16 September. Too small to move direction or percentage, and named here so the reader can judge which denominator matters.

Drivers & Movers

Policy

The first hike in three years

Fed funds now 3.75%–4.00% after the 16 September move. As of Friday's close, futures were pricing roughly 4.2% by December and about 4.7% by September 2027, with investors anticipating three further hikes by mid-2027, one of them still in 2026. The next FOMC meeting is 27–28 October.

Rates

The long end did the pressuring

The 10-year Treasury yield is the week's live argument. Trading Economics' own dated line has it rising to 5.00% on 18 September, +0.07 percentage points on the session; a separate write-up of the same session has it holding around 4.94% after losing almost 10 basis points the day before. Both are printed below; neither is adopted.

Commodities

Crude above $100, and still arguing

WTI settled at $100.30 on Friday — CNBC puts the move at −1.6%, Trading Economics at −1.58%, close enough to call one number. Brent is not: CNBC gives $103.87, −0.9%; Trading Economics gives $103.21, −1.54%. A settlement price and a board read are different instruments.

Carried, dated

Xenon Pharmaceuticals, Friday's standout

From this desk's 18 September ledger: XENE traded −26% to $42.30 premarket after Xenon submitted an NDA to the FDA for azetukalner in focal seizures and, separately, paused new patient enrolment in its depression studies over reports of neuropsychiatric adverse events. Analyst moves that day: Deutsche Bank (David Hoang) Buy→Hold, PT $90→$46; Baird (Brian Skorney) Outperform held, PT $97→$90; Needham (Serge Belanger) Buy held, PT $78→$60. On the Radar

  • A week light on data and heavy on speeches. The 21–25 September economic calendar carries few major releases but no fewer than ten appearances by Fed speakers, as the market looks for the path after the September hike.
  • Monday specifically: Chicago Fed President Goolsbee speaks at 10:30 AM ET, and the Chicago Fed National Activity Index lands at 12:30 PM ET.
  • Earnings are thin but not empty: 14 companies report Monday, 11 Tuesday, 11 Wednesday, 18 Thursday and 2 Friday. Darden is expected at $2.05 per share, up 4.1% year over year, on revenue of $3.2 billion, +6.7% year over year.
  • The consumer signal is ugly. The University of Michigan Consumer Sentiment Index fell 7.5% from August to September to 47.8 — described as the second-lowest reading on record.
  • Next policy date: the FOMC meets 27–28 October.
