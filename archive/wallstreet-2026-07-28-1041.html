The Lead

Mid-morning: the Dow rises about 0.8% while a deepening chip rout drags the Nasdaq — AMD and Nvidia down about 5%

The split has held into the second hour. As of roughly 10:35 AM ET, 24/7 Wall St's live index board (fetched this run) had the Dow up 0.84%, the S&P 500 up just 0.03%, the Nasdaq 100 down 0.75% and the Russell 2000 up 0.13%; Yahoo Finance's live blog had the Nasdaq Composite off about 1%, and Bloomberg reported that a gauge of semiconductor powerhouses sank 6% amid renewed worries about the sustainability of the AI boom, with the rotation out of chipmakers driving the Nasdaq 100 toward a technical correction. Earlier, as of roughly 10:10 AM ET, 24/7 Wall St's live blog had the Dow up about 0.77%, or roughly 400 points, the S&P 500 up just 0.03% — "struggling to stay green," in the blog's phrase — and the Nasdaq down about 0.8%, or roughly 225 points, with the Russell 2000 up 0.13%. On its mid-morning boards, AMD was off 5.2% and Nvidia 5.0%, while Workday (+9.0%), Autodesk (+7.7%) and Palantir (+7.0%) topped the gainers as money rotated toward this season's earnings winners. CNBC's live blog had AMD and Teradyne down 5% and 4% respectively leading the decliners, with Micron shedding about 2%.

Stocks had opened mixed as the semiconductor sell-off was offset by rotation into traditional blue chips reporting strong earnings: shortly after the open (~9:45 AM ET), TheStreet's live blog had the Dow up 0.93%, the S&P 500 up 0.13% and the Nasdaq down 0.63%. Sentiment on the AI trade soured further amid reports that Nvidia is exploring a $250 billion funding backstop for OpenAI — further intertwining the two companies and heightening worries about circular financing — while traders fretted that Chinese competition is narrowing the AI gap (Yahoo Finance).

The chip damage started in Asia. South Korea's Kospi benchmark tumbled more than 10% Tuesday as investors dumped the top memory chipmakers — SK Hynix sank more than 14% and Samsung Electronics more than 13% (Yahoo Finance) — extending the profit-taking that has gripped AI trades all week. Stateside, a closely watched semiconductor ETF tumbled 3.6% in premarket trading (TipRanks), and Micron, AMD and Marvell Technology all lost ground before the bell (TheStreet).

Earnings did the lifting. Coca-Cola climbed about 3% in premarket trading after beating second-quarter earnings and revenue forecasts and raising its guidance (TipRanks/Benzinga). Boeing edged higher after reporting a narrower net loss of $428 million (67 cents a share) versus $612 million (92 cents) a year earlier, with revenue topping forecasts on 171 commercial airplane deliveries despite a $280 million charge tied to the new Air Force One program; UPS delivered a strong beat and raised its outlook, and JetBlue narrowed its losses (Bloomberg/TheStreet).

The macro clock is ticking: the FOMC opened its two-day meeting today, with the decision due Wednesday at 2 PM ET and Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30. Fifth Third's Bill Adams expects a hold at 3.50–3.75% — a fifth straight unchanged meeting since the December 2025 cut — with the September hold-or-hike call framed as data-dependent (TheStreet). Oil extended its slide on the U.S.–Iran pause: WTI fell 1.80% to $81.12 and Brent 2.14% to $86.47 in early trading, and gold slipped 1.19% to $4,087 (TheStreet, ~7 AM ET). Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon — four of the Magnificent 7 — all report this week.
