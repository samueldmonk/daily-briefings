The Lead

As of late morning: the tape treads water at the highs while earnings do violent sorting — Honeywell Aerospace craters on its first solo report, Motorola Solutions and Parker-Hannifin surge

Thursday is a stock-picker's tape, not an index story. As of the latest late-morning reads (~10:30–10:45 AM ET), the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down about 0.2–0.3% — putting its record-breaking win streak at risk — while the S&P 500 held just above the flat line and the Nasdaq Composite rose about 0.2% as chip stocks reversed early declines (Yahoo Finance live coverage; MarketScreener's session headline: "S&P 500 flat as chip, software weakness offsets broader gains"; TheStreet has the S&P 500 rising as investors weigh jobs data). Semis did the lifting: Broadcom rose nearly 2%, Marvell Technology climbed 3% and Nvidia traded in the green (Yahoo Finance). Early in the session, 8 of 11 S&P 500 sectors traded higher, with materials leading at +2.87% and energy lagging at −0.96% (StockTitan sector board).

Underneath the flat indexes, earnings are sorting winners from losers at industrial scale. The session's wreck: Honeywell Aerospace, the engine maker spun off from Honeywell in June, plunged 20.8% through 9:50 AM ET after its first earnings report as a standalone company — pro forma EPS of $1.87 missed the $2.13 analysts expected (The Motley Fool), and the company cut its 2026 organic sales growth forecast to 4–5% from 7–9% as supply-chain constraints capped its ability to meet aftermarket demand (Investing.com). The winners were just as emphatic: Motorola Solutions climbed about 11% — the S&P 500's top early performer — on an earnings beat and raised outlook (Investing.com; MarketScreener), and Parker-Hannifin rose about 8.5% after forecasting full-year profit above estimates (MarketScreener). Memory stayed pinned — Western Digital down 18% on a disappointing forecast and Sandisk down 13% (Yahoo Finance) — while Datadog fell 16% in early trade on weaker forward revenue guidance (StockMarketWatch), roughly the move options had priced (Market Rebellion). And SpaceX's IPO lockup is now live: insiders got their first chance today to sell up to 911.5 million shares, though another 455.8 million remain restricted because of the stock's weak performance (MarketWatch via TS2); the stock was up 1.7% premarket but sits near all-time lows after Wednesday's 13.6% slide to $108.27 — 19.8% below its $135 June IPO price (Benzinga; TS2/TheStreet).

The labor data leaned reassuring ahead of Friday's main event. First-time jobless claims ticked up slightly to 199,000 in the week of August 1 (Yahoo Finance) — still below the ~203,000 forecast (Investing.com) — and Challenger, Gray & Christmas reported U.S. employers announced just 33,429 job cuts in July, the lowest monthly tally in two years (Challenger via Trading Economics/Investing.com). That sets up Friday's July payrolls report, where consensus runs ~83,000 jobs with a 4.3% unemployment rate per Yahoo-cited forecasts (Schwab cites 86,000) after June's +57,000 and Wednesday's soft +44,000 ADP print. The Fed backdrop sharpened too: Governor Lisa Cook said Wednesday she is "prepared to act" on a rate hike unless disinflation resumes (CNBC), and JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon warned that leverage across markets is elevated, with "margin debt being the highest it has ever been" (Yahoo Finance). Wednesday's context: the Dow rose 263.24 points (+0.49%) to a record 54,349.12 — its fifth straight positive day — while the S&P 500 (−0.17%) and Nasdaq (−0.83%) pulled back, and Nvidia rose over 3% on SpaceX's exclusive-chips commitment (CNBC; Yahoo Finance).

Movers & Drivers

New▼ Spinoff stumble

Honeywell Aerospace (HONA)

The session's biggest wreck: the jet-engine and avionics maker, spun off from Honeywell in June, plunged 20.8% through 9:50 AM ET after missing on its first earnings report as a standalone company — pro forma Q2 EPS of $1.87 vs. the $2.13 expected (The Motley Fool). It also cut 2026 organic sales growth guidance to 4–5% from 7–9%, blaming supply-chain constraints that limited its ability to keep pace with surging aftermarket demand (Investing.com). Seeking Alpha's earlier read had the stock down 14%; the losses deepened from there.

New▲ Industrial beats

Motorola Solutions (MSI) & Parker-Hannifin (PH)

The other side of earnings day: Motorola Solutions climbed about 11% — the S&P 500's top performer in early action — after an earnings beat and a raised outlook (Investing.com; MarketScreener coverage), and Parker-Hannifin rose about 8.5% after the motion-and-control technology firm forecast full-year profit above estimates on its Aug 6 report (MarketScreener; The Motley Fool had flagged the setup in its earnings preview). Old-economy industrials, not AI, are carrying Thursday's leaderboard.

▼ Memory

Western Digital (WDC) & Sandisk (SNDK)

The premarket dip became an intraday rout: Western Digital shed 18% after its first-quarter forecast disappointed investors, and Sandisk dropped 13% on fiscal fourth-quarter results that failed to impress (Yahoo Finance live blog). Both had shown only ~5.4% declines on Yahoo's premarket board. Schwab had flagged the setup going in: with the global memory shortage lifting valuations, "winning the telephone game" of whisper numbers was the bar to clear — neither cleared it.

◆ Lockup day

SpaceX (SPCX)

The IPO lockup is live: insiders got their first chance today to sell as many as 911.5 million shares — roughly $99 billion worth — though another 455.8 million shares stay restricted because of the stock's weak performance (MarketWatch via TS2). The stock was up 1.7% premarket (Benzinga) but sits near all-time lows after Wednesday's 13.6% slide to $108.27, 19.8% below its $135 June IPO price and far off the June 16 record of $225.64 (TS2; TheStreet). Behind the slide: Q2 capex of $18.37 billion — 86% of it AI infrastructure — against 92% revenue growth to $7.81 billion, a $541 million net loss and 12 million Starlink subscriptions (TS2).

▼ Guidance

Datadog (DDOG)

The software casualty: the cloud-monitoring and security platform fell 16% after issuing weaker forward revenue guidance, with investors also weighing broader software-sector AI concerns and insider selling pressure (StockMarketWatch live read). The move roughly matched what the options market had braced for — the Aug 7 weekly straddle was priced for about a 15% swing into the results (Market Rebellion pre-market IV report).

▲ Reversal

Chips turn the Nasdaq green

The Nasdaq began the session lower, then rose about 0.2% as chip stocks reversed early declines — Broadcom up nearly 2%, Marvell Technology up 3%, and Nvidia in the green (Yahoo Finance live blog). Nvidia, which rose over 3% Wednesday on SpaceX's exclusive-chips commitment, had been up another ~1% on an early live read (StockMarketWatch). The reversal is selective: Micron dropped 5.6% in early trade and the memory names stayed pinned, so the "AI capital requirements and monetization" sorting continues inside the complex, not just around it.
