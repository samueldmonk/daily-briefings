The Lead

Official close: S&P 500 tops 7,700 and the Dow tops 54,000 — both for the first time ever — on Palantir's "otherworldly" quarter and a 5% oil slide

The rally that opened August compounded straight through the closing bell. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,736.52, up 1.79% — its first-ever finish above the 7,700 line (CNBC/CNN); the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 907.47 points, or 1.71%, to 54,085.88, closing above 54,000 for the first time (CNN); and the Nasdaq Composite surged 2.59% to 26,584.99 (Yahoo Finance). CNN called it the S&P 500's first record high in two months, supported by strong corporate earnings, a rebound in tech shares and hopes for progress on reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Each close reconciles to Monday's verified finish plus the quoted change — the Dow's to the penny (53,178.41 + 907.47).

Two engines are driving it. First, earnings: Palantir delivered what CEO Alex Karp called an "otherworldly" quarter and the stock rocketed as much as 27% (CNBC) and closed up 29% — its strongest daily gain in more than two years (Yahoo Finance). With roughly 85% of S&P 500 companies beating estimates and aggregate profit growth tracking above 47% this season, "investors have a genuine, bottom-up reason to stay invested," Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn told TheStreet. Second, oil: crude climbed overnight on fresh reports of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, then reversed hard — WTI fell 5.1% to about $76.21 and Brent 4.8% to about $79.73 by early afternoon — after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. and Iran could reach an agreement to reopen the Strait, and Qatar signaled progress toward a short-term deal (Trading Economics) — natural gas and gasoline fell roughly 4% alongside crude. The diplomacy remains fragile: Iran disputed that talks were even happening, and President Trump called the Islamic Republic "unbelievably duplicitous," adding "this is the last chance for them to sign a good document."

On the data front, the morning's JOLTS report showed job openings fell by 178,000 to 7.359 million — the first labor-market reading in the run-up to Friday's July jobs report. After the bell, the earnings baton passed to AMD and SpaceX — both beat estimates, and both stocks fell in extended trading (see After-Hours Movers below).

Movers & Drivers

▲ Earnings

Palantir (PLTR)

Rocketed as much as 27% (CNBC) after Q2 revenue of $1.935 billion (vs. $1.81B expected) grew 93% year over year, adjusted EPS of $0.41 beat the $0.35 consensus, and U.S. commercial revenue more than doubled. The full-year revenue outlook was raised from a $7.656 billion midpoint to $8.154 billion. CEO Alex Karp: "otherworldly." The stock swung with the tape — pared to a roughly 20% gain near $151 by early afternoon (Benzinga movers list) — before ripping back to close up 29%, its strongest daily gain in more than two years (Yahoo Finance).

▲ Semis

Chip rally: Intel, Marvell, Micron, Broadcom

Semiconductors rallied broadly behind the AI-earnings signal: Intel gained 10.8%, Marvell 13%, Micron 7.6% and Broadcom 6.6% — with Micron’s advance returning the memory maker to a $1 trillion market cap (TradingKey).

▲ Dow leader

Caterpillar (CAT)

Led the Dow with an 11% gain at the open (Trading Economics) after quarterly sales and revenues topped $20 billion for the first time. The gain pared through the morning — midday coverage had CAT, the Dow's second-largest component by weight, up about 6% (Yahoo Finance), and a Trading Economics components read later in the session showed it up 6.8% at $886.10. Cisco (+3.9%) and Goldman Sachs (+3.0%) rounded out the early Dow leaders.

▲ Earnings

Zebra Technologies (ZBRA) & Gartner (IT)

Two of the session's biggest earnings pops outside megacap land: barcode-scanner maker Zebra surged 18.6% after beating second-quarter estimates, and research firm Gartner jumped 16.3% on better-than-expected results. Online retailer Wayfair also surged nearly 19% on its Q2 beat.

▼ Decliner

Aptiv (APTV)

Fell 15.5% — the day's sharpest large-cap decline — after the automotive-technology company issued weak forward guidance and reported a large sequential revenue decline following its recent business spin-offs.

▼ Energy

Crude's round trip

Oil climbed overnight on fresh reports of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz (TheStreet), then reversed: by a ~1:35 PM ET Trading Economics read WTI was down 5.1% near $76.21 and Brent down 4.8% near $79.73 after Treasury Secretary Bessent said a deal to reopen the Strait could come soon. WTI had traded as high as $82.32 overnight (Investing.com).

▲ Small-cap pops

Ibotta (IBTA) & Backblaze (BLZE)

The session's biggest percentage movers were small caps: cash-back platform Ibotta jumped 50.1% to $36.89 after better-than-expected Q2 results and above-consensus Q3 sales guidance, and cloud-storage firm Backblaze gained 50.8% to $23.51 after beating Q2 estimates and raising full-year sales guidance (Benzinga movers list).

▲ Follow-through

Amazon (AMZN)

Monday's engine is still running: Amazon's market value closed above $3 trillion for the first time in Monday's session and the stock remains at records as hyperscaler earnings continue to validate AI capital spending. Palantir's guidance hike Tuesday reinforced the same AI-demand story.

After-Hours Movers

▼ After hours

AMD (AMD)

Fell about 8% in after-hours trading (CNBC after-hours movers) despite beating on both lines: Q2 revenue of $11.536 billion (vs. $11.284B expected), up 50% year over year, and adjusted EPS of $1.66 (vs. $1.62 consensus), with data-center growth driven by Instinct MI-series AI accelerators (TheStreet earnings-call coverage). The reaction read as a bar-was-higher story — "it wasn't the blowout investors were looking for, even if the guidance does look healthy" — after the stock had gained 5.3% in the regular session ahead of the report (TradingKey). That guidance: Q3 revenue of approximately $13 billion (±$300 million), roughly 41% year-over-year growth (Investing.com after-hours movers).

▼ After hours

SpaceX (SPCX)

Fell 7% in extended trading (CNBC after-hours movers) after its first earnings report as a public company beat expectations: revenue of $7.81 billion (vs. $6.93B expected), up 92% from a year earlier, with the loss per share of 9 cents far narrower than the 26-cent consensus and net loss narrowing to $541 million from $1 billion (CNBC/CNN). The drag: capital spending — the AI segment's outlay ballooned to $15.8 billion from $7.7 billion in Q1 — plus a looming lockup expiration that frees up to 20% of shares for sale (Fast Company/CNBC). Operational metrics also fell short of elevated expectations: 38 rocket launches in the quarter versus the 43 analysts anticipated, and total mass delivered to orbit of 485 metric tons versus a 580.93 MT consensus (Investing.com after-hours movers).

▲ After hours

Arista Networks (ANET) & Booking Holdings (BKNG)

The after-hours tape wasn't all red: Arista Networks gained 11% after Q2 results surpassed estimates — adjusted EPS of $1.02 on revenue of $3.04 billion — and Booking Holdings advanced more than 5% after Q2 gross bookings of $51 billion topped the Street's estimate (CNBC after-hours movers).

▼ After hoursNew

Pinterest (PINS)

Fell 8% in extended trading after guiding to third-quarter revenue that trailed Wall Street's expectations (CNBC after-hours movers / Investing.com).
