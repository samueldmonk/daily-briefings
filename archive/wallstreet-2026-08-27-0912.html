The Lead

New · 9:05 AM ETPre-open

Three earnings beats and a 203,000 claims print carry the tape into the open

With about half an hour to the open, the tape is led by technology and the lead has narrowed rather than broadened. Yahoo Finance's Thursday-morning read has Nasdaq 100 futures up 0.9%, S&P 500 futures up 0.4% and Dow futures hovering near the flat line — a step back from the 8:40 AM ET read this page carried 25 minutes earlier, when all three were green and Dow contracts were up 0.23%. Bloomberg separately reported S&P 500 contracts up about 1% in early Thursday trade after CFO Colette Kress signalled strong sales growth into fiscal 2028. The two headline ETF proxies tell the same story: QQQ +1.04% against SPY +0.4%.

The 8:30 data is in, and it is good. Initial jobless claims for the week ending August 22 came in at 203,000, down 4,000 from the prior week's upwardly revised 207,000 (first reported at 206,000). The four-week moving average rose 1,250 to 205,500. Trading Economics notes the print extends a run of unusually low claims since a near-60-year low of 189,000 in mid-July. This replaces the "no figure corroborated" line this page carried at 8:40 — and it is not the 232,000 number that circulated earlier, which traced to a 2022 report and was rejected.

The quarter itself, from Nvidia's own filing: revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year and 18% from the prior quarter; Data Center revenue of $89.0 billion, up 117%; GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins both 75.0%; and diluted EPS of $2.46 GAAP / $2.22 non-GAAP. Management guided third-quarter revenue to $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, with non-GAAP gross margin near 74% — a figure that excludes any Data Center compute revenue from China, where Hopper shipments were under 1% of Data Center revenue in the quarter. CNBC reported CEO Jensen Huang forecasting roughly 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, well above estimates.

The move is not confined to Nvidia, and it is not confined to semiconductors. Nvidia rose 7.4% in pre-market trade, Marvell Technology 5.8% and Micron 4.5%, while the VanEck Semiconductor ETF jumped 3.5% and the iShares Semiconductor ETF gained 3%. Software and security joined in: Salesforce climbed nearly 12% and CrowdStrike about 8.9% pre-open on their own results, with Okta also among the morning's risers.

That follows a flat-to-lower Wednesday session: the S&P 500 closed at 7,675.70, down 0.02%, with the Dow off 0.08% and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.16%.

CarriedSoftware

Salesforce (CRM) — up nearly 12% pre-open

Shares jumped nearly 12% in pre-market trade on Thursday. Revenue of $11.35 billion, up 11% year over year and ahead of a $11.32 billion consensus. Full-year fiscal 2027 revenue guidance was raised to $46.1–$46.4 billion from $45.9–$46.2 billion. Agentforce and Data 360 annual recurring revenue reached nearly $3.9 billion, up more than 210%; Agentforce ARR alone topped $1.5 billion, up more than 240%. The stock traded to $231.70 after hours, +$26.08 / +12.68%; a separate tally put the move at +11.8%. An "adjusted EPS of $5.90 versus $3.27 expected" line has now appeared in pre-market coverage two runs running. It is still not published here: a beat of that magnitude is far more likely a mismatched consensus figure than a real result, and no company filing confirming it has been fetched.

CarriedCybersecurity

CrowdStrike (CRWD) — the quarter now has numbers

Fiscal second-quarter 2027 revenue, for the quarter ended July 31, rose 26% year over year to $1.47 billion, ahead of a $1.44 billion consensus, with adjusted EPS of $0.31 against $0.29 expected. The company raised its full-year outlook. Shares were up about 8.9% pre-open, having closed Wednesday at $189.18, up 2.05%, and traded 10.49% higher at $209.02 in the after-hours session. HP (HPQ) is also on the earnings watchlist this morning.
