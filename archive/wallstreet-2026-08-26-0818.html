The lead

Two hours to the inflation print, a full day to Nvidia — and futures are refusing to guess

U.S. markets are not open yet. Wall Street begins Wednesday, August 26 in a holding pattern, with Yahoo Finance reporting that US stock futures traded flat in premarket trading ahead of a key inflation reading and Nvidia’s earnings. Futures on the Dow and the S&P 500 hovered near the flat line and the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 fell 0.2%, giving back a little of what the market added on Tuesday.

On Yahoo Finance’s own quote board, read at roughly 4:25 a.m. ET, S&P futures stood at 7,687.00, down 5.00 points or 0.07%; Dow futures at 53,701.00, up 56.00 or 0.10%; Nasdaq futures at 29,215.50, down 61.25 or 0.21%; and Russell 2000 futures at 3,014.70, up 0.30 or 0.01%. The VIX was 15.67, up 0.22 or 1.42%. A separate premarket board surfaced later in the morning had the ETF proxies telling the same story — SPY −0.04%, QQQ −0.21%, DIA +0.06%, IWM −0.03%. Neither reading is a session move; both are a market declining to take a position before 8:30.

The first of the two events lands at 8:30 a.m. ET, when investors find out whether the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge continued its downward trajectory, stalled, or reaccelerated — the last of which, in Yahoo’s framing, would put more pressure on the central bank to raise rates. Economists expect a core PCE of 3.3%, unchanged from the month before. Note the direction of that pressure: this is a market pricing the risk of a hike, not a cut. Bloomberg reported Tuesday that falling oil prices were curbing expectations for more than one Federal Reserve interest-rate hike in the coming year — the debate is over how many, not whether.

The second lands after the closing bell, when Nvidia opens its books in what Yahoo Finance calls “a quarterly yardstick for the health of the artificial intelligence trade.” Expectations are high, with investors increasingly focused on whether the enormous AI capital budgets of the last two years are producing returns. The company guided to $91.0 billion plus or minus two percent for the quarter; Visible Alpha consensus is $92.2 billion and a 40-analyst poll has $91.85 billion. Against the year-ago quarter’s $46.74 billion, consensus implies roughly a doubling. CrowdStrike, Williams-Sonoma, Okta and Abercrombie & Fitch also report Wednesday.

Per-share estimates for Nvidia’s quarter came back mutually inconsistent across the previews surfaced this run — one gives $2.08, another centres on $0.65–$0.68 adjusted — a spread that cannot be reconciled from the snippets available. No EPS estimate is published here. The revenue figures above each carry their stated source. Overnight futures are not an opening print; the levels quoted are stamped to the time they were read.

Movers & drivers
NewMacro8:30 a.m. ET

Core PCE is the number the whole session hangs on

The Fed’s preferred inflation gauge is released at 8:30 a.m. ET, with economists expecting core PCE of 3.3%, unchanged from the month before. Yahoo Finance frames the three outcomes plainly — continued downward trajectory, stall, or reacceleration — and notes that the third “would put more pressure on the central bank to raise rates.” The report lands amid what the same piece calls angst in the bond market and ahead of the Jackson Hole gathering, where Chairman Kevin Warsh will speak on the central bank’s future plans.

NewEarningsAfter the close

Nvidia reports tonight, and the AI trade is the thing being measured

Nvidia opens its books after Wednesday’s close. The company’s own guide was $91.0 billion ±2%; Visible Alpha consensus sits at $92.2 billion and a 40-analyst poll at $91.85 billion, against $46.74 billion in the year-ago quarter. The Nasdaq-100 was already the weak leg of the futures complex overnight, down 0.2% while the Dow held flat — the index most exposed to the print is the one giving ground into it.

Tuesday closeRetail

DICK’S Sporting Goods still sits at the bottom of the board

Yahoo Finance’s trending-tickers board, read this run, has DKS at $124.31, down $55.02 or 30.68% — Tuesday’s close, and the single largest move on the tape. The company cut full-year GAAP EPS guidance to $10.94–$11.94 from $13.27–$14.27, roughly $2.33 off the midpoint, alongside adjusted EPS of $3.53 against $3.78 expected and revenue of $5.59B versus $5.65B — a revenue figure that was nonetheless up 53.2% year on year on the Foot Locker consolidation, where pro-forma comparable sales fell 3.6% and the operating loss was $31.9 million.

Tuesday closeSoftware

Intuit and Zoom carried Tuesday’s guidance damage into Wednesday

Both names closed lower Tuesday before their post-close reports made it worse: INTU $357.46, −$12.46 or 3.37% and ZM $100.92, −$3.91 or 3.73%, per Yahoo’s board this run. After the bell, Intuit’s FY27 guidance of $22.88–$23.12 EPS on revenue of $23.3–$23.5B came in under consensus of roughly $27.30 EPS on $23.74B, with slower-than-expected TurboTax growth cited. A market summary this run notes the two names as the reason Nasdaq futures turned lower overnight.
