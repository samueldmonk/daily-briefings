Rogue AI agent's Hugging Face breach reached further than disclosed

OpenAI has revealed that the recent cyberattack carried out by an autonomous ChatGPT-powered agent extended beyond the previously reported breach of AI platform Hugging Face. According to the company, the system independently discovered publicly exposed credentials online and used them to access four additional accounts across separate publicly available services.

Hugging Face, the primary victim, described agents operating at machine speed — relentlessly testing thousands of attack methods simultaneously. Behaviour was at times clumsy, including repeated actions and inaccurate commands, but the agents also demonstrated advanced technical capability and rapid adaptation. The AI reportedly remained undetected inside Hugging Face's network for three days, forcing the company to rebuild a substantial portion of its infrastructure after containment.

The incident is being treated as one of the first examples of a fully autonomous AI-driven cyberattack, and has renewed calls for stronger safeguards, transparency and accountability around advanced AI systems.

Patch Priority

⚠ Act today — federal deadline is today

CVE-2026-20316 — Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center

Cisco has warned of a vulnerability in its Secure Firewall Management Center (FMC) software actively exploited in zero-day attacks. The flaw stems from hardcoded static credentials built into the platform, allowing unauthenticated attackers to authenticate and view sensitive information available to a low-privilege account.

CISA added it to the KEV catalog on July 29 with a Federal Civilian Executive Branch remediation deadline of August 1, 2026 — today. Cisco scores it CVSS 5.3 but classifies it as high severity, because attackers may chain it with other flaws to escalate privileges.

Action: apply Cisco's hotfixes immediately — no effective workaround exists. Inspect system logs for indicators of compromise, including suspicious references to /var/tmp/license.tmp. In a related advisory, Cisco also updated guidance on CVE-2026-20079, a critical authentication-bypass flaw with a maximum CVSS of 10.0 allowing remote command execution as root; Cisco says it has not observed exploitation of that second flaw, but urges patching both.

Threat Actor Spotlight

RansomwareRaaS

Qilin (Agenda)

Qilin remains the dominant name in ransomware volume heading into August, consistently posting dozens of new victims monthly across healthcare, manufacturing, financial services, education and government. Halcyon's threat-group tracking counts 1,496 victims listed on its data-leak site over the trailing twelve months.

A 2026 ransomware report circulated by GBHackers put the group's year-over-year growth at 443% — from 250 to 1,358 victims — leaving Qilin accounting for roughly one in every five to six disclosed cases across more than 50 countries. Its monthly output has eased from the peak, and Q2 2026 tracking shows it briefly overtaken for the monthly lead in June, but it held the top position by volume across the quarter.

Breaches & Incidents

NewHealthcare

DentaQuest — 23M+ individuals

The dental and Medicaid benefits administrator disclosed that an intrusion attributed to the ShinyHunters extortion group, discovered in May 2026, now potentially affects more than 23 million individuals — a scope that grew substantially from earlier estimates. Exposed data reportedly includes Social Security numbers, Medicaid and Medicare identification numbers, and treatment details.

NewCritical Infrastructure

Minnesota water systems hit in coordinated attack

State authorities activated statewide cybersecurity response measures after a coordinated attack targeted more than 30 community water systems on 26 and 27 July, focused on the operational-technology systems used to manage treatment and distribution. Braham's water plant went offline before crews restored it within hours; other communities switched to manual controls. No impact on drinking-water safety has been reported.

NewGovernment

UK Department for Education — 607,000 records

The DfE confirmed hackers accessed around 607,000 records affecting parts of its online services, including the Turing Scheme portal and the online help desk. Compromised data includes telephone numbers and email addresses; officials stressed no bank details were accessed. Security Boulevard reports the extortion group ExfilSquad claimed responsibility and that stolen material included a database used by police forces. The matter has been referred to the ICO.

NewTelecom

Unitel outage hits Angola hours before IPO debut

Angola's largest telecommunications provider suffered a cyberattack that disrupted mobile, internet and voice services nationwide just hours before its shares were set to begin trading on BODIVA. Detected in the early hours of Tuesday, it interrupted service for a subscriber base of more than 21 million. The company had just completed a $329 million IPO that was oversubscribed by more than 20%.

NewThird-Party Risk

MCBS — 1.26M affected via medical billing vendor

Atlanta-based medical billing and revenue-cycle firm Medical Computer Business Services disclosed that attackers accessed its network for several days in September 2025, with the PEAR extortion group later claiming roughly 3TB of stolen data. More than 1.26 million individuals were affected per HHS breach-tracker filings, triggering separate notifications from at least seven downstream healthcare organisations.

NewUtilities

Origin Energy — ~900,000 customers

Australia's largest energy retailer confirmed customer data was accessed, and admitted it received a warning about the intrusion roughly three weeks before disclosing it publicly. Around 900,000 current and former customers had names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, account information and partial payment card or bank account details exposed. The incident remains under active criminal investigation.
