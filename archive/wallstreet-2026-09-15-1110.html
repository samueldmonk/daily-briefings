The Lead

As of ~11:00 AM ET · Tuesday 15 September

The selling deepened through the morning, with the 10-year at a nineteen-year high and the Fed in the room

Three readings of the tape were returned this run and they are printed as a sequence, not averaged, because they describe different points in a session that has been getting worse. At the opening bell, the ETF proxies had the S&P 500 down 0.28%, the Dow down 0.73% and the Nasdaq down 0.16%. TheStreet’s Tuesday update has the S&P 500 −0.16%, the Dow −0.49%, the Nasdaq −0.26% and the Russell 2000 −0.40%. A later intraday quote returned this run is materially worse: the Dow down 509.54 points, −0.97%, and the Nasdaq down 210.89 points, −0.81%.

That last reading arrived without an as-of time, which would normally disqualify it from being published as current. It is published because it reconciles exactly against Monday’s verified closes — 52,421.20 − 509.54 = 51,911.66 and 26,186.41 − 210.89 = 25,975.52, with both percentage moves matching to the basis point — which makes it demonstrably a live current-session quote rather than a stale snapshot. The direction of travel across the three readings is the point: losses roughly doubled between the early quotes and the later one.

The driver is in the bond market, not the stock market. The 10-year Treasury yield rose as much as four basis points to 5.02% on Tuesday, pushing above its 2023 peak to reach its highest level since 2007, and scaled 5.041% during the session — the highest since July 2007. Surging energy prices, mounting debt and inflation are the cited causes, with the latest leg following oil’s move on Middle East supply risk. The FOMC began its meeting today; traders are pricing better than a 92% chance of a 25 basis point hike on Wednesday, which would be the first increase since July 2023.

Consumer

Retail and discretionary take the brunt

The SPDR S&P Retail ETF (XRT) fell 1.75% and the Consumer Discretionary Select Sector SPDR (XLY) fell 1.05%, which the source reads as concern over cooling consumer spending. With crude up and the 10-year at a nineteen-year high, the discretionary complex is being squeezed from both the input and the financing side.

On the Radar

  • Wednesday 16 September — FOMC decision. The dot-plot and Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference land with it. A hike would be the first since July 2023, and pricing has firmed from around 90–91% yesterday to better than 92% today.
  • The Saudi east-west pipeline is still shut. The outage could remove as much as 4% of global oil supply and may take weeks to repair, on top of fresh attacks on the kingdom. The pipeline matters because it is Saudi Arabia’s route to its Red Sea coast — the alternative to exporting through the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Oil is now the transmission mechanism into rates. The yield move is being attributed directly to energy prices; with crude bid on supply risk, the 10-year has a floor under it that has nothing to do with the Fed’s decision.
  • A quiet tape by design. Little in the way of economic data or earnings on Tuesday, which leaves the session to positioning ahead of Wednesday.
