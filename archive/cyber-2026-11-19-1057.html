Top Story

JPMorgan says vendors were hit, after gangs claimed NVIDIA, Visa and JPMorgan

NVIDIA. The Gentlemen ransomware group added NVIDIA to its leak site with a countdown ending Nov. 28 but has shared no proof. NVIDIA told BleepingComputer it has “found no indication that NVIDIA systems or networks were affected” and is investigating a separate incident that may have affected a third-party partner. The Gentlemen, a RaaS operation offering affiliates up to a 90% cut, claims more than 1,100 victims (BleepingComputer, Nov. 19).

Visa and JPMorgan Chase. The new n0n group listed both with a 48-hour deadline and posted no samples. Visa confirmed a cybersecurity incident at a third-party provider, saying “Visa’s systems were not involved and were not impacted” and there is no indication card numbers were affected. JPMorgan said its service disruption came from a software bug in on-premise hardware with “no evidence it is cyber-related” (TechRadar, citing Cybernews and Reuters).

New JPMorgan update (Thursday). JPMorgan Chase said some of its third-party service providers were targeted in a cyberattack, which it said was not connected to this week’s outage. There was no disruption to JPMorgan or Chase services, but the attackers may have accessed personal data including some customer names and email addresses; the bank did not say how many or which third parties, or how many customers, were affected, and will contact those customers directly (Yahoo Finance, ~10:30 AM ET). Yahoo’s report does not connect the incident to n0n’s claim.

Action: leak-site listings are claims, not confirmations — and all three companies now point to a vendor, partner or bug rather than a breach of their own systems. Review which third parties hold your data and credentials.

Also Leading

Optimizely flaw used to plant malware on the sites of Zoom, BBC, CNN, HSBC and 170+ others

An attacker exploited a vulnerability in Optimizely’s web experimentation platform to add malicious JavaScript to customer projects; Optimizely says it fixed the flaw and removed the code. Report URI’s Scott Helme found it served to visitors of 178 organisations on Nov 16. The kit swapped crypto wallet addresses, skimmed cards, stole cookies and screenshots, and on iPhones and iPads delivered the Coruna and DarkSword exploit kits; only 24 affected organizations (15%) had a CSP strict enough to block it (Help Net Security, desk record). Audit third-party scripts served from vendor CDNs.

Microsoft patches exploited Outlook zero-day in 1,093-CVE November release

Microsoft’s November 2026 Patch Tuesday (Nov. 10) addressed 1,093 CVEs — 63 rated Critical, 1,029 Important and one Moderate — including CVE-2026-124194, a Microsoft Outlook spoofing vulnerability that was exploited in the wild as a zero-day. It carries a CVSSv3 score of 6.5 and is rated Moderate; an attacker could exploit it by convincing a target to open a crafted Microsoft Office file. Tenable notes the advisory credits the discovery to a researcher who used Anthropic’s Claude (Tenable).

SOCRadar says a crafted message file makes Outlook connect to an attacker-controlled location without the usual external-content warning, leaking the user’s NTLMv2 hash; the fix ships through Office updates rather than the Windows cumulative update, so Office channels should move first. It reports CISA added the flaw to the KEV catalog on Nov. 10 with a Nov. 24 remediation deadline for federal agencies, and recommends blocking outbound SMB (TCP 445) to the internet and enforcing SMB/LDAP signing and EPA as general hardening (SOCRadar).

Other flaws to prioritize, per Tenable: Active Directory Domain Services RCE CVE-2026-118146 (CVSS 8.8, Critical, “Exploitation More Likely”), Exchange Server RCE CVE-2026-118207 (8.8, Critical, More Likely), Windows Schannel RCE CVE-2026-119952 (8.1, Critical, unauthenticated, More Likely) and SharePoint Server elevation of privilege CVE-2026-120002 (9.9, Important, Less Likely) (Tenable).

Threat Actor Spotlight

Ransomware / extortion

n0n: three months old, 365 claimed victims

n0n emerged in September 2026 and runs double extortion through a Tor leak site. SOCRadar says it gets in with compromised credentials, exposed cloud access keys and vulnerable remote-access infrastructure, and found evidence of AI coding agents used to build and maintain its tools; ZeroFox rates it financially motivated and opportunistic with low-to-medium sophistication. Ransomnews has recorded 365 victims, including CarNow and Patagonia, with a heavy pharmaceutical focus. This week it listed Visa and JPMorgan Chase (TechRadar).

Breaches & Incidents

Arrests

Ukraine and FBI arrest four in $40.5M ransomware ring

Ukrainian police, working with the FBI, detained four suspected key members of a group tied to at least 28 companies that paid more than $40.5 million; court records name Chaos as the ransomware used, and the crew posed as IT support using AI-generated voice calls. Investigators ran 66 simultaneous searches and seized nearly $370,000 in cash (BleepingComputer, Nov 18).

Patch

Drupal patches critical core RCE (CVE-2026-132165)

Anonymous attackers can abuse install.php to reinstall Drupal under specific conditions, leading to site takeover or RCE; the flaw was found by Anthropic’s OSS Scanner. Drupal warned exploits “might be developed within hours or days”; if you can’t upgrade now, block /core/install.php (BleepingComputer, citing Drupal SA-CORE-2026-017).

Claim

n0n lists Visa and JPMorgan Chase

New JPMorgan said Thursday its vendors were targeted in a separate cyberattack that may have exposed some customer names and emails (Yahoo Finance). Visa confirms a third-party provider incident in which its own systems were not impacted; JPMorgan blamed a software bug for slow service (TechRadar).

Data breach

Uber notifies 149,346 drivers after Fireworks breach

Names, driver’s license numbers, dates of birth and addresses were taken through Uber’s account at a third-party provider, which TechCrunch identified as AI infrastructure firm Fireworks; Uber was notified Oct. 9 and sent letters Nov. 10. The Fireworks breach also hit Cursor and Notion (BleepingComputer).

Cloud breach

Barracuda: AI pentest framework ARTEX used in 44-hour cloud intrusion

An attacker used a cloud access key Barracuda never retired after a vendor project — stolen from the vendor’s GitHub repo — to reach about 10 cloud accounts on Nov. 1–2, moving through analytics, source code and Kubernetes. It reached a store of Barracuda XDR integration credentials; Barracuda found no evidence they were taken but asked affected customers to rotate them (BleepingComputer).

Advisory

CISA urges immediate patching of “Java4Shell” (CVE-2026-114938)

A memory-corruption flaw in OpenJDK’s Base64 decoder can let unauthenticated attackers run code on servers hosting Java apps; Oracle and the OpenJDK project fixed it Tuesday. Oracle rates it 8.1 (CVSS 3.1); finder Calif rates it 9.2 (CVSS 4.0). There is no evidence of successful attacks yet, but CISA “strongly urges” organizations to patch now. Calif says it achieved unauthenticated RCE in default configurations of Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, JFrog Artifactory, Jenkins and PingFederate. VulnCheck says the bug only triggers on CPUs with AVX-512 VBMI; ARM64 is not affected. Corretto, Temurin, Microsoft’s OpenJDK, Zulu, SapMachine, Liberica and Red Hat OpenJDK builds are also affected. CISA’s interim advice: inventory JDKs, validate and size-limit Base64 input and limit unauthenticated access (Help Net Security, citing CISA).

Supply chain

Optimizely customer sites served card skimmer and iOS exploit kits

178 organizations on Nov 16; banks and financial firms were the largest group (32). Salesforce told Reuters it was disabling scripts as a precaution (Help Net Security).

UpdatedClaim

NVIDIA: no sign its systems were hit

NVIDIA says it is investigating an incident that may have affected a third-party partner after The Gentlemen listed it; the gang’s countdown ends Nov. 28 (BleepingComputer).

Espionage

AI-run Cairn ops hit a teachers' union, a chipmaker and a standards body

Brute-forced production credential led to new admin accounts at a semiconductor company; a standards-ballot document was overwritten via an unauthenticated cloud storage location (Help Net Security).

Intrusion

ScreenConnect campaign hits 288 hosts at 48 organizations via RMM+ extension flaw

Huntress says a threat actor compromised at least 6 MSPs and 288 hosts across 48 organizations on Nov. 11, hitting all 288 within 90 minutes by abusing cloud-hosted ScreenConnect panels to deploy rogue clients and a self-replicating toolkit called SCKit. Entry was via RMM+, a third-party extension whose versions 1.0.31 and 1.0.32 accept a blank access key if one was never configured. ConnectWise disabled five RMM+-family extensions across about 400 ScreenConnect environments; Huntress and ConnectWise published IoCs and advise disabling the extensions on on-prem deployments (SecurityWeek, Nov. 12; Huntress).

Ransomware

Maersk confirms data theft; WhiteArmor claims 100GB, demands $5 million

In a Nov. 11 statement Maersk said data was extracted from a system inherited through an earlier acquisition; “our findings so far strongly suggest no sensitive data has been disclosed,” the incident is isolated to a local system, and no customer disruption is expected. WhiteArmor, which listed Maersk on its leak site on Nov. 7, claims about 100GB from a platform tied to LF Logistics, the Hong Kong logistics firm Maersk bought in 2022 — including ~29,000 driver records (16,093 with plaintext passwords) and 16,123 staff accounts — and gave 30 days to pay $5 million in bitcoin. The claims are unverified and Maersk has not confirmed WhiteArmor’s involvement (SecurityWeek, Nov. 12; BleepingComputer also reported the breach Nov. 11).

Ransomware

New “Citrus” ransomware hides C2 as HTTPS, abuses “Certighost” AD CS flaw

Kaspersky documented a previously undocumented group, Citrus, hitting Russian companies via compromised contractors and stolen VPN/RDP credentials, then escalating through CVE-2026-54121 (“Certighost”), an Active Directory Certificate Services flaw Microsoft patched July 14, 2026, that can yield certificates for domain controllers. It tunnels traffic over port 443 with Xray VLESS/REALITY disguised as Yandex requests, uses LLM-generated scripts, and deploys the Go-based Sn00py payload (.citrus extension). Defenders: patch every Enterprise CA for CVE-2026-54121 and enable AD CS auditing (GBHackers, Nov. 12, citing Kaspersky).

Ransomware

Synology NAS boxes hit by C3RB3R ransomware via old, patched DSM flaws

Synology is urging customers to update DiskStation Manager after attackers exploited a vulnerability patched in December 2024 to encrypt and extort devices running outdated DSM; it did not name the exact flaw but linked an advisory covering CVE-2024-10441 (CVSS 9.8, system plugin daemon RCE), CVE-2024-50629 and CVE-2024-10445 — fixed in DSM 7.2.2-72806-1, 7.2.1-69057-6, 7.2-64570-4, 7.1.1-42962-7 and 6.2.4-25556-8 or later. Users report files locked with a “.L0CK3D” extension; Qihoo 360 says C3RB3R has also exploited two DSM flaws Synology fixed in September 2026, CVE-2026-13684 and CVE-2026-13639 (both CVSS 9.8) (The Hacker News, Ravie Lakshmanan, Nov. 3).

Data breach

Railway: stolen customer AI keys ran up ~$740,000 in unauthorized charges

AI-provider keys that customers stored on platform-as-a-service provider Railway were stolen in an October breach and used for an estimated $740,000 in unauthorized spending, the company said; it has now promised to pay those charges itself when an AI provider will not refund them, reversing an Oct. 26 update that said it was unable to refund such spend. The attacker decrypted environment variables in at most 1.85% of workspaces (about 100,000), its database queries looked for AI-provider keys, and the decrypted data also included linked-account tokens for 1,852 users (The Hacker News, Nov. 11). Rotate any provider keys stored as Railway variables.

Zero-day

Zyxel GS1900 “fixed” switches still open — Patch 3 closes zero-day CVE-2026-107441

Binarly found that Zyxel’s June 2.90 Patch 2 firmware for CVE-2026-7273 does not block the exploit attackers used: the new 2.90 Patch 3 build fixes a separately tracked out-of-bounds write, CVE-2026-107441, that lets anyone reaching a switch’s web management interface run commands without logging in. GreyNoise had tied data theft from 996 GS1900 switches around Aug. 17 to CVE-2026-7273; 564 still used factory-default credentials. Zyxel lists 10 affected models and no workaround; the vendor’s CVE entry rates it 8.8 (Binarly says 9.8), and CVE-2026-107441 is not in CISA’s KEV catalog (The Hacker News, Nov. 5).

Exploited

Critical Xen Orchestra auth bypass exploited for admin access

Vates VMS warned that a critical authentication-bypass flaw in Xen Orchestra, tracked as VSA-2026-062 and not yet assigned a CVE, is being actively exploited: a crafted REST cookie is parsed into an empty object that the token lookup treats as unfiltered, authenticating the attacker as the owner of the first unexpired token and enabling arbitrary actions with Admin privileges — ultimately code execution on managed pools (The Hacker News, Nov. 10).

Exploited

Attackers hit AhsayCBS backup servers, hide malware in PNG images

Huntress investigated compromises of AhsayCBS backup servers via CVE-2026-105133, an authentication bypass, and CVE-2026-105134, unauthenticated remote code execution with SYSTEM privileges; it found five webshell families, a second cryptocurrency miner and a host-bound Python implant, but could not confirm every payload or whether backup data was stolen. Ahsay released hotfix 10.3.4.45 for version 10 and 9.15.2.70 for versions 7 through 9; Huntress recommends rebuilding from trusted backups and rotating reachable credentials (Cyber Security News, citing Huntress, Nov. 11).

Zero-day

Chrome patches eighth exploited zero-day of 2026

Google fixed CVE-2026-125350, a high-severity use-after-free in the XML component with an exploit in the wild; update to 157.0.8103.50/.51 on Windows/macOS and 157.0.8103.50 on Linux (The Hacker News, desk record).

Cloud breach

Attacker abuses Qubole’s trusted AWS access to raid customer accounts

An attacker broke into AWS accounts belonging to multiple Qubole customers between Oct. 14 and Oct. 26 and stole stored secrets and data, Wiz said. The attacker used credentials for the AWS account Qubole’s platform uses to reach customers, assuming IAM roles whose trust policies still trusted Qubole’s account — most of them otherwise unused; how the credentials were obtained has not been disclosed. Qubole is a data-lake platform owned by Idera (The Hacker News, Nov. 9). Audit and remove IAM roles that trust third-party accounts you no longer use.

Ransomware

BYOD gang claims Texas HHSC breach of ~2 million residents’ records

The BYOD ransomware gang claims on its leak site to have stolen records belonging to about two million Texans from the Texas Health and Human Services Commission, including Medicaid, SNAP, TANF, disaster-assistance and banking records, and says a vulnerability in the agency’s system let it in. The gang has not released data samples, its leak site was down at the time of writing, and HHSC had not commented — the claim is unverified (Cybernews, Nov. 10).

Ransomware

Vanderbilt begins restoring systems after BYOD attack

A ransomware attack discovered Monday appears to have compromised large amounts of student and employee personal data. As of Tuesday morning the university said campus Wi-Fi, Okta, Outlook, Brightspace, Zoom and YES were safe to use again and users could resume using Vanderbilt devices, while others such as CampusPrint remain restricted; it is working with government agencies. BYOD claimed the attack on its leak blog and claims to hold more than one million Social Security numbers — not confirmed by the university (CyberScoop, Nov. 10).

Supply chain

Ethersphere’s bee-js and Swarm Desktop releases trojanized

Attackers used the project’s own release automation to publish malicious bee-js 13.3.0 and Swarm Desktop 0.56.2, which steal the Swarm node wallet key and its stored password and install a backdoor on Windows, macOS and Linux. Both releases were pulled and a clean Swarm Desktop update shipped, but that does not remove an installed backdoor or protect a key already sent; no theft of funds had been reported (The Hacker News, Nov. 9).

Exploited

Node-RED missing-auth flaw exploited to drop Linux bots and webshells

VulnCheck observed in-the-wild exploitation of CVE-2026-58115 on Oct. 30 and 15 more attacks from the same IP by Nov. 1, deploying a Linux bot and persistent webshells via a single HTTP request against Node-RED instances without adminAuth. Siemens disclosed the bug in August in SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced devices; version 4.3.4.1 and later contain the fix. VulnCheck: check whether Node-RED is reachable from outside your network today and review flows for unexpected exec nodes (SecurityWeek, Nov. 3).

Patch Day

SAP fixes four CVSS 10.0 flaws in NetWeaver and Web Dispatcher

SAP’s November Security Patch Day fixed four flaws attackers could exploit without logging in, all rated 10.0 by the vendor: memory-corruption bugs in the Internet Communication Manager (CVE-2026-76964) and Web Dispatcher (CVE-2026-89126) that could allow code execution, a SAML 2.0 service-provider flaw (CVE-2026-120017) that could let an attacker log in as any user on some SAML single-sign-on setups, and an SAP Gateway access-control flaw (CVE-2026-89103). None requires user action; SAP strongly recommends applying the notes as a priority (The Hacker News).

Exploited

CISA flags exploited Aspera Faspex, FreeSwitch, GPUStack flaws — all due Nov. 12

IBM Aspera Faspex CVE-2026-123218 (CVSS 9.8) is an authorization bypass letting authenticated users gain full admin rights; SignalWire FreeSwitch CVE-2026-116779 (9.8) is a code injection via inbound SIP MESSAGE chat handling. CISA also warned of exploitation of GPUStack auth bypass CVE-2026-102895 (9.8) and OpenSSH SSHole (SecurityWeek). eSecurity Planet confirms all four entries carry a Nov. 12 federal deadline under BOD 26-04, which also requires forensic triage; GPUStack before 2.2.0 can treat remote requests as trusted local traffic, letting attackers create admin API keys — and upgrading does not revoke keys already created, so review and rotate them (eSecurity Planet).

ICS / OT

France blames Iran for refrigeration sabotage

Last week a cyberattack on several French blood banks tried to raise storage temperatures but was thwarted, and supermarkets in Sweden, Belgium, Luxembourg, Ireland and Estonia reported fridge and freezer problems apparently caused by cyberattacks (AP via SecurityWeek). See Threat Actor Spotlight.

Exploited

QNAP: NAS devices breached via Plex2Shell

QNAP says some Plex Media Server installs on its NAS devices were compromised through four Plex flaws (CVE-2026-96652, -96654, -96655, -96656); update to Plex 1.43.3.10861, treat exposed devices as compromised and stop forwarding port 32400 if remote access isn’t needed (BleepingComputer, Nov. 6).

Crypto / supply chain

Web3Auth flaw used to drain Keplr wallets

An attacker exploited Web3Auth infrastructure to steal crypto from some Keplr wallets created with Google or Apple sign-in; Bitquery counted at least $6.01 million moved from more than 44,000 wallets into recovery wallets, and GoPlus estimates at least about $1 million was stolen (The Hacker News, Nov. 9).

Ransomware

Hawkmoth (Anubis) plants new Lindnor backdoor

Symantec says the Anubis RaaS operator it tracks as Hawkmoth planted a custom backdoor, Lindnor, on two Microsoft SQL Servers at a U.S. organization in October; the encryptor was blocked (GBHackers, citing Symantec).
