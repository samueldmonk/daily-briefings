The Lead

The Fed hiked for the first time in three years, and the Dow took the damage

The closing print has been restated again by independent sources this run and it has not moved. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 631.21 points, or 1.21%, to close at 51,461.90, with Goldman Sachs leading the index lower. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,551.81. The Nasdaq Composite finished down 0.01% at 25,978.42 — effectively unchanged.

The spread between those three numbers is the session. A 1.21% drop in the Dow against a flat Nasdaq is not a market selling risk; it is a market selling rate sensitivity. The Federal Open Market Committee voted 12–0 to raise the target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% — the first increase in three years — and the losses landed on the rate-sensitive end of the tape.

What repriced the afternoon was the guidance rather than the hike. Chair Kevin Warsh, at his 2:30 p.m. ET press conference, said inflation has been “too high … for too long” and that the Committee “must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to our objective clearly and at sufficient speed.” The updated projections showed 16 of the 18 participants see another hike as possible before the end of 2026; for 2027 the committee split eight for a further hike, six for holding and four for cuts. Long-dated yields rose with it, and the 10-year Treasury yield topped 5% again.

A note on the round numbers in circulation: CNBC’s live blog is headlined “Dow falls 700 points” and Yahoo Finance’s “Dow sinks 600 points”. Both are rounded restatements of the same slide at different points in it. The settled figure is −631.21, and that is what this page leads with. Arithmetic check: 52,093.11 − 631.21 = 51,461.90.
