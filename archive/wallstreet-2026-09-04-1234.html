The Lead

The split closes into a sell-off: Dow −382, and Trump tells the Fed to cut or he stops trading

THE TAPE TURNED WHILE THIS EDITION WAS BEING COMPILED. The morning’s story was a three-way split — Dow down, Nasdaq up. It is not that any more. A later reading has the Dow down 382 points, or 0.7%, the S&P 500 down 0.5% and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.4%: all three negative, and the Nasdaq flipped from +0.12% at ~9:56 AM to −0.4%. That reading carries no clock time, so it is not given one here — but it is demonstrably later than the 9:56 strip, because TheStreet’s own live blog re-headlined itself from “S&P 500 edges lower after key jobs report” to “Dow falls 300 points after…” between the two fetches this run. The driver is unchanged: traders are raising bets that the Fed hikes this month.

The other new thing this morning is political. Reacting to the much-stronger-than-expected August jobs report, President Donald Trump demanded on Truth Social that the Federal Reserve cut rates, and threatened to cut off trade if it does not. In his own words: “We should have the LOWEST RATE of any country in the World … LOWER THE RATE OR I’LL STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE HAVE A DEFICIT, which the U.S. Supreme Court, in its ridiculous and very costly Tariff decision, strongly acknowledged ‘the President’ has an absolute right to do. IT’S BETTER THAN TARIFFS! The Fed Board, with its great new leader, must get smart – BE PATRIOTS for a change.” He added that “High interest rates put the U.S.A. at a very unfair disadvantage, and I won’t allow that to happen!” The post names the chairman, Kevin Warsh, and tells the Fed to “get smart” and cut.

The demand runs straight into the chair’s own words. One week before the post, at Jackson Hole on August 28, Warsh told the symposium the Fed had “work to do” if above-target inflation persists — remarks widely read as opening the door to rate increases rather than cuts. Trump is pressing for cuts from a chair the market reads as leaning the other way.

Underneath it, the jobs number is unchanged. The Labor Department reported 162,000 jobs added in August, decisively beating consensus; the unemployment rate was unchanged at 4.1%. Stocks did not rally on it. “One of the head-scratching terms that come out of Wall Street is that ‘Good news is bad news,’ and that’s the case this morning,” said Chris Zaccarelli, chief investment officer for Northlight Asset Management, “where a great Jobs report – 162k jobs created vs 55k expected – had an instant reaction of stocks and bonds selling off.”

The consensus figure is still a four-way split and is still not merged. 53,000 (CNBC, citing economists polled by Dow Jones), 55,000 (TheStreet and the Yahoo article text), 56,000 (a Reuters poll) and 58,000 (Yahoo’s own economic-data calendar). Every source agrees the print beat it decisively; they do not agree on what “it” was. 162,000 and 4.1% are common to all of them.

Two timestamped index moments, and two that carry no time. At the 9:35 AM ET opening bell (TheStreet): S&P 500 −0.05%, Dow −0.16%, Nasdaq +0.09%, Russell 2000 −1.40%. At ~9:56 AM ET — a Yahoo Finance market strip that self-dates through its own “U.S. markets close in 6h 4m” counter — S&P 500 7,741.86 −5.85 / −0.08%; Dow 53,509.14 −176.97 / −0.33%; Nasdaq Composite 26,615.08 +31.02 / +0.12%; Russell 2000 2,954.93 −13.34 / −0.45%; VIX 14.13 −0.19 / −1.33%. A third reading — S&P 7,739.40 −8.31 / −0.11%, Dow 53,512.64 −173.47 / −0.32%, Nasdaq 26,600.53 +16.47 / +0.06% — is internally consistent and reconciles to Thursday’s closes, but carries no timestamp, so it is logged as a further undated moment and not dated. It is not the same set as the Dow −382 reading above, which is later and percentage-only.

The Fed is still the whole argument. “The August jobs report was much better than expected, focusing the Fed squarely on controlling inflation when they meet next in September,” said Bill Adams, chief U.S. economist at Fifth Third Commercial Bank. “The next Fed decision will be finely balanced: Next week’s releases of the CPI and PPI reports have the power to decide whether the Fed hikes or holds.” Pricing for a September hike sits at roughly 50% after Governor Christopher Waller’s Thursday remarks, per Kyle Rodda of Capital.com. The FOMC meets September 15–16. Note the direction of travel: Rodda’s “back to roughly 50%” describes pricing falling to a coin flip on Waller’s dovish remarks Thursday; the later reading behind today’s sell-off has traders raising hike bets again on the payrolls beat. No post-print probability figure was returned this run, so none is published.

One more session, then a long weekend. U.S. stock and bond markets are closed Monday, September 7 for Labor Day.

NewDownSpace

SpaceX slips as four U.S. space firms skip Macron’s summit

Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX) slipped 0.97% to $148.52 in early trading (TheStreet, 10:16 AM ET). SpaceX was one of four U.S. space companies to say it would skip French President Emmanuel Macron’s space summit in Paris next week after coming under pressure from the Trump administration; Blue Origin, rocket maker Stoke Space and Starcloud notified French authorities Thursday that they were “canceling their participation.” A French official: “It is hard not to draw a connection to the rumors of pressure that have been circulating in the press.” SpaceX debuted at $135 on June 12 and hit a record $225.64 on June 16. Corroborated: a second, independent quote this run has SPCX at $148.69, −1.05 / −0.70% — same direction, level within about twenty cents.
