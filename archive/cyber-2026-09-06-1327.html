Top Story

MikroTrick — six RouterOS flaws, two of them a full takeover chain

CERT Polska published on 5 September 2026 that it identified and coordinated disclosure of six vulnerabilities in MikroTik RouterOS. Combining two of them — a chain the team named MikroTrick — allows an attacker to take full control of the device without authentication, if the device accepts remote access over SSH. CERT Polska states it has confirmation that attackers are exploiting this combination in the wild against RouterOS devices reachable from the internet, and that the released patches prevent the observed attacks.

The vulnerabilities touch the SSH server and client, the bandwidth-test service, X.509 certificate handling and WebFig. MikroTik’s own bulletin, dated 3 September 2026, confirms fixes in 7.25 beta 3, 7.24.2, 7.23.4 and 6.49.21 and says explicitly that, to give administrators time to update, “we are not currently publishing detailed information.” CERT Polska notes MikroTik also sent a push notification to users of its mobile app — for the first time in its history.

Indicators of compromise, per CERT Polska: log lines reading login failure for user -2 from <ip> via ssh and user <name> added by ssh:-2@<ip>, and the presence of a highly privileged user named ops. Successful attacks observed so far, including creation of the “ops” account, originated from 82.192.72.4 and have been occurring since at least 2 September; 103.102.31.18 was used in further attempts. The patched builds set a “Flagged” marker when they detect known traces of tampering — but CERT Polska is emphatic that absence of the marker is not proof the device is safe.

One further detail worth recording: CERT Polska says the flaws were found with LLM assistance — GPT-5.5-cyber and GPT-5.6-sol, inside an agent-based research environment, under the team’s access to the OpenAI Government and Trust Agency Collaboration programme — while stressing that every hypothesis still required confirmation on real hardware and that the models “did not replace” the verification stages.

Patch Priority

Today’s single most urgent action

Update MikroTik RouterOS now to 7.25 beta 3, 7.24.2, 7.23.4 or 6.49.21 — then check the logs for the compromise message, read the flagged marker via /system/device-mode/print, and audit for unknown users, scripts and scheduler tasks. If patching has to wait, CERT Polska’s interim advice is to block SSH, WWW/WWW-SSL and the bandwidth-test server from everything outside trusted management networks.

Why this box is red: this desk’s rule is that the callout goes red only when a deadline is today or overdue, or when a maximum-severity flaw is under active exploitation. No federal deadline attaches to the MikroTik chain — it is not in the KEV entries this run could verify, and no due date is asserted for it here. The red is earned on the severity branch instead: CERT Polska confirms an unauthenticated full-device-takeover chain being exploited in the wild right now.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Rhysida

Active since May 2023, Rhysida runs double extortion against government, healthcare and education targets, typically exfiltrating quietly and then posting the victim to a leak site with a countdown once remediation is already under way. Reporting returned this run lists the British Library, the Chilean Army, healthcare delivery organisations and Holding Slovenske Elektrarne among its earlier victims.

Attribution remains open. Coverage this run says it is unclear where the group is based; it notes that Cynet observed in 2023 that Rhysida’s ransomware, ransom notes and leak site sometimes contained snippets of Russian and that the group conspicuously avoided targets in Russia and other post-Soviet states. That is a hint, not an attribution, and this page does not treat it as one.

Berlin, 2026: the attack was detected 14 August, at which point all Berlin Senate departments were disconnected from the central network. Forensic investigators traced exfiltration to a window of 7–12 August — systems were not isolated until the 14th, a gap reporting has focused on. The demand is 30 bitcoin for 5.79 TB. Berlin has said it will not pay, and the State Criminal Police Office, the public prosecutor’s office and federal security agencies are investigating together.

Breaches & Incidents

NewRansomwareGovernment

Berlin city administration confirms theft after Rhysida claim

Berlin confirmed data theft and an extortion attempt after Rhysida listed the city on its leak site. The actor claims 5.79 TB across roughly 1.44 million files: government, legal, financial, contractual, HR, infrastructure, health and mapping records; thousands of names, emails and phone numbers plus 148 IBANs; plaintext credentials, database accounts, payment-system data, password vaults and credentials of senior officials; personnel files, payroll, administrative-offence records, email archives and SQL dumps; allegedly classified material including Bundesrat committee records; critical-infrastructure security assessments concerning Berlin’s water supply; and more than 3,200 documents marked as NDAs. The city says it will not be blackmailed.

NewHealthcareThird party

Aesto Health breach affects more than 9.5 million patients

Aesto LLC, trading as Aesto Health, provides SaaS that healthcare organisations use to migrate, archive and access patient data when replacing EHR systems or acquiring practices. The intrusion occurred in December 2025 and was only disclosed recently. Data involved includes full names, dates of birth, medical information, driver’s licence numbers, financial account numbers, health insurance information, ITINs, other government identification numbers and Social Security numbers. It indirectly touches 29 healthcare providers, among them VillageMD, Everside Health (Marathon Health), Marana Health and Together Women’s Health.

HealthcareExtortion

McKesson confirms incident; ShinyHunters’ 284M figure stays a claim

The distribution giant disclosed a cybersecurity incident involving unauthorised access to third-party applications and data theft, discovered 25 August 2026. ShinyHunters claims 284 million patient records covering names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, patient IDs, Medicaid numbers, medical record numbers, medication and allergy information, prescriptions and shipments, invoices, Salesforce records and internal communications. 284 million is the attackers’ figure, not a company-confirmed one, and the investigation is ongoing.

NewTransportExtortion

FulcrumSec claims 86 GB from Manchester Airports Group

Following MAG’s disclosure of a breach affecting customers at Manchester, London Stansted and East Midlands, FulcrumSec claims 86 GB. Reporting describes consolidated profiles combining customer identifiers with historical booking activity and marketing classifications, plus nearly 200,000 records relating to upcoming travel during the remainder of 2026. Sampled records went well beyond what MAG initially described — booking references, product selections, prices, discounts, parking dates and times, historical spending, IP addresses, approximate locations and device information. BleepingComputer says it validated one traveller’s record.

NewEmployee data

Hasbro discloses employee data breach

The toy and games company says attackers accessed personal and financial information of an undisclosed number of employees — potentially email, address, phone number, national ID number or financial information. For 436 Hasbro employees in Massachusetts, Social Security numbers, financial account information, credit and debit card numbers and driver’s licence information were affected. The incident likely dates to around 28 March, when Hasbro disclosed a cyberattack and temporarily took systems offline.

HealthcareIdentity

Novocure: 1,400+ cancer patients, and an FBI probe into a 153M-licence service

Oncology company Novocure says a mid-August attack exposed records of more than 1,400 U.S. cancer patients — ID numbers without names for most, with identifying and provider contact information for fewer than 50 patients in the western U.S. — plus contact details for an undisclosed number of employees. Separately, Brian Krebs reported this week on a service called Nexus claiming more than 153 million driver’s licences for the U.S. and Canada, 10 million-plus ID cards, 3 million-plus travel or international IDs and at least 579,000 medical cards, with the source apparently tracing back to age-verification firm IDScan.net. These are the claims as reported; the FBI is probing.

Version note — a discrepancy this desk will not resolve

Reporting on the ASUS bulletin says ACC versions through 4.0.0.2 are affected and that ASUS advises updating to version 3.1.0.9 or later. Those two statements cannot both be read the ordinary way — 3.1.0.9 is a lower version than 4.0.0.2. The primary ASUS advisory did not load on fetch this run, so both figures are printed exactly as reported and neither is asserted as the fixed build. Administrators should take the version from ASUS directly. The interim mitigations are consistent across returns: isolate ACC management interfaces from public networks, restrict traffic on TCP port 2222, and audit hosts for unexpected SSH listeners.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • 4 September — CVE-2026-85046 (Google Chromium V8, type confusion, CVSS 8.8), the day’s single addition. Reporting on the entry gives a remediation due date of 18 September 2026 (a Friday) — 12 days left. This is published attributed to reporting, not asserted: the CISA alert page itself returned empty on fetch again this run, as it has on every attempt for four consecutive runs.
  • 2 September — seven additions: CVE-2026-9586 (Sangoma Switchvox, SQLi), CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette, request smuggling), CVE-2026-49869 (Kestra OSS, command injection), CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM, improper authentication), CVE-2026-82329 (JFrog Artifactory, improper authentication), and CVE-2026-83548 + CVE-2026-83549 (SonicWall SMA1000). No due date is asserted for this batch. Reporting has circulated a 5 September date for the SonicWall pair; with the CISA page unreadable this desk declines to publish it, and the Patch Priority box above says the same thing in the same terms.
  • 31 August — two additions, both PaperCut: CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078.
  • 11 August — three additions: CVE-2026-20349 (Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD), CVE-2026-68820 (Windows use-after-free), CVE-2026-72898 (Metabase SQLi).
  • On the deadline model itself: the uniform three-week window under BOD 22-01 has been superseded by BOD 26-04, a risk-based regime under which a vulnerability meeting all four high-risk criteria — publicly exposed, in KEV, automatable, granting total control — must be mitigated in no more than three days. That is why per-CVE windows now vary, and it is why this page does not extrapolate a due date for any entry it could not read.
