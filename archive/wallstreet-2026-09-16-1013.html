The Lead

Fed decision day: stocks are higher after the open, with a quarter-point hike priced for 2 p.m. ET

As of the late morning, with the market open, stocks were climbing as Wall Street waited on the Federal Reserve’s decision, due at 2 p.m. ET; TheStreet’s live blog for the session is headlined "Dow edges higher ahead of Fed decision." Futures had edged higher before the open. Futures markets were pricing a quarter-point increase with 92.5% odds on CME FedWatch as of TheStreet’s 8:50 AM ET update. Kiplinger reports that a hike of that size would put the target range at 3.75% to 4.00%, and would be the first increase since July 2023. The meeting also brings the Summary of Economic Projections and the dot plot, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference.

The hike itself is not really the question. "The reaction will depend on the commentary offered by Fed Chair Kevin Warsh," said TheStreet Pro contributor James "Rev Shark" DePorre. "Investors want to know whether this is the start of a series of hikes or just fast, preemptive action that ends here… A hike paired with reassuring language could actually calm the bond market. A hike with hawkish or vague commentary could push yields higher and take stocks down with them."

The set-up is lopsided. In Jon Hilsenrath’s Duke survey of former Fed officials and staff, 29 of 32 respondents said the Fed should raise this week; one said hold, two did not answer. Deutsche Bank strategists calculate that a decision to hold "would be the biggest dovish surprise at a scheduled FOMC meeting on record," going back to 1994.

Tuesday gave the Fed a market already under pressure. All three indexes closed lower for a second straight session: the S&P 500 −0.45% to 7,585.73, the Dow −0.63% (−328.09) to 52,093.11, and the Nasdaq Composite −0.78% to 25,981.57. Breadth was worse than the headline: DePorre put Tuesday at just 32% positive, with new 12-month lows expanding past 350 names against 100 new highs.

On the Radar

  • 2:00 p.m. ET — FOMC decision, plus the Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot; Chair Warsh’s press conference follows (typically 2:30 p.m., though Kiplinger notes this could change under Warsh).
  • The dot plot is the real event. June’s dots implied one quarter-point hike in 2026; futures traders are now pricing two by year end. Johnson Investment Counsel’s Brandon Zureick expects the bond market is pricing one more hike this year and one to two more in 2027.
  • Remaining 2026 FOMC meetings: 27–28 October and 8–9 December. Kiplinger’s David Payne argues the October meeting sits too close to Election Day for comfort, making September and December the politically safer moves.
  • Oil supply: Macquarie estimates crude, condensate and product flows through the Strait of Hormuz have stayed resilient and may have risen above 7.5 million barrels a day since fighting resumed on 30 August. Saudi Arabia’s Red Sea pipeline remains damaged after drone attacks.
  • OpenAI is in early talks with investors about a round valuing it at more than $1.2 trillion ahead of an IPO, Bloomberg reports; the talks were initiated by investors. For scale: Anthropic raised in May at a $965 billion valuation, and SpaceX raised a record $86.3 billion in its June offering.
  • Starship’s 14th test flight is targeted for as soon as 22 September, pending regulatory approval.
