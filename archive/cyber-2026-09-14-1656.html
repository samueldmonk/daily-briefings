Top Story

Patch Tuesday · 8–14 September 2026

Microsoft’s biggest patch day ever carries two zero-days already being exploited

Microsoft’s September 2026 Patch Tuesday is a record. Counts differ by how vendors tally: 966 flaws (BleepingComputer, Windows Report), 964 CVEs (Tenable, Malwarebytes) and 974 (SecurityWeek, The Hacker News). Tenable breaks its 964 into 104 rated critical and 860 important.

Two of them were being exploited before the fix shipped. CVE-2026-81963 is an elevation-of-privilege bug in the Windows Update Stack — an improper link-resolution (“link following”) defect that yields SYSTEM. Seven Windows Update Stack EoP flaws have been patched since 2022; this is the first exploited as a zero-day. CVE-2026-85880 is a heap-based buffer overflow in Windows Advanced Local Procedure Call (ALPC), also local-to-SYSTEM — the first ALPC bug in a Patch Tuesday since April 2023. Neither grants remote access on its own, but SYSTEM is exactly what an intruder who already has a foothold from phishing or stolen credentials wants next. Both are on CISA’s exploited-vulnerabilities catalog, added 8 September.

Separately, Microsoft has confirmed that the September updates cause Remote Desktop Services failures on Windows Server systems — a detail worth knowing before a rushed weekend rollout.

Patch Priority

Do this first · deadline todayGitLab CVE-2026-85706 — CVSS 10.0 — self-managed GitLab CE/EE. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 September and set a Federal Civilian Executive Branch remediation date of 14 September 2026 — today, flagging it for forensic triage under Binding Operational Directive 26-04. It is an unauthenticated path traversal in the repository commits API: one HTTP request reads arbitrary files, which in practice means logs, configuration, credentials, access tokens, CI/CD secrets and SSH keys. GitLab shipped fixes on 10 September; watchTowr saw probing early on 11 September, inside 24 hours of the patch. Fixed in 19.1.8, 19.2.6 and 19.3.2 (versions from 18.7 up are affected). GitLab.com is already patched and Dedicated customers need take no action. Patching is not sufficient on its own — rotate every secret the flaw could have exposed.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ShinyHunters

The extortion crew that took Florida’s driver database through one police officer’s phone.

ShinyHunters claimed a breach of DAVID, the Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles platform for driver records, and said it took more than 200,000 records. The group told BleepingComputer it got in via a password-reset flaw that let it compromise multiple accounts, allegedly including DMV staff and an FBI agent, with access beginning 3 September; as proof it shared a screenshot of a DAVID record for Jeffrey Epstein containing personal and vehicle information. FLHSMV’s own account, confirmed 11 September, is narrower: the intrusion used login credentials belonging to a single Plant City Police Department employee that had been improperly stored on the officer’s personal device. The actors say they have since lost access and that the password-reset flaw is being patched. Both versions are printed here because the two accounts of the entry point do not agree.

Breaches & Incidents

GovernmentConfirmed

Florida DMV confirms DAVID database breach

FLHSMV confirmed on 11 September that its DAVID driver database was accessed using credentials belonging to one Plant City Police Department employee, improperly stored on a personal device. ShinyHunters claims more than 200,000 driver records.

FintechExtortion

Revolut handed customer data to a government impostor

An unauthorised party submitted fraudulent information requests from a legitimate government agency email domain; because the mail carried valid domain authentication, Revolut fulfilled them. Exposed: names, dates of birth, postal and email addresses, phone numbers and copies of passports and driver’s licences, possibly also verification selfies, account statements and transaction histories. Funds were not affected. A threat actor is now demanding payment not to publish more. Revolut has not named the agency or the domain.

NewSupply chain

Malicious Twitch extension leaked ~31,000 OAuth tokens

“Twitch Enhanced Viewer | JeetBot”, developer HISHIMIRO/jeetbot.cc, was live on both the Chrome Web Store and Firefox Add-ons when Socket published on 11 September — roughly 30,000 Chrome users and 552 on Firefox. It routes Twitch playlist requests through JeetBot proxies and the user’s OAuth token rides along as a URL query parameter, in cleartext, into the proxy’s logs. Those tokens allow account actions without the password or 2FA. For ten hardcoded Russian-language channels it proxies traffic with no token attached at all — it never needed the token to work.

NewAvailability

September updates break Remote Desktop Services

Microsoft has confirmed reports that its September 2026 security updates cause Remote Desktop Services failures on Windows Server systems — a live consideration for anyone racing the KEV clock on the same servers.
