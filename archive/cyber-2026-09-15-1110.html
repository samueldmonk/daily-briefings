Top Story

AI-orchestrated campaign · GreyNoise

Hundreds of AI agents ran a global intrusion campaign — and ignored the operator’s own no-go list

GreyNoise has published “Agents Gone Wild”, an account of a campaign in which a likely Russian-speaking actor pointed hundreds of AI agents — built on OpenAI’s Codex harness and a DeepSeek model, alongside commodity offensive tooling — at two flaws in PaperCut NG/MF, the print-management software. The agents were tasked with building, testing and refining exploits for CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078, chaining an authentication bypass to remote code execution. The operator first stood up a private lab with a vulnerable PaperCut copy and an Active Directory server to develop against.

The numbers are the story. At least 440 PaperCut instances across 395 identified victim organisations in 48 countries were compromised. From the campaign’s start on 31 August, the actor reached remote code execution against a real victim in under four hours and domain admin two hours after that; once the campaign was running at scale, at least eleven organisations were compromised in 26 seconds. At one US high school, initial access to domain admin took seven minutes. Credentials were harvested from 280 victims, operating-system or domain secrets from 147, and administrator privileges obtained at 12. Education was the worst-hit sector by a wide margin, with 204 victims — which GreyNoise attributes to PaperCut’s customer base rather than deliberate targeting.

The finding that gives the write-up its name is a control failure, not a capability one. The actor instructed the agents to avoid targets in 28 countries, among them Russia, China, Iran, Belarus and Ukraine. GreyNoise nevertheless observed victims in some of the countries on that list. Its conclusion is worth quoting in substance: if autonomous offensive agents cannot reliably honour the constraints their own operator sets, then defenders cannot treat absence from a threat actor’s target list as protection. PaperCut has since replaced its emergency patches with full fixes for both flaws.

Patch Priority

Do this first

CVE-2026-76461 — Cisco Secure Email Gateway (AsyncOS), CVSS 9.8. Unauthenticated root command execution via a crafted email, exploited in the wild as a zero-day. CISA added it to KEV on 14 September with a federal remediation deadline of 17 September 2026 — 2 days left. Treat any internet-reachable appliance as suspect rather than merely unpatched: Cisco’s own remediation for virtual appliances is a rebuild on a fixed release plus full credential and cryptographic-key rotation, because a root-level attacker can remove the indicators of compromise Cisco published.

Second in line, and cheaper: PaperCut NG/MF. Full vendor fixes for CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 have now replaced the emergency patches, and the campaign above is evidence of exploitation at scale rather than a theoretical risk.

Threat Actor Spotlight

China-linkedEspionage

UNC3569 — one click, one backdoor

Gen Threat Labs found CVE-2026-51990 while investigating a live intrusion by UNC3569, a group Google Threat Intelligence ties to China and places in the country’s hacker-for-hire scene, targeting government, education, technology and finance mostly across East and Southeast Asia. The flaw is in Tencent’s Sogou Input Method for Windows — an IME with hundreds of millions of installations — and chains three weaknesses (unvalidated command-line argument injection, unrestricted URL navigation, and an outdated un-sandboxed Chromium engine) so that a single crafted link silently opens a backdoor, with no user interaction beyond the click. The payload is GRAYRABBIT, a small implant the group has used for years: remote shell, two-way file transfer, and further modules pulled from the attacker’s server on demand. Fixed in Sogou 16.3.0.3498.

Breaches & Incidents

NewEducation

Mathspace: more than a million people, through a self-hosted reporting tool

Mathspace, an education platform used across Australia and New Zealand, has disclosed a breach affecting more than 1 million people. The attackers exploited CVE-2026-72898 in Metabase, the self-hosted analytics tool, to reach an internal reporting database. Exposed data covers names, email addresses, usernames and locations; passwords and academic records were not affected. The shape of it is familiar — the crown jewels were not in the application the organisation thought of as its product, but in the reporting layer bolted alongside it.

Identity

IDScan.net — an identity-verification provider loses identity documents

IDScan.net, a US identity-verification provider, disclosed a breach after detecting unauthorised access on 1 September. Exposed data includes names and government identification numbers, and a criminal marketplace has advertised a collection said to contain millions of identity documents. A verification vendor is a concentration point by construction: its customers hand it exactly the material that cannot be rotated after a breach.

Government

Japan’s Digital Agency: ~246,000 people, and the patch was known

Unauthorised access to servers at Japan’s Digital Agency may have leaked personal data on about 246,000 people — roughly 189,000 government employees and 57,000 private-sector workers engaged in government duties. Addresses and phone numbers were included; My Number identification numbers and bank-account details were not. The intrusion hit the Government Solution Service via an account belonging to a maintenance and operations contractor, and investigators traced the entry point to a VPN device vulnerability the agency knew about but had not yet patched. SecurityWeek headlines the same breach at 240,000; both figures are in circulation and neither has been withdrawn.

Supply chain

Ransomware crews keep working the mid-market

CYFIRMA’s September reporting lists a run of disclosed victims with no household names among them: Hansler Smith Limited, Teletek Structures Inc. and MedEvolve (a US medical-billing company) claimed by the Settra group; Petrocare Construction and Star Aviation, Inc. claimed by Storm; and Brazilian adhesive manufacturer Engefitas hit by Vexy. The pattern across all of them is extortion-by-leak-threat with negotiations unresolved at time of listing.

  • CVE-2026-85706 — GitLab CE/EE path traversal, CVSS 10.0. Added 11 September, due 14 September — overdue by 1 day. Flagged for mandatory forensic triage under BOD 26-04.
  • CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060 — MikroTik RouterOS. Added 10 September, due 13 September — overdue by 2 days. CISA flagged 86060 as requiring forensic triage.
  • CVE-2025-25249 (Fortinet FortiOS / FortiSwitchManager / FortiSASE, CVSS 7.3), CVE-2026-19490 (Citrix NetScaler), CVE-2026-87491 (Chromium V8 out-of-bounds write, fixed in Chrome 153.0.8010.36) and CVE-2026-20079 (Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center) — added 9 September, due 12 September — overdue by 3 days.
  • The 2 September batch of seven — including CVE-2026-82329 (JFrog Artifactory) and CVE-2026-83548 / CVE-2026-83549 (SonicWall SMA1000) — carried deadlines that have all passed.
Countdowns are computed from today, 15 September 2026, against the date CISA states for each CVE. Deadlines are no longer three weeks from the add date: BOD 22-01 was revoked by BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk”, issued 10 June 2026, which replaces the uniform window with a 16-tier matrix keyed to public exposure, KEV membership and whether exploitation can be automated. The most dangerous tier must be remediated within three days with mandatory forensic triage; agencies must be operating to these timelines by 7 December 2026. Short windows are now normal and are not errors. Note also the corollary of how KEV works: entry requires evidence of exploitation, so a severe flaw with no confirmed exploitation carries no federal deadline at all. Absence from KEV is not a severity judgement.
