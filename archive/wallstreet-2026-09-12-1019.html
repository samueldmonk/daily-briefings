The lead

Stocks closed Friday up about 1% across the board — and still finished the week lower

US equity markets are closed for the weekend; the figures below are Friday 11 September’s official close, the most recent session. The Dow added 509.19 points, or 0.98%, the Nasdaq Composite rose 0.96% and the S&P 500 gained 0.86% — enough for all three to snap four-day losing streaks, and not nearly enough to rescue the week, which still ended lower.

Two things did the work. Oil pulled back from the spurt that had driven the selling, and the August CPI landed close to expectations: +0.4% month-on-month and 3.4% year-on-year, unchanged from July’s annual rate. The detail was less comfortable — core CPI rose 0.3% on the month, a tenth above forecast — but the headline was enough to halt a week-long global bond selloff that had carried the 10-year yield within striking distance of 5%.

That framing matters going into next week. The Federal Reserve meets 15–16 September, and futures are pricing roughly a 71% probability of a 25-basis-point increase — a market that rallied on inflation merely not getting worse, into a decision on rates going up.

Movers & drivers

NewUp ~12%

Hewlett Packard Enterprise led the S&P 500

HPE rose about 12% — 12.2% by one session tally, and “nearly 11%” by another — to close around $61.62, adding roughly $7 billion of market value in a single session and lifting its capitalisation to about $81.8 billion. The driver was AI infrastructure and server-refresh demand, reinforced by Oracle’s results and by the raised fiscal-2026 earnings and revenue guidance HPE itself issued on 2 September.

Up ~11%

Dell moved with it

Dell Technologies gained 11.5% on one tally and “almost 11%” on another, after RBC initiated coverage with an outperform rating. Server hardware moved as a bloc rather than on single-name news.

Communication services

T-Mobile carried the leading sector

T-Mobile rose more than 3.5% inside communication services, which was the day’s best-performing sector. One read puts the sector up 1.9%; another has it above 1%.

Up 53.8%

Tenon Medical and ACV Auctions ran hardest

Away from the large caps, Tenon Medical (TNON) jumped 53.8% and ACV Auctions (ACVA) 44.8% — the two largest percentage moves of the session on this desk’s reads, and a reminder that the biggest mover and the most important mover are rarely the same stock.

EarningsDisputed

Oracle beat; its Friday move is still in dispute

Oracle’s fiscal Q1 came in at an adjusted $1.92 on revenue of $19.35 billion, against LSEG consensus of $1.74 and $19.14 billion. The share move is another matter: carried reporting has it +10.3% intraday finishing near +0.3%, while a movers roundup read in an earlier edition today says +6%. No source read states an official closing change, so both are printed and neither is presented as the close.

Down 2%

Adobe slipped on in-line guidance

Adobe fell about 2% after current-quarter guidance came in in line rather than ahead — a small move, but a clean read on how little tolerance this tape has for software guidance that merely meets.

Chart of the day

Hewlett Packard Enterprise — the S&P 500’s biggest mover on Friday, up about 12%. Tenon Medical (+53.8%) and ACV Auctions (+44.8%) posted larger percentage gains market-wide; HPE is charted here because a roughly $7bn single-session gain in an index heavyweight is the move that actually shaped the tape.

Sector heat — live

Friday’s breadth: nine of the eleven S&P 500 sectors finished higher, led by communication services, with health care and utilities the laggards. Advancers beat decliners by roughly 2.1 to 1 and the Russell 2000 added 0.45%.

On the radar

  • The FOMC meets 15–16 September. Futures price roughly a 71% probability of a 25-basis-point increase, with officials weighing persistent inflation and higher energy costs. The decision lands Wednesday.
  • Core inflation is the awkward number. August core CPI rose 0.3% on the month, a tenth above forecast, even as the headline held at 3.4% year-on-year. The bond market took the headline; the Fed will be reading the core.
  • Oil is the swing factor in both directions. Brent near $99 with Middle East hostilities escalating is what drove the four-day slide, and its pullback is what ended it. The same input can reverse the tape next week.
  • The AI-infrastructure trade broadened. Oracle’s quarter and HPE’s guidance moved server hardware as a group rather than individually — worth watching for whether that persists or reverses as a crowded position.
