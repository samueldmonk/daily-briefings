The Lead

A hot jobs number turned a rate-cut trade into a rate-hike trade

The August employment report landed well above expectations — 162,000 nonfarm payrolls against a consensus clustered around 55,000–56,000, with the unemployment rate holding steady — and the market read it as removing the case for near-term easing. Treasury yields jumped, the 10-year rising nearly three basis points to about 4.79%, and all three major U.S. equity indices finished lower. The declines were orderly rather than violent: the Dow gave up 271.86 points, or 0.51%, to 53,414.25; the S&P 500 slipped 0.38% to 7,718.60; and the Nasdaq Composite eased 0.29% to 26,506.99. Beneath the index level the session was defensive in a very literal sense: utilities was the only sector to finish green, with consumer cyclicals and communication services taking the largest losses and chemicals down about 1%. The small-cap Russell 2000 was the lone advancing major index, up 0.25%. With the market now shut until Tuesday, this is where the week ends.

Movers & Drivers

Guidance cut

Lululemon — to seven-year lows

Shares cratered to their lowest in seven years after the company cut full-year sales guidance again, now seeing FY sales falling 5% to 7%.

Restructuring

Volkswagen — higher on 50,000 job cuts

Europe's largest automaker rose after its board approved plans to cut up to 50,000 more jobs as part of a historic transformation plan. Sources this edition put the move at between 6.1% and 7%; because they disagree, the range is printed rather than a single figure.

Semiconductors

Sandisk and KLA — chips against the tape

Both traded sharply higher into a down session, Sandisk up more than 8% and KLA up 7% — a pocket of strength in a market where only utilities closed green at the sector level.

Earnings beat

Snowflake — beat on both lines

Quarterly adjusted earnings of $0.62 per share against a $0.45 consensus, on revenue of $1.55 billion, surpassing the estimate by 4.91%.

Guidance cut

Oxford Industries — guidance lowered

Cut its full-year outlook, citing softness at Lilly Pulitzer alongside broader macroeconomic pressure.
