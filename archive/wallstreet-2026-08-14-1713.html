The Lead

The consumer blinked — and the record rally paused, but the S&P 500 still banked a third straight winning week

Stocks closed lower on Friday, one session after the S&P 500 posted a record close and cleared 7,800 intraday for the first time. The broad index ended down about 0.2% at 7,785.76. The Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 107.58 points, or 0.2%, to 53,732.41, closing out the week with a loss. The Nasdaq Composite shed about 0.3% to 26,729.16, still eking out a slight gain for the week.

Two consumer datapoints did the damage. July retail sales fell 0.6% from June — the first decline in nine months and the steepest drop since May 2025 — against a Dow Jones consensus for a 0.1% rise. Then the University of Michigan's preliminary August consumer sentiment reading came in at 51, down from 55.2 in July and well below the 54.5 economists expected, a fall of almost 8% that ended two straight months of improvement. Investors turned cautious in afternoon trading as the sentiment figure landed, with the survey pointing to low-income consumers, older Americans and those without a college degree as the sources of the deterioration.

The damage was concentrated in chips. The S&P 500 information technology index fell 0.5%, with Broadcom down about 5.5% — a slide The Motley Fool put at 5.6% and roughly $105 billion of market value, making it the single largest drag on both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq. Energy went the other way, climbing 1.5% alongside crude, and data-storage names defied the tape entirely.

The soft data cut against the hawkish read that had been building: markets now price roughly a 40% chance of a 25 basis-point Federal Reserve hike in September, down from close to 50% a day earlier, after July CPI slowed for a second consecutive month to 3.4%. The 10-year Treasury yield sat at 4.65%, extending its pullback from the prior session, while the 2-year added 3 basis points to 4.171% and the 30-year advanced nearly 6 basis points to 5.267%. Yields had jumped earlier after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told Newsmax that fresh measures aimed at the "economic isolation" of Iran were coming.
