The lead

No session today — the last print is Friday’s, and the argument now is how many more hikes are coming

Markets are closed for the weekend. There is no intraday direction to report, no after-hours block, and nothing on the tape carries a 19 or 20 September stamp. Everything below is Friday 18 September unless it says otherwise.

Friday ended a volatile week mixed and very nearly unchanged: the S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 0.39% to 26,522.55, and the Dow shed 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64 — a session shaped by rising Treasury yields, elevated oil and the Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in three years. It was also a quadruple-witching expiration of roughly $7 trillion notional, described as the second largest on record.

The week-ahead story is a gap between the Fed and the market. At Wednesday’s meeting policymakers penciled in one more rate increase in 2026 — but fed funds futures were last pricing roughly 42% odds that two more are actually coming. Investors got past the meeting relatively unscathed and are settling in for higher-for-longer, with choppiness expected in the months ahead. Futures were pointing higher into Monday’s open, and next week’s US–China summit is named as the wildcard.

A conflict worth naming. A separate account of the same session has the S&P 500 falling 0.17%, the Dow losing 0.40%, the Nasdaq 100 up 0.02% and the Russell 2000 down 0.75% — the same magnitude as the close, with the sign inverted on the S&P. It reads as a description of the session as it traded rather than where it settled. The closing figures above are corroborated by independent outlets and are what this page publishes; the alternative is printed here rather than buried.
