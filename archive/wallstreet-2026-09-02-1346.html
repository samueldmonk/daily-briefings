The Lead

Stocks claw back as the bond market keeps the upper hand — as of the afternoon session

Two search returns fetched this run place the S&P 500 up 0.52% heading into the afternoon portion of the session, described as leading among large-cap indexes, with a second phrasing that “the S&P 500 and the Dow rose 0.5% each, while the Nasdaq 100 remained slightly below the flatline.” That is a rebound from two losing sessions: stocks closed lower Tuesday with the Dow down more than 400 points on the U.S.–Iran conflict, rising oil and surging bond yields.

The clock on the day is what actually moved. At the 9:35 AM ET opening bell TheStreet recorded the S&P 500 +0.06%, the Dow +0.37%, the Nasdaq −0.06% and the Russell 2000 −1.23%. The afternoon read is a widening of that same shape — the cyclical indexes up, the growth complex not.

Underneath it all is the 10-year Treasury note, which hit a high of 4.814% on the day, its highest level since November 2023, per CNBC. Schwab notes the 10-year posted its highest close since late 2023 on Tuesday and sits well above its 50-day moving average of 4.32%. New York Fed President John Williams told CNBC that higher yields reflect economic strength rather than inflation fear, and that he sees inflation slowly moving lower.

Refusals, printed on purpose. Two summarizers disagree on the Nasdaq: one gives −0.61% for the afternoon session, another says the Nasdaq 100 is only slightly below the flatline, and a third block has the Nasdaq higher. No Nasdaq magnitude is published as current — direction only, and even that is flagged. Separately, the Yahoo Finance quote board fetched this run again returned a header reading “U.S. markets close in 6h” (≈10 AM) — the fourth consecutive run to catch the same cached board — so none of its levels are printed as current.

On the Radar

  • Broadcom (AVGO), Snowflake (SNOW) and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) report after today’s close. Schwab frames Broadcom as a barometer for the chip industry and a gut check on Nvidia’s recent results; in June the company missed on revenue and did not raise its full-year $100 billion AI chip sales target.
  • ADP said private employers added 38,000 jobs in August — below the 47,000 consensus and down from an upwardly revised 46,000 in July, the slowest month since January. Education and health services added 45,000, leisure and hospitality 16,000 and construction 12,000; manufacturing lost 17,000, professional and business services 16,000, and both natural resources/mining and trade, transportation and utilities shed 5,000 each.
  • Thursday, September 3: August ISM Services PMI — Schwab flags its prices-paid component as a touchpoint for yields — plus earnings from Ciena (CIEN) and lululemon (LULU).
  • Friday, September 4: August nonfarm payrolls and the unemployment rate. Schwab says analysts expect 45,000 new jobs and notes a soft print might blunt rate worries.
  • Monday, September 7: U.S. markets closed for Labor Day.
  • A Bank of Japan hike is expected the week after next, per Schwab, with a weak yen and rising Japanese yields threatening to narrow the premium U.S. yields hold over Japan’s.
  • Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told CNBC the administration is developing a tariff framework for semiconductors: “if you build here, you don’t pay, but if you don’t build here, expect to pay.”
  • Geopolitics. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two oil tankers struck naval mines attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz; Iran targeted Jordan, the UAE and Kuwait with missiles and drones overnight in retaliation for U.S. airstrikes. Trump said he is “not trying to force Iran to the bargaining table” and claimed “almost total control” over the Strait.
