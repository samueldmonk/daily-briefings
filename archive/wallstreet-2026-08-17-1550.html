The Lead

Ceasefire lapses, crude firms, long yields push higher — stocks give ground into the close (as of ~3:45 PM ET)

Through Monday afternoon the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down about 0.5%, the S&P 500 about 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite about 0.3%, with a separate afternoon read putting the moves at −0.48%, −0.45% and −0.27% respectively. The driver is geopolitical: the interim ceasefire between the United States and Iran formally expires today with negotiations to end the conflict and reopen the Strait of Hormuz deadlocked, and President Trump said he does not see the war ending anytime soon. Oil and Treasury yields rose together on the news, and the 30-year Treasury yield reached its highest level in decades.

The tape was not uniformly weak. Chipmakers rallied after Anthropic reported second-quarter revenue above $11.5 billion — a fourteen-fold year-over-year jump — which investors read as evidence that AI infrastructure spending is durable rather than plateauing, and that the bellwether is no longer any single vendor's order book. Energy and AI-linked names provided a cushion against deeper losses. Ahead of a light economic calendar, attention turns to a heavy week of retail earnings and to Wednesday's release of the Federal Open Market Committee minutes.

Movers & Drivers

Gainer

SanDisk (SNDK) — ~+6.7%

SanDisk led premarket gains Monday alongside Micron, surging roughly 6.7%, as Anthropic's revenue surge strengthened the case that spending on AI storage and memory keeps climbing. SanDisk closed Friday at $1,528.11 after a 13.67% move on its 2026 Investor Day and a J.P. Morgan Overweight reinstatement.

Gainer

Micron (MU) — ~+4.5% early

Micron rose about 4.5% in early trading, the other half of the memory-and-storage pair that carried Monday's AI bid. Both companies supply the infrastructure AI systems consume at scale, and the market treated Anthropic's revenue print — not just Nvidia's or OpenAI's — as the read on whether that demand holds.

On the Radar

  • FOMC minutes, Wednesday. The record of the last meeting could offer more detail on how officials are weighing a mixed inflation-and-jobs picture.
  • Big-box retail earnings all week. Walmart, Target, Lowe's and Home Depot report, giving the first real read on consumer health through the back-to-school season.
  • Reddit joins the S&P 500 before the open on Tuesday, August 18, replacing AvalonBay Communities.
  • Jackson Hole and the September question. Traders have pulled September rate-hike odds down to under one-third as the data has cut both ways.
  • Deficit arithmetic. The Congressional Budget Office last week raised its expectation for the annual U.S. budget deficit to $2.1 trillion, $200 billion above its February estimate — part of what is pushing long-dated yields.
  • The Strait of Hormuz. Traders are weighing the risk of deeper disruption against a resolution in which the strait reopens and crude falls sharply.
