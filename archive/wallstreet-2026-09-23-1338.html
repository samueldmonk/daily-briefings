The Lead

Stocks slide as the 10-year yield hits its highest since 2007 on a hot PMI (as of midday ET)

Wall Street extended its losses into midday Wednesday: by CNBC’s midday read the S&P 500 was down 0.7%, the Nasdaq Composite had shed 1.2% and the Dow was off 318 points, or 0.6%, as Treasury yields marched higher on worries that more Federal Reserve rate hikes are coming. The trigger was S&P Global’s flash U.S. Composite PMI, which jumped to 58.4 in September from 56.0 in August — its highest since July 2021 — with factory hiring rising at the fastest pace since February 2021, per Reuters.

The benchmark 10-year yield climbed more than 13 basis points to 5.104%, a level not seen since July 2007, while the 2-year rose to 4.914%, its highest since May 2024 (CNBC). Fed funds futures now price 73% odds of another quarter-point hike in October, up from 55% on Tuesday (CME FedWatch via CNBC), and Fed Governor Michael Barr said the central bank will likely need to deliver further hikes. Utilities, consumer discretionary and communication services led the decline, each down more than 1%. Oil added to the pressure — U.S. crude rose 1.49% to $91.87 and Brent 2.39% to $101.62 by late morning (Reuters) — as traders weighed possible U.S.-Iran talks and Thursday’s Trump-Xi summit in Washington.

Earlier: at the 9:32 AM ET open the S&P 500 was down 0.10%, the Dow 0.30% and the Nasdaq 0.15% (TheStreet); Reuters’ late-morning read had the S&P 500 down 0.53% and the Nasdaq down 1.05%. Sources: CNBC live blog · Reuters (via GV Wire) · CNBC (yields) · TheStreet
