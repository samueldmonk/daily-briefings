The Lead

Hot retail sales, easing oil, and a Fed about to hike for the first time in three years — as of ~11:10 a.m. ET

Wall Street carried modest gains into the late morning with the Federal Reserve set to announce its decision at 2 p.m. ET. TheStreet’s live blog was headlined “Dow edges higher ahead of Fed decision” at its 11:10 a.m. ET update, with stocks “climbing” but no index percentages attached. The most recent reading carrying its own timestamp came from Yahoo Finance at 11:08 a.m. ET: the S&P 500 up 0.2%, the Nasdaq Composite up 0.4%, and the Dow dipping below the flat line. At the 9:32 a.m. ET opening bell TheStreet had the S&P 500 +0.25%, the Dow +0.15%, the Nasdaq +0.44% and the Russell 2000 −0.76% — the same small-cap weakness that left that index at a three-month closing low on Tuesday.

The morning’s data ran hot. August retail sales rose 1.2% from July against an 0.8% consensus, with the control group up 1.4% and only one industry — building materials — lower. “Retail sales registered a strong bounce back across the board following July’s weak report, which was also upwardly revised,” said Nathan Peterson of the Schwab Center for Financial Research. Restaurant and bar sales rose 1.2% month over month, a seventh straight monthly increase. Export and import prices rose 0.6% and 0.7%.

Futures markets put the odds of a quarter-point hike at 93% on the CME FedWatch Tool as of Schwab’s 9:13 a.m. note, 92.5% in TheStreet’s 11:10 a.m. blog, and 92.9% in Yahoo Finance’s 11:08 a.m. read — each printed with its own timestamp rather than averaged. A hike would be the Fed’s first since 2023 and would lift the target range from 3.50%–3.75% to 3.75%–4.00%. Futures also imply 77% odds of a second hike before year-end, and CME trading projects three to five hikes by next September, with the highest odds at around four.

TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre framed the risk plainly: “A hike paired with reassuring language could actually calm the bond market. A hike with hawkish or vague commentary could push yields higher and take stocks down with them.”

On the Radar

  • Today, 2 p.m. ET — FOMC decision, Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference. In June the average projection had the funds rate ending 2026 at 3.8% (one hike) and 2027 at 3.6%; June PCE estimates for 2026 were 3.6% headline and 3.3% core, and Schwab expects both to rise.
  • Later today — an updated Atlanta Fed GDPNow estimate for third-quarter growth. The most recent reading was 4.4% annualised, against an average analyst estimate still below 3%.
  • Thursday, 17 September — August housing starts and building permits.
  • Friday, 18 September — Bank of Japan rate decision, August industrial production, and the Conference Board’s August leading indicators.
  • Context for the vote — July’s FOMC voted 9–3 to pause. Schwab notes today may be the last chance to hike at a regularly scheduled meeting until December, because October’s meeting falls just before the mid-term elections.
  • Off the tape — European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed opening the door for Canada to become the EU’s first “associate member,” NPR reported; the status is new, its terms undefined, and it would need approval from EU member states. Separately, Bloomberg reported OpenAI is in early talks on a funding round valuing it above $1.2 trillion ahead of an IPO.
