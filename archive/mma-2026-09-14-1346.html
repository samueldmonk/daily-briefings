Tom Aspinall vacates the UFC heavyweight title

Aspinall announced this morning that he is giving up the undisputed heavyweight championship and focusing solely on his recovery. “Ongoing complications with my eyes mean I cannot get medically cleared to fight right now,” he said. “I’ve had multiple surgeries, accidents and infections, and done everything possible to get back to competing.” He had already agreed a fight date and was back sparring when, he says, an accident caused further damage to his right eye.

“I’ve asked the UFC to allow me to make this statement because I don’t want to hold the heavyweight division up,” he said. “I know what it’s like to have your career put on hold and I want the other fighters to have the opportunity to compete for the title.” He added that the UFC and Hunter had been kept informed throughout and had seen the tests and medical records, and signed off: “I’m absolutely gutted. I’ve chased this dream since I was 10 years old.” He is explicit that this is not a retirement — “stepping away from the title is not stepping away from the sport.”

The injury traces to the eye pokes he took in the no-contest against Ciryl Gane at UFC 321 last October, an absence approaching the year mark that had prompted rumblings of discontent inside the UFC. The men’s heavyweight championship is therefore vacant as of today, with Gane the interim titleholder. This site’s previous edition, built at 1:19pm, still listed Aspinall as the reigning champion; the champions board below is corrected.

Prospect Watch

Prospect

David Martinez — 4-0 in the Octagon

The Mexican prospect swept the cards against Dan Ige to open the Noche UFC main card, pushing his UFC record to 4-0 and his overall winning streak to eleven. UFC.com’s recap calls him a quick, well-rounded striker clearly a step ahead of the veteran set at 135 pounds.

Prospect

Tommy McMillen — 12-0 and asking for more

The DWCS Class of ’25 featherweight survived being dropped in the second and took the third outright to move to 12-0. He called for a fourth fight this year in Las Vegas and promised another $100,000 bonus.

Around the Sport

  • Aspinall is not retiring. “I’ll continue doing everything I can to get healthy, get cleared and get back to competing as soon as possible,” he said in the same statement. His return date is unclear.
  • Alexa Grasso has positioned herself for the next flyweight title shot. Her unanimous decision over Manon Fiorot was a second straight win; UFC.com calls her the clubhouse leader in the chase for the next championship opportunity — a chase with a vacant belt at the end of it on 3 October.
  • Brandon Moreno stopped the bleeding. The former flyweight champion entered on a two-fight skid and took a split decision from Joseph Morales in his first Noche UFC appearance.
  • Curtis Blaydes is back in the win column after a strategic decision over Waldo Cortes Acosta — a performance UFC.com itself describes as leaving a lot to be desired.
