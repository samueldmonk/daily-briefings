Top Story

SonicWall SMA1000 zero-days are being chained for unauthenticated remote code execution

SonicWall has confirmed that attackers are exploiting two previously undisclosed flaws in its SMA 1000 remote-access appliances and is urging customers to apply the hotfix as soon as possible. CVE-2026-83548 is a pre-authentication server-side request forgery issue in the Appliance Work Place interface, carrying a CVSS score of 10; it can be exploited remotely without authentication to reach sensitive functionality and carry out unauthorised operations. CVE-2026-83549 is an OS command injection flaw in the Appliance Management Console that, in specific conditions, can be exploited by an authenticated attacker with administrator privileges to run code on an unpatched appliance. Researchers report threat actors chaining the two in remote code execution attacks.

Affected models are the SMA1000 6210, 7210 and 8200v. Hotfixes 12.4.3-03526 and 12.5.0-02952 and higher versions carry the fix. There is no workaround. Where indicators of compromise are found, SonicWall advises administrators to re-image the appliance, change all user and administrator passwords, and reset TOTP tokens.

Both CVEs were among the seven CISA added to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Wednesday, September 2, with a federal remediation date of Saturday, September 5.

Patch Priority

Patch SonicWall SMA1000 today — the federal deadline is Saturday

If you run an SMA1000 6210, 7210 or 8200v, install hotfix 12.4.3-03526 or 12.5.0-02952 (or higher) now. CVE-2026-83548 is a CVSS 10.0 pre-authentication SSRF being exploited as a zero-day and chained with CVE-2026-83549 for remote code execution, there is no workaround, and the CISA remediation date for both is Saturday, September 5, 2026 — 2 days away. Treat any appliance showing indicators of compromise as fully owned: re-image it, rotate every user and administrator password, and reset TOTP tokens.

Threat Actor Spotlight

The operator who let AI agents run the whole intrusion

Unit 42 has documented an intrusion in which a human ransomware attacker used frontier AI models and agentic attack frameworks to breach an enterprise network in under 10 hours — work the researchers say would normally take human operators around two weeks. The attacker told the victim’s negotiators that frontier models and agentic attack frameworks had been used.

Tactical execution was left to the agents, which monitored, evaluated, acted and re-planned in real time. They ran reconnaissance, gained access by breaching a public API endpoint to tunnel into the network, mapped internal microservices, and used subagents to scrape code repositories for hard-coded tokens and service passwords. Those tokens got them into the organisation’s secret-management system, where they took master administrative credentials, and they went on to hijack CI/CD workflows to steal cloud access keys. Reporting counts more than 50 ATT&CK techniques used across cloud, identity, CI/CD and SaaS defences. The agents then generated an 80-page security audit and left it for the victim.

Breaches & Incidents

NewIndictment

Russian national charged over an Excel-macro campaign against 80,000 freelancers

Searzhudin Tamirlanovich Aktulaev, 40, was arrested in Cyprus in May 2025, extradited on August 28, 2026 and made his first San Francisco court appearance on August 31. Prosecutors say he and co-conspirators built about 255 fake accounts on a well-known freelance work platform and used them to message roughly 80,000 users posing as prospective clients, attaching malicious Excel files that prompted victims to run a macro. The macro downloaded TVRAT (also known as TVSPY or TeamSpy), which abuses a flaw in TeamViewer for remote control, and DarkVNC, which does the same through VNC Viewer. The campaign ran in 2016 and 2017. He faces charges including conspiracy, transmission of malicious code and aggravated identity theft, and up to 20 years.

NewRansomware

An agentic ransomware intrusion ended with an 80-page audit

Unit 42’s account of an enterprise compromise driven end to end by AI agents — recon, initial access through a public API endpoint, credential theft from code repositories and a secret-management system, and CI/CD hijacking — completed in under 10 hours. See the spotlight above.

NewPolicyUK

UK moves to block high-risk suppliers after a power plant was taken offline

The Telegraph reported on August 22 that Iran-linked adversaries had forced a small-scale UK energy facility offline for four days. The outage did not disrupt the wider grid, but security experts said it showed how relatively modest assets can carry systemic risk as the electricity system becomes more decentralised and more reliant on remote access. On August 24 the government tabled late amendments to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill that would give ministers powers to stop critical-sector organisations using technology suppliers deemed high risk. The bill has passed the Commons and is in the Lords.

BreachCourts

Thomson Reuters court files taken from C-Track

Files were taken in March 2026 and the unauthorised activity was not discovered until June 30, 2026. The number of affected jurisdictions is reported two ways and neither is adopted: Help Net Security says at least 12 U.S. states, the U.S. Virgin Islands and Canada, while other coverage says 11 U.S. states plus Canada and the Virgin Islands. Data may include names with Social Security numbers, driver’s licence numbers, medical information, dates of birth and health insurance information. The incident was in Thomson Reuters’ own cloud environment rather than court networks; C-Track is fully operational and 12 months of credit monitoring is being offered.
