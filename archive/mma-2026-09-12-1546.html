Top Story

Sean King III ends it in 36 seconds with a slam — and the result this page refused last run is now official, while three others returned by the same search still are not

The second bout of the Noche UFC prelims lasted barely half a minute. Sean King III def. Jessie Rosas by KO (slam), round 1, 0:36, in a meeting of featherweight newcomers. The round-by-round account: King ducked under, picked Rosas up and drove him into the canvas — the reporter's comparison is to the Rock Bottom, professional wrestling's slam finisher — and, in the reporter's words, Rosas hit his head on the canvas and was out before King could follow up with punches. King came in 6-0 per UFC.com, which makes him 7-0; the derivation is stated rather than implied. He was a −200 favourite. Rosas came in 8-1.

That is the second official result of the card, after Regina Tarin def. JJ Aldrich by unanimous decision, 29-28 on all three scorecards, in the opener. Tarin arrived 8-0 off a February debut win and leaves 9-0; Aldrich is 15-7 and the division's No. 15 ranked flyweight. Both are published because a live blog carries them under a filled "Official decision" heading with a round-by-round account attached — not because a search returned them.

The King result is the one this page refused last run, and the refusal was correct at the time: the live blog then carrying the card listed the bout as "Next Up", and UFC.com carried it as a matchup. What has changed is not the strength of the search result but the existence of a filled result field. That distinction is the whole method, and this run it cut in the other direction too — the same search that produced King also produced three more prelim winners that remain unconfirmed, and tracing them this time identified the mechanism. See the refusal block below: the culprit is a betting-picks list written in "def." syntax, sitting inside the very same live blog that carries the real results.

Refused This Run

Named, traced, not published

Three prelim winners — Bahamondes over Salikhov, Gantt over Klose, and Rongzhu over Rafa García "by unanimous decision (29-28, 29-28, 29-28)". All three came back from the same search that produced the two real results above, and none is published. The live blog carrying the two official decisions lists Rafa Garcia vs Rongzhu, Drakkar Klose vs Tommy Gantt and Ignacio Bahamondes vs Muslim Salikhov as matchups in the same quick-results list where King and Tarin appear in bold as results.

And this run finally identifies where those phantom results come from. The Yahoo/Uncrowned live blog contains a block headed "Prelim Picks", posted before the card started, and every line in it is written in exactly the syntax of a result: "Thomas Gantt (−450) def. Drakkar Klose (+350)", "Rong Zhu (−180) def. Rafa García (+145)", "Ignacio Bahamondes (−600) def. Muslim Salikhov (+425)". Those are predictions with betting odds attached. Every "result" refused above is a line from that list, and the "29-28, 29-28, 29-28" attached to the Rongzhu line is character-for-character the scoreline of Tarin's real decision — the only 29-28 sweep confirmed anywhere on this card. ⭐ The lesson: a picks list uses the same verb as a results list. "Def." is not evidence, favourites winning is not confirmation, and a correctly ordered set is what a picks list looks like by construction. Last run this page traced a fabricated set to a prediction table on an outside site; this run the same thing was sitting inside a live blog that also carries a genuine result of its own.

The remaining Athlon Sports predictions — a page titled "Noche UFC Prediction, Results and Highlights", written in the past tense and containing no results — stay unpublished. Two of its seven calls have now been vindicated by real results; that changes nothing about the other five, which remain forecasts.

Any champions list seating Valentina Shevchenko at 125 pounds is stale and refused on sight, and Alex Pereira is not the light-heavyweight champion however often a cached page says so.
