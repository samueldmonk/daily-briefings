The Lead

Sunday edition: a volatile week ended with all three indexes lower — now comes Fed week and four megacap earnings reports

U.S. markets are closed for the weekend. Friday's session ended split: the S&P 500 rose 3.68 points, or less than 0.1%, to 7,411.98; the Dow Jones Industrial Average recovered 235.60 points, or 0.5%, to 51,947.25; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 161.87 points, or 0.6%, to 24,975.82 as tech weakness continued (AP/Motley Fool). All three indexes finished the week lower — the S&P 500's second straight losing week — as investors weighed a new set of global tariffs, AI-spending jitters, rising oil prices and elevated bond yields (AP/Motley Fool).

Chips stayed at the center of the selling. Intel gave back 6.5% as investors looked past its blowout Thursday-night earnings beat, while SanDisk dropped almost 11% and other memory names slid after a Korea-led selloff in SK Hynix spilled into U.S. stocks (Motley Fool/24/7 Wall St). Technology fell 2.37% as a sector Friday while real estate and financial services led the gainers (Motley Fool) — and Apple, up 3.5% on Yahoo's closing board, helped the Dow claw back part of Thursday's 507-point drop.

The relief valve was oil: Brent slid almost 4% to close at $96.78 — back below the $100 mark it breached Thursday — and WTI shed 3% to $89.31 after reports that Pakistan, with China's support, is trying to revive U.S.–Iran negotiations (TradingEconomics/CNBC). The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.67%, still hovering near its highest levels since January 2025. The week ahead is the test: the FOMC decides rates Wednesday at 2 PM ET, with Microsoft and Meta reporting after Wednesday's close, Apple on Thursday, and Amazon also due midweek (CNBC).

Movers & Drivers

Intel (INTC) −6.5%

Fell 6.5% Friday as investors looked past Thursday night's beat — Q2 revenue of $16.1B, up 25%, its fastest growth since 2011 — and focused instead on the long-term foundry roadmap and the cost of its manufacturing ramp (Motley Fool/Yahoo Finance close wrap).

SanDisk (SNDK) ~−11%

Tumbled almost 11%, the day's headline decliner, alongside other memory stocks (Motley Fool/Yahoo). 24/7 Wall St traced the pressure to a Korea chip selloff that hit SK Hynix and spilled into U.S. memory names.

Memory complex: Micron, SK Hynix ~−6%

SK Hynix and Micron sank about 6% as the Korea-led memory rout spread — a sharp reversal from the AI-memory euphoria that drove the group's run earlier in the week (24/7 Wall St).

Apple (AAPL) +3.5%

Closed at $333.02, up 3.53% on Yahoo's quote board — a rare megacap gainer into Thursday's earnings report, and a key support under the Dow's recovery.

SpaceX (SPCX) −2.68%

Stumbled ahead of another key test flight (Motley Fool), closing at $115.07 on Yahoo's board — still recovering from Thursday's record-low territory near $110.

Sentiment check: BofA Bull & Bear

Bank of America's Bull & Bear Indicator hit its highest level since 2021 — a contrarian "sell signal" reflecting extreme bullishness in fund flows, hedge-fund positioning and breadth that has often preceded selloffs (Motley Fool).

On the Radar

  • FOMC, Tue–Wed Jul 28–29: decision Wednesday at 2 PM ET with Chair Warsh's press conference at 2:30. Economists polled by FactSet expect a hold at 3.50–3.75% — which would be the fifth straight unchanged meeting — and rate-hike odds have collapsed — from over 40% earlier last week to below 17% — after June CPI printed below expectations (3.5% headline) and June payrolls came in at a soft 57,000 (CNBC next-week outlook). Forbes had pegged hike odds near one-in-three on Jul 23, before Friday's oil slide unwound the inflation-scare trade. It's a non-SEP meeting, so no new dot plot (CME Group/KuCoin).
  • Megacap earnings gauntlet: Microsoft and Meta after Wednesday's close, Apple Thursday — shortly after the Fed — and Amazon also midweek. The question: whether the market keeps punishing big AI spenders after Alphabet's capex guide of up to $205B sent its shares down 7.1% Thursday (CNBC). Also on deck: PayPal, Coca-Cola, Boeing, Visa and Ford on Tuesday; ARM and Qualcomm Wednesday; Chevron and ExxonMobil close the week Friday (IG).
  • AI capex scoreboard going in: Microsoft has signaled roughly $190B for calendar 2026, Amazon about $200B, Meta $125–145B — versus Alphabet's $195–205B (CNBC).
  • Oil diplomacy watch: whether the Pakistan/China push to restart U.S.–Iran talks holds is the swing factor for Brent, yields and the inflation debate after Friday's ~4% slide (TradingEconomics/CNBC).
  • Reddit reports Thursday, Jul 30 — its first print since the WSJ reported its Google AI content-licensing deal may end (consensus $0.97 EPS / $732M revenue, TradingKey).
