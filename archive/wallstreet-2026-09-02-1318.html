The Lead

Dow out front as oil slips and the 10-year pauses — as of the ~10 AM ET board

US stocks turned higher on Wednesday after Tuesday’s slide, with a rally in crude stalling and Treasury yields taking a breather. On the freshest quote board this desk could date — Yahoo Finance, stamped “U.S. markets close in 6h”, i.e. roughly 10 AM ET — the S&P 500 was +0.20%, the Dow +0.53%, the Nasdaq Composite +0.03% and the Russell 2000 +0.75%, with the VIX at 15.90, −2.69%.

TheStreet’s own 9:35 AM opening-bell entry reads differently and is printed as its own snapshot: S&P +0.06%, Dow +0.37%, Nasdaq −0.06%, Russell 2000 −1.23%. That Russell reading and the ~10 AM +0.75% are a 1.98 percentage-point swing in twenty-five minutes; both are printed and neither is reconciled here.

The driver is the same one that broke the market on Tuesday, running the other way. Crude retreated after climbing to more than one-month highs earlier in the session — WTI −0.71% to $89.58 and Brent −0.39% to $94.28 as of 9:24 AM — following the heaviest US–Iran exchange of fire in weeks. The benchmark 10-year Treasury note yield still hit a high of 4.814% on the day, which TheStreet, citing CNBC, calls its highest since November 2023.

On the Radar

  • ADP private payrolls, August: +38,000 — below the Dow Jones consensus of +47,000 and down from an upwardly revised +46,000 in July, the slowest month since January. Education and health services led with +45,000; leisure and hospitality +16,000; construction +12,000. Manufacturing shed 17,000 and professional and business services 16,000. ⚠ Yahoo’s 5 AM preview listed a different pair for the same release (+46,000 expected, +44,000 previously); both are printed.
  • Friday’s monthly jobs report is the next read on a labour market that Yahoo describes as having job openings “frozen.”
  • FOMC, September 15–16. Odds of a September hike were 66.1% on Monday per CME FedWatch, nearly double where they sat before Fed Chair Kevin Warsh spoke at Jackson Hole; a separate report puts the jump at 57.5%, up from 35.4% one day earlier. The figures come from different days and are printed as such, not averaged.
  • After the bell: Broadcom (AVGO), Snowflake (SNOW) and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) report, alongside NetApp, Five Below, Brown-Forman and FuelCell Energy.
  • Semiconductor tariffs. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick told CNBC the administration is developing a tariff framework for chips: “if you build here, you don’t pay.” No rate or date has been detailed publicly.
  • US–Iran. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards say two oil tankers struck naval mines attempting to transit the Strait of Hormuz and were disabled; Iran targeted Jordan, the UAE and Kuwait overnight in retaliation for US airstrikes. Trump says he is “not trying to force Iran to the bargaining table” and claims “almost total control” of the Strait. Saxo’s Ole Hansen calls it “a binary risk” with a potential $5 move in either direction.
  • Shutdown averted. Congress has sent a funding bill to the President, per Yahoo Finance’s overnight roundup.
