Top story

Iran-linked hackers took a UK power plant offline for four days

Hackers linked to Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps shut down a small British power plant for four days in July, in what reporting describes as the first cyberattack to bring a UK generating station to a standstill. The story was broken by The Telegraph on August 22 and picked up by CNBC, SecurityWeek and international outlets on August 23.

The UK government confirmed the incident but said there was no risk to the country’s energy system. Neither the government nor the National Cyber Security Centre would identify the site, citing security reasons. The generating station was taken offline for four days and staff had to restore operations manually.

The Telegraph via CNBC and SecurityWeek, August 22–23, 2026. This displaces the GitLab exploitation story as the lead; that item now sits in Breaches & incidents below and in the vulnerability table. The timing matters for the other desk: at 1 p.m. ET on Monday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced “Operation Economic Outcast,” sanctioning more than 60 entities, individuals and vessels accused of helping Iran procure nuclear and missile technology, conduct cyber operations or generate oil revenue, and broadening secondary-sanctions risk across Iran’s digital-asset, technology, gold, aviation and shipping links.

Patch priority

Do this first — the clock runs out today

CVE-2026-73570 — Zimbra Collaboration Suite — CVSS 8.9. An OS command-injection flaw reachable via crafted SMTP requests, giving remote code execution as the Zimbra user on servers with zimbra-snmp installed. Exploitation was reported by CERT Polska. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on August 21 with an action due date of August 24 — today. Patch to 10.1.20 or later.

Three items outrank it on raw severity but are already overdue rather than due: CVE-2026-72898 (Metabase, CVSS 10.0, exploited as a zero-day) is ten days past due, CVE-2026-59310 (VMware vCenter, CVSS 9.8, path traversal to arbitrary code execution) is three days past due, and CVE-2026-72529 (TrueConf Server, CVSS 9.8) — last edition’s priority — slipped past its August 23 deadline yesterday. Zimbra leads here because its deadline is the one that expires today; the overdue entries should already be closed out.

Due dates are the ones stated in the KEV catalog and are assigned per-CVE under BOD 26-04 (risk-based), not on a fixed three-week schedule. The dates used here match the KEV section below exactly.

ShinyHunters moves from threatening to publishing

The extortion crew has posted on its dark-web site what it says are 7.1 million Salesforce records stolen from medical products maker Baxter International, complete with a download button. “The company failed to reach an agreement with us despite our incredible patience, all the chances and offers we made. They don’t care,” the group wrote. Baxter was first listed on August 14. Fresh extortion deadlines dated today are outstanding against BOK Financial, NovoCure and a third, partly redacted listing.

Breaches & incidents
RansomwareSonicWall SMA 1000

INC ransomware has become the dominant crew working the SonicWall SMA 1000 flaws

Resecurity reports that INC Ransomware has accelerated sharply since the start of August 2026, listing multiple new victims on its leak site; the group claims 885 victims to date, with the most recent posted on August 2. The intrusions are suspected to chain CVE-2026-15409 and CVE-2026-15410 in SonicWall SMA 1000 appliances — a pair that lets an unauthenticated remote attacker open a WebSocket tunnel to restricted services and escalate to root. SonicWall released fixes in mid-July 2026. The reason patching alone is not remediation: Rapid7 found the intrusions extracted high-value credentials, active session databases and time-based one-time-password MFA seed configurations, giving the attackers persistence and lateral movement that survives a firmware update. Recent targets span the private and public sectors in the United States, Australia, the United Arab Emirates, Colombia and Switzerland. If you run SMA 1000, patching is step one and rotating credentials, invalidating sessions and re-seeding TOTP enrolments is step two.

EducationDisruption

UT San Antonio starts its fall semester today, three days late, after a cyberattack

The University of Texas at San Antonio pushed the start of its fall semester back by three days after a cyberattack hit its academic network over a weekend. Classes that were due to begin on Wednesday, August 19 start instead today, Monday, August 24. Help Net Security reported the delay on August 19. It is the clearest recent example of an attack on an academic network translating directly into lost teaching days rather than lost data — and the kind of operational disruption that lands in the first week of a semester, when enrolment, timetabling and identity systems are all under peak load.

RansomwareJoint advisory

Medusa has now breached more than 500 organisations, the FBI and CISA say

Medusa ransomware has hit over 500 organisations since it first appeared in June 2021, according to an updated joint advisory from the FBI, CISA and the Department of Health and Human Services. The update builds on an advisory first issued in March 2025 and draws on FBI investigations conducted as recently as April 2026. HHS’s presence on the byline is the tell: healthcare remains a priority sector for the group. Separately, France’s tax authority disclosed a breach of the General Directorate of Public Finances exposing data on 678,000 individuals and professionals, after an attacker using the alias “ZeroBytes” claimed credit on a cybercrime forum and listed the database for sale.

RetailCredential attack

ASOS says stolen credentials from elsewhere opened customer accounts

The online fashion retailer confirmed that attackers used credentials obtained outside the company to get into customer accounts holding personal information and limited payment card details — a credential-stuffing-style attack rather than a compromise of ASOS’s own systems. The incident was reported to the attorneys general of Texas and Vermont on August 21, 2026, with 9,412 Texas residents and 80 Vermont residents confirmed affected. Exposed fields include full names, addresses, dates of birth and financial information such as account and card numbers. When the intrusion occurred, how it was found and the full scope remain undisclosed. The practical lesson is the familiar one: password reuse turns someone else’s breach into yours, and account-takeover defences — MFA, credential-stuffing detection, breached-password screening — do the work here, not patching.

ExploitedGitLab

A GitLab flaw went from disclosure to exploitation in days

CVE-2026-19478 (CVSS 9.4) is a code-injection flaw that lets an unauthenticated attacker modify or delete publicly accessible GitLab projects and rewrite their data under certain conditions — no credentials, no user interaction, no obscure configuration. GitLab said it can be exploited via a GraphQL directive. Exposure-management firm watchTowr told The Hacker News it reproduced the vulnerability within minutes and that it is now under active exploitation. Affected builds are GitLab CE and EE 18.2 before 18.11.11, 19.0 before 19.0.8, 19.1 before 19.1.6 and 19.2 before 19.2.4; fixes shipped in 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8 and 18.11.11. No CISA KEV entry exists for this CVE.

EspionageQUICSILVER

QUICAgent backdoor rides graduation invitations into Myanmar government networks

Seqrite Labs flagged a cyber-espionage campaign, codenamed Operation QUICSILVER, targeting government and information technology organisations in Myanmar with a Go backdoor called QUICAgent. The activity is assessed with moderate confidence to be the work of a China-nexus threat actor and was first observed in April 2026, when it delivered a file named “HolidayNotice.pdf.exe” alongside a fabricated Belgian–Myanmar public holiday calendar. Two later artefacts, detected in June and July 2026, use a Virtual Hard Disk file containing a Windows shortcut that mimics a PDF; opening it displays a decoy official graduation ceremony invitation written in Burmese.

CybercrimeUAT-10147

A Chinese-speaking crew is using AI to scale server attacks

Cisco Talos detailed UAT-10147, a Chinese-speaking cybercrime group targeting Windows and Linux web servers globally across education, media, technology and gaming, with most victims in Brazil, Bolivia, China, Canada and Vietnam. The group deploys a payload dubbed SPECTRE with EDR bypass and a Linux rootkit. “The actor leveraged publicly disclosed vulnerabilities to gain initial access at scale,” Talos wrote, describing a mix of open-source offensive frameworks including Metasploit, ysoserial, PentestGPT and DeepAudit plus multiple privilege-escalation exploits used to automate intrusions and establish persistence. The findings came from an open directory at 139.180.197[.]150 observed talking to a compromised machine.

Shadow AIAkamai

A handful of AI super-adopters carry most of the enterprise risk

Akamai’s State of the Internet: Enterprise AI Usage Risk Report 2026, published August 24 and built on real-world usage and telemetry data alongside threat analysis, finds that the top 5% of enterprise power users interact with AI models at 12 times the rate of the bottom 50% of the workforce. Akamai’s argument is that security teams policing casual ChatGPT and Claude drafting are watching the wrong population: the small group hardcoding unvetted tools into critical business operations is the one expanding shadow AI, widening data-leakage exposure and introducing autonomous agents that run inside the enterprise but outside its guardrails.

CVSS 10.0SAP Commerce Cloud

A maximum-severity SAP flaw is being probed — three days after the patch shipped

CVE-2026-58231 carries the maximum CVSS score of 10.0 and sits in SAP Commerce Cloud. Per CVE.org, “SAP Commerce Cloud allows an unauthenticated attacker to abuse a default authentication client and submit specially crafted input to certain functions lacking sufficient validation,” with successful exploitation enabling arbitrary code execution and compromise of internal components — high impact on confidentiality, integrity and availability. SAP patched it on August 11. Threat-intelligence firm Defused Cyber told The Hacker News that exploitation attempts began hitting its honeypots merely three days after the patch, and KEVIntel independently confirmed two attempts detected on August 14 from a single IP address located in the United States.

SAP security firm Onapsis recommends customers patch to the fixed Commerce Cloud release levels and re-build and re-deploy, and offers a temporary mitigation: configure an IP Filter Set in SAP Commerce Cloud to restrict access to the vulnerable endpoint. Nobody has been publicly attributed to the activity, though prior SAP flaws — CVE-2025-31324 in NetWeaver — were weaponised by China-nexus espionage clusters including UNC5221, UNC5174 and CL-STA-0048, and by the BianLian and RansomExx cybercrime groups.

Stated precisely, because the sourcing is nuanced: what is confirmed is exploitation attempts against honeypot and sensor infrastructure. Defused Cyber said in the same post that the vulnerability “has no public PoC and is not known to be exploited,” and CVE-2026-58231 does not appear in the CISA KEV catalog as of this edition, so it carries no federal remediation deadline. It is listed here as a maximum-severity flaw under active probing, not as a confirmed in-the-wild compromise.

FinanceSocial engineering

Apollo Global says a social-engineering intrusion reached Social Security numbers

Apollo Global Management disclosed that attackers breached its systems in July and accessed personal information, in what the firm describes as a social-engineering incident — manipulating people rather than exploiting a bug — to reach data held in Apollo’s cloud. The investigation found unauthorised access to certain cloud platforms between July 6 and July 10. Apollo says it learned on August 12 that the potentially affected information included names, dates of birth, contact information, home addresses and Social Security numbers. Notification letters to affected individuals are dated August 21. The firm notified law enforcement and engaged outside cybersecurity and forensic experts.

Bloomberg placed the disclosure in a run of attempted or successful cyberattacks against major hedge funds and alternative-asset managers in recent weeks. The pattern is the one running through several items on this page: the initial access is human, not technical, and the exposure sits in a cloud tenancy rather than on a server anyone owns.

Regulation

TikTok to pay $400 million over US child-privacy claims

The Department of Justice announced that ByteDance-owned TikTok will pay $400 million to settle a 2024 lawsuit accusing it of violating child privacy laws. $300 million is payable immediately, with a further $100 million due “upon entry of an order vacating a prior consent decree entered against TikTok’s predecessor, Musical.ly.” The August 2024 complaint, filed with the FTC, alleged the company knowingly let under-13s create accounts and collected data from them in “Kids Mode.”

IoTOperation CameraSwarm

One operator quietly took over 14,500 Dahua cameras

Hunt.io researchers reconstructed a 35-day campaign that compromised more than 14,530 Dahua devices between June 17 and July 22, 2026, working from a 407 MB exposed working directory holding 2,616 files across 234 subdirectories. Three methods ran in parallel: credential attacks, two older authentication-bypass flaws (CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045), and a peer-to-peer relay that reached 283 cameras sitting behind NAT using nothing but a device serial number. A persistent account was configured on 1,923 cameras. Confirmed compromises cluster in Ukraine and Russia; language artifacts point to a Russian-speaking operator, but the activity has not been attributed to any named group or government.

Endpoint

Microsoft Defender’s own driver can be turned against security software

Check Point Research disclosed a technique that uses Defender’s legitimately signed boot-time remediation driver, BTR.sys, to perform arbitrary kernel-level file and registry operations on Windows 7 through Windows 11 25H2 — exploiting no software flaw and importing no outside driver. Because BTR.sys is a required Windows component, it cannot be added to Microsoft’s Vulnerable Driver Blocklist or blocked with WDAC without breaking Defender itself. Jiří Vinopal presented the research at Black Hat USA 2026 and DEF CON 34 and published a proof-of-concept tool, BTR_CLI, on August 20. Check Point said it found no evidence of real-world use.

Supply chainNo CVE

Malicious Rust crates ran code at build time

The Rust Project deleted malicious versions of three widely used crates after a compromised maintainer account published releases that added a typosquatted dependency whose build script downloaded and executed a remote payload during compilation. The affected releases — arrayref 0.3.10, internment 0.8.7 and append-only-vec 0.1.9 — were all published August 20 from the same account and removed within 86 to 107 minutes. Because the code sat in a build script, merely resolving the dependency was enough to run it. There is no patched version and no CVE; developers are advised to search ~/.cargo/registry/cache and pin arrayref at 0.3.9 or earlier.

Supply chainnpm

14 trojanized npm packages drop a Linux backdoor

TrendAI, Trend Micro’s enterprise business, identified 14 npm packages posing as calendar and streak utilities that deliver an AI-powered Linux implant dubbed RedC2 4.0. “When the module loads, it locates the bundled binary, marks it executable, and launches it as a detached background process,” the researchers wrote — no install hook required, and a single import anywhere in the dependency graph, even a transitive one, is enough to execute the payload.

Critical infrastructureJoint advisory

AI-generated exploit scripts are probing US industrial controllers

NSA, CISA, FBI, the Department of Energy and the EPA published a joint advisory warning of an “active threat” against US critical infrastructure using AI-generated exploit scripts disguised as legitimate monitoring tools. The activity targets Siemens S7 Series programmable logic controllers for reconnaissance and capability development, though the agencies assess the broader PLC targeting is not limited to Siemens. Operators are using internet scanning services such as Censys and ZoomEye to find exposed, outdated or poorly protected controllers across critical manufacturing, energy and water.
