The Lead

At the close: stocks finish near flat after erasing a yield-driven morning slide on U.S.–Iran Hormuz talk

Wall Street ended Thursday close to where it began. In preliminary figures shortly after the 4 PM bell, the S&P 500 was up about 0.1% (roughly +10 points), the Nasdaq Composite about 0.1% higher and the Dow Jones Industrial Average about 0.2% lower (roughly −78 points), per Fool.com quote data — a sharp turnaround from the open, when the S&P 500 fell 0.51%, the Dow 0.32% and the Nasdaq 0.78% (TheStreet). The swing came around midday, when sources told Reuters that U.S. and Iranian negotiators meeting in New York are discussing a phased deal under which Tehran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz in exchange for Washington lifting its economic blockade (Benzinga). Official closing levels will be added to the Weekly Scorecard once confirmed.

The bond sell-off remained the day’s other driver: the 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.446%, its highest since June 2004, and the 10-year hit 5.15%, matching a July 2007 high (Motley Fool), while FedWatch odds of an October rate hike stood at 71% (Motley Fool). Oil climbed too: Brent rose about 2.5% to roughly $105.7 a barrel and WTI about 2.3% to roughly $94.3, off a session high of $108.23 for Brent reached after Iran-allied Houthi militants fired missiles at Saudi Arabia (CNBC). On the Dow, Goldman Sachs and Caterpillar did most of the damage in the morning, and Oracle led tech lower on its data-center force-majeure report (Motley Fool). President Trump and Chinese leader Xi Jinping met in Washington; the two sides extended their trade truce by two months past its Nov. 10 expiration (Motley Fool).

Closing figures are preliminary quote data shown shortly after 4 PM ET and may shift slightly as official prints settle. Sources: Motley Fool (Sept 24, with live quotes) · TheStreet live blog (Sept 24) · Benzinga (1:32 PM) · CNBC oil (Sept 24)

Movers & Drivers

+18.1%

Everpure (P)

Rallied 18.1% by early afternoon on forced index-fund buying tied to its promotion into the S&P 500 and expectations for an earnings beat (Benzinga); TheStreet noted the data-storage company reaffirmed fiscal 2027 guidance and said revenue growth will keep accelerating in fiscal 2028.

−10.5%

MGM Resorts (MGM)

Fell 10.5% after Barry Diller’s People Inc. withdrew its long-standing buyout offer of $48.30 a share, with Diller saying “the mix was [not] coming together” as hoped (Benzinga).

−3.75%

Oracle (ORCL)

Down about 3.75% at $139.14 late in the session (Fool.com quote data), after losing as much as 6.44% earlier (TheStreet), on a Bloomberg report that it sent a force-majeure notice to the Blue Owl Capital unit developing its New Mexico “Project Jupiter” data-center campus; Oracle has said the project remains on schedule.

−14.2%

Viking Therapeutics (VKTX)

Sank 14.2% after pricing an upsized $500 million offering of common stock and convertible senior notes (Benzinga).

−12.2%

TD Synnex (SNX)

Slid 12.2% despite beating fiscal third-quarter revenue estimates at $21.6 billion, as gross-margin compression and a $975.6 million free-cash-flow outflow tied to AI-server inventory overshadowed the top line (Benzinga).

+3.5%

Eli Lilly (LLY)

Rose 3.5% after winning FDA approval for its once-weekly insulin, Onswik, and signing a licensing deal with InnoCare worth up to $3.35 billion (Benzinga).

GDDY +3.5%

GoDaddy / Gen Digital

GoDaddy jumped 3.5% after the Financial Times reported Gen Digital made a preliminary all-cash takeover approach valuing it near $12.2 billion; Gen Digital fell 8.2% on financing and dilution concerns (Benzinga).

FSLR ~−8%

Solar stocks

Rising borrowing costs hit project-finance-heavy solar names: First Solar tumbled roughly 8%, SolarEdge about 5% and Enphase around 4% (Benzinga).

+3.4%

Meta Platforms (META)

Up about 3.4% at $769.63 late in the session (Fool.com quote data), after analysts raced to raise price targets on its Muse AI agent (Motley Fool); it had extended its gain to 3.3% by midday after unveiling new VR glasses and monetization plans for its Muse AI agent at Connect (Benzinga) — reversing a premarket dip.
