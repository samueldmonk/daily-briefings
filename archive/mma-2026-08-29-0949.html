Top Story

Song Yadong knocks out Umar Nurmagomedov in Shanghai. The card is complete, and the bantamweight picture just changed.

UFC Fight Night: Nurmagomedov vs Song ran from the Oriental Sports Center in Shanghai's Pudong District — prelims from 3:00 AM ET, main card from 6:00 AM ET, on Paramount+ in the United States. A rare morning card for a U.S. audience, and it finished between this edition and the last one.

The headliner went the home fighter's way. It resolved during the 9:15 edition, not this one, and is repeated here with the finishing sequence now filled in. Song Yadong (23-9-1, 1 NC), the No. 6 bantamweight, knocked out Umar Nurmagomedov (20-1) — the No. 3 contender and a former title challenger — by punch at 1:48 of round two. Nurmagomedov had been a heavy favourite at every book this page recorded, as short as −625; Song had said before the fight that beating him would guarantee a title shot. UFC.com's pre-fight copy listed Nurmagomedov at 20-1, so this is the second defeat of his career — a derivation from the pre-fight record, not a figure any source stated after the fight.

The result that moves a second division came in the co-main. Denise Gomes, the No. 14 strawweight and a +125 underdog against a −150 favourite, stopped Yan Xiaonan — No. 4 in the division and a former title challenger — by knockout with elbows and punches at 4:49 of round one. Gomes had rocked her with an overhand right roughly two minutes in; Yan came straight back at her, and the fight stayed competitive until Gomes landed the finishing sequence in the closing seconds of the round. Gomes entered on a four-fight winning streak.

Around it, the card was violent and it belonged to the newcomers. Ten of the thirteen bouts ended inside the distance — three went to the judges. Francesco Nuzzi, Hector Santiago, Bilal Hasan, Liu Ce and Cam Nelson all won in their first Octagon appearance, four of them by knockout; two of the three Road to UFC winners on the card lost their debuts.

Bilal Hasan

Re-verified against UFC.com's own event page fetched this run, which states each of the following. A Dana White's Contender Series season 10 standout, 9-0 with 1 no contest, fighting out of Everett, Washington by way of Jakarta. He debuted less than three weeks after earning his contract and knocked out fellow unbeaten newcomer Nilson Rojas (9-0 entering) at 2:28 of round two with a right hand over the top followed by a left on the ground — after Rojas had briefly turned the fight with power punches moments before.

Rei Tsuruya

Two first-round submission wins in three months for the 24-year-old, the latest a rear-naked choke of Kevin Borjas at 4:14 of round one after taking him down inside a minute. He has spoken about wanting a place among the flyweight division's rising names; UFC.com's own write-up calls it mission accomplished.

Cam Nelson

The Canadian took the fight on three weeks' notice, out-wrestled Chinese veteran Ding Meng for a unanimous decision, pushed his winning streak to six — and became the first competitor from Prince Edward Island to compete and win inside the Octagon.

Around the Sport

  • Still no bonuses, on a fifth check — and one name is already ruled out. No $100,000 Fight of the Night or Performance of the Night award has been announced for this card in any source fetched this run, several hours after the main event ended. Nothing is guessed here. What is sourced: a live results blog states that Julia Polastri would have won a Performance of the Night bonus for her head-kick knockout had she made weight — the same disqualifying mechanism already recorded for Andre Lima. That is a reporter’s assessment, not an announcement, and it is printed as one. The next edition re-checks.
  • Three fighters missed weight on the same card; two of them are covered here. Andre Lima missed for the second time in his UFC tenure — he still closed the prelims with a third-round guillotine and moved to 5-1 in the promotion after suffering the first loss of his career in June, but is ineligible for a bonus. Julia Polastri also missed; her head-kick knockout levels her UFC record at 3-3 and continues a pattern of alternating results across her appearances.
  • Short notice, long night for the debutants. Hector Santiago, a 33-year-old Brazilian kickboxer, took his debut on short notice and stopped Road to UFC winner Lawrence Lui at 0:53 of round two. Two of the three Road to UFC winners on the card — Lui and Namsrai Batbayar — lost their debuts.
  • The weight problem on this card was collective, not individual. Three fighters missed across thirteen bouts — thirteen is the number of contests on the card, not the number of competitors on it. Lima forfeits 20% of his purse to Batbayar; the Xiong–Polastri bout was re-made as a catchweight after both women came in heavy, which is why that row above carries no divisional label. Batbayar's six-fight winning streak ended with the guillotine.
  • The fastest finish on the main card, described. Kai Asakura landed a head kick roughly thirty seconds into the second round against Aoriqileng, dropped him with it, followed him to the ground and finished with grounded strikes — the official time is 0:34 of round two, which the description and the clock agree on. Aoriqileng was fighting in his own city; Asakura had collected his first UFC win on the same Macau card from which Aoriqileng was rebounding.
  • Sean Woodson's return. Competing for the first time since last April, Woodson dropped Jack Jenkins twice in the opening round, lost the second to Jenkins's grappling, and took a split decision. UFC.com puts him back “on the doorstep of the Top 15”.
  • Spelling splits preserved, not normalised. This page carries both Aoriqileng and Qileng Aori, and both Sumudaerji and Su Mudaerji. UFC.com's article text uses the first form of each while its own athlete URL for the flyweight reads su-mudaerji. Anglicised Chinese names appear in more than one order and this page will not guess a fighter's own preference.
