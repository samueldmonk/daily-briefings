The Lead

A one-day reprieve into the Fed's hardest meeting of the year

US markets are closed for the weekend; the most recent session is Friday 11 September, and it was a rebound. The Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq all snapped four-session losing streaks as oil eased off its spurt and an inflation reading landed close to expectations. Trading Economics’ own news stream carries the session under a headline beginning “Wall Street Ends Four-Day Losing Stre…” — truncated in the listing returned this run, and not completed here.

What set it up was worse. August producer prices rose 5.4% year on year, above the 5.3% consensus and up from July's 4.8%, with wholesale prices +0.4% on the month. Escalating Middle East hostilities pushed US crude up about 4% past $100 and the 10-year Treasury yield touched 4.92% after the report. Rate futures responded: the implied probability of a 25bp hike at the 15–16 September FOMC moved to roughly 70%, from 61.2% a day earlier, and the odds of a second increase in December went to nearly 60%.

The decision lands Wednesday 16 September at 2:00 PM ET, with a press conference at 2:30 and an updated Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot. The current target range is 3.50%–3.75%, held at the July meeting on a 9–3 vote that Chair Kevin Warsh described as a “good family fight.” The data since has cut both ways: July payrolls fell 23,000 with unemployment at 4.1% and May–June revised down a combined 103,000; July CPI was 3.4% y/y with core at 2.5%; July retail sales fell 0.6%, the first decline in nine months.

Two readings of the hike probability are in circulation and both are printed rather than merged: the post-PPI figure above is ~70% (Bloomingbit, IndexBox, CoinCentral, all 11 September), while a separate week-ahead summary returned this run puts it at 85%. This page leads with 70% because that figure is tied to a stated prior (61.2%) and a stated catalyst.

New−2.37%Geopolitics

Crude paused at $100 as Tehran agreed to meet Gulf states

WTI settled at $100.05, down 2.43 (−2.37%), after Iranian state media said Tehran would meet Gulf states in Oman about the Strait of Hormuz. Brent fell 2.81% to $104.61. It was still a 9.7% week for crude — though two Trading Economics items from the same day give the weekly move as 9.7% and 9%, and both are printed rather than merged. WTI is up 20.15% on the month and 59.59% on the year.

NewDemand cutSupply

The IEA cut demand harder than OPEC did

The International Energy Agency sharply lowered its outlook, forecasting a 2.5 million-barrel-a-day contraction in global oil demand in 2026 — the largest annual decline since the Covid-19 pandemic — and warned it could fall further if the Iran conflict persists. OPEC cut its own 2026 demand-growth forecast for a fifth consecutive time. On the supply side the EIA raised its 2027 US crude production forecast to 14.3 million barrels a day.

On the Radar

  • FOMC, Tuesday–Wednesday 15–16 September. Decision and statement Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET, press conference 2:30, plus the Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot. Futures price roughly a 70% chance of a 25bp hike and nearly 60% odds of a second by December.
  • Gulf–Iran talks in Oman, Monday. Gulf Cooperation Council foreign ministers are expected to meet their Iranian counterpart to discuss a temporary arrangement for managing shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. It is the single most direct swing factor for crude this week.
  • The supply picture behind the headline. Saudi Arabia has shut its East–West crude pipeline as a precaution after multiple attacks, and Iran-backed Houthis have reportedly advanced to Yemen's Perim Island, near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait. Separately, China is on track to import roughly the same crude volume in September as in August, recovering from June's decade low.
  • US CPI is the week's other pivotal release, following a PPI print that came in hot.
  • Dreamforce 2026, Tuesday–Thursday in San Francisco — the largest scheduled corporate event of the week.
  • Two other central banks report. The Bank of England and Bank of Japan also decide this week, against the same backdrop of rising oil and mounting inflation pressure.
