The Lead

Stocks off their lows in the early afternoon as the AI trade absorbs a warning from the AI labs

The session opened badly and has been repairing itself since. TheStreet’s opening-bell read at 9:33am had the S&P 500 down 0.75%, the Dow off 0.28%, the Nasdaq Composite down 1.17% and, tellingly, the Russell 2000 up 0.45%. By 11:26am the S&P was off 0.65% with just 233 holdings advancing. The Motley Fool’s midday card, stamped 11:38am, had the S&P and the Nasdaq Composite both down 0.44% and the Dow down 0.36%. The same publisher’s live quote strip, rendered when this edition was built, showed the S&P off 0.29%, the Nasdaq Composite off 0.34% and the Dow off just 0.05%. Every reading is printed as its own timestamped snapshot; none is averaged into a single “session” figure.

The cause is unchanged from the open. Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei published an essay urging the industry to slow frontier-model development on safety grounds; OpenAI’s Sam Altman backed it, warning the pace “could go very badly” with humans losing control, and Elon Musk backed it too. Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned last week alleging both companies are “gambling with our lives.” President Trump rejected the framing on Truth Social, calling a “STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT” the only guardrail AI needs and describing a “SICK conspiracy” against AI and data centres. Semiconductors sold off anyway.

Two other pressures are stacked on top. Oil kept climbing after Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia’s King Khalid Air Base, the capture of Perim and the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands, and a drone strike that prompted Saudi Arabia to shut its East-West pipeline — the workaround for the closed Strait of Hormuz. Oman postponed its Hormuz talks with Iran and set no new date. And the 10-year Treasury yield rose more than 2 basis points to around 5%, its highest since October 2023, two days before a Fed decision the market expects to be a hike.

On the Radar

  • FOMC, 15–16 September. Decision and press conference Wednesday, with the Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has said he will not provide forward guidance. Monday and Tuesday carry no major US data.
  • Consumer sentiment. September’s preliminary University of Michigan index came in at 47.8, its second-lowest reading on record, against 51.5 expected, 51.7 in August and 55.1 a year earlier; year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.6% from 4.0%. The final reading lands 25 September.
  • The counter-thesis. BofA Securities remains bullish on semiconductors, calling the frontier-lab controversy “noise” against an AI-capex market it expects to surge roughly threefold to $3tn+ by decade-end, while flagging near-term volatility from rates, Mideast tensions and the 2 November US midterms.
  • Oil forecasts are moving. HSBC has raised its oil forecast as the Hormuz backup route burns, per a TheStreet headline read this run. No revised price target appeared in the material read, so none is stated.
  • SpaceX. SPCX fell 2% to $148.16 premarket amid the AI-linked selloff and post-IPO lockup releases; David Einhorn’s Greenlight second-quarter letter called the $1.75 trillion IPO valuation a marker that a “speculative top is near.” Sunday’s SES launch was SpaceX’s 700th Falcon flight (687 Falcon 9, 13 Falcon Heavy).
