The Lead

Oil retreats, and the rally broadens by mid-morning — index figures as of the 9:31 AM ET open, single names as of 10:28 AM ET

Stocks rose at the opening bell on Monday as crude retreated. At the open the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up 0.61%, the S&P 500 up 0.67% and the Nasdaq up 0.77%, while the Russell 2000 slipped 0.50% (TheStreet opening-bell entry, 9:31 AM ET). No later index reading appeared in anything read this run, so the open still stands as the timestamped figure. Semiconductors led: the PHLX Semiconductor Index climbed more than 2% on Monday morning to its highest price since 9 September.

What has changed since the last edition is the size of the single-name moves. TheStreet’s market-movers entry, timestamped 10:28 AM ET, has Warner Bros. Discovery up 9.77%, Intel up 9.7%, AMD up 9.17% and Paramount Skydance up 9.1% — each materially larger than the same names carried on the 8:42 AM premarket list. On the other side, Novo Nordisk is down 8.01%, roughly double its premarket decline.

Oil is the relief valve. West Texas Intermediate fell 2.50% to $97.79 a barrel and Brent sank 2.49% to $101.30 as of 7:42 AM ET, and Brent has now fallen for a fourth straight day — its longest such streak in three months. The supply picture is easing: Saudi Arabia expects to restore about half of its East-West pipeline capacity within days, its crude exports have surged past 4 million barrels a day this month against a 13-year low of 2.4 million in August, and shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have reached a six-month high. JPMorgan analysts put total Gulf oil flows at 17.1 million barrels a day over the past 10 days.

“Markets are starting the week on a more constructive footing, with falling oil prices helping revive risk appetite after several weeks dominated by inflation and interest-rate concerns,” said Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com, who added that Asian equities advanced overnight led by technology and semiconductor stocks. She cautioned that none of it resolves the Middle East conflict and that Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure remain an obvious risk.

The backdrop is last week’s Federal Reserve rate hike — a 25-basis-point move to a 3.75%–4.00% target range on 16 September, the first increase since July 2023, passed 12–0, with the Fed signalling at least one more this year. Stocks finished mixed on Friday and the Dow booked its third straight weekly loss. Ahead this week: a Trump–Xi summit in Washington on Thursday, 24 September, covering tariffs, critical minerals and artificial intelligence.

What this desk would not print as current: a second, untimed account of Monday morning — carrying the S&P 500 up 0.6%, the Nasdaq Composite up 0.8–0.9% and the Dow up 153 points, or 0.3%, with crude down 3% to above $96 — surfaced in the previous edition and is retained here as a second reading rather than substituted for the timestamped figures above; it was not restated in anything read this run. New this run and reproduced as its own claim: one account says the advance erased the S&P 500’s decline for the month, with no monthly percentage attached because none was stated.

Movers & Drivers

+9.77%

Warner Bros. Discovery leads on merger progress

WBD is the largest single-name gain on TheStreet’s 10:28 AM ET movers list, up from +7.12% premarket, after The Wall Street Journal reported progress in negotiations aimed at resolving legal challenges to its proposed merger with Paramount Skydance. CNN reports Paramount is in advanced talks with state attorneys general to settle the antitrust suit: California’s Rob Bonta, who leads the 12-state coalition, has pressed for a deal, while New York’s Letitia James is resisting the terms and seeking additional worker protections, and Connecticut plus at least two other states are not yet on board.

+9.7%

Intel surges on the AUO Optronics tie-up

The chipmaker extended its premarket gain of 5.76% following reports that it is partnering with the Taiwanese display-panel manufacturer AUO Optronics to jointly develop Micro LED advanced packaging technology.

New+9.17%

AMD jumps on reported AI accelerator price rises

Advanced Micro Devices climbed following reports that the chipmaker plans to raise prices by 10% on some AI accelerators and GPUs. AMD is new to this desk’s movers list this edition — it did not appear on the 8:42 AM premarket entry.

New+9.1%

Paramount Skydance moves with its merger partner

PSKY rose alongside WBD on the same report of progress toward resolving legal opposition to the $81 billion combination, against +6% on the premarket list.

−8.01%

Novo Nordisk’s decline roughly doubles

The drugmaker fell further after its long-term strategy and obesity ambitions failed to ease investor concerns about its ability to compete in an increasingly crowded weight-loss market, per CNBC. The premarket reading was −4.35%.

−3.39%

HP warns on the 2027 PC market

HP fell after warning that it expects global PC unit volumes to shrink by mid-single digits in calendar 2027 compared with 2026. Its decline has narrowed from −4.75% premarket.

New−2.78%

UPS slips on dividend and cash-flow concerns

United Parcel Service fell amid investor concerns about its unusually high dividend yield and tight free cash flow. UPS is new to this list this edition.

On the Radar

  • Trump–Xi summit, Thursday 24 September, Washington. Talks are expected to cover extending the tariff truce, critical minerals and artificial intelligence. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent met Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng ahead of the visit, called the preliminary talks “very successful” and proposed a US–China AI safety notification mechanism covering incidents that rise to a national-security level; Xinhua acknowledged only that the two sides discussed AI. The South China Morning Post reports the two sides are discussing announcements on military-to-military ties. Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, OpenAI’s Sam Altman, former Apple CEO Tim Cook, Qualcomm’s Cristiano Amon and Microsoft’s Satya Nadella are set to attend a dinner with Xi.
  • UN General Assembly, Tuesday. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian will lead a delegation to New York amid renewed hopes for a diplomatic track, with Trump indicating he is willing to meet him. Iranian state agency IRNA says the US has refused visas to certain members of that delegation, including its communications team.
  • Oura files to go public. The smart-ring maker and some backers are seeking as much as $2.2 billion, marketing 50 million shares at $40–$44 under the ticker “OURA”; at the top of the range that is a $14.1 billion market value, against about $11 billion at its 2025 Series E. The company sold about 3.6 million rings in the past year, retailing between $399 and $499. Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Allen & Company and Jefferies are leading the offering.
  • AI liability. Bessent told CNBC that AI developers “need to take responsibility for themselves” rather than expect a federal liability shield: “It is humans who are responsible, not the AI.”
  • Press access litigation. CNN, MS NOW and Politico have sued to overturn their White House ban; the four other members of the TV press pool say they will stop covering the President’s events after CNN was barred from serving as designated pooler.
