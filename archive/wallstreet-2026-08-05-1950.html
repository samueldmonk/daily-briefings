The Lead

Official close: the Dow grinds to a third straight record at 54,349.12 while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq snap their four-day run

Wednesday split the tape. The Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 263.24 points, or 0.49%, to a record close of 54,349.12 — its third consecutive record finish — while the S&P 500 pulled back 0.17% to 7,723.55 from Tuesday's all-time high and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.83% to 26,363.44, ending a four-day winning streak for both (Yahoo Finance coverage; CNBC's evening headline: "Stock futures are little changed after blue-chip Dow posts new record close"). CNBC's session banner credited Nvidia with boosting the Dow as the broader rally lost momentum. Earlier in the session the S&P 500 printed a fresh all-time intraday high of 7,793.68 and the Dow was up as much as 456 points before the afternoon fade (TheStreet live blog, direct fetch this edition). Every close reconciles exactly with Tuesday's verified finish plus the quoted change — the Dow's to the penny (54,085.88 + 263.24).

The macro tape leaned soft. ADP reported private employers added just 44,000 jobs in July, below the roughly 65,000 economists expected (Bloomberg consensus via Yahoo Finance; CNBC), with all of the gain coming from services (+47,000) while goods producers shed 3,000; annual pay rose 4.4%, and pay growth for job-switchers accelerated to 7%, the fastest in nearly a year (ADP). The ISM services PMI edged up to 54.1 from June's 54.0, and the 10-year Treasury yield slipped toward 4.6%, a third straight decline, as reports of an imminent U.S.–Iran interim agreement to reopen the Strait of Hormuz kept pressure on oil and inflation expectations (Trading Economics). Yahoo's live banner summed up the equity story: the Dow's record came "on hopes for an Iran deal."

Earnings did the sorting. Eli Lilly added more than 5% after beating revenue consensus and raising full-year guidance, and Arista Networks soared as much as 15% on its revenue beat, while SpaceX slumped 10% — erasing Tuesday's pre-earnings surge, with a large slug of IPO shares soon to be freed for sale — and AMD sank roughly 8% despite doubling data-center revenue (Trading Economics summary; Motley Fool's midday wrap: "Eli Lilly Pops and AMD Drops in Mixed Morning Markets"). After the bell, the sell-the-guidance mood continued: AppLovin cratered 22% and DoorDash fell 5% (see After-Hours Movers below).

▲ Follow-through

Arista Networks (ANET)

Tuesday's after-hours beat carried into the regular session — Arista soared as much as 15% Wednesday after posting better-than-expected revenue (Trading Economics summary), extending the roughly 11% after-hours jump on its $1.02 EPS / $3.04B quarter (CNBC, Aug 4).

▼ Reversal

SpaceX (SPCX)

The June IPO slumped double digits, erasing its surge from the prior session — reads ran from −10% on Trading Economics' summary to "tumbling nearly 12%" on TheStreet's live blog — as heavy AI spending and capital expenditures overshadowed its Q2 earnings beat, with a large portion of IPO shares soon to be released for sale (Trading Economics; TheStreet). TheStreet notes the stock traded below its $135 June IPO price, far off the $225.64 record it touched June 16. It fell 7% in Tuesday's extended session after reporting 38 launches vs. 43 anticipated.

▼ Semis

AMD (AMD)

Extended its post-earnings slide into the regular session, sinking about 8% despite doubling data-center revenues in Q2 (Trading Economics summary); an afternoon movers read had it down 6.5% in active turnover (MarketChameleon). The $11.54B revenue quarter and ~$13B Q3 guide weren't enough for a stock priced for more.

Insulet (PODD)

Shares of the medical-device maker sank 17% after it lowered full-year 2026 revenue-growth guidance and cut its U.S. Omnipod sales outlook (MarketChameleon movers report) — one of the session's sharpest earnings punishments.

The IT-solutions provider fell 12.8% as investors reacted to squeezed profitability and news of an upcoming executive departure (MarketChameleon movers report).

AppLovin (APP)

Cratered 22% after the bell.
