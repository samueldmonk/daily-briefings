The lead

The Treasury blinked at the long end — and as of this edition, roughly 11:15 AM ET, the tape turned with it, while Moderna more than doubled on a cancer-vaccine result

Wednesday reversed Tuesday’s two problems at once. US stocks rose after the US Treasury Department said it would increase buybacks of long-dated government debt, pulling yields sharply back, and after President Trump paused the tariffs on Canadian goods that were scheduled to take effect at midnight. Yahoo Finance’s live Wednesday coverage has the Dow up about 0.4% and the S&P 500 up about 0.5%, with the Nasdaq Composite edging above the flat line amid continued weakness in chip stocks; a second morning read of the same session renders it S&P 500 +0.43%, Nasdaq +0.40%, Dow +0.25%. Both are given, because they disagree at the margin and neither is stamped to the minute. What is not in dispute is the direction: this is the first up-tape of the week after three straight down days.

The buyback is the mechanism. The yield on the 30-year US bond fell nine basis points to 5.20% as the Treasury said it will at least double the “liquidity support” buyback operations for longer-dated debt, to at least $4 billion per operation. That matters because the long end has been the single input driving equities all week: on Tuesday the 30-year topped 5.33%, its highest level since April 2007, and long-duration equities were marked down accordingly. Take nine basis points out of the 30-year and the pressure on the most extended part of the equity market eases mechanically, without anything changing about earnings.

The single biggest move on the board is not macro at all. Moderna and Merck announced that their individualised mRNA cancer vaccine, intismeran, combined with Merck’s Keytruda, met the primary endpoint and a key secondary endpoint in the pivotal Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial in patients with surgically removed high-risk melanoma — 1,137 patients with completely resected stage IIB–IV cutaneous melanoma. It is the first positive Phase 3 result for an individualised neoantigen therapy and, by the companies’ framing, for any mRNA-based cancer treatment. CNBC’s headline has Moderna stock more than doubling; the reported morning moves range from about 90% (Forbes, GuruFocus) to 93% at roughly $121.90 (24/7 Wall St.) to more than 120% at the open, briefly $139.70 (Quartz/BigGo), with one live read this run at +132.89%. The spread is wide enough that no single figure is asserted here — every read is given with its source. It is on course for the stock’s best session on record. Merck rose too, rendered between 6% and 7% in the reporting and over 11% to $150.59 in one account.

Underneath the rally, the semiconductor damage has not been repaired — it has been exported. Asia took Tuesday’s US chip rout and amplified it overnight: South Korea’s KOSPI fell between 5% and 5.4% depending on the read, with one account describing a drop of over 6% that triggered a circuit breaker, and Japan’s Nikkei fell 3%–3.2%. Samsung Electronics closed down between 7.54% and 7.82% — the larger figure was accompanied by an estimate of roughly KRW 135 trillion erased from its market capitalisation — and SK Hynix fell between 9.75% and 9.93%. Japan’s benchmark long-term rate briefly touched 2.945%, a fresh roughly-30-year high. The read-through is that the memory complex, not the index, is where the global repricing is happening.

The retail slate delivered the week’s cleanest divergence. Target reported net sales of $26.5 billion, up 5.3%, comparable sales up 3.8% on a 3.6% traffic gain, digital comps up 8.7%, operating income nearly doubling to $2.56 billion and diluted EPS of $4.11 against $2.05 — and raised full-year guidance to about 5% sales growth and $9.90–$10.90 EPS. The caveat matters: a $994 million tariff refund accounted for 40% of the quarter’s EPS. Lowe’s went the other way, posting $25.96 billion in sales against $23.96 billion a year earlier but comparable sales up only 0.2%, and trimmed its full-year outlook to $92 billion in sales, flat comps and $12.25 adjusted EPS. TJX raised its FY2027 profit forecast to $5.31–$5.36 from $5.08–$5.15 but guided Q3 adjusted EPS to $1.30–$1.32 ex-tariff-benefit against a $1.35 consensus. The consumer is holding; the margin story is being written by tariffs, in both directions.

Still ahead today: the FOMC minutes from the July meeting, which will show how close the committee came to moving and in which direction — the Fed left rates unchanged last month with three officials dissenting in favour of a rate hike.

MRVL +11%

Marvell — Google gets the right to buy $12.2 billion of the company

Marvell issued Alphabet’s Google a warrant to buy up to 58.97 million Marvell shares at $206.58 each — about $12.18 billion if fully exercised — as part of a deal to develop custom silicon for Google. Shares jumped more than 11% in pre-market trading. The structure is the interesting part: most of the warrant vests only if Google meets agreed purchasing targets through fiscal 2033, tying the size of the stake to how much Google actually buys. A holding of that size would make Google Marvell’s fifth-largest investor. Marvell will build AI inference accelerators, storage, networking, memory-interface controllers and near-memory computing technologies for Google — a hyperscaler paying a merchant-silicon vendor in its own equity to keep it aligned.
