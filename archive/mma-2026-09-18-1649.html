Top story

Everyone made weight — and the fight that ended in 26 seconds finally gets its second act

Joshua Van and Alexandre Pantoja each weighed 125 pounds at Friday’s official weigh-ins in Los Angeles, making Saturday’s flyweight championship rematch official at Crypto.com Arena. All 24 scheduled athletes recorded weights inside their divisional limits — a clean board, which is rarer than it sounds on a card this size.

The rematch has been nine months in the making for a plain reason: the first fight lasted 26 seconds. At UFC 323 in December 2025, Van took the belt by technical knockout when Pantoja suffered an arm injury that early, which is the kind of ending that settles a title and answers nothing. Pantoja gets the answer on Saturday.

The market makes it close: Van −138, Pantoja +108 on the fight-week board. That is a champion favoured by barely more than a coin flip — the market pricing the 26 seconds as close to no information at all.

Weigh-in figures and the 26-second detail are from Yahoo Sports’ weigh-in coverage read this run; odds are the Forbes/Yahoo early fight-week board published 15 September and are attributed as such rather than presented as the current line.

Prospect watch

Prospect

Sean King III — 33 seconds on debut

Needed just 33 seconds to finish Jessie Rosas with a slam on his UFC debut at Noche UFC. A debut that ends inside a minute by a method that rarely ends anything is the sort of thing that buys a fighter a real opponent next time out.

Prospect

David Martinez — the 135-pound climb

The Mexican bantamweight contender welcomed former featherweight contender Dan Ige to 135 pounds with a dominant unanimous decision, continuing his rise up the division. Contender, not challenger — no title fight is claimed for either man.
