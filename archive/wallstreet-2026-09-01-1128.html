The Lead

War risk reprices the open: crude and yields up, stocks down as September begins (as of ~11:30 AM ET)

The United States and Iran returned to open hostilities over the weekend. U.S. forces struck an Iranian island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday and Tehran retaliated against U.S. military targets in the Middle East; President Trump told a Fox News reporter “We’re going to hit them hard… There will be a response.” After six months of war the conflict remains at an impasse, with Tehran keeping the Strait closed and Washington maintaining a counter-blockade of Iranian ports (AFP via Yahoo Finance). Schwab’s market-open note adds an overnight attack on a cargo ship transiting the Strait. Two further supply facts landed this run: a supertanker caught fire in the Strait after striking two naval mines, and refinery strikes in Russia have tightened global refining capacity, driving refined-product margins to fresh highs (Trading Economics).

That pushed the inflation trade back to the front. Futures were already soft before the bell — S&P 500 futures −0.57%, Dow futures −0.53% and Nasdaq-100 futures −1.02% at ~5:00 AM ET (Yahoo Finance) — and the weakness carried into the session. The session now has a shape rather than a single snapshot. Right after the opening bell the S&P 500 was down 0.7% and the Nasdaq Composite 1.3%, with the Dow off 363 points, or 0.7%. By late morning the Dow had recovered to a loss of roughly 0.1%, the S&P 500 was down about 0.3% and the Nasdaq Composite around 0.7% (Yahoo Finance live blog). Every one of those figures is intraday and none is a close; no September 1 index level is published on this page.

The macro backdrop: the 10-year Treasury yield is quoted at 4.75% (Yahoo Finance) to 4.78%, up 0.03 (Schwab) — around its highest since January 2025. WTI crude was quoted at $87.92, up $2.16 or 2.55%, in Schwab’s market-open note and at $87.75, +2.32%, on the October contract (Yahoo); a later Trading Economics read has WTI at $86.57 (+0.94%) and Brent at $91.28 (+0.87%). Those are different clocks on the same up-move, not competing claims, and both are printed. The Cboe Volatility Index is up roughly 6–7% to about 15.8–16.0.

Movers & Drivers

Single stock

GoPro +46.06%

Quoted at $0.8762, up $0.2763, on Yahoo Finance’s trending-ticker board, after surging roughly 79% in the premarket. The move has a filing behind it: an SEC Schedule 13G dated August 20 shows YouTube creator Markiplier (Mark Fischbach) holding 13.5 million GoPro Class A shares, an 8.5% stake worth roughly $9.3 million — the company’s largest position. He has said he viewed the stock as undervalued and wants to help turn the company around. The stock had already risen 22% to $0.73 in Monday’s midday trading as the news landed.

Energy

Oil majors lead +2.1% / +2.7%

Chevron advanced 2.1% and ExxonMobil 2.7%. The Energy Select Sector SPDR (XLE) hit a new all-time intraday high dating to its 1998 inception, up nearly 1%; the Vanguard Energy ETF (VDE) also notched a fresh all-time intraday high, up about 1%.

DragSemis

Chips and megacap tech −1% to −2%

Nvidia, Advanced Micro Devices and Micron Technology were all down around 2%; Microsoft lost more than 1%, as did Alphabet. Schwab: “Tech took the brunt of the blow, especially chip stocks.”

The session’s top S&P 500 gainers also included CrowdStrike +3.48% and CF Industries +3.46%.

  • The rate debate has a date, a size and — at last — attributed probabilities. The FOMC next meets September 15–16, and the move under discussion is a 25 basis-point hike: the size this page has left deliberately blank for three editions is now sourced. The probabilities do not agree and are printed as they were found: CME FedWatch has been cited at a 66% chance of a quarter-point hike; Kalshi shows 57% for a 25bp hike against 42% for no change; and a separate prediction-market read puts a hold at 56.8%. Those last two flatly contradict each other and neither is adopted here. The arc is clearer than the level: before Chair Kevin Warsh’s Jackson Hole speech the market put a September hold at nearly 70%; immediately after it, Kalshi had a 25bp hike at 48%. Fed funds futures late Friday implied 57% for a hike. Backdrop: the July 29 hold left the target range at 3.50%–3.75% with three policymakers dissenting in favour of a 25bp hike.
