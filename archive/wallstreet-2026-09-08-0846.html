The Lead

Oil at the door of $100 sets the tone for the first bell since Labor Day — as of ~8:35 AM ET, pre-open

Regular trading resumes at 9:30 AM ET today, the first cash session since Friday, and the setup is a geopolitical one rather than an economic one. Brent crude is up 1.45% at $99.73 and WTI up 1.50% at $94.28, with the move attributed to Houthi attacks on Saudi energy facilities and to wider Middle East tensions. Read on a screen from roughly 8:04 AM ET, Dow futures were down 0.78%, S&P 500 futures down 0.28% and Nasdaq-100 futures down 0.06%.

Those futures figures have moved during the morning and this page prints the drift rather than smoothing it. An earlier read carried by this desk at around 8:05 AM gave Dow futures down 0.6–0.7% against a shallower S&P decline and a fractionally positive Nasdaq-100. The direction has not changed — the Dow is the weakest of the three in both reads — but the size has, and the later read is the one the lead uses. Separately, Trading Economics has its US500 index at 7,707, off 0.15% from the previous session.

The second story of the morning is trade. Canada has followed through on its retaliation, imposing duties of up to 50% on a range of US goods after talks collapsed, affecting about $20 billion of US imports and taking effect today. Between crude and tariffs, both of the day's drivers push in the same direction on inflation — which matters more than usual with August CPI due Friday and a Fed meeting the week after.

ExpandedTrade

Canada's tariffs bite today

Duties of up to 50% on a variety of US goods, covering about $20 billion of imports, take effect Tuesday after trade talks collapsed. This is the retaliation Canada had threatened rather than a new escalation, but the effective date is today, which puts it in this session rather than the last one. Tagged Expanded, not New: the $20 billion figure and today's effective date were both on this page's previous edition; the up to 50% rate and the collapse of the talks are what is added.
