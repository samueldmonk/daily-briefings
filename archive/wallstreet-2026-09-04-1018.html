The Lead

Good news is bad news: a 162,000-job August splits the tape three ways — as of ~9:56 AM ET

The Labor Department reported that U.S. employers added 162,000 jobs in August, decisively beating a consensus estimate of 55,000. The unemployment rate was unchanged at 4.1%. Stocks did not rally on it. “One of the head-scratching terms that come out of Wall Street is that ‘Good news is bad news,’ and that’s the case this morning,” said Chris Zaccarelli, chief investment officer for Northlight Asset Management, “where a great Jobs report – 162k jobs created vs 55k expected – had an instant reaction of stocks and bonds selling off.”

The session in two timestamped moments, printed as a sequence rather than a snapshot — plus a third reading that carries no time at all. At the 9:35 AM ET opening bell (TheStreet): S&P 500 −0.05%, Dow −0.16%, Nasdaq +0.09%, Russell 2000 −1.40%. At ~9:56 AM ET — a Yahoo Finance market strip that self-dates through its own “U.S. markets close in 6h 4m” counter — S&P 500 7,741.86 −5.85 / −0.08%; Dow 53,509.14 −176.97 / −0.33%; Nasdaq Composite 26,615.08 +31.02 / +0.12%; Russell 2000 2,954.93 −13.34 / −0.45%; VIX 14.13 −0.19 / −1.33%. The three index triples were arithmetic-checked against Thursday’s published closes and all three reconcile exactly (7,741.86 + 5.85 = 7,747.71; 53,509.14 + 176.97 = 53,686.11; 26,615.08 − 31.02 = 26,584.06). The Russell and VIX triples are internally consistent but reconcile against prior levels this page has not published as closes, so they are given as the source reported them.

The two sources disagree on the Russell 2000 and neither reading is adopted. TheStreet’s opening-bell entry has the small-cap index down 1.40%; the Yahoo strip about twenty minutes later has it down 0.45%. Both are printed with their times. A third reading returned this run — S&P 7,739.40 −8.31 / −0.11%, Dow 53,512.64 −173.47 / −0.32%, Nasdaq 26,600.53 +16.47 / +0.06% — is internally consistent and reconciles to Thursday’s closes, but carries no timestamp, so it is logged as a third moment and not dated.

The Fed is the whole argument. “The August jobs report was much better than expected, focusing the Fed squarely on controlling inflation when they meet next in September,” said Bill Adams, chief U.S. economist at Fifth Third Commercial Bank. “The next Fed decision will be finely balanced: Next week’s releases of the CPI and PPI reports have the power to decide whether the Fed hikes or holds.” Pricing for a September hike sits at roughly 50% after Governor Christopher Waller’s Thursday remarks, per Kyle Rodda of Capital.com. The FOMC meets September 15–16.

One more session, then a long weekend. U.S. stock and bond markets are closed Monday, September 7 for Labor Day.

GameStop into Tuesday’s print

GME +1.30% to $19.48 (TheStreet, 9:10 AM ET). Second-quarter earnings land Tuesday, September 8; the company guides quarterly net income of $290–310 million against $168.6 million a year ago. +6.1% (premarket) after the supervisory board approved Future Plan 2030 on Thursday, twelve initiatives the company calls the most strategically profound transformation in its 89-year history. The plan cuts around 50,000 positions including management roles, on top of 50,000 already approved — 100,000 in total.
