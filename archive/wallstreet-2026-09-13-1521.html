The Lead

Markets are closed for the weekend. Friday ended a four-day slide across all three indexes — and the week ahead hands the tape to four central banks in eight days.

There is no live U.S. session to report: it is Sunday, and the most recent close is Friday 11 September. On that session the S&P 500 rose 65.28 points, or 0.86%, to 7,656.98; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 509.19 points, or 0.98%, to 52,573.29; and the Nasdaq composite rose 251.31 points, or 0.96%, to 26,333.04. All three halted four sessions of losses as oil prices and Treasury yields paused their surge — and all three still finished the week lower: S&P 500 −0.8%, Dow −1.6%, Nasdaq −0.7%.

Those two facts are not in tension; they are the same week seen at two lengths, which is why the scorecard below carries both. The week-ahead note read in full this run describes the index as having been on track for its deepest four-day decline since June, with WTI crude above $100 and the US 10-year yield closing in on 5%, before Friday’s buyers stepped in. It attributes the losing week to a warmer-than-expected producer price index, surging energy prices and higher bond yields.

The week ahead is not really an equity story. Four major central banks report inside eight days and the ECB has already gone, raising by 25 basis points on 10 September with hawkish revisions to its inflation forecasts. The Fed decides Wednesday at 2:00 p.m. ET; the Bank of England follows Thursday; the Bank of Japan Friday. The Fed starts from 3.50–3.75%, held on 29 July on a 9–3 vote with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan dissenting for a hike, and a move Wednesday takes the range to 3.75–4.00%.

The hike is close to fully priced, and the number that priced it was one-tenth of a percentage point. Futures imply roughly a 90% chance of a hike; the finer reading, from the CME FedWatch Tool, is 88% after Friday’s CPI print, from about 71% late the previous day. What moved it was core CPI at +0.3% month-on-month against a +0.2% consensus — unrounded, 0.289%, so not a rounding artefact. The same release contains the one figure a dissenter would reach for: annual core CPI fell to 2.4%, a five-year low. The monthly print moved the odds; the annual one argues the other way, and this page prints both rather than picking.

A note on what is not here. Because it is a weekend, there are no intraday levels, no session movers and no after-hours block — the last of those belongs only to a trading day after 4 p.m. ET. Every single-name move named below is explicitly a Friday-morning quote, because that is the timestamp on the note it came from. This page does not promote a morning quote to a close.

The ECB has already moved, and hawkishly

The European Central Bank raised its key lending rates by 25 basis points on 10 September, as expected, and made hawkish revisions to its inflation forecasts. It is the first of four major central-bank decisions inside eight days — ECB, then Fed, BoE and BoJ.

PPI 5.4% y/yCarried

Producer prices, not consumer prices, did the week’s damage

August PPI accelerated to 5.4% year-on-year from 4.8%, above the 5.3% expected, and core PPI rose to 4.6% from 4.2%, in line. Some components point to a firmer PCE reading later this month — which is the number the Fed actually targets. Initial jobless claims eased by 1,000 to 205,000.

On the Radar

  • Monday 14 and Tuesday 15 September — quiet by design. The week-ahead note lists no major U.S. earnings or data on either day. The week’s risk is back-loaded.
  • Wednesday 16 September — the Fed, and the only U.S. event that matters this week. FOMC announces at 2:00 p.m. ET. Futures price roughly a 90% chance of a 25 bp hike to 3.75–4.00%; the finer reading is 88% after Friday’s CPI, up from about 71% late the day before, per the CME FedWatch Tool. August retail sales land the same morning, and Lennar (LEN) reports.
  • Thursday 17 September — housing, and the Bank of England. August housing starts and building permits. The BoE decides with Bank Rate at 3.75% and only a 25% chance of a hike priced; a full 25 bp is priced for November and another for December, which would put Bank Rate at 4.25% by year end. Governor Andrew Bailey has pushed back on the idea that a hike is only a matter of time.
  • Friday 18 September — the Bank of Japan, the most confidently priced of the three on the finer Fed reading. Markets price a 90% chance of a 25 bp hike to 1.25%, with a second rise to 1.50% around 70% priced by December. Japan’s August inflation lands the same day. Note the ordering: the BoJ is priced higher than the Fed’s own 88%, by two points.
  • Tuesday 15 September — China’s monthly batch. August industrial production, retail sales and year-to-date fixed asset investment, plus the house price index.
  • The set-up going in. The S&P 500 closed Thursday at its 50-day moving average of 7,590, its first break since late July, with RSI at 43 from 65-plus and 100-day support just below 7,500. The SOX semiconductor index sits more than 20% below its June peak.
