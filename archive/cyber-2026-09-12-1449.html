Top Story

NewAI-orchestratedActively exploited

Hundreds of AI agents ran a global PaperCut exploitation campaign from an empty workspace in four hours

A threat actor GreyNoise describes as likely Russian-speaking used hundreds of AI agents to build, test and refine exploits for CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 — an authentication bypass and remote code execution chain in PaperCut NG/MF print-management servers. The campaign began 31 August, combining OpenAI's Codex harness with a DeepSeek model and commodity offensive tooling, and generated target lists through the Netlas internet-scanning platform.

GreyNoise data indicates the operation compromised at least 440 PaperCut instances linked to 395 distinct organisations across 48 countries. The attacker harvested credentials from 280 victims, obtained operating-system or domain secrets from 147, and reached administrator privileges at 12 organisations. Most victims were in the education sector, roughly half of all breaches; the United States was the most-targeted country, followed by the UK, France, Spain and Canada.

The speed is the story. GreyNoise records the adversary going from an empty workspace to first RCE against a real victim in just under four hours, first domain admin two hours after that, and — once the campaign launched — at least 11 organisations compromised in 26 seconds. In one case the actor went from initial access to full domain administrator in seven minutes against a high school in the United States.

Three post-exploitation paths were observed: dumping LSASS memory and registry secrets from domain-joined PaperCut servers then passing recovered hashes to domain controllers; the noPac attack against environments still vulnerable to CVE-2021-42278 and CVE-2021-42287; and directly adding a new account to Domain Admins where PaperCut ran on a domain controller or under a domain administrator service account. In all cases the attackers used DCSync to obtain a full NTDS.DIT dump. The toolkit included Ligolo-ng, Mimikatz, Certipy, BloodHound, Rubeus, Impacket, NetExec and custom Rust credential-collection utilities.

Two details are worth stating precisely. GreyNoise could not determine the campaign's objective — the access could serve data theft or ransomware, and neither is asserted here. And the actor supplied the agents a list of countries to avoid — Russia, China, Iran, Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Brazil and South Africa — which the agents did not consistently follow. PaperCut's emergency updates and its 27 August security bulletin are the remediation.

CVE-2026-20079 — Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center — CVSS 10.0 — federal deadline 12 September, today

An unauthenticated authentication bypass (CWE-288) that executes scripts as root. Added to the KEV catalog 9 September with a three-day window that closes today. Cisco Talos attributes exploitation to three clusters: UAT-11988 (Qilin ransomware affiliates), UAT-11823 (tooling overlapping Sandworm, deploying a Cyclops Blink variant via a Netcat reverse shell in license.tmp run as root through package_info.pl) and UAT-12197 (a JSP web shell, cmd.jar). The companion flaw CVE-2026-20316 is only CVSS 5.3 but Cisco rates it High because it chains — and it is the one Qilin actually used for entry.

Hot fixes cover branches 7.0, 7.2, 7.4, 7.6, 7.7 and 10.0. Affected branches are 6.4, 7.0, 7.1, 7.2, 7.3, 7.4, 7.6, 7.7 and 10.0 — so 6.4, 7.1 and 7.3 are not on the hot-fix list, with Cisco noting a comprehensive release follows. Hot fixes do not remediate an existing compromise.

Why these windows differ

BOD 22-01 is revoked. CISA's BOD 26-04, issued 10 June 2026, replaced the uniform three-week window with a 16-tier remediation matrix keyed to whether an asset is publicly exposed, whether the CVE is in KEV, and whether exploitation can be fully automated. The most dangerous tier must be remediated within three days, with mandatory forensic triage; agencies must be operating to these timelines by 7 December 2026.

The catalog now shows five add dates demonstrating that the interval tracks the individual CVE rather than the batch: 9 September produced both a three-day deadline (Cisco, Citrix, Fortinet) and a fourteen-day one (Chromium) from the same alert; 10 September and 11 September each produced three days; 4 and 8 September each produced fourteen. A descriptive pattern, offered as five observations and not as a CISA rule: three-day deadlines have so far attached to perimeter network and security appliances and to unauthenticated maximum-severity flaws, fourteen-day ones to browser and local-privilege bugs. GitLab — CVSS 10.0 and exploitable without authentication — fits that shape.

The corollary matters for triage: KEV entry requires evidence of exploitation, so a severe flaw with no confirmed exploitation carries no federal deadline at all — as with the two Check Point VPN flaws above. Absence from KEV is not a severity judgement.
