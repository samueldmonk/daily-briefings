Top Story

Shevchenko vacates the women’s flyweight title

Valentina Shevchenko has vacated the UFC women’s flyweight championship. The announcement was made on the UFC Paris broadcast, and the reason given is injury — reporting this run describes ligament damage to the shoulder and back, with an absence expected to run about a year. She had been booked to defend against Natalia Silva on 3 October; instead Silva now meets Wang Cong for the vacant belt in the UFC 332 main event at the Delta Center, Salt Lake City. The promotion is reported to have said Shevchenko will receive a title shot on her return.

Corroboration and its limits. UFC.com’s own Noche UFC fight-by-fight preview, fetched directly in the 10:22 edition, refers twice to “the forthcoming bout for the vacant flyweight title at UFC 332” and to Silva facing “Wang Cong for the vacant title next month,” and separately says Shevchenko is “said to be out for the next year” — that is the promotion’s own site, and it is what this page follows. Search returns this run add the date and the medical detail. One divergence is printed rather than resolved: two headlines (Yahoo Sports, Sherdog) describe UFC 332 as an interim title fight, while UFC.com and the body text of the other returns describe the belt as vacant and the bout as being for the undisputed championship. Because she vacated rather than being kept as an injured champion, “vacant” is what this desk carries; the interim wording is recorded here so a later run does not mistake it for a correction.

Why this is the lead, and what it cost. This board has gone nineteen consecutive editions without a champions regression, and the discipline that produced that streak nearly caused the opposite error today. The previous edition promoted the Shevchenko row from “carried” to fully verified on the strength of an ESPN-sourced list that supplied her date, method, opponent, event and defence count — every field correct, and the row wrong, because the list predated the vacancy. An ESPN-sourced return this run still names her champion. ★ The standing rule this adds: a belt can change hands without a card being contested. Every prior version of the reasoning on this page said some form of “no event has been held since Paris, so no belt could have moved” — true of fights, false of titles, and a title can change hands without a punch being thrown. Vacancies, strippings and retirements move belts on a press release.

Parnasse arrives: a first-round finish of Dan Hooker, on debut, in a main event, at home

Salahdine Parnasse made his UFC debut in the UFC Paris main event on Saturday 5 September at Accor Arena and stopped Dan Hooker inside a round. About halfway through the opening round Parnasse landed a body kick that, in UFC.com’s account, “got Hooker’s attention,” then followed with a flurry against the fence to send Hooker crashing to the canvas. Yahoo Sports’ account of the same sequence, sourced in the 9:48 edition, is more specific about the weapons: a clean kick to the liver and hard left hands to put Hooker out. The two descriptions agree — body shot first, hands to finish — and the UFC.com wording is kept as the primary because it is the promotion’s own. Also sourced in the 9:48 edition: Parnasse called out Max Holloway afterwards. UFC.com’s results page, read directly in the 10:22 edition, does not mention the callout; it reports instead that Losene Keita called out Lerone Murphy and that Daniil Donchenko asked for Daniel Rodriguez before year-end. No bout has been announced, and none is implied here. The 28-year-old has now won six straight, all by stoppage, and UFC.com says he “instantly enters the highly touted lightweight Top 15.” Post-fight records are sourced this edition for the first time: Parnasse moves to 24–2, Hooker falls to 24–15 (Yahoo Sports / MMA Mania, sourced in an earlier edition; editions before that carried Parnasse’s pre-fight 23–2). A record that settled on Saturday night is a newly-sourced detail rather than a new development, so it is not tagged New.

Parnasse is a former two-time KSW featherweight champion and one-time KSW lightweight champion — UFC.com calls him “a two-division champion outside of the UFC” and, in its main-card report, “the former KSW two-division champion” — who signed with the promotion in late July 2026 having previously turned it down, and was handed a five-round main event on debut. Champions Board

A different trap fired in an earlier edition, and it is the oldest one on this desk’s list: a stale champions list, presented as current. An ESPN-sourced return gave ten champions with dates attached — and two of them were superseded nine months ago. It filed Merab Dvalishvili at bantamweight (“won title Sept. 14, 2024”) and Alexandre Pantoja at flyweight (“won title July 8, 2023”). Both lost those belts on the same night: UFC 323, 6 December 2025 — Petr Yan by unanimous decision over Dvalishvili, Joshua Van by first-round TKO over Pantoja. Neither name was taken, and the table below is unchanged. The giveaway is internal to the return itself: it lists Pantoja as the sitting flyweight champion while this same page carries a sourced, separately-verified booking in which Pantoja challenges Van for that title at UFC 331 in thirteen days. A champion does not challenge for his own belt. This is the failure mode this board was built to catch — a list whose champions were right at the time it was written and have not been right since. It is logged rather than quietly discarded. Verification status. The ESPN-sourced return in the 8:47 edition re-confirmed Aspinall, Ulberg, Strickland, Makhachev, Gaethje and Volkanovski directly, with the same dates, methods and defence counts printed above, and it placed Kayla Harrison correctly. Its Shevchenko row has since been falsified — correct on the day it was checked, overtaken by the vacancy announced on the UFC Paris broadcast, which is the whole lesson of the Top Story and the reason that row now reads Vacant. It also, in the same breath, offered two nine-month-stale names at bantamweight and flyweight, which were refused; see the note above the table. That is the point worth keeping: the same return can be authoritative on eight rows and wrong on two, so this board is checked row by row against the most recent card rather than adopted wholesale. This run supplied the sharpest illustration yet, and it was refused wholesale. A fresh ESPN-sourced champions list returned this morning named Alex Pereira at light heavyweight, Khamzat Chimaev at middleweight, Ilia Topuria at lightweight and Valentina Shevchenko at women’s flyweight — four rows wrong in one list, and every one of the four is a regression this desk has a standing correction against. The dates it attached give it away: Pereira “won Oct. 4, 2025”, Chimaev “Aug. 16, 2025”, Topuria “June 28, 2025” — all superseded by UFC 327 (11 Apr 2026), UFC 328 (9 May 2026) and Freedom 250 (14 Jun 2026) respectively. Nothing from that list was adopted. The middleweight regression-attempt count now stands at forty-five. Gane, Yan, Van and Dern were not confirmed by any return in recent editions and are carried from this desk’s standing record, labelled as carried above. UFC Paris was a non-title card — and a belt still changed hands during it, because Shevchenko’s vacation of the women’s flyweight title was announced on the broadcast. Nineteen consecutive editions carried some version of “no card, so no belt could move”; the streak of clean editions ends here, and it ends in the right direction — not with a stale champion published as current, but with a stale champion caught and removed. The distinction this desk should keep: a regression is republishing a name the sources have already superseded. That has still not happened, and the four named past regressions (Pereira at light heavyweight, Chimaev at middleweight, Topuria at lightweight, a vacant featherweight title) remain absent. What happened today is the opposite failure mode, caught one edition after it was introduced: the 9:48 run upgraded the Shevchenko row to “verified” on a list that was already out of date. Verification is not a ratchet. A row can be more confidently wrong after checking than before, if the thing checked against is older than the fact.
