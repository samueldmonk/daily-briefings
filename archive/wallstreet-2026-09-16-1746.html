The Lead

Stocks close lower after the Fed’s first hike in three years — Dow −631.21 to 51,461.90

Roughly 95 minutes past the bell, the closing print has been restated again by independent sources this run and it has not moved. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 631.21 points, or 1.21%, to close at 51,461.90. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,551.81. The Nasdaq Composite finished down 0.01% at 25,978.42 — effectively unchanged, and the single most telling number on the page.

That gap is the whole story of the session. A 1.21% fall in the Dow against a Nasdaq that did not move is not a market selling risk; it is a market selling rate-sensitivity. The Federal Reserve raised its target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% from 3.50%–3.75% — unanimously, and for the first time in three years — and the damage concentrated precisely where a higher path for rates does the most harm.

Stocks turned lower during Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference, which markets read as hawkish on inflation. The Fed’s own projections point to one additional hike in 2026 before holding steady in 2027, and that forecast — rather than the hike itself, which markets had priced at above 90% odds going in — is what repriced the afternoon. Long-dated yields rose with it: the 10-year Treasury climbed back to the 5% mark.

A note on the round numbers still circulating: CNBC’s live blog is headlined “Dow drops 600 points”, and Yahoo Finance’s is headlined “Dow sinks 600 points”. Both are rounded restatements of the same session. The settled figure is −631.21, and that is what this page leads with.

Banks take the hit: worst day since February

This is where the hike landed. The State Street SPDR S&P Bank ETF shed 2.6%, putting it on track for its worst day since 27 February, when it dropped nearly 5%. Goldman Sachs, Wells Fargo, Bank of America and Citigroup all fell more than 3%. The fear named in the reporting is not this hike but the next one.

On the Radar
