The lead

A Friday bounce that didn’t save the week

US markets are closed for the weekend, so the most recent session is Friday, August 21. Stocks rose across the board to cap a volatile week: the Dow added 0.98%, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite each gained 0.43%, and the Russell 2000 rose 0.85%. Healthcare did the heavy lifting for the Dow, with Merck and Johnson & Johnson among the names supporting the index.

It wasn’t enough. All four indexes still finished the week lower — the S&P 500 down about 1.4% — after a Treasury sell-off put pressure on tech and AI stocks. Relief from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s Thursday plan to expand bond buybacks beyond $4 billion per issue lasted only briefly; markets read it as a limited fix and the 10- and 30-year yields snapped back to their previously held higher levels. “Part of it is signaling here to show that we believe yields don’t reflect the underlying fundamentals,” Bessent told CNBC.

Bitcoin ran the other way, reaching $77,000 for its best week in two years. Ahead: Bessent details the plan to economically isolate Iran on Monday, Nvidia reports Wednesday after the bell, and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh gives his first Jackson Hole keynote on Friday morning — the same morning July core PCE lands.

On the radar
  • Monday, August 24 — Bessent’s Iran press conference. The Treasury Secretary unveils details of the US plan to economically isolate Iran, the latest phase of the Middle East war. Earlier in the week Trump threatened “TREMENDOUS Economic Consequences” for any country trading with Iran, putting the focus on China, which sources oil from the Gulf. Also Monday: the Chicago Fed National Activity Index and the Dallas Fed Manufacturing Survey.
  • Tuesday, August 25. July durable goods orders, the Conference Board’s Consumer Confidence Index, new home sales and the S&P Case-Shiller Home Price Index, plus earnings from Intuit.
  • Wednesday, August 26 — Nvidia. Nvidia reports fiscal Q2 after the bell, the next major test of the AI trade’s durability. The same morning brings the second estimate of Q2 GDP and the Census Bureau’s July durable goods orders. Correction to earlier editions: Nvidia reports Wednesday the 26th, not Thursday the 27th.
  • Thursday–Saturday, August 27–29 — Jackson Hole. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair on Friday, August 28 — the same morning July core PCE, the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, is released.
