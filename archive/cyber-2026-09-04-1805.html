Top Story

CISA puts the Chrome zero-day on the federal clock

Yesterday it was a browser patch. Today it is a compliance deadline. CISA added CVE-2026-85046 — a type-confusion bug in V8, Chrome’s JavaScript and WebAssembly engine — to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 4, the single addition of the day, with a remediation due date of September 18 in the catalog data returned this run.

Google’s own advisory, published Thursday, says only: “Google is aware that an exploit for CVE-2026-85046 exists in the wild.” The flaw carries a CVSS score of 8.8 and allows a remote attacker “to execute arbitrary code inside the sandbox via a crafted HTML page.” It is fixed in Chrome 152.0.7977.82/.83 for Windows and macOS and 152.0.7977.82 for Linux, in a release Help Net Security reports patched 12 vulnerabilities in total.

It was reported by researcher Salvatore Gulizia (Serotav) on August 4, 2026, for a $1,000 bug bounty. In his own write-up he described the root cause as a compiler bug that lets an array holding PACKED_ELEMENTS receive the map PACKED_SMI_ELEMENTS, which “can be turned into arbitrary read/write on the JavaScript heap.” Google shared no details of the in-the-wild exploitation, and none are asserted here. It is the sixth Chrome zero-day Google has fixed in 2026.

DO NOT MERGE THESE TWO CHROME UPDATES. A separate stable-channel release, build 152.0.7977.75/.76, fixes 26 flaws including two the vendor rates critical — CVE-2026-84353 (Shared Tab Groups) and CVE-2026-84352 (WebGL), both use-after-free, both CVSS v3 9.6, both sandbox escapes. That is not the zero-day build, and no exploitation is claimed for either. This desk kept the two apart last edition and keeps them apart now.

Patch Priority

Most urgent action for defenders today — deadline tomorrow

CVE-2026-9586 — Sangoma Switchvox SQL injection. Added to the CISA KEV catalog on September 2 and due for federal remediation on September 5 — one day left. If you run Switchvox, this is the one with the clock on it.

Immediately behind it: Chrome. Force the update to 152.0.7977.82/.83 and restart the browser rather than waiting for the automatic cycle — CVE-2026-85046 is confirmed exploited and joined KEV today with a September 18 due date, 14 days out. The same due dates appear in the KEV section below and nowhere on this page do they differ.

Breaches & Incidents

NewAttacker claimAviation supply chain

Storm claims Star Aviation, a Boeing and Airbus supplier

Star Aviation Inc. of Goshen, Kentucky was listed on Storm’s leak site on September 2. The company repairs and tests engine wire harnesses and electronic wire interconnect systems used in commercial aircraft, and develops FAA-approved repairs and alternative parts certified through the FAA’s Parts Manufacturer Approval system. Samples released by the attackers and examined by Cybernews researchers include technical schematics and what appear to be employee identification cards.

THIS IS AN ATTACKER CLAIM, NOT A CONFIRMED BREACH. The scope is unknown, Star Aviation has not responded to a request for comment, and no record count, no data volume and no ransom figure was returned — so none is published.

Thomson Reuters — C-Track court case management

An unauthorised party obtained files from C-Track, the court case management platform sold by Thomson Reuters’ West Publishing Corporation unit. Access to the storage location ran from March 1 to June 29, 2026, and West Publishing discovered the activity on June 30. Courts in 11 U.S. states, the U.S. Virgin Islands and Ontario, Canada are affected. A subset of records may contain names, Social Security numbers, driver’s licence numbers, dates of birth, medical information and health insurance information.

A count discrepancy, printed rather than resolved. One aggregation headlines the incident as hitting “12 states”; the body text of the coverage returned this run says 11 U.S. states plus the U.S. Virgin Islands and Ontario. This page uses 11. No threat actor has been named, no extortion demand reported and no leak-site listing found — this is not being described as ransomware.
AIUnit 42

An AI-agent intrusion finished in under ten hours

Unit 42 detailed a ransomware operation in which a human attacker used edge AI models and agent frameworks to compromise a corporate network almost autonomously. By the attacker’s own account during negotiations, the agents executed more than 50 MITRE ATT&CK techniques and completed in under 10 hours an intrusion that would normally take around two weeks of coordinated manual work. After initial access through a public API endpoint, the agents mapped the internal architecture, pulled tokens and passwords out of code repositories, escalated to master credentials for the secret manager, hijacked CI/CD pipelines to exfiltrate cloud access keys, and then used the victim’s own compromised cloud AI infrastructure as post-compromise infrastructure.

RATProofpoint

PackClient — a commercial RAT sold on Telegram

Proofpoint identified PackClient, a modular remote access framework sold through Telegram channels and used by the Chinese actor TA4922 against organisations in China and India. It carries dual C2 channels, a keylogger, screenshot capture, SOCKS tunnels, remote system access and specific monitoring of applications such as Telegram Desktop. Campaigns use tax-related lures with ZIP and IMG files that install it by DLL sideloading. Its commercial availability raises the odds other Chinese-speaking actors adopt it.
