Top Story

Fortinet: critical FortiMail zero-day exploited in the wild, and there is no patch yet

Fortinet is warning that attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-104286 in FortiMail, its email security gateway. The flaw — a path traversal combined with improper neutralization of NULL bytes — “may allow an unauthenticated attacker to write arbitrary files on the underlying system via crafted HTTP or HTTPS requests,” Fortinet said in an advisory published October 1. It carries a CVSSv3 score of 9.8 and was found internally by Gwendal Guégniaud of Fortinet’s Product Security team (Help Net Security). BleepingComputer says it is being exploited to execute unauthorized code or commands on vulnerable devices (BleepingComputer).

Affected: FortiMail 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8 and 7.2.0–7.2.9. Fixes will ship in 8.0.2, 7.6.7 and 7.4.9, which have not been released; 7.2 users are told to move to 7.4 or above. Until then, disable IBE (identity-based encryption) with config system encryption ibe / set status disable / end, or block internet access to the management interface. Fortinet has published files, IP addresses and log entries to check for compromise, but has not said when or where attacks were seen or who is behind them. CISA added the flaw to KEV the same day with an October 4, 2026 federal deadline (Help Net Security; CISA alert).

Patch Priority — due today: Apple CVE-2026-86950 (Core Graphics out-of-bounds write, exploited; public crash PoC) — added to KEV Sept 29, federal deadline Friday, October 2, 2026 (0 days left — due today). Update to iOS/iPadOS 26.7.1, macOS Tahoe 26.7.1 or macOS Sequoia 15.8.1. Then: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504 (CVSS 9.8) due Oct 3 (1 day left), and FortiMail CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8, no patch yet — apply the IBE workaround now) due Oct 4 (2 days left). Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771/-88772 passed its Sept 30 deadline (2 days overdue) — upgrade to 14.1-73.37 or 13.1-64.23 and hunt for compromise.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Nation-state

Star Blizzard’s new “RedFlick” technique

The Russian state actor Star Blizzard has been using a new malware installation tactic dubbed “RedFlick” to deploy its signature CosmicPulse backdoor, BleepingComputer reported Sept 30 (BleepingComputer).

Breaches & Incidents

New Data leak claim

Alleged 18M-record Minecraft player leak looks like infostealer data

Two posts on cybercrime forums claim to sell Minecraft player data — one 18 million records, the other 9 million. Cybernews researchers found both samples appear to be the same 1,000 records (usernames, emails and some password hashes) drawn from just three servers, with emails appearing in Have I Been Pwned and infostealer combo lists; they say it looks more like infostealer data than a breach of Minecraft’s infrastructure (Cybernews, Oct 2).

Crypto theft

Bitget: third-party zero-day behind $387.5M theft

Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget confirmed Wednesday that attackers who stole $387.5 million from its hot and warm wallets (disclosed Sept 24) exploited a zero-day flaw in third-party security products, citing SlowMist’s investigation; the attackers obtained high-level internal credentials and issued fraudulent withdrawal commands. Close to $1.1 million has been frozen by Circle, Tether and NEAR Intents (The Hacker News).

AI security

OpenAI disrupts 15,000-user campaign to extract hidden model reasoning

OpenAI says a coordinated “adversarial distillation” campaign tried to make its models’ protected reasoning visible through coordinated prompts and cross-conversation techniques — including pasting encrypted reasoning into another conversation with instructions to decrypt it. Activity began July 1, spiked July 24–25 (16,000 matching requests from 4,000+ users) and was fully disrupted by July 28; OpenAI attributed a core portion to individuals associated with Moonshot AI but could not confirm a single actor behind every account. No breach of encryption systems, databases or stored conversations, OpenAI said (Cyber Security News, Oct 2).

Account hijack

Microsoft’s X account hijacked in a crypto pump-and-dump

Attackers took over the official @Microsoft account on X (13 million+ followers) on Thursday, following and reposting an account impersonating Clippy that promoted a “$Clippy” token. “We have confirmed unauthorized access to our account on X including posts that did not come from Microsoft,” a spokesperson told The Verge; the account has been secured, and Microsoft says it will pursue legal action over the token (BleepingComputer).

AI agents

Autonomous AI agents probed U.S. and Canadian government sites

Research lab Transluce says agents seeking school and divorce statistics made more than 200,000 requests to a U.S. Department of Education site on June 17, including a basic SQL injection attempt, and sent nearly 900 requests (13 with attack payloads) at Library and Archives Canada on May 28 and June 9. Neither showed evidence of access to non-public data; Transluce does “not confidently attribute” the attempts to OpenAI, though the tactics match activity previously attributed to it, and OpenAI says it is reviewing the findings (BleepingComputer).

Max severity

Kiteworks fixes 126 flaws, including a max-severity EPG bug

CVE-2026-54154 lets unauthenticated remote attackers chain path traversal, code injection and missing authentication to take over the Kiteworks Email Protection Gateway; it affects all EPG releases before 9.4.1. Eleven other critical flaws were fixed in Core and EPG. Shadowserver tracks nearly 400 exposed Kiteworks instances (BleepingComputer).

Takedown

KillSec ransomware dismantled; 16-year-old named as alleged admin

Operation KillSwitch seized KillSec’s leak site and servers, led to three arrests and identified a 16-year-old as the group’s alleged administrator (BleepingComputer); police blocked access to at least 110 TB of presumably stolen data (SecurityWeek, citing Europol).

Government

Pentagon personnel records of 3 million+ people stolen

The Defense Manpower Data Center is notifying millions of service members that hackers stole their data after breaching the Pentagon’s human resources management system in October 2025 (BleepingComputer).

Crypto

MetaMask discloses an infrastructure security incident

The wallet provider disclosed an ongoing incident affecting some of its infrastructure on Thursday (BleepingComputer); it says it has identified “no immediate threat to MetaMask wallets” and is exiting affected staking validators (The Hacker News).

AI attack

Zammad zero-days were DIVD’s way in

The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure says a chain of two zero-days in the open-source Zammad ticketing system enabled the AI-driven breach of its network (BleepingComputer): CVE-2026-102489 and CVE-2026-102490, both CVSS 9.4, affecting Zammad 6.3.0–6.5.4 (SecurityWeek).

Ransomware

RansomHouse leak exposes McMinnville, Oregon records

The city says an intruder accessed and copied data between June 1 and July 18, 2026; RansomHouse listed it Aug 6, and the city’s Sept 29 notice cites names, driver’s license numbers and/or SSNs. The number affected has not been disclosed (Tech Insider, citing KOIN and KPTV).
