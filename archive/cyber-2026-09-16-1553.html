Top Story

One crafted email, root on the gateway: federal agencies have until tomorrow to fix Cisco’s email-security zero-day

CVE-2026-76461 is an SQL-injection flaw (CWE-89) in the email-parsing logic of Cisco AsyncOS for Secure Email Gateway, carrying a CVSS v3.1 base score of 9.8. An unauthenticated, remote attacker sends a crafted email containing malicious SQL statements through an affected gateway; successful exploitation leads to arbitrary SQL execution and, ultimately, command execution with root privileges on the underlying operating system.

Cisco’s PSIRT confirmed active exploitation in September 2026 while working a Cisco TAC support case, and the flaw was added to CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day the vendor disclosed it — the signature of a bug exploited as a zero-day before disclosure. CISA added it on 14 September and set a 17 September remediation deadline for federal civilian agencies, described in coverage read this run as one of the shortest windows CISA has applied to an email-security flaw, with the roughly 72-hour turnaround signalling a high assessed exploitation intensity.

There is no workaround that addresses the flaw, so the software update is the only remediation. Affected branches take separate patch tracks: 15.5.x → 15.5.5-014, 16.0.x → 16.0.4-302 and 16.5.x → 16.5.0-780. CISA has also marked the issue as requiring forensic triage under BOD 26-04, which means patching alone is not treated as a complete response.

Patch Priority — Cisco Secure Email Gateway, CVE-2026-76461, due tomorrow (1 day left)

CISA added CVE-2026-76461 to the KEV catalog on 14 September with a federal remediation deadline of 17 September under BOD 26-04, and flagged it as requiring forensic triage. The flaw is under active exploitation, scores 9.8, and needs only a crafted email to reach root. No workaround exists — upgrade AsyncOS to 15.5.5-014, 16.0.4-302 or 16.5.0-780 depending on branch, then treat exposed appliances as suspect: review mail-processing and database logs, look for unexpected OS-level processes or new accounts, and rotate credentials the appliance holds. The ConnectWise ScreenConnect deadline below is already two days past due and remains the second item on the list.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Qilin (also tracked as Agenda)

Qilin remains one of the most active ransomware-as-a-service operations of 2026. Tracking read this run puts 1,403 victims between July 2025 and June 2026 — an average of about 117 a month, or a new victim roughly every seven hours — after a 2025 in which the group claimed more victims than any other criminal operation, passing 1,000. The United States accounts for about 40% of its global victims, followed by Canada and Western Europe, and its target list skews toward small and mid-sized organisations: local courts, school districts, healthcare practices, water utilities. Recent reporting links Qilin deployments to exploitation of CVE-2026-50751, a critical VPN authentication-bypass flaw in Check Point products.

DHS confirms an intruder sat inside the Homeland Security Information Network for weeks

An unknown threat actor gained unauthorised access to HSIN — a sensitive-but-unclassified platform used by federal, state, local, tribal, territorial, international and private-sector partners — sometime between late May and early June 2026, reaching both HSIN servers and a SharePoint system used for inter-agency collaboration. September 2026 report · Mandiant

An AI coding-assistant session was hijacked, and Shai-Hulud reached about 100 repositories

Mandiant describes an attacker who took over an active AI coding-assistant session at an unnamed SaaS provider. The assistant had recommended software the attacker poisoned, the recommendation was accepted, an infostealer went in through a poisoned PyPI package, GitHub OAuth tokens were stolen, and the self-spreading Shai-Hulud worm reached roughly 100 internal repositories, taking repository secrets and source code. The public case study does not say when the intrusion happened or how the session was taken over.
