The Lead

A four-session losing streak snapped on Friday — but the week still closed lower, and the Fed meets Wednesday with a hike priced

US markets are closed for the weekend. The most recent session is Friday 11 September, and it was a broad recovery: the S&P 500 rose 0.9%, the Dow added 1% — more than 500 points — and the Nasdaq Composite gained 1%, with all three breaking four-session losing streaks. An inflation reading close to expectations and a pullback in crude prices did the work.

The week as a whole told the opposite story. The S&P 500 finished the week down about 0.6%, the Nasdaq about 0.7% and the Dow 426 points lower — roughly 0.8% against Friday's close. Beneath that, the breadth reading published in earlier editions still frames it best: the equal-weight S&P 500 fell 1.87% against the cap-weighted index's 0.68%, and small caps dropped 2.38% — a handful of mega-caps masking a considerably broader decline.

The week's other data left the inflation picture mixed. August PPI accelerated to 5.4% year-on-year from 4.8%, above the 5.3% expected, with core PPI rising to 4.6% from 4.2%. WTI crude punched above $100 during the week and the US 10-year yield closed in on 5%. Against that, annual core CPI slowed to 2.4%, its lowest since 2021, even as core rose 0.3% month-on-month.

The FOMC announces Wednesday 16 September at 2 PM ET, with a Summary of Economic Projections. The Fed has held at 3.50%–3.75% since its 29 July meeting, a 9–3 decision with Hammack, Kashkari and Logan all dissenting in favour of a hike. Chair Kevin Warsh used Jackson Hole on 28 August to say that if the Fed is not confident inflation is moving toward 2% "clearly and at sufficient speed," it has work to do. Market pricing for a hike to 3.75%–4.00% is in genuine dispute and is not averaged here: one week-ahead read puts it at roughly 70%, a post-CPI read at ~71%, up from 61%, and a carried CME FedWatch read at ~90%.
