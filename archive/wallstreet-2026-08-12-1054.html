The Lead

CPI cools to 3.4% and the S&P 500 tests its record as AI earnings deliver (as of ~10:30 AM ET)

The week's main event landed tame. July's consumer price index rose just 0.1% on the month, easing the annual rate to 3.4% from 3.5%, while core CPI rose 0.2% to run at 2.5% year over year — all in line with Dow Jones consensus forecasts (CNBC, TheStreet, Yahoo Finance). The tape is holding its early gains: Trading Economics' board at roughly 10:30 AM ET read the S&P 500 +0.28% and "testing its record", with the Nasdaq 100 up 0.6%, the Dow up 0.14% after opening about 147 points higher, the Russell 2000 up 0.4% and the VIX easing to 14.77. At the open, Yahoo Finance and TheStreet had the S&P 500 +0.30%, the Nasdaq +0.59% and the Dow +0.11%. Quartz's headline went further, calling it a push to a record high — Friday's official record close of 7,757.64 is the bar. After the print, Yahoo reports traders tilted their bets toward the Fed holding rates steady in September, from a roughly 50-50 split the day before.

Under the CPI hood: energy fell another 1.5% in July after June's 5.7% slide (though it is still up 14.7% over the year after the spring's Hormuz-driven spikes), food and shelter each rose just 0.1% — shelter still drove about two-thirds of the headline gain — and airline fares jumped 2.2% (CNBC). LPL's Jeffrey Roach says falling energy prices "helped soften the inflation pressures of the month" and sees inflation decelerating to 2.7% by year-end, but expects a "lively" September FOMC debate — his baseline is a hold, "but an increasing number of voting members are hawkish and could convince the majority to implement a hike" (TheStreet). Fifth Third's Bill Adams says the report "narrowly meets the bar to nudge the Fed toward holding rates steady," and Kitco's morning report puts market-implied odds of a September quarter-point hike near 48% — this cycle's live debate is hike-or-hold, not cuts.

The other engine is AI earnings. CoreWeave surged about 20% after revenue more than doubled and guidance blew past estimates, Nebius jumped 16.5% as its AI-cloud revenue rose nearly sixfold, and Super Micro rallied hard on an above-consensus full-year outlook (TheStreet, CNBC, Trading Economics). Strong Tencent results added a global read-through for hyperscalers and chipmakers, with Nvidia and Oracle higher (Trading Economics) — and the AI-infrastructure test isn't over: Cisco and Coherent report after tonight's close, with Cisco already the Dow's early leader at +2% (AskTraders, Trading Economics). Uncertainty over the return of Middle East energy supply is still the tape's standing inflation worry: Houthi rebels killed six people on a Red Sea vessel — the first fatalities from shipping attacks in more than a year — and U.S. forces fired on a container ship allegedly trying to breach the blockade of Iranian ports (CBS News via TheStreet).
