The Lead

Closing bell: Dow +0.83%, Nasdaq +0.64%, S&P 500 +0.59% as tech rebounds; real estate and health care lead, telecoms drag communication services

Official close (4 PM ET). “The U.S. market is now closed. All four major indexes closed higher, while volatility declined modestly,” TheStreet wrote in its 4:00 PM ET market wrap: the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 423.37 points, or 0.83%, to 51,655.01; the Nasdaq Composite 172.83 points, or 0.64%, to 27,366.17; the S&P 500 46.15 points, or 0.59%, to 7,811.51; and the Russell 2000 0.52%. Real estate and health care were the dominant S&P 500 sectors, up 1.85% and 1.48%, while communication services fell 1.62%; earnings season “informally kicked off” with Delta’s disappointing third quarter, “clearing the way for bank earnings next week” (TheStreet). All three changes reconcile to the cent with Thursday’s closes; the S&P 500 and Nasdaq match Yahoo Finance’s closing strip, while Dow prints differ by cents across feeds (Yahoo 51,655.18; CNBC 51,654.95 per search listing). Yahoo said the S&P 500 and Nasdaq “remained on track for weekly gains after touching record highs earlier this week” (Yahoo Finance).

The week. TheStreet’s 4:13 PM “Look Back” said stocks “had a good week, even despite the moves lower yesterday,” with “some tension in the tech portion of the market” and the Russell 2000 the only major index to decline on the week (TheStreet).

Bull market birthday (Yahoo Finance, ~2 PM ET). The current bull market — the 11th since the 1950s — is on track to become the seventh to complete at least four full years on Oct. 12, according to an analysis by Truist co-chief investment officer Keith Lerner reported by Yahoo Finance’s Brian Sozzi; its 119% advance sits near the middle of the pack, well below the 401% gain of the 2009–2020 cycle and the 582% gain of 1987–2000 (Yahoo Finance).

Fed (Yahoo Finance, ~1:13 PM ET). President Trump has established a committee to investigate allegations against Federal Reserve governor Lisa Cook, including mortgage fraud and whether her statements constituted legal “cause” for her removal; the group will report to him whether there is legal cause, Yahoo Finance’s Jennifer Schonberger reported. The Supreme Court ruled in June that Cook can continue serving on the Fed’s Board of Governors but did not rule on the merits of the allegations, which Cook denies (Yahoo Finance).

Gulf supply (TheStreet, 11:09 AM ET). Hurricane Isaias has shut in nearly 63% of current daily oil production in the Gulf of America — an estimated 1,282,879 barrels per day — with personnel evacuated from 121 offshore production platforms and five drilling rigs, the Marine Minerals Administration said, up from about 25% offline on Wednesday, CBS 12 News reported. Isaias strengthened into a Category 3 hurricane Friday with sustained winds of 120 mph; the National Hurricane Center forecast landfall late Friday near the Alabama–Florida line (TheStreet).

Consumer sentiment (10 AM ET; TheStreet 10:07 AM, FXStreet). The University of Michigan’s preliminary October Consumer Sentiment Index “dropped to 46.3 from 48.1 in September,” below the 47.6 forecast cited by FXStreet, TheStreet reported. “Frustration over cost-of-living continues to mount, as consumers across the political spectrum believe that the trajectory of the economy has weakened since the beginning of the year,” Surveys of Consumers director Joanne Hsu said (TheStreet). FXStreet reported the Current Conditions index fell to 44.7 from 50.9 while the Expectations gauge rose to 47.3 from 46.3, and one-year inflation expectations rose to 4.7% from 4.6% and the five-year outlook to 3.5% from 3.4% (FXStreet). Bloomberg headlined the reading a five-month low.

Three Saudi nationals, including a Saudia pilot, were killed in Houthi-claimed attacks on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport on Thursday, CNBC reported, per TheStreet; separately, Iran’s IRGC said it struck an LPG carrier, NV Sunshine, south of the Strait of Hormuz, Al Jazeera reported (TheStreet).

Treasury hire (TheStreet, 9:15 AM ET). Judy Shelton, whose 2019 Fed nomination was blocked in the Senate in November 2020, has been appointed a counselor to Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to advise on currency policy, “with a particular focus on evaluating financial conditions in China,” CNBC reported, per TheStreet (TheStreet).

Apple and the telecom debate (TheStreet, 8:40 AM ET; Invezz). Apple shares fell 2.23% ahead of the bell after Nikkei Asia reported the company told some suppliers to cut component orders for the iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max, with October orders cut by at least 15% from the levels originally requested; TheStreet’s live blog, updated at 9:00 AM ET, still headlined “Nasdaq futures rise as tech rebounds” (TheStreet). Invezz reported Nvidia up 1.6% premarket and Tesla and Amazon up about 1% each; it said Citi analyst Michael Rollins argued the telecom sell-off looked excessive, with a meaningful financial impact from SpaceX unlikely before at least 2029, and that Morgan Stanley analysts had not detected major supply-chain changes at Apple and kept their estimates broadly intact (Invezz).

Market open is 9:30 AM ET; the live quotes above show the real-time picture.

Why tech is bouncing. Tech recovered after Thursday’s scare, when the Financial Times reported OpenAI’s annualized revenue topped out at $50 billion, $20 billion short of estimates; Bloomberg later reported OpenAI expects to reach or exceed $70 billion in annualized revenue by the end of the year, “helping quell worries that the AI boom is running out of steam,” Yahoo wrote (Yahoo Finance). Stocktwits, via Yahoo, said Nvidia, Micron and TSMC were up more than 1% in early premarket trading (4:38 AM ET article) (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance).

Oil and Iran. At 7:20 AM, West Texas Intermediate was down 0.64% at $90.90 and Brent down 1.08% at $103.20 after President Trump said the U.S. “will not be attacking Iran at any time prior to the Midterm Elections” on Nov. 3; Iran’s Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said Tehran was reviewing a U.S. response to its proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz within seven days, and China is set to resume fuel exports after its Golden Week pause (TheStreet). Hathorn cautioned that “continued attacks on shipping around the Strait of Hormuz mean the geopolitical premium is unlikely to disappear quickly.”

Fed. Fed Governor Christopher Waller on Thursday signaled that two additional rate hikes could be appropriate if data stay on their current trajectory, and St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem said further tightening may be needed; CME FedWatch priced only a 19% chance of a hike at the October meeting, with 81% expecting a hold (Stocktwits via Yahoo Finance).
