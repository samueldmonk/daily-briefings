The Lead

Into the final hour: the Dow holds a 1.2% gain — about 650 points — the Nasdaq hugs the flat line after a 1.3% morning rout, and Coca-Cola heads for its best earnings day since 2009

The divergence has held all the way into the final hour. As of roughly 3:30 PM ET, The Motley Fool's live boards had the Dow Jones Industrial Average up about 1.2% — a gain of roughly 650 points — with the S&P 500 up about 0.3% and the Nasdaq Composite pinned to the flat line, "kind of impressive," as the Fool's market writer put it, "considering it was down 1.3% in the morning session." CNBC's afternoon count told the same story — the Dow more than 600 points higher, the S&P 500 up 0.3% and the Nasdaq a hair lower, with a rally in software stocks helping lift the tech-heavy index off its session low — and a 3:31 PM reading had the Composite within a few points of unchanged. The equal-weight S&P 500 ETF (RSP) was up about 1.1%, significantly outperforming the cap-weighted index — mega-cap tech is creating most of the drag — and the Nasdaq-100 is flirting with correction territory, down about 10% from its record high (The Motley Fool/CNBC).

The memory-chip panic remains the story under the surface. After a volatility-based trading halt in Seoul overnight — the Kospi tumbled more than 10%, and SK Hynix dropped about 14.5% at the Korean close, with Acadian Asset Management's Owen Lamont telling CNBC the "sharp swings in SK Hynix shares underscore the uncertainty surrounding the AI investment cycle" — the damage spread across the U.S. sector: into the final hour Sandisk was down about 15% (now roughly 50% below its June peak, yet still the S&P 500's best performer this year at over +360%), Micron about 9%, U.S.-listed SK Hynix shares about 8%, AMD about 7% and Intel about 5% (Motley Fool live boards, ~3:30 PM ET). Nvidia, remarkably, round-tripped: down about 5% at mid-morning on the losers board, it was up about 0.7% by late afternoon even as reports that it is exploring a $250 billion funding backstop for OpenAI kept circular-financing worries front and center (24/7 Wall St/Motley Fool/Yahoo Finance). On the other side of tech, Microsoft and Alphabet were each up more than 2% on the Fool's boards — the software rally CNBC credits with lifting the Nasdaq off its session low.

The other side of the tape is having a famous day. Coca-Cola was up almost 6% and on track for its best earnings-day gain since February 2009 after beating on earnings — adjusted EPS of 97 cents against a 93-cent consensus, on revenue of $13.38 billion versus $13.16 billion expected — and raising its full-year outlook, with revenue now seen growing about 5% and EPS 9–10% and the CFO saying Diet Coke is having "a moment" as health-conscious consumers, America 250 and FIFA World Cup celebrations all feed the quarter (Yahoo Finance/TradingKey). Sherwin-Williams surged about 8–9% on its own beat — "paint seems more exciting than AI chips today," the Fool quipped — while Caterpillar, down about 4%, was the Dow's biggest drag at roughly 200 index points, the downside of its connection to AI data-center buildouts. Dell Technologies fell about 10% to around $384 as worries grew among investors about the concentration of its AI-server business (Yahoo Finance). Boeing rose about 4% after its narrower-than-a-year-ago net loss of $428 million (67 cents a share) on revenue that topped forecasts with 171 deliveries, absorbing a $280 million Air Force One charge; PayPal gained about 4%; and UPS, which beat and raised this morning, reversed to trade about 5% lower on Yahoo's afternoon board. Corning was the board's biggest decliner at about −16%; no company news explaining the move was verified this run (Motley Fool/Yahoo Finance/TheStreet).

The macro clock is ticking: the Fed kicked off its two-day meeting today — "one of the hardest policy decisions for the market to call in years," in Yahoo Finance's framing, with traders' bets skewing toward a hold but without conviction — and the decision lands Wednesday at 2 PM ET with Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30. Fifth Third's Bill Adams expects a hold at 3.50–3.75%, a fifth straight unchanged meeting since the December 2025 cut (TheStreet). Oil kept falling as the U.S.–Iran pause held: Brent traded near $84 by early afternoon, down about 2.2% (Motley Fool, ~12:45 PM ET), after WTI fell 1.80% to $81.12 in early trading (TheStreet); President Trump said aboard Air Force One there's "a good chance that something could happen" in diplomatic talks, and Iran is negotiating an oil-shipping deal with Oman while Trump hosts Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelenskiy and Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu at the White House in separate meetings (Yahoo Finance/Motley Fool). The day's one big data point broke soft — the Conference Board's consumer confidence index fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June — and the earnings gauntlet now runs straight into the Fed: Microsoft and Meta report Wednesday, Apple and Amazon Thursday (Motley Fool).
