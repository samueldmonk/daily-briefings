The Lead

Stocks snap a four-day skid as Hormuz talks pull crude back from its spike — Friday’s official closing levels were not yet published at build time

The week’s losing streak is over. Benzinga’s 11:50 AM ET board had the S&P 500 up 1.1%, the Dow up 566 points, or 1.1%, the Nasdaq 100 up 1.1% and the Russell 2000 lagging at +0.7%. Yahoo Finance’s market strip, read at about 2:21 PM ET, had the S&P 500 +0.99% (+75.23 points), the Dow +1.08% (+562.90) and the Nasdaq Composite +1.08% (+282.24), with the Russell 2000 +0.64%. Both reads reconcile exactly against Thursday’s official closes.

The trigger was not the inflation print. Iranian state media said Tehran will sit down with Gulf states in Oman next week to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, and that was enough to take the war premium back out of crude: WTI settled down 2.4% at $100.05 a barrel and Brent down 2.8% at $104.61, after intraday reads as low as $99.36. Both benchmarks still finish the week higher — WTI +8.6%, Brent +8.4% — and AAA reported U.S. diesel at a record $6.05 a gallon on Friday.

August CPI arrived close enough to script to be ignored by equities and acted on by rates. Headline prices rose 0.4% on the month, the most in three months, with a 3.9% jump in gasoline accounting for more than a third of the increase; the annual rate held at 3.4%, in line with the Dow Jones consensus. Core CPI rose 0.3%, above the 0.2% consensus and the fastest since April, even as the annual core rate eased to a five-year low of 2.4%. Market-implied odds of a hike at the 15–16 September FOMC meeting jumped from roughly 70% to around 90%.

As of this edition’s build, shortly after 4 PM ET, no source read this run had yet published Friday’s official closing levels for the three major indexes. Movers & Drivers

driverNew

Hormuz talks drain the war premium out of crude

Iranian state media said Tehran will sit down with Gulf states in Oman next week to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, and Benzinga reports the headline was enough to knock the war premium out of oil. West Texas Intermediate futures fell 2.4% to settle at $100.05 a barrel and Brent slid 2.8% to $104.61, per a CNBC read — even so, WTI is up 8.6% on the week and Brent 8.4%.

hardware

Dell rises about 11% on an RBC initiation

Dell Technologies gained 11.3%, among the biggest advances in the Russell 1000, after RBC initiated coverage at Outperform, pointing to free cash flow and an edge in AI-infrastructure deployment. The company joins the S&P 100 on 21 September.

hardware

HPE adds 9.1% as the AI-server read-through widens

Hewlett Packard Enterprise rose 9.1%, extending a post-earnings re-rating after record quarterly revenue of $12.2 billion, up 34% year over year, with $9.0 billion from Cloud & AI. Super Micro Computer rose 6.1% on the same theme.

m&aNew

Skyworks climbs 8% as the Qorvo merger nears the line

Skyworks Solutions gained 8.0% in a second straight session of gains after chief executive Phil Brace told a Goldman Sachs conference that the company’s $22 billion merger with Qorvo is in its final stages and should close by year-end.

earnings

Oracle trades flat, but its capex bill lifts the AI complex

Oracle posted fiscal first-quarter adjusted EPS of $1.92, up 30%, on revenue of $19.35 billion against a $19.14 billion consensus, with cloud-infrastructure revenue up 121% to $7.4 billion and remaining performance obligations up $209 billion year over year to $664 billion. Management guided full-year capital expenditure to $90–95 billion. The shares traded flat; the spending plan did the lifting.

downsideNew

Oklo drops 7.1% on a $1 billion equity programme

Oklo tumbled 7.1% after entering an equity distribution agreement on Friday to sell up to $1 billion of Class A common stock through ten sales agents including Goldman Sachs, BofA Securities, Citigroup, J.P. Morgan and Morgan Stanley. X-Energy fell 5.12% alongside it — the downside was dominated by nuclear.

downgradeNew

Chewy slides 4.9% after a JPMorgan downgrade

JPMorgan cut the online pet retailer to Neutral from Overweight and lowered its price target to $24 from $29, citing pressure on organic growth from macro headwinds and deteriorating discretionary and premiumisation trends. SailPoint fell 4.0% and AeroVironment 3.6%, both still unwinding from earlier reports.

earningsNew

Kroger beats and rises 2.1%

The grocer reported EPS of $1.09 before the open against a $1.06 consensus and rose 2.1%. Adobe, which reported alongside Oracle after Thursday’s close, was flat.

Chart of the Day — Vicor, the session’s best large-cap performer

Vicor was the single best performer in the large-cap index on Friday, up 11.84% on Benzinga’s 11:50 AM ET board, just ahead of Dell at +11.28%.

On the Radar

  • FOMC, 15–16 September. The decision lands Wednesday at 2:00 PM ET alongside a Summary of Economic Projections. Market-implied odds of a hike rose from roughly 70% to around 90% after the CPI print; the target range going in is 3.50–3.75%.
  • A bank sanction on Monday. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. will sanction a “large bank” on Monday as part of its Iran strategy, holding the announcement back out of respect for the 9/11 anniversary, and said a Turkish bank that had been dealing with Iran will be closed.
  • Iran and the Gulf states meet in Oman next week to discuss the Strait of Hormuz — the headline that took the war premium out of crude on Friday, and the one most likely to put it back.
  • Dell joins the S&P 100 on 21 September.
  • Skyworks–Qorvo, a $22 billion merger, is in its final stages and is expected to close by year-end.
