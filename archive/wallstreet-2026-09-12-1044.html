The Lead

Cheaper oil and an in-line CPI ended a four-day slide — but the week still finished red

U.S. markets are closed for the weekend. The last completed session was Friday, 11 September, when all three major indexes rose about 1% and snapped a four-session losing streak. Crude pulled back from its recent spurt and the August CPI report came in close to economists’ expectations, which together calmed a market that had spent the week selling off on energy-driven rate-hike fears.

The rally was concentrated in AI infrastructure. Oracle’s fiscal first-quarter results and its capital-spending guidance — CFO Hilary Maxson confirmed full-year capex of $90–95 billion — sent that spending straight through to rack, cooling and networking vendors. Hewlett Packard Enterprise and Dell both surged about 11% to lead the S&P 500; Super Micro climbed 7%.

One good day did not rescue the week. The S&P 500 fell 0.8%, the Dow fell 1.6% and the Nasdaq fell 0.7% over the five sessions. Attention now turns to the FOMC meeting on 15–16 September, where futures moved to roughly a 90% probability of a quarter-point increase after the core CPI print.

On the Radar

  • FOMC, 15–16 September. Futures price roughly a 90% chance of a quarter-point hike after hotter-than-expected core CPI; a 25bp move is now the expected outcome as Iran-related supply shocks keep energy costs high.
  • Oil is the swing factor. Brent near $99 and WTI above $94 have been driving the rate-hike bid all week; Friday’s pullback was the proximate cause of the equity rebound, so any renewed spike reverses the trade.
  • The AI capex cycle has a new proof point. Oracle’s $90–95 billion full-year capex guidance is now the reference number for server, networking and cooling demand — the mechanism behind Friday’s HPE, Dell and Super Micro moves.
  • August CPI. Headline +0.4% m/m and 3.4% y/y, unchanged from July, with core +0.3% m/m — a tenth above forecast. Yields retreated after the print, ending a week-long global bond selloff that had carried the 10-year within striking distance of 5%.
