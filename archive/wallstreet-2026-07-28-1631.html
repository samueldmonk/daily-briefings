The Lead

The close: the Dow gains 537 points and Coca-Cola books its best earnings day since 2009, while the Nasdaq claws a 1.3% morning rout back to a 0.2% dip

The rotation carried all the way to the bell. At Tuesday's close the Dow Jones Industrial Average finished up 537.45 points (+1.03%) at 52,747.53, the S&P 500 added 0.22% to 7,429.22, and the Nasdaq Composite slipped just 0.22% to 24,876.91 (Yahoo Finance closing board; CNBC's wrap counted the same 537-point Dow gain) — a mixed finish that looked far better for tech than the morning did, when the Composite was down as much as 1.3% before a software rally, with Microsoft and Alphabet each up more than 2% on afternoon boards, lifted the index nearly back to flat (CNBC/Motley Fool). The Russell 2000 eked out a 0.04% gain at 2,949.33 and the VIX eased about 2% to 18.29 (Yahoo Finance board). Under the surface the equal-weight trade kept winning: the Invesco S&P 500 Equal Weight ETF (RSP) was up about 1.1% in the afternoon, far ahead of the cap-weighted index, as money rotated out of chips and into industrials, consumer names and software (Motley Fool).

The memory-chip panic remained the story under the surface. After a volatility-based trading halt in Seoul overnight — the Kospi tumbled more than 10%, and SK Hynix dropped about 14.5% at the Korean close, with Acadian Asset Management's Owen Lamont telling CNBC the "sharp swings in SK Hynix shares underscore the uncertainty surrounding the AI investment cycle" — the damage ran through the U.S. session to the end: by the close CNBC's wrap had Micron down about 10% and AMD off 8%, with the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) down more than 3% for a fourth straight losing day. Sandisk, about −15% on late-afternoon boards (roughly 50% below its June peak, yet still the S&P 500's best performer this year at over +360%), U.S.-listed SK Hynix shares (~−8%) and Intel (~−5%) rounded out the wreckage (CNBC/Motley Fool boards, ~3:30 PM ET). Nvidia, remarkably, round-tripped: down about 5% at mid-morning on the losers board, it was back up about 0.7% by late afternoon even as reports that it is exploring a $250 billion funding backstop for OpenAI kept circular-financing worries front and center (24/7 Wall St/Motley Fool/Yahoo Finance). Yahoo's live blog framed the day's tension plainly: earnings optimism and falling oil against souring chip sentiment, with tech traders also nervous that Chinese competition is narrowing the AI gap.

The other side of the tape had a famous day. Coca-Cola popped nearly 5% at the close — its best earnings day since 2009 (CNBC/Yahoo Finance) — after beating on earnings — adjusted EPS of 97 cents against a 93-cent consensus, on revenue of $13.38 billion versus $13.16 billion expected — and raising its full-year outlook, with revenue now seen growing about 5% and EPS 9–10% and the CFO saying Diet Coke is having "a moment" as health-conscious consumers, America 250 and FIFA World Cup celebrations all feed the quarter (Yahoo Finance/TradingKey). Sherwin-Williams surged about 8–9% on its own beat — "paint seems more exciting than AI chips today," the Fool quipped — and Coke and Sherwin led the Dow all session, while Caterpillar, down about 4%, was its biggest drag at roughly 200 index points, the downside of its connection to AI data-center buildouts. Dell Technologies fell about 10% to around $384 on worries about the concentration of its AI-server business (Yahoo Finance). Boeing, Ford and PayPal all rose after reporting results (Yahoo Finance) — Boeing on a narrower-than-a-year-ago net loss of $428 million (67 cents a share) with revenue that topped forecasts on 171 deliveries, absorbing a $280 million Air Force One charge — while UPS, a morning beat-and-raise, reversed to finish 6.5% lower and Corning dropped 12.1% on Yahoo's closing board, with no company-specific driver for Corning verified this run.

Oil's slide deepened into settlement: WTI settled down 4% at $79.26 and Brent fell 4.8% to close at $84.09 (CNBC) as the pause in U.S.–Iran fighting held — President Trump said aboard Air Force One there's "a good chance that something could happen" in diplomatic talks — and Iran negotiates an oil-shipping deal with Oman, while Trump hosted Ukraine's Volodymyr Zelenskiy and Israel's Benjamin Netanyahu at the White House in separate meetings (Yahoo Finance/Motley Fool). The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.62%, a third straight decline (TradingEconomics/CNBC). The macro clock now runs out tomorrow: the Fed wrapped day one of a meeting Yahoo Finance calls "one of the hardest policy decisions for the market to call in years," with the decision Wednesday at 2 PM ET and Chair Kevin Warsh's press conference at 2:30 — Fifth Third's Bill Adams expects a hold at 3.50–3.75%, a fifth straight unchanged meeting since the December 2025 cut (TheStreet). The day's one big data point broke soft — the Conference Board's consumer confidence index fell to 90.8 in July from 92.2 in June — and the earnings gauntlet runs straight into the Fed: Visa reports after tonight's bell and SK Hynix around 8 PM ET, then Microsoft and Meta Wednesday, Apple and Amazon Thursday (Benzinga/Yahoo Finance/Motley Fool).
