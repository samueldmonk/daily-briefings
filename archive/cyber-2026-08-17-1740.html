Top Story

Millions of corporate directory records from McDonald's, TCS and Vodafone tenants go up for sale — no Azure flaw required

A threat actor operating under the name TheHatman is advertising on underground marketplaces what it says is employee information exfiltrated from Microsoft Azure environments run by nine large organisations, according to research published by Hudson Rock and reported on August 17. The listings span more than 3.6 million records in total. This edition adds the full victim breakdown, now verified: McDonald's the largest at roughly 1.7 million, Tata Consultancy Services at around 800,000, HCL Technologies at about 250,000, Vodafone at roughly 425,000, InterContinental Hotels Group at more than 185,000, Kyndryl at approximately 170,000, Gap Inc. at around 80,000, Hexaware Technologies at about 20,000 and Wyndham Hotels at roughly 9,000. The advertised fields are employee emails, phone numbers, job titles, names and workplace addresses — and, in the samples Hudson Rock examined, departments, designations, assignments, manager and reporting relationships, group memberships, service accounts and information identifying Global Administrator accounts. Not payment data, but a near-complete internal directory with the privileged accounts labelled.

The mechanism matters more than the volume. TheHatman claims the data was obtained using compromised credentials, and Hudson Rock says it identified infostealer malware infections linked to Azure credentials associated with several of the named companies. That is not a vulnerability in Azure — it is valid logins, harvested from infected endpoints, used to query a tenant's own directory through the interfaces it is designed to expose. Nothing about it trips an exploit signature.

None of the named companies has publicly confirmed the alleged exposure, and the records remain claims by a seller with an obvious financial interest in overstating them. Treat the volumes accordingly. The defensive read stands regardless: an employee directory is the raw material for convincing phishing and voice-based social engineering — which, as the Levi Strauss case below shows, is how intrusions are being bought rather than built. If your organisation is on this list or resembles it, credential hygiene, session revocation and conditional access on directory-read scopes are the controls in scope, not another patch.

Patch Priority

Do this first
CVE-2026-12569 — PTC Windchill and PTC FlexPLM, CVSS 9.3. The federal remediation deadline was June 28, 2026 — long past due. This is the flaw behind today's top story: unauthenticated remote code execution against internet-exposed PLM servers, exploited to drop JSP web shells and, per this week's reporting, to steal engineering data from 43 organisations including Shell, General Electric and Philips. PTC has shipped fixes since June 17 and keeps updating its advisory with indicators of compromise. Patching alone is not sufficient — any instance that was exposed and unpatched between mid-June and now needs a web-shell hunt.
Then this
CVE-2026-68820 — Microsoft Windows Ancillary Function Driver for WinSock (afd.sys), CVSS 7.0. Federal remediation deadline Tuesday, August 25, 2026 — 8 days away. Not a theoretical elevation-of-privilege bug: Check Point attributes it to Lazarus Group, which ran it as an unpatched zero-day against defence and aerospace targets before Microsoft fixed it in the August 11 Patch Tuesday release. Microsoft assigned the identifier on August 5 and shipped the patch on August 11; CISA added it to the catalog the same day. In those intrusions the flaw carried the FudModule rootkit to SYSTEM privileges and switched off endpoint detection tooling. Four other catalog entries below — Metabase, Cisco Secure Firewall, JetBrains TeamCity and Progress LoadMaster — are already past their due dates.

Threat Actor Spotlight

APT

Lazarus Group — Operation Dream Job, 2026 wave

Check Point Research's August 17 threat intelligence report again flags a new wave of the Lazarus-linked Operation Dream Job campaign, aimed at defence organisations in Europe, India and Brazil. Attackers approach targets with fraudulent job opportunities and deliver malware through trojanised PDF software. The campaign has been active since early 2026 across aerospace, aviation and military technology firms, including organisations working on surveillance sensors, drones and robotics. Operators pose as recruiters, most often over LinkedIn or direct message. The intrusion chain paired the afd.sys zero-day (CVE-2026-68820) with the FudModule rootkit for SYSTEM access and EDR shutdown, while the MISTPEN in-memory downloader routed command-and-control through Microsoft's Graph API and OneDrive so the traffic sat inside normal Microsoft flows. Reporting this week adds detail on the delivery stage: a trojanised PDF viewer deployed a new backdoor tracked as Troy before the operators escalated privileges and installed an updated build of FudModule.

Breaches & Incidents

Ransomware

Clop lists 43 victims — Shell, GE and Philips among them — after a zero-day run at PTC Windchill

Clop has added 43 organisations to its leak site in a campaign researchers tie to exploitation of CVE-2026-12569, a critical flaw in internet-exposed PTC Windchill and PTC FlexPLM product-lifecycle-management servers. Attackers dropped JSP web shells on unpatched instances. Shell — one of the world's three largest oil and gas companies, with 85,000 employees across more than 70 countries — confirmed on August 14 that it is investigating a "potential incident," telling BleepingComputer it is "working with our security teams and relevant experts to investigate"; Clop claims 89 GB of engineering drawings, facility testing reports and project plans. Philips confirmed a compromise of "a specific enterprise server related to internal data" while saying customer environments were unaffected; Clop claims roughly 13.5 GB, mostly diagrams and blueprints. General Electric is also investigating and Fiserv appears among the listed names. Neither Shell nor Philips has said data was exfiltrated — the volumes are Clop's claims. What is advertised is the design record rather than customer data: stolen personal data ages, a blueprint does not.

NewUnpatched

A VoLTE video call reaches the Android kernel on Unisoc phones — and the chipmaker has not replied

SSD Secure Disclosure published an advisory on August 17 detailing a two-stage exploit chain that achieves full Android kernel access on devices running Unisoc modem firmware, triggered through a VoLTE video call. It is the second half of a chain that began in March 2026, when the same team disclosed remote code execution in that firmware via a malformed SIP video call; this week's instalment supplies the privilege escalation that turns modem-level code execution into kernel control. Researchers confirmed the escalation flaw on a Motorola E13 carrying a February 2025 security patch and a Xiaomi Redmi A5 carrying a January 2026 patch. Unisoc — the Shanghai chipmaker formerly known as Spreadtrum, supplying Motorola, Realme and Xiaomi across more than 140 countries — has issued no fix; SSD says it "tried to reach out to the vendor through multiple channels (email and LinkedIn) but have not been able to receive any response." Two conditions bound the risk and are worth stating plainly: the attacker must control a private 4G cellular network, and the victim must answer the incoming video call. That is targeted-attack territory rather than mass exploitation — but on budget handsets with no patch path, there is nothing to deploy.

Exploited

N-able N-central taken over after the first patch proved incomplete

Attackers are compromising N-able N-central servers — the remote monitoring and management platform used by managed service providers — through CVE-2026-18577, an authentication bypass that exists because the vendor's first fix for CVE-2026-18556 closed the primary exploit path but left an alternate one open. N-able shipped its initial hotfix and build 2026.3.1.7 on August 2; exploitation of the follow-on flaw has been observed in the wild since August 1. CISA added CVE-2026-18577 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 3 and CVE-2026-18556 on August 5. Once inside the console, operators abused N-central's built-in Take Control feature to reach managed endpoints and deployed Cloudflare Tunnel (cloudflared) for persistent remote access — meaning a single compromised console reaches every downstream customer it manages.

Breach

Nearly 19 million Poles' medical records stolen from a single software supplier

Polish authorities are investigating a major attack on MyDr, which supplies the software running electronic medical records for around 12,000 medical facilities. Personal data belonging to close to 19 million people was reportedly compromised; the stolen database runs to more than two terabytes and covers primary-healthcare users, holding prescriptions, appointment records, registered medicines and documents supplied to doctors. The attackers demonstrated the breach by leaking a senior Polish politician's PESEL national identity number, phone numbers and 25 prescriptions. Poland's Central Cybercrime Bureau is running the investigation and there is no indication so far of a Russian or other state attack. Facilities are operating normally; the Ministry of Digital Affairs advises citizens to check the database and block their PESEL number.

Insider threat

A North Korean IT worker was on the payroll of a U.S. federal agency

The FBI is investigating how an unidentified federal agency was swept up in the long-running campaign in which North Korean remote IT workers fraudulently obtain jobs at major companies and other organisations. The individual was discovered in July 2026, and Todd Hemmen, deputy assistant director of the FBI's Cyber Capabilities Branch, disclosed the case on July 28 at a Digital Government Institute conference in Washington. The agency has not been named, no details of the worker's duties were provided, and it remains unclear whether any sensitive information was accessed or stolen. The United Nations estimates these remote workers generate between $250 million and $600 million a year for North Korea.

Ransomware

Colombia's Ministry of Justice hit days before a presidential transition

Colombia's Ministry of Justice was struck by ransomware on August 2, 2026, affecting part of its technology infrastructure and reducing the availability of several public-facing digital services. Services connected to illicit-drug monitoring and legal processes were among those disrupted. Officials confirmed that some files were encrypted but said no data theft was detected during the incident. The timing — immediately ahead of the presidential transition — is the part defenders should note.

Ransomware

Akira claims Minnesota Health Insurance Network

Minnesota Health Insurance Network reported a cybersecurity incident after the Akira ransomware group claimed responsibility for an April 2026 attack. Client personal information reportedly exposed includes passport information, home addresses, phone numbers and email addresses, with similar employee information also allegedly compromised, alongside project information, financial records, contracts and agreements. The organisation had not issued a public statement confirming details at the time of the reports. Akira is one of the most prolific ransomware groups targeting healthcare through 2025 and 2026, typically stealing data before encrypting systems.

Breach

Levi Strauss & Co. — corporate data exfiltrated after three employees were socially engineered

Levi Strauss reported on August 7 that an unauthorised third party reached its systems by social-engineering three employees into granting access to their devices — psychological manipulation rather than a software exploit. The company says "certain corporate information was accessed and exfiltrated," with no evidence to date of consumer data being compromised. Day-to-day operations were not disrupted and Levi Strauss does not expect a material impact on its operations or financial results.

Ransomware

INC accelerating through August via SonicWall SMA 1000 flaws

Resecurity tracks INC as the dominant ransomware actor since the start of August, leaning on vulnerabilities in SonicWall's SMA 1000 series appliances for access. Ransomware.Live counts 885 claimed victims to date. Two SMA 1000 CVEs — CVE-2026-15409 (CVSS 10.0, server-side request forgery) and CVE-2026-15410 (CVSS 7.2, code injection) — sit in the CISA catalog flagged for ransomware use.

The Wider Picture

  • Ransomware volume is still climbing. Check Point Research counted 2,139 publicly reported ransomware victims in the second quarter of 2026, up 33% year over year, with the ecosystem expanding to 93 active groups.
  • Attackers are using AI to build their own tooling. Leaked communications show the group known as The Gentlemen using AI coding assistants to speed up development of operational tooling.
  • Attack volume against the average organisation. Organisations absorbed an average of 2,336 weekly cyberattacks during July 2026, a 16% year-over-year increase, while generative AI usage continued to expose corporate information through high-risk prompts submitted to external AI services.
