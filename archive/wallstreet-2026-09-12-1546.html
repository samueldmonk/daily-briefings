The Lead

Markets are closed for the weekend. Friday's rebound broke what one week-ahead piece calls the S&P 500's deepest four-day decline since June — and the week ahead is a Federal Reserve week.

There is no live U.S. session to report: it is Saturday, and the most recent close is Friday 11 September. On that session the S&P 500 rose 65.28 points, or 0.9%, to 7,656.98, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 509.19 points, or 1%, to 52,573.29, and the Nasdaq composite rose 251.31 points, or 1%, to 26,333.04. The Russell 2000 added 13 points, or 0.4%, to 2,903.94. The S&P 500's gain broke a four-day losing streak.

The Associated Press attributes the rebound to two things: a drop of nearly 3% in Brent oil's price, which took pressure off inflation, and an inflation update that came in close to economists' expectations. In the same session, expectations strengthened in the bond market for an upcoming hike to interest rates by the Federal Reserve — a rebound in equities and a firming hike bet arriving together.

Two things are newly sourced this run. First, the Friday percentages now have a second decimal that reconciles with the AP wire's rounded ones: S&P 500 +0.86%, Dow +0.98%, Nasdaq composite +0.96% — each consistent with the points figures above and with AP's "0.9%" and "1%". Second, the context for that rebound: a week-ahead piece describes the index as having been on track for its deepest four-day decline since June, with WTI crude above $100 and the 10-year yield closing in on 5%, before buyers stepped in on Friday to halt the slide. The same piece attributes the losing week to a warmer-than-expected producer price index, surging energy prices and higher bond yields.

The correction, carried forward: earlier editions of this page carried a weekly Dow move of −1.51%, and the previous run replaced it with a sourced −426 points and a derived ≈−0.80% on the grounds that −1.51% was arithmetically impossible against Friday's close. The AP wire, read in full this run, gives the week as −840.96 points, or 1.6%. That reconciles against Friday's close; the −426-point figure was the bad datum, and the percentage it displaced was roughly right. Both −1.51% and −426 points / ≈−0.80% are retired.

On the Radar

  • Who is deciding, and what the expectation is. The week-ahead source read this run names Federal Reserve Chairman Kevin Warsh and says he will "likely deliver a hike" on Wednesday after the latest inflation data failed to settle fears about pricing pressure. It also describes a crucial week for global markets with the Fed, Bank of England and Bank of Japan all deciding against a backdrop of rising oil prices.
  • Federal Reserve decision — Wednesday 16 September, 2 PM ET. The date and time are confirmed by two independent week-ahead sources. One of them publishes in Australian time and lists the Fed at "Thursday, 17 September at 4.00am AEST", which converts exactly to 2 PM ET Wednesday; two earlier editions of this page recorded that source as wrong about the date, and that reading — not the source — was the error.
  • Hike odds are in genuine dispute and all three reads are printed, not averaged. A post-CPI read gives ~71%, up from 61%; a third source read this run gives about 70% for next week, with a move fully priced in by October instead of December; a CME FedWatch read, now re-sourced this run and explicitly dated to 11 September, gives roughly 90%. Two of the three cluster near 70% and the third does not; all three are printed because the dispute is real.
  • The inflation data are mixed, not one-directional. Annual core CPI slowed to 2.4%, its lowest since 2021, against core +0.3% month-on-month (up from +0.2%). August PPI ran 5.4% year-on-year (from 4.8%), core 4.6% (from 4.2%). Jobless claims fell 1,000 to 205,000.
  • Diesel past $6 a gallon. A wire headline dated Friday reports U.S. diesel prices soaring past $6 a gallon and "deepening strain for hauling everyday goods". The headline is what is sourced; this page makes no further claim about where that cost lands.
  • A $6 billion Treasury bond buyback plan attributed to Bessent accompanied the 10-year yield touching its highest since 2023 earlier in the week. No official title is given in the source read, so none is asserted here.
  • Also next week, converted from the same Australian-time source: Bank of England Thursday 17 September, 7 AM ET; Bank of Japan Thursday 18 September, 11 PM ET. The European Central Bank raised by 25 basis points.
