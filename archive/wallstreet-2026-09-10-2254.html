The Lead

Post-close: a fourth straight down day on oil and yields — and then Oracle turned the after-hours mood

The regular session is over. Thursday was the fourth consecutive daily decline for the major averages, and the figures below now carry a full set of corroborated closing levels for the first time this week:

  • S&P 500 7,591.70, down 44.66 points (−0.58%); Dow Jones Industrial Average 52,064.10, down 316.56 (−0.60%); Nasdaq Composite 26,081.72, down 0.65%. All three reconcile to the cent against Wednesday's verified closes — 7,636.36, 52,380.66 and, from the AP wire, 26,253.34 — so the Nasdaq level that two earlier editions withheld is published here for the first time.
  • The driver was crude. Stocks dropped after U.S. oil topped $100 a barrel on fears that a prolonged Middle East war feeds through into inflation. At 9:47 a.m. ET West Texas Intermediate was up 4.2% at $100.10 and Brent up 3.6% at $105.37, after what one wire called the biggest spike in attacks on shipping since the U.S.–Iran war began.
  • The bond market did the rest. A 3:48 p.m. ET curve read shows 52-week highs at every tenor, with the 10-year at 4.954%, up 11.4 basis points, and the 2-year at 4.567%, up 14. Odds of a Federal Reserve rate hike next week ran near 70% on CME FedWatch.
  • Then the bell rang and the tape flipped. Oracle beat on revenue and earnings, raised its full-year targets and disclosed a $664 billion book of remaining performance obligations; shares jumped about 7% in extended trading after closing down roughly 3.5%. Adobe also beat and raised. Details in After-Hours Movers below.

Wholesale inflation landed in line: August PPI rose 0.4% month over month, matching the Dow Jones consensus, but the annual rate climbed to 5.4% from 4.8% in July, slightly above forecasts. The August CPI is due Friday at 8:30 a.m. ET, five days before the Fed decides. One caution carried forward: TheStreet's own headline calls Brent's level the highest since July while its 9:47 a.m. entry says highest since May. Neither “highest since” claim is published here.

Carried+4.7%

Reddit (RDDT) gained 4.7% on positive reaction to new Piper Sandler data showing an 8% month-over-month increase in the platform's user base in August. Charter Communications (CHTR) jumped 4.5% on a technical rebound after Wednesday's sharp selloff, and Elevance Health (ELV) rose 4.1% after saying it plans to reaffirm full-year 2026 earnings and benefit-expense guidance.

CarriedRetail

Macy's beats and raises — and slips anyway

Macy's (M) reported adjusted earnings of 63 cents a share against a 37-cent consensus and up from 35 cents a year earlier, with net sales up 1.1% to $4.87 billion. It lifted full-year adjusted EPS guidance to $2.15–$2.35 and revenue to $21.68–$21.83 billion. Bloomingdale's comparable sales rose 11.3% against just 1.1% at the namesake chain. Shares were down 1.4% premarket; the guidance includes a benefit from $116 million of tariff refunds received so far.

NewHousing

Existing home sales fall to the slowest pace since June 2025

Sales of previously owned homes fell 2% in August from July to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.98 million, the National Association of Realtors said — the lowest since June 2025 and down 1.2% year over year. Inventory reached 1.62 million homes, a 4.9-month supply that NAR calls the highest in more than a decade, while the median price rose 1.6% to $429,100.

NewCentral banks

The ECB hiked, and the war is why

The European Central Bank raised its key deposit rate 25 basis points to 2.5% from 2.25%, a move markets had priced at 100% on LSEG data. Uncertainty over the U.S.–Iran war continues to cloud the outlook as higher energy prices feed inflation; ECB officials have taken a meeting-by-meeting approach since the war began.

After-Hours Movers

New+6–7% AH

Oracle: a beat, a raise, and a $664 billion backlog

Fiscal Q1 2027 revenue of $19.35 billion against $19.14 billion expected, up 30% year over year, with adjusted earnings of $1.92 a share against $1.74 expected. Remaining performance obligations rose to $664 billion, up $209 billion year over year, and Oracle said it booked more than $30 billion of additional AI cloud contracts in the quarter. For fiscal 2027 it now guides to $8.10 in adjusted EPS on at least $90 billion of revenue. Shares rose in extended trading — CNBC put the move at 7%, a second after-hours round-up at 6%; both reads are given rather than one picked. The same round-up attributes the beat to $11.6 billion of cloud revenue.

Context for the move: ORCL closed the regular session down about 3.5% — one read has it off 3.48% to $156.00, another 3.66%, and a third “nearly 4% to $155.22” — and options had implied roughly an 11% swing. CoreWeave rose about 1.7% in sympathy on the read that Oracle's cloud-infrastructure growth points to broader AI-workload demand.

NewBeat & raise

Adobe: small beats on both lines, targets lifted

Q3 fiscal 2026 adjusted earnings of $6.13 a share against $6.09 expected, on revenue of $6.76 billion against $6.70 billion expected — beats of roughly 1% each. Revenue rose 13% year over year and EPS 15%. Adobe raised its full-year 2026 revenue target to $26.58–$26.63 billion, said AI-first annualised recurring revenue expanded more than 150% year over year, and claimed 1 billion monthly active users across its creativity and productivity products.

A post-bell move is now sourced for the first time: an after-hours round-up read this run has ADBE up about 1%, having first dipped roughly 2%. The same read gives fourth-quarter guidance of $6.8–$6.85 billion in revenue and $6.30–$6.35 in adjusted EPS, which it calls in line. Earlier editions today withheld any ADBE post-bell figure because the only moves on offer were dated to the September leadership announcement, not to tonight. The stock entered the print down roughly 18% in 2026 after a 21% fall in 2025, and with a leadership change already announced — Anil Chakravarthy becomes president and chief executive on 1 December, succeeding Shantanu Narayen, who moves to executive chair.

On the Radar

  • August CPI, Friday 11 September at 8:30 a.m. ET. The Dow Jones consensus calls for a 0.4% monthly increase and 3.4% over twelve months, with core prices expected up 0.2%. Fed Governor Waller has said he would vote to hold at 0.2% and would support a hike above it — a single tenth of a percentage point could decide next week's meeting.
  • University of Michigan consumer sentiment, Friday 11 September at 10:00 a.m. ET. The August reading fell by more than 10 points to 70.3; September is expected to recover slightly, to about 72. Thursday's put-call ratio ended at 0.85.
  • The FOMC meets next week, with markets pricing a hike near 70%. The argument for one, per TheStreet Pro: a war that has kept oil elevated for six months, new tariffs and a large AI buildout all keeping inflation pressure alive.
  • Oil is now a political variable. President Trump said Wednesday that prices likely will not fall until after the midterms, and warned Iran “not to get cute” over activity at the suspected nuclear site at Pickaxe Mountain. Multiple U.S. military aircraft were damaged in Iranian strikes on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan; no U.S. deaths were reported.
  • The “Trump Dividend.” At the Republican midterm convention in Dallas, the president proposed sending every American adult $5,000 if Republicans retain the House and Senate — a plan the AP notes could cost more than $1 trillion and Reuters estimates at about $1.35 trillion, and which would require congressional approval. Vice President Vance later suggested wealthier Americans might be excluded and that tariff revenue could fund it.
  • SpaceX (SPCX) slipped 0.52% to $146.78 as up to 319 million previously locked-up shares became eligible for sale following the June IPO lockup expiry. Shares debuted at $135 on 12 June and hit a record $225.64 on 16 June.
