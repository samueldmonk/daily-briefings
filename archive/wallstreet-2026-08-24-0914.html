The lead

A tariff-and-sanctions Monday — and a 30-year yield at a two-decade high

US markets have not opened yet. As of the Yahoo Finance market bar read at roughly 6:45 AM ET, S&P 500 futures were at 7,679.00, down 12.25 points or 0.16%; Dow futures at 53,331.00, down 22.00 or 0.04%; Nasdaq futures at 29,236.50, down 151.25 or 0.51%; and Russell 2000 futures at 3,019.60, down 2.50 or 0.08%. Yahoo Finance’s live blog, filed at 4:03 AM ET, described Dow futures as edging into the red with the S&P 500 and the tech-heavy Nasdaq slipping 0.2% and 0.7% respectively, after all the major indexes posted weekly declines. TheStreet’s Monday market report, read later in the pre-market, has Dow futures down 0.1% at 53,306.00 (−47.00, −0.09%) and Nasdaq futures at 29,227.50 (−160.25, −0.55%), with S&P 500 futures off 0.1% and the Nasdaq contract down 0.4%. The pre-market ETF proxies are pointing the same way: SPY −0.31%, QQQ −0.77%, DIA −0.23% and IWM −0.24%.

Two policy shocks landed over the weekend. Trade talks between the United States and Canada collapsed late Friday, and additional 50% duties on a slate of Canadian goods took effect at the stroke of midnight under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — covering alcohol, hockey equipment, cement, dairy, building materials and certain kinds of clothing. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney called the move “a miscalculation,” blamed the breakdown on “uneconomic” and “unfair” US demands, and said retaliatory tariffs will take effect on September 8 targeting dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics. “You’re at war when you get attacked. We got attacked,” he said at a Saturday press conference. The Canadian dollar dipped; the US dollar added 0.2% against it to 1.3790.

The second is Iran. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who is scheduled to hold a press conference Monday afternoon, wrote in a Financial Times op-ed over the weekend that the coming measures will constitute “an economic D-Day — the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary.” Share markets slipped in Asia and oil prices eased as investors waited for the detail.

Underneath it all, the bond market is the constraint that overrides everything else. TheStreet puts the 30-year Treasury yield at roughly 5.28% on Monday, describing it as having climbed above 5.3% to its highest level in nearly two decades, with government bond yields in Japan, France and Germany also at multi-year highs. Bloomberg framed the same move as investor angst over surging government spending, a flood of long-dated bond sales and inflation stuck above the Fed’s target for five years. The Treasury’s response — at least doubling the maximum size of its long-term debt buybacks from $2 billion to at least $4 billion for purchases running September through November — bought only temporary relief; yields rebounded last week and wiped out the dip that followed Bessent’s intervention. The US 10-year sits near a 20-month high at about 4.74%, and that is the backdrop for a US Composite PMI of 56, with services at 56.8 and manufacturing at 53.2. The week’s two big tests arrive together on Wednesday — July PCE in the morning and Nvidia’s fiscal Q2 after the bell, where FXStreet notes more than $92.05 billion of quarterly revenue is expected — before Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote on Friday.

On the radar
  • Monday, August 24 — Bessent’s Iran press conference. The Treasury Secretary is expected to detail new US economic sanctions on Iran at an afternoon press conference, after writing in the Financial Times that the measures amount to “an economic D-Day.” Also today: the Chicago Fed National Activity Index and the Dallas Fed Manufacturing Survey, and PDD Holdings reports Q2 2026 results pre-market.
  • Monday, August 24 — the Canada tariffs are live. The additional 50% US duties took effect at midnight. Canada’s retaliatory tariffs are scheduled for September 8, which puts a two-week negotiating window on the calendar.
  • Tuesday, August 25. The Conference Board’s Consumer Confidence Index (90.8 previously), July new home sales (+1.6% previously), the Richmond Fed manufacturing index and the FHFA house price index, plus earnings from Intuit, Zoom, Bank of Montreal and DICK’S Sporting Goods.
  • Wednesday, August 26 — PCE and Nvidia. July PCE (−0.1% m/m previously, +3.7% y/y) and core PCE (+0.1% m/m, +3.3% y/y) land in the morning, with CNBC’s week-ahead putting the consensus for the core annual rate at 3.3%, alongside the second estimate of Q2 GDP (+1.5% annualized on the first estimate) and July durable goods orders (+0.5% previously). Nvidia reports fiscal Q2 after the bell, with FXStreet noting expectations of more than $92.05 billion in quarterly revenue; Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Okta, Synopsys, Veeva, Williams-Sonoma and Nutanix also report.
  • Thursday, August 27. Jackson Hole opens, and Marvell Technology, Workday, Autodesk, Dollar General, Dollar Tree and Ulta Beauty report. Also: initial jobless claims (206,000 previously) and the advance July goods trade balance.
  • Friday, August 28 — Warsh at Jackson Hole. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair, scheduled for 10 a.m. He is expected to stick to his practice of avoiding forward guidance. The data slate is lighter: the MNI Chicago PMI and the final August University of Michigan sentiment and inflation-expectations readings.
