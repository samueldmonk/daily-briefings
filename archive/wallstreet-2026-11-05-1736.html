The Lead

Closing bell: S&P 500 −4.36%, Nasdaq −5.52% into a correction, Dow −1,833 (−3.44%) as the AI trade melts down

New Official closes: the S&P 500 finished at 7,353.73, down 334.89 points (−4.36%); the Nasdaq Composite at 24,315.00, down 1,421.52 (−5.52%); and the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 51,523.06, down 1,833.06 (−3.44%) — all consistent with Wednesday’s closes (JournalArta close report, data as of 4:16 PM ET; S&P figure matches CNBC, which headlined that the Nasdaq “tumbles into correction territory”). JournalArta called it the Nasdaq’s biggest one-day drop in 19 months; the S&P 500 is now 5.95% lower over one month though still up 7.42% for the year, and the Nasdaq is down 11.9% over the month (JournalArta). The 10-year Treasury yield eased to 4.837% from 4.894%, and WTI crude rose 2.75% to $90.54 (JournalArta).

New Late in the session, retail investors were capitulating: they sold a net $358 million of individual U.S. stocks as of 2:58 p.m. ET, per Vanda Research, on track for their biggest single-day net selling since March 17, 2020 — “total capitulation,” said Vanda’s Viraj Patel. Nomura’s Charlie McElligott described hedge funds rushing to lock in 2026 gains: “They’re shooting the generals.” As of 3:47 p.m. ET the PHLX Semiconductor Index was down 10.65%; Lam Research is down 36% over the past month and Applied Materials 30% (Yahoo Finance live blog, 3:47 PM ET).

How the day unfolded (intraday reads, each labelled with its time):

How close to a halt? Decrypt (via Yahoo Finance, published about 1:05 p.m. ET) noted the S&P 500 had plunged as much as 3.8%, putting market-wide circuit breakers in focus: the first, 7% threshold corresponds to an S&P 500 level of 7,150.41 for Thursday’s session and, if hit before 3:25 p.m. ET, triggers a 15-minute halt; the index had not reached it at the time of that report (Decrypt via Yahoo Finance). The AP’s 12:26 p.m. ET update had the Dow down 1,433 points (−2.7%), the S&P 500 −3.5% and the Nasdaq −4.7%, with every sector in the S&P 500 losing ground; Nvidia −7.5%, AMD −14.6%, CoreWeave −12.1%, GE Vernova −6.1%, while AppLovin jumped 28% and Tapestry 6.6%. Brent rose 2.6% to $97.11 and the 10-year yield eased to 4.83% (AP via Dayton Daily News, 12:26 PM ET).

The S&P 500 had shed more than $2 trillion in market value by midmorning, Yahoo Finance’s Jared Blikre wrote — Nvidia alone more than $400 billion, with chipmakers as a group losing well over $1 trillion; the PHLX Semiconductor Index was more than a third below its June record, and both the Nasdaq Composite and Nasdaq-100 were trading below their 200-day moving averages (Yahoo Finance). Allianz’s Mohamed El-Erian told CNBC “the market is overdoing it,” though he warned the U.S. economy is “really levered on AI right now” (Stocktwits, 12:49 PM ET).

At the session’s worst, the Nasdaq-100 fell as much as 6.24%, per Breitbart’s John Carney (published ~11:57 a.m. ET), and Investing.com put the S&P 500’s intraday low at as much as 3.8% down (Breitbart; Investing.com). Investing.com tied the liquidation of AI and data-center stocks to fresh questions about the financing behind the industry’s infrastructure boom — including Anthropic’s potential IPO (Investing.com).

As of about 1:01 p.m. ET, Yahoo Finance’s quote strip showed the S&P 500 at 7,379.33, down 309.29 points (−4.02%); the Dow at 51,684.10, down 1,672.02 (−3.13%); and the Nasdaq Composite at 24,378.98, down 1,357.54 (−5.27%). The Russell 2000 was off 4.31%, the PHLX Semiconductor Index 10.87%, and the VIX was up 55% at 25.76 (Yahoo Finance quote strip, ~1:01 PM ET). Yahoo’s Jared Blikre wrote that the Dow was heading for its worst second session after a midterm election in data going back to 1896, that the Nasdaq took six minutes to erase its entire post-election rally, and that the S&P 500 and Nasdaq were on pace for their worst day since the April 2025 tariff crash (Yahoo Finance).

Earlier, Wall Street’s main averages fell heavily after Wednesday’s big gains as investors resumed a tech rout that had the Nasdaq shed more than 5%, Seeking Alpha reported at 12:32 p.m. ET; its summary put the S&P 500 down 3.5%, said the VIX surged as much as 70% to a seven-month high, and noted Bitcoin fell about 5% while Apple launched a debt sale (Seeking Alpha, 12:32 PM ET). Reuters headlined its update “Nasdaq set to confirm correction as AI stocks tumble” (Reuters via Investing.com); at an earlier snapshot the Nasdaq’s 3.2% drop left it about 10% below its Oct. 6 record close, per The FINANCIAL’s summary of Reuters (The FINANCIAL).

Another wipeout for stocks tied to the buildout of AI data centers is overwhelming Wall Street and more than erasing all of Wednesday’s gains, the AP reported. The S&P 500 fell 3.2% after opening with a loss that accelerated through the morning, putting it on track for its worst drop since the April 2025 “Liberation Day” tariff shock; as of 11 a.m. ET the Dow was down 1,146 points, or 2.1%, and the Nasdaq Composite was 5% lower (AP, via Spectrum News). Nvidia fell 8.3% — by far the heaviest weight on the S&P 500, a day after dropping 3.2% — while AMD tumbled 15.9%, CoreWeave 14.9% and GE Vernova 9.1%; roughly two of every five S&P 500 stocks still rose (AP, via Spectrum News).

Earlier, TipRanks, in a piece updated at about 11:28 a.m. ET, had the S&P 500 down 2.8% and the Nasdaq-100 down 4%, with the PHLX Semiconductor Index off more than 7% (TipRanks). Its read of the selloff: UBS strategists estimate project delays could affect around 30% of expected 2028 data-center capacity additions, and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack backed a gradual approach toward further rate increases, with markets anticipating a possible December hike (TipRanks). Memory, storage and networking names were among the hardest hit at one intraday checkpoint — Sandisk −20.6%, Western Digital −17.1%, Lumentum −16.7%, Seagate −16.3%, Micron −15.7% — figures Eurasia Business News itself flags as unverified intraday readings (Eurasia Business News).

The driver: Democrats’ nationwide midterm wins raised the possibility that governments could force pauses in data-center construction, which would mean fewer AI-chip purchases, on top of months of worry that expensive borrowing could slow the buildout (AP, via Spectrum News). Voters sided against AI data centers in 40 of 41 local ballot measures, CNBC reported, per 24/7 Wall St., which described money rotating away from chip suppliers toward hyperscalers that already own AI capacity (24/7 Wall St.). Yahoo Finance’s live blog, in a later update summarized in search at about 11:37 a.m. ET, had the Dow down 2.2% (more than 1,200 points), the S&P 500 −3.3% and the Nasdaq −5%, and said the S&P 500 had shed more than $2 trillion in market value by midmorning (Yahoo Finance, via search).

The day started very differently. Stocks opened mixed — the Dow +0.6%, the S&P 500 −0.3% and the Nasdaq −1% — as the post-election rally faded and tech came under renewed pressure (Yahoo Finance). By 10:10 a.m. ET Yahoo’s quote strip had the S&P 500 −2.03%, the Dow −0.94% (−499.45 points), the Nasdaq −3.61% and the Russell 2000 −1.19%, with the VIX up 21.73% to 20.20 (Yahoo Finance quote strip, ~10:10 AM ET). Before the bell, index funds had pointed to a flat S&P open, with Nvidia −2% and Intel −3% premarket and the 3x-leveraged SOXL chip fund indicated −11%, per 24/7 Wall St. at about 8:20 a.m. (24/7 Wall St.).

Elsewhere, Treasuries rallied: the 10-year yield fell to 4.84% from 4.90% late Wednesday, versus 4.95% on election night and above 5.30% last month, even as fewer Americans filed jobless claims and productivity beat forecasts (AP, via Spectrum News). Brent crude rose 2.2% to $96.70, recovering part of Wednesday’s nearly 9% drop (AP, via Spectrum News). South Korea’s Kospi tumbled 5.3% and Japan’s Nikkei 1.8%, while London’s FTSE 100 dipped 0.1% after the Bank of England raised rates (AP, via Spectrum News). Correction There is no Fed policy decision today, contrary to an earlier edition: the Federal Reserve’s November calendar lists the minutes of the Oct. 27–28 FOMC meeting for Nov. 18 at 2 p.m. ET (Federal Reserve calendar).

Movers & Drivers

NewDown

Retail “capitulation” — biggest selling since 2020

Everyday investors sold a net $358 million in individual U.S. stocks as of 2:58 p.m. ET, per Vanda Research, breaking their habit of buying the dip; Vanda saw “no signs of buying the dip” in single stocks, though some kept buying leveraged ETFs such as TQQQ and SOXL. (Yahoo Finance)

Down

Where the $2 trillion went

Through midmorning, Nvidia lost about $430 billion of market value, TSMC about $220 billion, SpaceX about $200 billion, Tesla and Microsoft about $170 billion each, and Broadcom, Micron and SK Hynix about $150 billion each. Tesla, SpaceX, Robinhood, Coinbase and Strategy were each down more than 10%. Few places to hide: AppLovin up nearly 30%, Motorola Solutions and Verisk about 10%, Aflac and Allstate edging higher. (Yahoo Finance)

Macro

El-Erian: “overdoing it” — but expect another hike

El-Erian said he expects “smarter adoption of AI rather than no adoption,” sees the 10-year yield staying around 5% absent a recession, and expects the Fed to hike at least once more but not “the three hikes that are priced in.” At midday, Stocktwits reported Nvidia down more than 7% (April lows), SanDisk more than 17%, Nebius 16.7%, AMD more than 15%, Micron more than 13%, and the 3x SOXL ETF more than 30%. (Stocktwits, 12:49 PM ET)

Macro

AI selloff becomes a test for Anthropic’s IPO

Vital Knowledge Media wrote that data-center “pick-and-shovel stocks have been in liquidation mode Wed and Thurs,” raising “serious doubts about whether Anthropic can raise ~$100B at a $1.5T+ valuation by Thanksgiving.” Investing.com notes Anthropic has not yet set formal offering terms. (Investing.com, 11:28 AM ET)

Down

AI compute renters and mega-caps hit hard

As of Breitbart’s late-morning read, CoreWeave fell about 14%, Nebius 17% and IREN 19%, while Nvidia, Broadcom and Oracle each fell about 9% and the iShares Semiconductor ETF more than 11%. Yorkville’s Dan Ives estimated roughly 10% of planned U.S. data centers “will not happen now,” saying Texas accounts for roughly 25%–30% of expected capacity. (Breitbart)

Down

SpaceX and Tesla each drop more than 9%

Shares of the two Elon Musk-led companies extended declines after Democrats won control of the House and Senate, raising concerns over regulatory hurdles for U.S. data-center buildouts and for a SpaceX–Tesla merger. Yorkville’s Dan Ives: the merger “now faces a much tougher regulatory roadmap” for 2027. (Yahoo Finance)

Down

Volatility spikes — biggest jump in over two years

The VIX surged more than 65% to as much as 27.45, the biggest one-day jump for the “fear gauge” in over two years, Futurum’s Shay Boloor noted; even Wednesday’s winners, including Apple, turned lower (Yahoo Finance). Earlier, Seeking Alpha put the intraday surge at as much as 70%, to a seven-month high; the S&P 500 erased the previous day’s rally; Bitcoin fell about 5% as investors shunned risk assets, and Apple launched a debt sale. (Seeking Alpha, 12:32 PM ET)

Macro

UBS: ~30% of 2028 data-center additions at risk

UBS strategists estimate potential project delays could affect around 30% of expected data-center capacity additions in 2028. Texas Gov.-elect Gina Hinojosa campaigned on extending a moratorium on new data-center approvals; Georgia also elected a Democrat who supported restrictions. (TipRanks)

Down

Qualcomm’s handset miss

Fiscal Q4 handset revenue fell 26% y/y to about $5.14 billion, below the $5.25 billion expected; total GAAP revenue fell 8% to $10.322 billion, while adjusted EPS of $2.17 edged past the $2.16 estimate and automotive grew 89%. (Eurasia Business News)

Macro

Fed hike talk resurfaces

Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack supported a gradual approach toward additional rate increases, and markets anticipate another potential hike in December, TipRanks reported; Friday’s October jobs report is the next test. (TipRanks)

Down

AI-infrastructure stocks crushed

As of the AP’s 11 a.m. ET read: Nvidia −8.3% (after −3.2% Wednesday), Advanced Micro Devices −15.9%, CoreWeave −14.9% and GE Vernova −9.1%. The AP notes AI chip stocks are among Wall Street’s most influential because of their size. (AP, via Spectrum News)

Up

AppLovin soars 30.2% on its Q3 beat

AppLovin led the S&P 500, up 30.2% per the AP, after revenue and profit topped expectations and it added $3.2 billion to its buyback for $3.6 billion total (AP, via Spectrum News). Q3 revenue was $2.18 billion vs. $2.07 billion expected and EPS $4.30 vs. $4.04 (Bloomberg estimates); Q4 revenue guidance of $2.37–$2.40 billion beat the $2.25 billion forecast. BTIG raised its target to $450 from $396; Needham reiterated Buy with a $475 target (Yahoo Finance).

Up

Tapestry rallies 6.7%

The Coach and Kate Spade owner beat profit expectations and raised its fiscal-year revenue and earnings forecasts. (AP, via Spectrum News)

Down

Qualcomm sinks after its results

Qualcomm sank after its quarterly results, while chip and data-center stocks were among the biggest laggards at the open. (Yahoo Finance) It reported fiscal Q4 non-GAAP revenue of $10.44 billion (−7% y/y) and non-GAAP EPS of $2.17, and guided fiscal Q1 revenue to $10.4–$11.2 billion (desk record, Qualcomm 8-K).

Down

Chips were slipping even before the bell

At ~8:20 a.m. ET: Nvidia −2% premarket, adding to an 18% drop over the past month; Intel −3% and down 51% in a month; SOXL indicated −11%. Amazon (+8% Wednesday) was up another 2% premarket and Apple (nearly +7% Wednesday) +1%. (24/7 Wall St.)

Deal

Wendy’s rejects Trian’s $1.4 billion offer

Wendy’s independent committee said Nelson Peltz’s Trian-led proposal “substantially undervalues” the company and unveiled a multiyear turnaround plan, “Dave’s Way”; shares were +1.5% premarket. (Yahoo Finance)

Up

Oil snaps back

Brent +2.2% to $96.70 as of the AP’s update, after both benchmarks tumbled more than 8% Wednesday to settle at their lowest since Aug. 31 (Reuters via Yahoo) — Brent at $94.62 (−8.7%), per the AP. Vice President JD Vance posted that “The reason we lost [the midterm elections] is the war.” (AP, via Spectrum News; Yahoo Finance)

Macro

Bank of England hikes for the first time since 2023

The BOE voted 8–1 to raise its key rate a quarter point to 4%, with one member favoring a bigger move; it sees inflation rising to 5% by mid-2027. Gov. Andrew Bailey: unless the Middle East conflict is resolved, “neither does it seem likely to be the last increase.” (Yahoo Finance)

Macro

Layoffs at their lowest since 2023

Employers announced 43,994 job cuts in October, up slightly from September but down 71% from a year earlier, Challenger, Gray & Christmas said. Separately, fewer workers filed for jobless benefits last week and Q3 productivity improved more than economists expected. (Yahoo Finance; AP, via Spectrum News)

Macro

A Democratic Senate blocks a Fed shake-up

Democrats’ Senate control effectively blocks President Trump’s campaign to reshape the Federal Reserve, since any new Fed governor needs Senate approval, UBS’s Jonathan Pingle wrote. (Yahoo Finance)

Chart of the Day
