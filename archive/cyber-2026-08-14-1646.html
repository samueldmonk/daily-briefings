Top Story

Metabase zero-day hands attackers admin without a password — and the federal clock runs out today

CVE-2026-72898 is an unauthenticated SQL injection in the business-intelligence platform Metabase, reachable through the /reset_password endpoint, that lets a remote attacker inject arbitrary SQL against the application database and take over the instance as an administrator. It carries a CVSS score of 10.0. From there an attacker can change application configuration, steal stored credentials for every connected database, read data through those connections and export it wholesale.

Metabase disclosed the flaw on August 6 and confirmed it was already being exploited against real-world environments; by the time of disclosure, five companies had lost customer data. Affected builds run from 0.59.0–0.59.20, 0.60.0–0.60.16, 0.61.0–0.61.10, 0.62.0–0.62.8 and 0.63.0–0.63.4 (plus the matching 1.x Enterprise trains); versions below 0.58 / 1.58 are not affected. Fixed releases are 0.58.24, 0.59.21, 0.60.17, 0.61.11, 0.62.9 and 0.63.5. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 11 with a federal remediation date of August 14, 2026 — today.

Patch Priority

Do this first
Metabase — CVE-2026-72898 (CVSS 10.0), federal due date August 14, 2026 (today). Upgrade every Metabase instance to 0.58.24 / 0.59.21 / 0.60.17 / 0.61.11 / 0.62.9 / 0.63.5 or the corresponding Enterprise 1.x build. Because successful exploitation exposes stored credentials for connected databases, patching alone is not enough — rotate the credentials of every data source attached to an instance that was internet-reachable. Cisco Secure Firewall ASA/FTD (CVE-2026-20349) carries the same August 14 deadline; Cisco has shipped hot fixes for all affected trains and there are no workarounds.

Threat Actor Spotlight

EspionageNew

Jewelbug — one infrastructure, two missions

Researchers on Broadcom's Threat Hunter Team published a report on August 13 on the group tracked as Jewelbug — also known as Ink Dragon, Earth Alux, REF770 and CL-STA-0049. Their finding: the same infrastructure supports espionage against governments and militaries across the Middle East, Southeast Asia and South Asia and a financially motivated operation targeting Chinese-speaking cryptocurrency users. The overlap collapses the usual analytic wall between state-aligned intrusion sets and profit-driven crews.

Breaches & Incidents

Data leak

RingCentral — 1.6 million accounts published

RingCentral confirmed a breach in July after attackers got in through what it described as a sophisticated social engineering campaign. The extortion group ShinyHunters claimed responsibility and leaked the stolen data after the company refused to pay, exposing information tied to 1.6 million accounts — names, email addresses, phone numbers and physical addresses.

RansomwareNew

Helix claims Morguard

On August 7 the Helix ransomware group announced a cyberattack against Morguard, a major Canadian real estate firm, and threatened to expose stolen data after negotiations broke down without a deal.

RansomwareNew

INC accelerates through August

Resecurity reports the INC ransomware operation has stepped up activity since the start of August, listing a run of new victims on its leak site, and has emerged as the dominant actor exploiting recently disclosed flaws in SonicWall Secure Mobile Access 1000 series VPN appliances. Ransomware.Live tallies 885 claimed victims to date, with the most recent listed on August 2.

Vishing

Questel confirms Microsoft 365 intrusion

Questel confirmed on August 13 that a voice-phishing campaign led to a breach of its Microsoft 365 tenant, with a Sales SharePoint site among the data touched. ShinyHunters claims more than 21 million records and over 147GB; Questel was listed alongside Alcon and Lumenis on August 1, with a contact deadline set for August 4.
