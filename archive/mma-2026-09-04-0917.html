Top Story

Paris weigh-in day: Parnasse hits 155 first, and the full card is published in running order

Weigh-ins for UFC Fight Night: Hooker vs. Parnasse took place Friday morning local time in Paris, and Salahdine Parnasse was first on the scale, at 155 pounds. Parnasse is the only fighter whose weight is confirmed in the sources fetched this edition — no source gives the other 27 fighters' figures, so this page does not assert that the rest of the card made weight. The card is UFC Fight Night 287: Saturday, September 5, at the Accor Arena, 14 fights, prelims at 12 PM ET and the main card at 3 PM ET on Paramount+ — an unusual daytime slot for a U.S. audience, dictated by the Paris start.

Parnasse is 23-2 and carries a five-fight winning streak into a UFC debut that is also a headlining slot — a rare piece of treatment. Cageside Press notes the last comparable case was Kai Asakura, a former RIZIN champion given a main event at the end of 2024, and an immediate title shot with it; the UFC is not going that far here. Parnasse arrives as a former KSW double champion, at featherweight and lightweight, nicknamed the “Lion of Atlas”, and won on a Netflix card in May.

Dan Hooker is 24-14 — 38 professional bouts — and took the assignment on short notice. He is the reason a debutant gets five rounds at all.

The line has moved a long way and is quoted differently everywhere, so all five readings are printed and none is adopted: Parnasse −649 / Hooker +420 and −600 / +440, against −620 / +400, −667 / +417, and an opening line of −357 / +275. The direction is unambiguous even where the number is not.

The order below follows the weigh-in-day listing because it is the most recent, but it rests on a single outlet and the split is not presented as settled.

Prospect Watch

prospect

Salahdine Parnasse, on the scale and into the main event

Not a prospect in the usual sense — 23-2, five straight wins, a former KSW double champion at featherweight and lightweight — but he is a UFC newcomer, and the promotion is treating tomorrow as a launch. A win puts him immediately near the top of a lightweight division facing turnover.

Around the Sport

  • The lightweight division is heading for a changeover, and that is the subtext of tomorrow's main event. In Cageside Press's reading: champion Justin Gaethje is mulling retirement though signs point to one more fight; Conor McGregor is all but done and last competed at welterweight anyway; Charles Oliveira, Beneil Dariush and Max Holloway are on the back side of their careers; and Islam Makhachev has already moved to 170. Ilia Topuria and Paddy Pimblett remain, and Parnasse is cast as one of the division's next stars. This is the outlet's assessment, attributed as such, not a promotion announcement.
  • UFC 332 still has no main event, a month out. Valentina Shevchenko's women's flyweight defence against No. 1 contender Natalia Silva collapsed when Shevchenko suffered an injury. She remains champion. The UFC is reported to be working on an interim women's flyweight title fight between Natalia Silva and Wang Cong — reported, not announced. Dana White says the call comes this week.
  • A disambiguation, because two different Silvas are live this month: Natalia Silva is the women's flyweight contender. She is not Jean Silva, who fights at Noche UFC on September 12.
  • Song Yadong demanded a title shot in his post-fight interview after the Shanghai upset, and the win puts him back in the mix at 135 pounds.
  • Contender Series Week 8 is scheduled for Tuesday, September 29.

Rankings & Business

Rankings movement. ESPN's divisional rankings update this week is headlined by Song Yadong's rise at bantamweight on the back of the upset knockout of Umar Nurmagomedov.

Business & broadcast. Freedom 250 drew roughly 34 million total global viewers (viewers watching at least one minute — not an average), with 17 million across the US and Latin America, an 8.2 million average across US+LatAm and a 7.0 million US average, making it the most-watched UFC event ever domestically. Paramount is in year one of a seven-year, $7.7 billion US rights deal. For comparison, UFC 324 in January 2026 drew a 4.96 million average-minute audience, 7.18 million households and a 5.93 million peak concurrent.
