The Lead

A hawkish hold: the Fed stands pat with three dissents for a hike — and the Dow’s 1,153-point dive is its worst day since April 2025

The close. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate at 3.50–3.75% Wednesday for a fifth consecutive meeting — but in a split decision, three officials dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike: Minneapolis’ Neel Kashkari, Dallas’ Lorie Logan and Cleveland’s Beth Hammack (CNBC/Bloomberg/CNN/US News). Chair Kevin Warsh shrugged off the division at his press conference — “I asked for a good family fight, and I got one” (Yahoo Finance/CNN) — but markets read the cliffhanger as a central bank at odds with itself over inflation, and the reaction was brutal: the Dow closed down 1,153.18 points (−2.19%) at 51,594.14, its worst decline since April 2025; the S&P 500 slid 1.52% to 7,316.15; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.74% to 24,442.94, leaving it more than 10% below its record (CNBC). The Nasdaq 100 finished in correction territory — down 1.8% on the day and more than 11% from its June peak, with a rout in chipmakers still pummeling the broader gauge (Bloomberg). CNBC’s wrap pins the selloff mostly on the bond market signaling the Fed “could be falling behind on the inflation fight”: the 10-year Treasury yield jumped about 7 basis points to above 4.67% after the decision — Yahoo Finance’s headline called it a “hawkish hold.” The drop more than unwinds Tuesday’s split close: Dow +537.45 (+1.03%) to 52,747.53, S&P 500 +0.22% to 7,429.22, Nasdaq −0.22% to 24,876.91 (Yahoo Finance closing board/CNBC).

The war premium held through the close. Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched what U.S. Central Command called an “attempted surprise attack” — ballistic missiles fired at U.S. forces, all intercepted, the first exchange since Friday’s pause in hostilities (CNN/Yahoo Finance) — and TheStreet’s Wednesday blog adds that Jordan’s military intercepted a fresh barrage of Iranian missiles Wednesday. President Trump told Fox News the U.S. “will hit Iran hard,” saying Iran “is going to get a beating,” and joint U.S.–Saudi strikes hit Iranian-backed forces in Iraq — killing at least 20, per CBS News reporting cited by TheStreet — after more than 30 drone attacks in three days by Iran-aligned groups (CNBC). Crude surged and stayed up: TradingEconomics’ late-day readings had WTI up 6.2% near $84.18 and Brent up more than 4% toward $88, snapping a three-day losing streak, after morning prints of $88.49–$89.94 Brent and $83.18–$84.69 WTI across TheStreet, 24/7 Wall St and CNBC. One offset in the mix: Oman has proposed a new regional deal over the Strait of Hormuz (TheStreet), and API data showed U.S. crude inventories down 3.3 million barrels last week. Gold futures were little changed early, at $4,087.70 (−0.27%), per TheStreet.

After the bell, the AI trade got its verdict — split. Microsoft rose about 2% in extended trading after fiscal Q4 EPS of $4.74 (vs $4.24 expected) on revenue of $90.01 billion (vs $87.62 billion), with Azure growth of 43% at constant currency beating the ~40% estimate and Azure’s fiscal-2026 revenue topping $100 billion for the first time (CNBC/Investing.com). Meta went the other way, sliding about 6% after EPS of $6.18 missed by roughly a dollar on a $2.4 billion legal charge: expenses jumped 55% to $42 billion, net income fell 14% to $15.8 billion, and Q3 revenue guidance of $61–64 billion carried a light low end against the $63.15 billion consensus (CNBC/Investing.com). The rest of the late tape leaned green — Starbucks jumped 11% on a top-and-bottom beat with global same-store sales up 7.9%, Fortinet surged 10% on a beat-and-raise and Chipotle rose 7%, while Qualcomm fell 4% on light Q4 guidance (Investing.com; see After-Hours Movers below).

Under the hood of the regular session, earnings still mattered. Garmin was the day’s standout gainer — up roughly 16% in board readings through the session — after a Q2 beat on $2.02 billion in revenue, a fitness business up 25% and a raised full-year EPS forecast of $10.00 (Benzinga). Humana fell 7.2% in the afternoon session after a solid Q2 beat was overshadowed by the insurer holding its full-year adjusted profit forecast unchanged for a second straight quarter (StockStory/Reuters via Investing.com). Ford faded with the tape but still closed up 2.14% at $15.28 on its beat-and-raise, well off its ~7.6% morning gain (StockStory/Motley Fool). The morning’s other stories held: CBIZ’s $55-a-share Grant Thornton deal, Vertiv’s ~11% premarket drop on light revenue, SoFi’s post-beat slide, and SK Hynix’s record-but-below-consensus quarter that had already handed the Kospi a ~6% loss. Thursday brings the week’s last megacap test: Apple (consensus EPS $1.88), Amazon (~$196 billion revenue expected) and Reddit all report after the close (Investing.com/TipRanks/Finance Calendar).

Grant Thornton Advisors will acquire CBIZ in a definitive all-cash deal: $55.00 per share, a $5 billion enterprise value and a ~54% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price, with the close expected in Q4 2026 and CBIZ shares to be delisted from the NYSE (Grant Thornton press release/Accounting Today/Investing.com). A live-blog reading had the stock up nearly 16.6% at $54.45, pinned just under the offer.

Vertiv (VRT) −~11% premarket

The data-center power-and-cooling maker fell 10.9% in premarket trade to $240.10 after second-quarter revenue of $3.274 billion came in about 3.1% below expectations (live-blog movers reading this run) — another data point for the AI-infrastructure nerves that have dogged the tape since Alphabet’s capex guidance.

SoFi (SOFI) −9.4%

Beat on both lines — EPS $0.12 vs $0.11 expected, revenue $1.2B vs $1.11B — and fell anyway, down 9.4% in late-morning trade, a sign investors wanted more than a clean beat; shares are down 42% year to date (24/7 Wall St).

Visa (V) −1.6% after hours

Beat and trimmed: fiscal Q3 EPS $3.32 vs $3.23 expected, revenue +14% to $11.63B vs $11.39B, processed transactions +10% to 71.7B and quarterly payments volume above $4 trillion for the first time — but shares slipped ~1.6% in late trading as Visa moderated full-year growth guidance and disclosed ~2,600 job cuts (~7% of staff) with $563M in related costs. CEO: consumer and business spending “remains resilient” (Seeking Alpha/Benzinga/Yahoo Finance).

SK Hynix / chips Kospi ~−6%

The memory bellwether’s Q2 was an all-time record — ₩79.32T revenue, ₩60.54T operating profit, up 257% and 557% year over year, with HBM4 mass production begun — yet it landed below the ~₩64T consensus, and the “record earnings, record low” reaction sent Seoul reeling again: the Kospi fell nearly 6% Wednesday as investors dumped Samsung and SK Hynix (SK hynix newsroom/Korea Times/TechTimes·KuCoin/Yahoo Finance). Stateside, the iShares Semiconductor ETF entered the day off four straight sessions with chip stocks down nearly 7% for the week, and SK Hynix’s U.S.-listed shares slipped again (24/7 Wall St).
