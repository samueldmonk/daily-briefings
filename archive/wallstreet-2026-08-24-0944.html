The lead

A chip-led risk-off open — Samsung and Alibaba set the tone

The US session opened at 9:30 AM ET. At the time this edition was built, no post-open index print had been verified against a source fetched this run, so the figures below are the last confirmed pre-market reads and are labelled as such. On Yahoo Finance’s market bar, S&P 500 futures stood at 7,674.25, down 17.00 points or 0.22%; Dow futures at 53,306.00, down 47.00 or 0.09%; Nasdaq futures at 29,175.50, down 212.25 or 0.72%; and Russell 2000 futures at 3,020.60, down 1.50 or 0.05%. Yahoo Finance’s live blog, filed at 4:03 AM ET, described Dow futures as edging into the red with the S&P 500 and the tech-heavy Nasdaq slipping 0.2% and 0.7% respectively, after all the major indexes posted weekly declines. The pre-market ETF proxies pointed the same way: SPY −0.31%, QQQ −0.77%, DIA −0.23% and IWM −0.24%. The live quote widgets at the top of this page carry the real-time tape.

The damage was done overnight. Asian stocks sank as Samsung Electronics and Alibaba led a technology rout, with the collapse of US–Canada trade talks adding to a broad risk-off tone. Samsung fell after a record shareholder-return plan — 2026 returns of up to ₩110tn — landed short of high expectations, with analysts fixed on the missing buyback and treasury-share-cancellation timetable; the declines reported for the session in accounts fetched this run range from 5.2% to 9%, so no single figure is asserted here. SK hynix went the other way, up 2.4% on a separate ₩40tn repurchase-and-cancellation plan. Micron was down roughly 3% in US pre-market trading as the Asian memory complex sold off. Japan’s Nikkei 225 slipped 0.10% and South Korea’s Kospi dropped 0.71%, while the small-cap Kosdaq gained 0.94% and Australia’s S&P/ASX 200 rose 0.33%. Europe’s Stoxx 600 opened 0.1% lower, technology off 0.78%, with travel, miners and food and beverage each up about 0.4%.

Two policy shocks landed over the weekend. Trade talks between the United States and Canada collapsed late Friday, and additional 50% duties on a slate of Canadian goods took effect at the stroke of midnight under Section 338 of the Tariff Act of 1930 — covering alcohol, hockey equipment, cement, dairy, building materials and certain kinds of clothing. Canadian Prime Minister Mark Carney called the move “a miscalculation,” blamed the breakdown on “uneconomic” and “unfair” US demands, and said retaliatory tariffs will take effect on September 8 targeting dairy, appliances, agricultural equipment, pulp and paper, and electronics. “You’re at war when you get attacked. We got attacked,” he said at a Saturday press conference. The Canadian dollar dipped; the US dollar added 0.2% against it to 1.3790.

The second is Iran. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who is scheduled to hold a press conference Monday afternoon, wrote in a Financial Times op-ed over the weekend that the coming measures will constitute “an economic D-Day — the single greatest financial offensive ever marshalled against an adversary.” Share markets slipped in Asia and oil prices eased as investors waited for the detail.

Underneath it all sits the bond market. TheStreet put the 30-year Treasury yield at roughly 5.28% on Monday, describing it as having climbed above 5.3% to its highest level in nearly two decades, with government bond yields in Japan, France and Germany also at multi-year highs; Bloomberg framed the same move as investor angst over surging government spending, a flood of long-dated bond sales and inflation stuck above the Fed’s target for five years. The latest quotes read this run, on Investing.com, show the long end backing off a little: the 30-year at 5.248%, down 0.028, and the 10-year at 4.712%, down 0.026, with the five-year at 4.406% and the three-month bill at 3.811%. The Treasury’s response to the selloff — at least doubling the maximum size of its long-term debt buybacks from $2 billion to at least $4 billion for purchases running September through November — bought only temporary relief. Against that backdrop the US Composite PMI reads 56, with services at 56.8 and manufacturing at 53.2. The week’s two big tests arrive together on Wednesday — July PCE in the morning and Nvidia’s fiscal Q2 after the bell, where FXStreet notes more than $92.05 billion of quarterly revenue is expected — before Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote on Friday.

Movers & drivers — the overnight tape and the pre-market
−8% HKPlacementNew

Alibaba sells $10.2 billion of stock to fund AI

Alibaba shares fell 8% in early Hong Kong trade on Monday and sank as much as 10.5% during the session, to a one-month low, after the company finalised a HK$80 billion (US$10.21 billion) placement of 710 million new shares at HK$112.70 apiece — an 8.4% discount to the last close. Reuters calls it the largest-ever primary follow-on offering by a Hong Kong-listed company, and the third-largest globally this year after offerings by Alphabet and Intel. Proceeds are earmarked for AI development, particularly infrastructure. In New York the US-listed shares were down 3.38% pre-market per Benzinga, and Reuters put the pre-market drop at 3.4%.

SamsungSK hynix +2.4%New

Samsung’s record payout misses, and Asian memory sells off

Samsung Electronics led the Asian technology decline after saying 2026 shareholder returns could reach ₩110tn — a record — without a buyback and treasury-share-cancellation timetable that analysts had expected. Reported declines for the session range from 5.2% to 9% across the accounts fetched this run, so no single figure is asserted. SK hynix rose 2.4% on its own ₩40tn repurchase-and-cancellation plan. A planned $4.9 billion IPO by YMTC adds a fresh extended-supply risk to the memory trade.

On the radar
  • Monday, August 24 — Bessent’s Iran press conference. The Treasury Secretary is expected to detail new US economic sanctions on Iran at an afternoon press conference, after writing in the Financial Times that the measures amount to “an economic D-Day.” Also today: the Chicago Fed National Activity Index and the Dallas Fed Manufacturing Survey, and PDD Holdings reports Q2 2026 results pre-market.
  • Monday, August 24 — the Canada tariffs are live. The additional 50% US duties took effect at midnight. Canada’s retaliatory tariffs are scheduled for September 8, which puts a two-week negotiating window on the calendar.
  • Tuesday, August 25. The Conference Board’s Consumer Confidence Index (90.8 previously), July new home sales (+1.6% previously), the Richmond Fed manufacturing index and the FHFA house price index, plus earnings from Intuit, Zoom, Bank of Montreal and DICK’S Sporting Goods.
  • Wednesday, August 26 — PCE and Nvidia. July PCE (−0.1% m/m previously, +3.7% y/y) and core PCE (+0.1% m/m, +3.3% y/y) land in the morning, with CNBC’s week-ahead putting the consensus for the core annual rate at 3.3%, alongside the second estimate of Q2 GDP (+1.5% annualized on the first estimate) and July durable goods orders (+0.5% previously). Nvidia reports fiscal Q2 after the bell, with FXStreet noting expectations of more than $92.05 billion in quarterly revenue; Salesforce, CrowdStrike, Okta, Synopsys, Veeva, Williams-Sonoma and Nutanix also report.
  • Thursday, August 27. Jackson Hole opens, and Marvell Technology, Workday, Autodesk, Dollar General, Dollar Tree and Ulta Beauty report. Also: initial jobless claims (206,000 previously) and the advance July goods trade balance.
  • Friday, August 28 — Warsh at Jackson Hole. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair, scheduled for 10 a.m. He is expected to stick to his practice of avoiding forward guidance. The data slate is lighter: the MNI Chicago PMI and the final August University of Michigan sentiment and inflation-expectations readings.
