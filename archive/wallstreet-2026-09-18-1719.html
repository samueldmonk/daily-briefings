The lead

A split close to a triple-witching week, and Buffett hands over the chair

Wall Street finished Friday divided. The Nasdaq Composite closed up 0.39% at 26,522.55, a gain of 104.25 points; the S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64. The session wrapped a volatile week in which traders absorbed the Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in three years, a 10-year Treasury yield back at the 5% line, and elevated oil.

It was also triple witching — the quarterly simultaneous expiry of index futures, index options and single-stock options. Citadel Securities data as of 16 September, cited by EBC Financial Group, put roughly $7 trillion of US equity options notional exposure expiring on the day, about 25% of total US options exposure and, on that reckoning, the second-largest expiration on record. TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre had flagged it in advance: expirations of that size “tend to produce heavy volume and sharp swings, particularly in the final hour.”

The day’s other story came from Omaha. Warren Buffett, 96, stepped down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, becoming chairman emeritus while remaining a director. Howard Buffett takes the chair and Susan Decker becomes lead independent director, a little more than nine months after Greg Abel, 64, became chief executive. “Father Time always wins,” Buffett said. “He has, however, been generous with me.” No verified percentage move for Berkshire stock appeared in anything read this run, so none is published.

The closing levels are admitted because all three reconcile against independently sourced prior closes. S&P: 7,650.50 against Thursday’s 7,637.72 is +12.78, or +0.167%. Nasdaq: 26,522.55 against 26,418.30 is +104.25, or +0.395%. Dow: −95.40 on 51,682.64 is −0.184%. One unsmoothed wrinkle — the Dow’s implied prior close of 51,778.04 sits 1.81 points below the 51,779.85 this desk recorded for Thursday. The gap is printed rather than split, and it is far too small to move the direction or the percentage.

Movers & drivers

Biggest declinerNew

Xenon Pharmaceuticals — good news buried by a safety pause

Xenon submitted a New Drug Application to the FDA for azetukalner in focal seizures, resting on the Phase 2b X-TOLE and Phase 3 X-TOLE2 studies, in which all four evaluated doses cut monthly seizure frequency significantly against placebo. It was overwhelmed by the second announcement: a voluntary pause on new patient enrolment in the ongoing depression studies after reports of neuropsychiatric adverse events. Shares fell about 26% in premarket trade, to $42.30. This desk separately logged the stock down about 29.1% at 11:44 AM ET; no verified closing percentage for it appeared in anything read this run.

Analyst movesNew

Three price targets cut on the same name

Deutsche Bank’s David Hoang downgraded Xenon from Buy to Hold and cut the target from $90 to $46. Baird’s Brian Skorney kept Outperform but trimmed from $97 to $90. Needham’s Serge Belanger kept Buy and moved from $78 to $60. A separate Benzinga analyst note argued the share reaction was overblown.

Decliner

Netflix slid at the open

Netflix fell about 5% as the Dow opened roughly 90 points lower. Both figures are opening-bell prints, explicitly labelled as such, not closing moves — no verified closing percentage for the stock appeared in anything read this run.

Advancers

Crypto-linked equities rose across the board

Cryptocurrency-related shares gained broadly after the SEC announced an innovation exemption for tokenized securities. Intraday winners logged by this desk at 12:26 PM ET included ALMR +15.87%, MSTR +13.14%, COIN +11.58%, MARA +10.22%, BitMine +7.92%, HOOD +7.87% and Galaxy +7.59%. Three different causal framings for the same rally appeared in one live blog — the SEC exemption, the Senate failing to pass crypto legislation, and “legislative progress in the U.S. House” — and none is adjudicated here.

On the radar

  • Monday’s index reshuffles take effect. SpaceX’s Nasdaq-100 weighting moves to roughly 2.82% from about 1.28% at Friday’s close, with $15.5–22 billion of programmatic buying attributed to Investing.com, effective Monday 21 September. SanDisk (SNDK) joins the S&P 100 the same day.
  • The rate path. With the funds target at 3.75–4.00% after the first hike in three years and FOMC projections pointing to one or two more, the 10-year’s behaviour around the 5% line is the number to watch next week.
  • Oil as the inflation input. Yields climbed this week on speculation that elevated energy costs feed inflation and therefore further tightening. Friday’s two-way action in crude, and the Strait of Hormuz tanker strike behind the afternoon bounce, keep that channel open.
  • Post-expiry positioning. Roughly a quarter of total US options exposure rolled off on Friday. Where dealers re-hedge into the new cycle is the first thing Monday’s tape will show.
