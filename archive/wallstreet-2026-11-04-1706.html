The Lead

Dow soars 1,569 points (+3.03%) for its best day since April 2025; S&P 500 +1.58%, Nasdaq +1.44% after the Democratic sweep

New At the close the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up 1,569.40 points, or 3.03%, at 53,356.12; the S&P 500 gained 1.58% to 7,688.62 and the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.44% to 25,736.51 (TradingKey; CNBC, via search). CNBC called it the Dow’s best day since April 2025, driven by a clear-cut Democratic win and falling oil (CNBC, via search). TradingKey said Democrats winning both chambers and President Trump accepting the results eased political uncertainty, with tech leading while AI-infrastructure names came under pressure: Microsoft finished +6.68%, Amazon +6.12%, Alphabet +3.18% and Meta +1.44%, while the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 3.13% to 10,268.65 with 26 of its 30 members lower (TradingKey).

With under half an hour left in the session, Yahoo Finance’s quote strip at about 3:34 p.m. ET had the Dow up roughly 3.2%, near 53,464, the S&P 500 up about 1.8% and the Nasdaq Composite up about 1.8% (Yahoo Finance, quote strip as summarized in search at ~3:34 PM ET; page itself returned a stale early version). At 3:12 p.m. Yahoo’s Singapore edition showed the Dow +3.06% and the S&P 500 +1.73% (Yahoo Finance SG, via search). CNBC said the Dow was on pace for its best day since April 9, 2025 (CNBC, via search).

The rally had held into mid-afternoon. At 2:02 p.m. ET the Dow was up 1,522 points, or 2.94%, the S&P 500 up 1.70% and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.76%, with the Russell 2000 +1.50% and the VIX down 18.39% to 17.00; the 10-year Treasury yield was 4.896%, down 5.6 basis points (Yahoo Finance live blog, strip as of 2:02 PM ET). Apple and Microsoft touched record highs — Apple’s market value topped $5 trillion — while chip stocks including Nvidia and Micron fell more than 1% as markets repriced the risk of curbs on data center construction; Micron was −5.97% (Yahoo Finance). Earlier, in CNBC’s 12:21 p.m. ET update, the Dow was up 1,514 points (+2.9%) and back above 53,000 for the first time since Sept. 8 (StockTitan, citing CNBC).

Democrats took control of both the House and the Senate in Tuesday’s midterms, and after an overnight slide in futures, stocks ripped higher. At 11:57 a.m. ET the Dow was up 1,514 points, or 2.9%, the S&P 500 1.7% and the Nasdaq Composite 1.9%, CNBC reported (CNBC, via search); TheStreet’s 12:26 p.m. update had the S&P 500 up 1.83%, and its 1:04 p.m. update had it up 1.93% with 377 of its members advancing (TheStreet). At the 9:30 open TheStreet had the S&P +1.16%, the Dow +1.90%, the Nasdaq +1.21% and the Russell 2000 +0.23%, and at 11:09 a.m. 353 S&P 500 members were advancing with the index up 1.33% (TheStreet).

It was a sharp reversal. Nasdaq-100 futures hit 27,615.75 overnight, their lowest since July 30, on fears a Democratic sweep could hurt the companies building AI data centers; by 6:25 a.m. ET they had climbed back to 28,545.50 after President Trump said he would accept the results and work with Democrats, CNBC reported (StockTitan, citing CNBC). Trump told CBS News: “I think they’re gonna want to work with me. Otherwise, I’d just veto everything and they’re not gonna want that.” (TheStreet). Morgan Stanley Investment Management’s Andrew Slimmon told CNBC the result means gridlock, which markets like more than one party with too much power (StockTitan, citing CNBC).

Big Tech caught up after lagging before the bell: at about 10:02 a.m. ET Amazon was up 8.12%, Microsoft 6.67%, Apple 6.25% and Alphabet 5.01%, while Nvidia slipped 0.56% (StockTitan real-time data). The data-center worry did not vanish — Democrat Gina Hinojosa won the Texas governor’s race after pledging a moratorium on new data center construction in a state Yahoo says accounts for more than 25% of planned U.S. capacity by 2030 (Yahoo Finance), and CoreWeave, Iren and Nebius fell (StockTitan, citing CNBC). Oil helped: CNBC tied the midday rally partly to a drop of more than 7% in U.S. crude (CNBC, via search). CNBC also notes the S&P 500 has risen after each of the last 11 midterm elections, 1982–2022 (CNBC, via search).

The rally held into the lunch hour. An Associated Press report published at 12:13 p.m. ET was headlined “US stocks surge, and the Dow soars 1,500 points as Brent oil’s price drops below $95 per barrel” — down from $102.30 early Wednesday (AP, via WPLG Local 10; TheStreet). Benzinga’s 12:01 p.m. update had the Consumer Discretionary Select Sector SPDR (XLY) leading the S&P 500 with a 4.3% gain, helped by Amazon (Benzinga, via search). On the data side, the ISM services PMI came in at 54.5% for October, its 28th straight month of expansion, down from 54.9 in September and below economists’ expectations, Yahoo reported (ISM via PR Newswire; Yahoo Finance).
