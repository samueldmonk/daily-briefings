Top Story

Noche UFC ended with Jean Silva asking for a belt — and with Alexa Grasso taking the shortest route to one.

The fourth Noche UFC closed in Glendale on Saturday night with a finish. Jean Silva did not throw a strike for the first half of the opening round and was dropped by a right cross from Jose Miguel Delgado, who had taken the main event on short notice, at home in Arizona, as a replacement. Silva woke up in the second, and in the third he dropped Delgado with a right hand along the fence, took the back in the ensuing scramble and finished with a one-armed rear-naked choke at 2:57 of round three. It is his second straight win, taking him to 7-1 in the Octagon with six finishes, and he used the microphone to make a case for a championship opportunity. The finish earned a Performance of the Night bonus.

The more consequential result came two bouts earlier, and it was a decision. Alexa Grasso beat Manon Fiorot by unanimous decision, 29-28 across all three cards, in a bout the UFC confirmed as a title eliminator. Grasso took it to Fiorot from the opening bell, put her in danger inside the opening thirty seconds and dropped her in the final thirty of round one; she kept the pressure on through the second despite a cut beside her right eye. It is her second straight win, and UFC.com’s own write-up calls her the clubhouse leader in the chase for the next 125-pound title shot.

That matters more than a normal contender win because there is no women’s flyweight champion right now. Valentina Shevchenko — the woman Grasso twice fought for this belt — vacated it while injured, and Natalia Silva and Wang Cong contest the vacant title at UFC 332 on 3 October. Grasso has just made herself the obvious next opponent for whoever wins.

Elsewhere on the card: Brandon Moreno ended a two-fight skid with an emotional split-decision win over Joseph Morales in the co-main; Tommy McMillen survived a second round in which Rahiki nearly finished him and took the third to go 12-0, sharing Fight of the Night; Curtis Blaydes out-jabbed and out-wrestled Waldo Cortes Acosta in a cautious heavyweight decision; and David Martinez opened the main card by chopping down Dan Ige’s lead leg for his fourth straight Octagon win and eleventh in a row overall.

Around the Sport

  • The flyweight belt is on the line six days after Noche UFC. Joshua Van defends against Alexandre Pantoja at UFC 331 on Saturday — a rematch of their UFC 323 title fight, which ended in injury. The books cannot separate them: Pantoja −115, Van −105.
  • Fiorot–Grasso was an official eliminator, not an implied one. Earlier editions of this page could only put that stipulation in the fighters’ mouths. It was subsequently confirmed on the UFC broadcast and reported as official, and Fiorot has said the UFC told her directly.
  • Two former champions moved in opposite directions on the same card. Grasso won her way to the front of the queue; Moreno, also a former flyweight champion, simply stopped a slide — his split-decision win over Morales ended a two-fight losing run, in his first Noche UFC appearance.
  • Blaydes wins, and UFC.com is unimpressed. Its own write-up says the decision “left a lot to be desired” and predicts the Colorado-based veteran will find himself matched against a younger, hungrier heavyweight next — while allowing that the win likely re-claims him a place in the top five.
  • Delgado took the main event on short notice. He stepped in as a replacement for the injured Yair Rodriguez, and fought at home in Glendale. Silva was headlining Noche UFC for the second year running.
