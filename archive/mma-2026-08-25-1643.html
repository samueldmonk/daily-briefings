Top story

Makhachev shuts the retirement talk down: “It’s not true, man. I want to keep going”

The welterweight champion has directly denied the reports that he will leave the Octagon after his next fight. “It’s like somebody put the fake news I’m going to retire after this fight,” Islam Makhachev said in a new interview with MMA Junkie. “No, it’s not true, man. I want to keep going.” He went further: “I want to keep fighting and beat all the records and we stay here more years.”

Where the rumour came from. Makhachev’s friend and mentor Khabib Nurmagomedov walked away after defending the lightweight title three times, and the parallel invited speculation that Makhachev would follow the same script — speculation Joe Rogan aired publicly around UFC 330. Makhachev has now shot it down more than once, and has floated 20 consecutive UFC victories as a target. Separately, and not in conflict with the denial, he has said he anticipates “at least three more very difficult fights ahead” — he is denying that this is the end, not claiming an unlimited career.

One number this desk will not assert. Makhachev broke Anderson Silva’s record for the longest win streak in UFC history with his decision over Ian Machado Garry at UFC 330 on August 15 — that much every source agrees on. The count does not agree: reporting fetched this run puts the streak at 18 straight UFC wins, while this desk’s standing record has it at 17. He has not fought since, so the number cannot have moved; the discrepancy is between sources, not between dates. Both figures are disclosed and neither is published as fact until a primary source settles it.

Dana White’s Contender Series, Season 10, Week 3 — and Bella Mir is closing the show

Five bouts, five sets of UFC contracts in play, at the Meta APEX tonight. The headline name is Bella Mir, the 23-year-old daughter of former UFC heavyweight champion Frank Mir, who meets Alexa Apodaca in the first women’s bout of the season — at bantamweight, a division that had not yet appeared on Season 10 either. Mir is 4-0 as a professional with her last three wins by first-round submission, and arrives with an unusual set of credentials for a prospect: a No-Gi world title at purple belt late last year, a Division I national championship in wrestling at North Central College, and a couple of wins under the UFC BJJ banner. If she earns a contract, UFC.com notes she would form the first father-and-daughter pair to compete in the UFC. Apodaca, out of Denver, lost her pro debut in early 2024 and has won three straight since, all by unanimous decision.

The rest of the card: Brazilian heavyweights Mario Piazzon vs Guilherme Uriel, both back for a second Contender Series try; Gary Balletto vs Sean Clancy Jr. at welterweight, the New England veteran against an 8-0 Scot with a 100% finishing rate and a Cage Warriors title; Ronald Humphrey vs Alexis Miranda, a 4-0 second-generation prospect against Entram Gym’s latest hopeful; and Carlos Petruzzella vs Nick Galanti opening at middleweight. Ten contracts have been awarded across the season’s first two weeks.

Prospect watch
Carried · 11:18 editionProspect

Bella Mir — 4-0, three straight first-round submissions, fighting for a contract tonight

The most-watched name on tonight’s Contender Series card. Mir has won all four of her professional MMA bouts, the last three by first-round submission, and has spent the past year collecting accolades outside the cage as well: a No-Gi world title at purple belt at the end of last year and a Division I national wrestling championship at North Central College, plus two wins under UFC BJJ. UFC.com’s framing is that expectations are “sky high” for the 23-year-old and that she “keeps crossing off the goals she’s set for herself.”

Prospect

Lucia Szabova — a two-weight OKTAGON champion, reportedly UFC-bound

Bloody Elbow reported on Saturday that the UFC has signed Szabova, whom it bills as an undefeated two-weight world champion. She holds belts at bantamweight and flyweight and has spent the majority of her professional career in OKTAGON at 135 pounds. Her promotional debut is reported to be set for October, against Tainara Lisboa. The UFC has not published a bout announcement, so treat the date and the opponent as reporting rather than confirmed booking.

Prospect

Anthony Wint — 8-0, and a week removed from a contract

Wint earned his UFC deal on the opening episode of Season 10 of Dana White’s Contender Series, then submitted Terrance Chatman in the first round of his promotional debut. There were dicey moments — Chatman threatened a kimura on the way down — but Wint worked free, took top position and clamped on an arm-triangle from knee-on-belly to put Chatman to sleep. He is the second heavyweight with NFL ties in as many years to come to the UFC through Contender Series.

Prospect

Bilal Hasan — contract to debut in a matter of days

Hasan finished Mridul Saikia in under a minute at bantamweight on the opening episode of Season 10 of Dana White’s Contender Series, and White confirmed on the night that he had earned a UFC deal. Days later he was booked for his promotional debut against Nilson Rojas on the UFC Shanghai card this Saturday, one of four contracts handed out in the season’s first week.

Prospect

MarQuel Mederos — 11 straight, unbeaten in the Octagon

Now 4-0-1 in the UFC and riding an 11-fight winning streak overall, which UFC.com notes is tied for the second-longest unbeaten run in the lightweight division alongside Tom Nolan and Arman Tsarukyan, one behind Chris Padilla and Quillan Salkilld. The MMA Lab product called for a top-15 opponent or a date with Beneil Dariush or Chris Duncan.

Prospect

Carli Judice — four in a row, three finishes

“Crispy” has won four straight with three finishes since dropping a split decision in her promotional debut. The Louisiana native closed the distance on Chaves, buried a front kick in her midsection and ended the fight in an instant. She is climbing the flyweight ranks and should be looking at a ranked opponent next.

Prospect

Francesco Nuzzi — signed on a ten-day turnaround

Nuzzi signed with the UFC on August 19 and was slotted straight into the UFC Shanghai card on August 29 — ten days from signature to Octagon walk, among the fastest turnarounds of the year.

The Nevada State Athletic Commission has withheld the fight purses of Max Holloway, Paddy Pimblett and Gable Steveson pending disciplinary hearings, after all three climbed out over the cage following their wins — a violation Nevada has enforced more strictly since Khabib Nurmagomedov jumped the fence at UFC 229. Holloway and Pimblett were both shown on the broadcast leaving the cage after first-round stoppages; Steveson was not caught on camera doing so. The hearings will decide whether the withheld money is returned or converted into fines. Pimblett’s manager, Cage Warriors CEO Graham Boyland, texted MMA Junkie: “For the record, this rule needs to be changed.” The precedent is small — Diego Lopes had $5,000 of his $100,000 show money frozen for scaling the cage at UFC 300 and was fined $2,500 — but the amounts withheld here have not been disclosed.
