The Lead

Final: Nasdaq +1.1%, S&P 500 +0.3%, Dow −0.5% — tech bounce can’t save the week

At the close: the Nasdaq Composite rose 1.1% (+264 points) to 24,579 — still in correction territory — and the S&P 500 gained 0.3% to 7,376, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.5% to 51,285, per Kiplinger’s closing report (Kiplinger). For the week the Nasdaq fell 3.4%, the S&P 500 2.7% and the Dow 0.9%. Kiplinger notes that reports Microsoft (−1.0%) plans to use fewer neoclouds hung over tech, even as Micron (+5.1%), Nvidia (+2.8%) and CoreWeave (+6.3%) bounced. Futures traders now price a 77% chance of a December Fed hike, up from 71% a week ago, per CME FedWatch, with October CPI due Tuesday morning (Kiplinger). Earlier post-close quotes at ~4:05 p.m. had the Dow at 51,286.27 (−0.46%) (Investing.com), and Yahoo Finance’s 4:02 p.m. update had the Dow down 0.4%, the S&P 500 up 0.3% and the Nasdaq up more than 1% — a late fade from the 3:04 p.m. read below (S&P +0.65%, Dow −0.19%). Last week the Fed held its benchmark rate at 3.75%–4% (Investopedia via Yahoo Finance).

Yahoo Finance at 3:04 p.m. ET: the S&P 500 was up 47.52 points (+0.65%) at 7,401.25, the Dow down 96.05 (−0.19%) at 51,427.01 and the Nasdaq Composite up 344.13 (+1.42%) at 24,659.13; the Russell 2000 was up 0.95%, the VIX down 12.88% at 24.55 and the 10-year yield at 4.857%. Nvidia was up 3.44% and AMD 10.58%. Yahoo’s Ines Ferré writes that the S&P 500 and Nasdaq, though higher on the day, “were on pace for their worst weekly performance since June 5” (Yahoo Finance live blog, 3:04 PM ET).

TheStreet at 2:57 p.m. ET: the Russell 2000 was up 0.91% and, outside the Nasdaq (+1.31%), was the best-performing major index of the day, with health care, energy, industrials and pockets of technology leading; 1,075 of its holdings had advanced. At 2:19 p.m., the iShares Semiconductor ETF was up 3.27%, though TheStreet notes the past few weeks have erased the majority of its months-long rally amid the AI washout (TheStreet, 2:57 PM ET).

Stocks stabilized after Thursday’s AI-led meltdown, holding roughly steady through midday. As of about 2:03 p.m. ET, the S&P 500 was up 52.83 points (+0.72%) at 7,406.56, the Nasdaq Composite up 346.10 (+1.42%) at 24,661.10 and the Dow down 32.57 points (−0.06%) at 51,490.49; the Russell 2000 was up 1.02% and the VIX was down 12.93% at 24.54. Nvidia was up 3.12% and AMD 10.08%. At about 11:02 a.m. the S&P had been up 0.83% and the Nasdaq 1.54%; at the open, the Dow had been down 0.9% while the S&P rose 0.4% and the Nasdaq more than 1.4% (Yahoo Finance live blog). Yahoo notes the major indexes remain on track for substantial weekly losses, and that Brent crude futures have slipped below $100 a barrel.

AP at 11:45 a.m. ET: the S&P 500 was up 0.9%, trimming its loss for the week to 2.1% and leaving it 5.1% below the all-time high set last month; the Dow was down 3 points and the Nasdaq Composite up 1.6%. Outside AI the market was mixed, with slightly more S&P 500 stocks falling than rising. The University of Michigan’s preliminary survey showed consumer sentiment unexpectedly improved and year-ahead inflation expectations eased; the 10-year Treasury yield edged up to 4.85% from 4.84% late Thursday (AP via The Korea Times, 11:45 AM ET).

Breadth and the week: at 10:21 a.m. ET, six of the 11 S&P 500 sectors were higher, led by information technology, with consumer staples and health care the laggards; the VIX had eased to 23.88. Traders lifted the odds of a December Fed hike to 78% from 70% earlier. Even with the bounce, the S&P 500 was on pace for its worst week since March 2025, the Nasdaq Composite was headed for a fourth straight weekly fall, and the S&P 500 energy index was on track for a weekly drop of more than 11% as oil prices headed for sharp weekly declines (Reuters via GV Wire, 10:21 AM ET).

U.S. employers added a surprising 165,000 jobs in October, far above the 65,000 economists expected, and the unemployment rate dipped to 4.1%, the AP reported. Revisions added a combined 84,000 jobs to August and September; 421,000 people entered the labour force; and average hourly wages rose 0.4% from September — the biggest monthly gain since January — though only 3.1% from a year earlier. “This was a blowout jobs report,” said Edward Jones’ Angelo Kourkafas. Traders now see a 77% chance of a December Fed rate hike, up about 10 percentage points from Thursday, per CME FedWatch (AP via CP24/BNN Bloomberg, 10:09 AM ET).

Before the bell, futures were split: as of 8:30 a.m. ET, Nasdaq-100 futures were up 1.49% and S&P 500 futures 0.17%, while Dow futures were down 0.93% (TipRanks, 8:30 AM ET). The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 4.85% as Treasury prices slipped (Coinpedia, 7:21 AM ET). TipRanks attributes the rebound to tech and AI names recovering after Thursday’s selloff pushed the Nasdaq-100 into correction territory.

Thursday’s damage, for context: the S&P 500 fell 4.36%, the Nasdaq Composite 5.52% — closing more than 10% below its recent high, in correction — and the Dow 3.44%, the indexes’ biggest one-day losses since April 2025; all 11 S&P sectors fell (TipRanks; official closes in the Weekly Scorecard, JournalArta). For the week, TipRanks puts the Nasdaq down 4.4%, the S&P 500 down nearly 3% and the Dow down just 0.5% heading into Friday (TipRanks).

Overnight abroad: South Korea’s Kospi plunged more than 6% to 5,059.69 (−11.7% on the week) and Japan’s Nikkei 225 fell 5.9%, entering bear-market territory; the Hang Seng fell 1.27% and the Shanghai Composite 1.10% (TipRanks).

Movers & Drivers

At the close: Airbnb +11.7%, DraftKings −21.3%

Airbnb finished up 11.7% after Q3 EPS of $3.16 (up 43% year over year) and revenue up 19% to $4.9 billion, its fastest growth in three years; UBS’s Stephen Ju raised his target to $191 from $181. DraftKings plunged 21.3% — its worst day since November 2022 — after a top- and bottom-line Q3 miss and a cut to its full-year forecast tied to prediction-market investment (Kiplinger).

Airbnb +12.1%, Coherent +11.8% lead S&P gainers

At 1:26 p.m. ET, with the S&P 500 up 0.48%, the index’s top gainers were Airbnb (+12.13%), Coherent Corp. (+11.77%), AppLovin (+9.97%) and Vistra (+9.79%) (TheStreet, 1:26 PM ET).

Oracle −2% on Microsoft contract report

Oracle fell about 2% after a report that Microsoft (−0.40%) is ending a computing contract with the company, extending a slide of nearly 10% over the last few sessions; TheStreet noted signs of modest recovery as dip-buyers arrived (TheStreet, 12:53 PM ET).

Earnings

Microchip −13.35% at midday

Microchip Technology was down 13.35% at midday on earnings-related fallout, with analysts cutting forecasts after results; Axon Enterprise (−18%) was the S&P 500’s weakest link. About 42.9% of U.S. issues were declining despite the index gains (TheStreet, 12:07 PM ET).

Macro

White House: labor demand “skyrocketing”

“Demand for labor is skyrocketing,” National Economic Council director Kevin Hassett told Yahoo Finance after the October jobs report, saying initial jobless claims are at their lowest since World War II. Yahoo notes payrolls are averaging 137,000 over the past three months, versus an average monthly gain of 48,000 over the past 12 (Yahoo Finance, ~1:40 PM ET).

Lam Research +9.9% on $25B buyback

The chip-equipment maker climbed 9.9% after announcing a program to return up to $25 billion to investors through share repurchases; CoreWeave rose 4.5%, Micron 5.9% and Nvidia 3.7% as of 11:45 a.m. ET (AP).

Charter −5% on surprise loss

Charter Communications sank 5% after reporting a quarterly loss when analysts expected a profit; Axon Enterprise was down 17.6% after weaker-than-expected results and a $1.2 billion investment in drone maker Skydio (AP, 11:45 AM ET).

Texas data-center pause call

Texas governor-elect Gina Hinojosa told CNN on Thursday evening: “We will craft legislation to protect Texas residents. Until those protections are in place, we must pause current data center construction activity” — a moratorium that would last until the legislature convenes in January 2027 (BigGo Finance, citing CNN).

Taiwan’s TAIEX −4.51%; stabilization fund on standby

The TAIEX closed down 1,873.08 points at 39,701.64 after its largest intraday point drop on record; Taiwan’s National Stabilization Fund authorized deployment “as market conditions warrant,” and regulators announced stabilization measures effective Nov. 9, including cutting the intraday short-sale cap from 30% to 3% (BigGo Finance).

Iren: AI cloud revenue nearly triples

Iren shares jumped as much as 20% Friday, rebounding from a roughly 17% drop Thursday, after the data-center operator reported $200 million in AI revenue for the quarter ended Sept. 30 — up from $71 million the prior quarter and $7 million a year earlier. It still posted a $263 million quarterly loss, wider than the Bloomberg-compiled estimate, and the stock remains down more than 50% year to date (Yahoo Finance).

Iren (IREN) +17.3%, Cloudflare (NET) +13.3%

As of 10:19 a.m. ET, the AI cloud and data-center provider Iren surged 17.33% on strong fiscal first-quarter 2027 results, and Cloudflare jumped 13.32% after a third-quarter beat and stronger-than-expected guidance; AMD rose 10.77% with the semiconductor rebound (TheStreet, 10:19 AM ET).

DraftKings (DKNG) −27.9%, Axon (AXON) −16%

DraftKings plummeted 27.85% after third-quarter results fell well short of expectations; Axon Enterprise sank 16% after missing forecasts and lowering its full-year 2026 outlook; Atlassian (TEAM) lost 14.42% on a sharp deceleration in its growth outlook (TheStreet, 10:19 AM ET).

Natera (NTRA) −12.4% as CEO steps down

The genetic-testing company reported a wider-than-expected third-quarter loss and said longtime CEO Steve Chapman is stepping down effective immediately, replaced by Solomon Moshkevich, former president of clinical diagnostics (TheStreet, 10:19 AM ET).

SpaceX (SPCX) +6.7% on FAA streamlining

Shares rose 6.68% to $131.87 as investors bought the dip and the FAA said it plans to streamline commercial launch and re-entry rules, overhauling its licensing framework in 2027. Scottish Mortgage said SpaceX was 11.1% of its assets as of Wednesday, down from 15% at Sept. 30, after it trimmed the holding and sold its remaining Tesla shares (TheStreet, 10:53 AM ET).

Hims & Hers +5.4% on Lilly deal

Eli Lilly will let the telehealth company sell its obesity drugs Zepbound and Foundayo directly on its platform, putting Lilly on equal footing with Novo Nordisk there (TheStreet, 9:53 AM ET).

Tesla to wind down Cybertruck in Texas

Elon Musk said Tesla will “be winding down” Cybertruck production at its Texas gigafactory around the middle of next year to make room for the Cybercab robotaxi; shares were up 2.05% to $350.73 in early trading (TheStreet, 9:13 AM ET).

Chipmakers bounce after worst rout since 2020

The PHLX Semiconductor Index bounced 4% after Thursday’s 12% plunge; AMD rebounded 8% after a 17% fall, Nvidia jumped 6% after a 9% drop and Intel rose nearly 6% (Yahoo Finance, 10:02 AM ET).

Anthropic IPO valuation pressured

Investors now eye a valuation of $1 trillion to $1.3 trillion for Anthropic’s IPO, per Bloomberg and The Wall Street Journal, far below the $2 trillion projected last month; the listing, expected before month-end, could slip (Yahoo Finance).

Airbnb “back in growth mode”

Q3 EPS of $3.16 beat the $2.85 estimate and revenue rose 19% to $4.87 billion; Q4 revenue guidance of $3.22–$3.28 billion topped the Street’s $3.16 billion midpoint, and shares jumped after hours Thursday (Yahoo Finance).

Yardeni: “speed bumps, not roadblocks”

Yardeni Research called Thursday’s data-center-backlash selloff overdone and a “buying opportunity,” keeping a 7,900 year-end S&P 500 target; Goldman Sachs estimates about 45% of planned U.S. data-center capacity faces possible restrictions (Yahoo Finance).

Macro · 165K jobs, 4.1% unemployment

Professional and business services added 70,000 jobs, healthcare nearly 42,000 and factories 19,000, while hotels, restaurants and bars cut 10,600. The AP says the strength gives the Fed leeway to focus on inflation and raise rates in December (AP).

Chips & neoclouds rebound premarket

Iren (IREN) rallied after strong fiscal first-quarter revenue, lifting peers CoreWeave (CRWV) and Nebius (NBIS); Micron (MU), SanDisk (SNDK) and AMD also rebounded in early trading (TipRanks).

Moderna pops on Q3 beat

Moderna (MRNA) rose after market-beating third-quarter results and a raised full-year sales-growth outlook (TipRanks).

Cloudflare (NET) jumps on guidance

Shares rose more than 17% in extended trading Thursday after Q3 revenue grew 39% to $782.5 million and Q4 revenue guidance of $845.0–$846.0 million beat the $798.2 million expected (Investing.com); the stock was still jumping before Friday’s open (TipRanks).
