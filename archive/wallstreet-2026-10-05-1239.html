The Lead

As of ~12:05 PM ET: S&P 500 +0.5%, Nasdaq +0.7%, Dow flat as the 10-year yield climbs to about 5.33%; Nasdaq-100 notches a record, SpaceX jumps on a Morgan Stanley call

NewMidday (Yahoo Finance, ~12:05 PM ET; TheStreet, 11:33 AM–12:36 PM ET). “US stocks struggled for direction on Monday,” Yahoo Finance wrote: the Dow fell before easing to the flat line, the S&P 500 rose 0.5% and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.8%, while the 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.33%, “hovering near its highest levels since 2007.” Yahoo’s index strip at about 12:05 PM ET read S&P 500 +0.47%, Dow −0.04% (quoted down 0.04% as of 12:06 PM), Nasdaq +0.72% and Russell 2000 +0.43%, with November WTI down 1.12% (Yahoo Finance). TheStreet’s 11:33 AM update said the Nasdaq-100 (+0.57%) hit a new record earlier in the day while the Nasdaq Composite was up 0.73%, as tech continued to lead “amid higher yields”; its midday movers lists named PTC, XP, Vaxcyte (PCVX), Nu Holdings (NU) and Ambev (ABEV) among the top gainers and C.H. Robinson, UMC, Insmed (INSM), Lennar (LEN) and Chipotle (CMG) among the biggest decliners with at least a $2 billion market cap (TheStreet). Separately, Morgan Stanley’s Adam Jonas reiterated his Outperform rating and $300 price target on SpaceX in a note titled “SPCX $159: Cheap and Getting Cheaper,” and the stock jumped 5% in early trade (Yahoo Finance).

Late morning (Yahoo Finance, ~11:10 AM ET; Reuters, 10:19 AM ET). The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 0.2%, the S&P 500 rose 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.7%, Yahoo Finance reported, with the Dow quoted down 0.24% as of 11:10 AM; the 10-year Treasury yield rose 2 basis points to 5.30%, “hovering near its highest levels since 2007” (Yahoo Finance). Reuters said the Nasdaq reached all-time highs as most megacaps advanced — Nvidia +1.1%, and Meta, Microsoft and Tesla each up more than 1% — with seven of 11 S&P 500 sectors higher, communication services and utilities leading, the Philadelphia chip index down 0.3% and the S&P 500 software & services index up 1.4%. Traders see an 80% chance the Fed holds this month, though a December hike “remains largely priced in” per CME FedWatch, and Brent hovered near $100 (Reuters via Lufkin Daily News). In Europe the euro hit a 17-month low as a French budget impasse widened French–German bond spreads; Paris’s CAC fell 0.6% (Yahoo Finance).

Late morning (TheStreet, 10:42–10:58 AM ET). “Stocks were mixed Monday as investors contended with rising bond yields and the upcoming release of the Fed’s meeting minutes,” TheStreet’s 10:58 AM update said; Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn noted the softer jobs report had reduced expectations for another Fed hike in October and helped the Nasdaq 100 close Friday at fresh highs, while the 10-year yield “is still around 5.25%.” Nike lost 2.88% to $32.90 in early trading after S&P Global Ratings downgraded its credit rating to “A” from “A+,” citing a turnaround in China and in its sportswear and Jordan businesses that will take “significantly longer” than forecast, plus $1 billion in incremental restructuring charges; the outlook is negative (TheStreet, 10:42 AM).

First hour (TheStreet, 10:23 AM ET). “Stocks were mixed Monday as investors contended with rising bond yields and the upcoming release of the Fed’s meeting minutes,” TheStreet wrote. Its 10:23 AM movers update had PTC up 34.76% on Schneider Electric’s $22.6 billion all-cash deal, Brazilian fintech XP up 34% and Itaú Unibanco up 17.46% as Brazilian shares rallied on Flavio Bolsonaro’s first-round lead; C.H. Robinson fell 12.40% after agreeing to acquire RXO, GlobalFoundries lost 3.79% and Intel 3.29% (TheStreet, 10:23 AM). The live quotes above show the real-time picture.

ISM services, 10 AM ET. The ISM Services PMI registered 54.9% in September, down from 55.4% in August and below the 55.2 expected in Yahoo Finance’s preview — the 27th straight month of expansion. The Prices Index rose 1.4 points to 74%, its highest since July 2022 (74.5%) and above 70% for the sixth time in seven months; Business Activity was 56.5%, New Orders 59.8% and Employment 50.1% (ISM via PR Newswire).

Opening bell (TheStreet, 9:35 AM ET). “Stocks were mixed in early trading as Wall Street responded to rising Treasury yields while gearing up for this week’s release of the Federal Reserve’s September meeting minutes.” The S&P 500 edged up 0.18%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 0.21% and the Nasdaq gained 0.42%; the Russell 2000 rose 0.94%. TheStreet Pro contributor James “Rev Shark” DePorre described “two markets”: mega-cap AI-related names “unaffected by the surge in interest rates,” and everything else, where rates are hurting valuations and many companies with anemic growth face stagflation. Brazil rally. Brazil’s real jumped more than 4% against the dollar, past 5.00 per dollar from around 5.22, after right-wing Senator Flavio Bolsonaro won 47% of the votes cast in Sunday’s first round, beating polls, against about 45% for President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva; the two meet in a runoff this month. Ibovespa futures soared more than 8% and the Frankfurt-listed MSCI Brazil ETF more than 16% (TheStreet, 9:59 AM, citing Reuters).

Before the bell. TheStreet’s live blog (9:08 AM ET) reported, citing Bloomberg, that Nvidia server-assembly partner Hon Hai Precision Industry (Foxconn) posted September-quarter sales of NT$3.03 trillion ($95.4 billion), up 47% and above the NT$2.83 trillion analysts expected — following Micron’s bullish forecast last week as further evidence that AI infrastructure spending keeps climbing; Hon Hai shares are up about 10% this year (TheStreet, 9:08 AM). Schneider’s PTC offer is $205 a share in cash, a 42% premium to PTC’s previous close, per deal coverage (GuruFocus; Quartz); Schneider’s own shares fell in Paris on the announcement (Invezz). TheStreet also lists Constellation Brands, Levi Strauss, PepsiCo and Delta Air Lines among this week’s earnings reports.

Latest pre-market read (TheStreet live blog, 8:56 AM ET). “Stock futures were edging lower Monday as investors contended with rising bond yields and the upcoming release of the Fed’s meeting minutes,” due Wednesday. The SPDR S&P 500 ETF (SPY) slipped 0.13% to $768.64 in early trading. Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn noted the 10-year yield “is still around 5.25%, despite the disappointing payroll report,” creating “an unusual divergence” in which growth stocks rally on softer Fed expectations while the risk-free rate stays exceptionally high. Oil was mixed as Saudi Aramco CEO Amin Nasser warned global stockpiles are “scarily thin” until the Strait of Hormuz fully reopens: WTI slipped 0.47% to $90.68 and Brent gained 0.64% to $102.90 (TheStreet, 7:12 AM). Gold futures rose 0.65% to $4,189.50 and silver 2.94% to $62.19 early. Iran’s foreign minister said there is “no military solution” to the conflict with the U.S., per Al Jazeera (TheStreet).

Earlier (Yahoo Finance live page, updated ~6 AM ET Monday). “US stock futures edged lower in premarket trading on Monday as investors took stock of rising bond yields and the upcoming earnings season ahead of a relatively quiet week.” In that version, Dow futures dropped 0.2% and S&P 500 futures fell 0.1%, while Nasdaq-100 contracts “also fell into the red.” Yahoo’s futures strip on the same page read S&P −0.12%, Dow −0.14% and Nasdaq −0.23%, with the VIX up 6.14% to 16.25, gold up 0.65% and November WTI down 0.64% at $90.53. Yahoo put the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.28% and Brent crude futures above $100 a barrel, noting the market has stayed “remarkably resilient” amid bond-market turmoil and the war in the Middle East. Monday’s calendar is light: S&P Global’s final September PMIs (services 58.7 expected, 58.7 prior) and the ISM services index (55.2 expected, 55.4 prior), with no notable earnings (Yahoo Finance). Figures below for Friday are the official close. Yahoo’s Ines Ferré notes October ranks among the top months by average return, at 1.9%, with a 63% win rate over the past 30 years (Yahoo Finance AlphaSpace data).

Friday’s official close. “U.S. stocks rose near their record after the latest jobs report cooled worries that a potentially hot U.S. economy could make inflation worse,” the AP reported. The S&P 500 rose 56.27 points, or 0.7%, to 7,722.72 and pulled within 1% of its all-time high; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 250.40 points, or 0.5%, to 51,176.96; the Nasdaq Composite rose 319.27 points, or 1.2%, to 27,190.86; and the Russell 2000 rose 26.27 points, or 0.9%, to 2,832.90. Traders pared bets that the Federal Reserve will hike later this month, and Treasury yields “initially tumbled but later retraced their losses as oil prices recovered most of an early swoon” (AP via KRMG).

The week. Per the AP, the S&P 500 finished the week down 0.3% and the Dow down 1.3%, while the Nasdaq rose 0.5% and the Russell 2000 slipped 0.2%; for the year the S&P is up 12.8%, the Dow 6.5%, the Nasdaq 17% and the Russell 2000 14.1% (AP). TheStreet’s 4:24 PM close update said the tech-heavy Nasdaq led the indexes, crude oil futures ended the week down 1.53% after the G7 and European nations agreed to release 100 million barrels of crude and diesel, gold fell 1.41% on the week and silver 2.59%, and that the coming week is relatively quiet for data as investors await third-quarter earnings (TheStreet, 4:24 PM).

G7 reserve release. The Group of Seven agreed Friday to coordinate the release of 100 million barrels of oil and fuel products from emergency reserves, beginning immediately and running over four months, with a substantial amount of diesel released within the first 20 days; the International Energy Agency will coordinate, and the G7 did not say how the total splits between diesel, crude and other products. U.S. diesel hit a record $6.52 a gallon on Sept. 22 and stood at $6.37 on Friday, per AAA (TheStreet, 12:39 PM, citing CNBC).

Nvidia at a record. “Stocks were rising, with Treasury yields and oil prices easing, as Wall Street digested a key jobs report,” TheStreet wrote in its live blog updated 11:10 AM ET. In that update it reported, citing Yahoo Finance, that Nvidia hit a new all-time intraday high Friday: the stock reached $237.55 in early trading after opening at $236.05, eclipsing its previous high of $235.54 and pushing its intraday market capitalization to $5.7 trillion (TheStreet, 11:10 AM).

Tesla beats on deliveries. Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in the third quarter, down 2.1% from last year’s record 497,099 but about 25,000 above the company-compiled consensus of 461,974; it produced 464,391 vehicles and deployed 13.7 GWh of energy storage, short of the 15.9 GWh analysts expected (Electrek).

Friday’s jobs report missed badly. Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 in September versus 90,000 expected; unemployment edged up to 4.2% from 4.1%; the participation rate rose to 61.8% from 61.6%; average hourly earnings rose 0.1% on the month (0.3% expected) and 3.0% on the year (3.2% expected); private payrolls rose 46,000 vs. 85,000 expected (investingLive; FXStreet). FXStreet reports August was revised down to 133,000 from 162,000. Just before the release, fed funds pricing showed a 28% chance of a rate hike, with the 2-year yield at 4.78% and the 10-year at 5.23% (investingLive). The dollar index turned lower on the print, last down 0.23% at 101.80 (FXStreet).

Oil and the Middle East. Early Friday, WTI fell 3.75% to $89.39 and Brent 2.34% to $99.92 on reports that governments are discussing more diesel and crude stockpile releases — EU governments discussed a French proposal to release 50 million barrels of diesel, with IEA members releasing 50 million barrels of crude (TheStreet, citing Reuters). Separately, TheStreet, citing The Independent, reported President Trump has sent 9,000 U.S. troops to the Middle East after warning new strikes on Iran could be coming; the Trump administration also imposed sanctions on Iran’s land transportation sector, including automakers IKCO and SAIPA (Benzinga, citing Reuters).
