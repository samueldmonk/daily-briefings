Aspinall vacates the heavyweight title: “Stepping away from the title is not stepping away from the sport”

Tom Aspinall has relinquished the UFC heavyweight championship. The 33-year-old has had multiple surgeries since suffering severe eye pokes in his title defence against Ciryl Gane at UFC 321 in Abu Dhabi in October last year — a bout that ended in a no contest. He said he had agreed a fight date and was back sparring and feeling great when an accident caused further damage to his right eye, and that he chose to give up the belt rather than hold the division up any longer while he recovers.

He is explicit that this is not a retirement. Writing on Instagram, Aspinall said “Stepping away from the title is not stepping away from the sport or the dream,” and he intends to compete again once he can be medically cleared.

The undisputed heavyweight title is therefore vacant. Ciryl Gane remains the interim champion, having stopped Alex Pereira by knockout in round two at Freedom 250 on 14 June. Al Jazeera frames Aspinall’s departure as clearing the way for Gane to be elevated to undisputed — this page reports that as the outlet’s reading, not as a promotion the UFC has announced.

Correction carried forwardThis site’s 1:19 PM edition today still listed Aspinall as reigning heavyweight champion. The statement had been published roughly eight hours earlier. The champions board below is corrected, and the error is logged in the repository’s standing corrections file.

Fight Week — Upcoming Cards

Sat 19 Sep · Crypto.com Arena, Los Angeles

UFC 331: Joshua Van (c) vs. Alexandre Pantoja 2

A flyweight title rematch of the UFC 323 fight that ended in injury, on a 13-bout card. Co-main: Arman Tsarukyan vs. Mauricio Ruffy, five rounds at lightweight, with Tsarukyan pushing toward a second title shot.

Odds — the main event has flipped. The early market made Pantoja a slight favourite at −120 with Van at +100. Fight-week pricing has moved the other way: LowKick’s Monday-afternoon survey lists one book at Van 8/11, Pantoja 11/10, and says other market reports put Van near −120 with Pantoja around even money or just below. In the co-main, Tsarukyan opened around −350 to −380 with Ruffy near +280; by fight week Tsarukyan had come in to about −325 and Ruffy to about +265. These are the numbers the article states, not a consensus line.

The first fight barely happened. Van took the belt at UFC 323 in December when Pantoja threw a high kick, Van caught it and dumped him, dislocating Pantoja’s arm 26 seconds into round one — which is why the rematch priced tighter than a normal champion-versus-former-champion line. Van is 17-2 and has since defended by fifth-round stoppage of Tatsuro Taira at UFC 328; his win over Brandon Royval featured 419 significant strikes, a UFC record for a three-round flyweight contest. Pantoja is 30-6, was a four-time defending flyweight champion before the injury loss, holds the division records for UFC wins, finishes and submissions, has 12 career submission wins and a three-inch reach advantage. Ruffy arrives on a knockout of Michael Chandler, having previously stopped Bobby Green.

The rest of the main card, confirmed this run: Renato Moicano vs. Brian Ortega 2, Patricio Pitbull vs. Doo Ho Choi and Charles Jourdain vs. Marlon Vera. Prelims begin 6:00 PM ET and the main card at 9:00 PM ET, streaming on Paramount+.

Prospect

Quentin Pasley

The light heavyweight from Connecticut headlined Dana White’s Contender Series Week 5 on 8 September and won by knockout, one of four contracts handed out that night. His opponent was Arlind Berisha, and Pasley went into it with just two professional fights on his record — which is the whole argument for and against signing him.

Rankings & Business

Rankings movement. Arman Tsarukyan is described as marching back toward a second lightweight title shot ahead of UFC 331; Movsar Evloev, the featherweight number-one contender, has his shot booked for UFC 333. No numbered ranking changes appeared in anything read this run, so none are asserted. The one measurable movement this run is on the betting board rather than the rankings: the UFC 331 main event has gone from Pantoja −120 at the open to Van favoured in fight week, and the Tsarukyan–Ruffy price has drifted from about −350/−380 toward −325 — movement that can reflect respected money, public money, book liability or one operator reacting to another, and proves nothing about the result.

Business & broadcast. Noche UFC set Desert Diamond Arena’s all-time single-day gross record, passing Drake, with ticket sales of $4,436,470 in front of more than 16,928 people — also billed as the UFC’s highest domestic Fight Night gate. It was the promotion’s first event at the Glendale venue. On the broadcast side, UFC 332 on 3 October will be the first numbered main card to air on CBS, simulcast with Paramount+ at 8 PM ET. Paramount is in year one of a seven-year, $7.7 billion rights deal with TKO Group covering 13 numbered events and 30 Fight Nights a year. The benchmark it set: TKO says UFC Freedom 250 drew 34 million total global viewers, with 17 million across the US and Latin America on Paramount+ and an 8.2 million average audience (Variety) — the biggest exclusive live event in Paramount+ history.
