Top Story

Breach · confirmed by the company

Revolut confirms it gave customer passports and transaction histories to a fake government agency

British fintech Revolut has confirmed a data breach caused not by an intrusion but by process: an unauthorised third party submitted what Revolut calls “fraudulent requests for information” from a legitimate government agency domain email, the requests carried valid technical domain authentication, and Revolut employees fulfilled them as routine legal compliance. The company describes it as “a sophisticated external impersonation scam.” It has not named the agency or the domain.

What went out is unusually complete for a breach of this shape: identity details (full name, date of birth, occupation), contact details (postal address, email, telephone), document and verification data including copies of passports and/or driver's licences and facial verification images, account statements including IBANs, withdrawal records, and full transaction history including Bitcoin transactions. Revolut says only a “very limited group of customers” was affected and that its systems and customer funds were untouched; on detection it blocked the address and notified the relevant government agency, law enforcement, and data-protection and financial regulators.

There is an extortion component. Those holding the data are threatening to release more of it every day until Revolut pays, and are demanding 10,000 Bitcoin — reported as equivalent to more than $782 million.

Why this one matters beyond Revolut. Every regulated institution has a legal-request channel that is designed to be answered quickly and without friction, and it authenticates on domain, not on identity. SPF, DKIM and DMARC all passed here; they were never the control anyone thought they were. The defensive question this raises is not “is our email authentication working” but “does anyone at our organisation verify a law-enforcement or agency request out-of-band, through a known contact, before an analyst attaches passport scans to a reply.” Not printed: no customer count, no dollar value of loss, and no attribution to a named group — none were stated in anything read this run.
Patch priority — do this first

GitLab CVE-2026-85706 — CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated arbitrary file read, and the federal deadline is TODAY, 14 September. Path traversal in the repository commits API; GitLab's own wording is “improper path confinement and missing authentication enforcement.” Affects Community and Enterprise Edition 18.7 < 19.1.8, 19.2 < 19.2.6 and 19.3 < 19.3.2; patched 10 September in 19.1.8 / 19.2.6 / 19.3.2. watchTowr observed in-the-wild probing from 06:00 UTC on 11 September and reports the flaw being used to read log and configuration files for credentials and secrets; the only precondition is at least one public project. CISA added it to KEV on 11 September with a federal civilian due date of 14 September — a three-day window, which is the high-risk tier under BOD 26-04 and not an error to be “corrected” to three weeks. If you run self-managed GitLab, this is the one thing on this page to do before lunch. The same releases fix CVE-2026-87719 (CVSS 9.9), an insecure-deserialization flaw in GitLab EE reachable via a crafted GraphQL subscription argument with Duo Chat access.

JeetBot — a Russian-language bot service running a browser extension as a token pipeline

The Socket Threat Research Team disclosed that a browser extension called “Twitch Enhanced Viewer | JeetBot,” installed by more than 30,000 users across Chrome and Firefox, forwards live OAuth session tokens to proxy servers operated by the Russian-language bot service JeetBot. Those tokens let anyone holding them act on the affected account without the password and without two-factor authentication — 2FA is not a control against a stolen session. Version 4.8, released in January 2026, POSTed captured tokens to a /set-token endpoint on JeetBot infrastructure, with backup endpoints on deno.dev and deno.net. The extension is marketed on genuinely desirable features — Twitch ad blocking, forced 1080p playback, regional stream unlocking, automatic channel-point collection — which is the entire business model. It remains listed under Chrome ID pnhhdhhcadcjfckjhpmjneldiegbojfb with roughly 30,000 users; the Firefox build reportedly has another 552. Remediation is not just uninstalling: sign out of all devices to invalidate existing sessions, then sign back in. Counts read this run range from “30,000” to “nearly 31,000” and both are printed.

Mathspace — breached through a self-hosted Metabase SQL injection

A data breach at Mathspace was caused by exploitation of CVE-2026-72898, a SQL injection vulnerability in the self-hosted Metabase analytics tool. Exposed: user names, emails, usernames and locations. No CVSS score and no record count were stated in anything read this run, so neither is printed. The pattern — an internal BI tool reachable and unpatched — is one of the more common paths to a customer-data incident and one of the least likely to be on an asset inventory.

Microsoft has confirmed reports that the September 2026 security updates cause Remote Desktop Services failures on Windows Server systems — re-confirmed in this run's reading, after being carried for several editions. Framed accurately: this is an availability defect with no CVE and no attacker, not a security incident, but it lands on the same admins in the same week as a 964-CVE patch cycle and a three-day KEV deadline, which is its own kind of risk.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • CVE-2026-85706 — GitLab. Added 11 September, federal civilian due date 14 September. 0 days left — due today.
  • CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060 — MikroTik RouterOS. Added 10 September, due 13 September. OVERDUE by 1 day. CVE-2026-86060 additionally carries mandatory forensic triage under BOD 26-04.
  • CVE-2025-25249 (Fortinet), CVE-2026-19490 (Citrix NetScaler), CVE-2026-87491 (Chromium V8 out-of-bounds write) and CVE-2026-20079 (Cisco Firewall Management Center). Added 9 September, due 12 September. OVERDUE by 2 days.
  • The 2 September batch — seven CVEs across Sangoma Switchvox, Kludex Starlette, Kestra OSS, BerriAI LiteLLM, JFrog Artifactory and SonicWall SMA1000. No due date for this batch was stated in anything read this run, so none is printed rather than derived.
  • BOD 22-01 is revoked — do not compute these as “three weeks from the add date.” CISA issued BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk,” on 10 June 2026, which supersedes and revokes BOD 19-02 and BOD 22-01. It replaces the uniform window with a 16-tier remediation matrix keyed to public exposure, KEV membership and whether exploitation can be fully automated. The most dangerous tier must be remediated within three days, with mandatory forensic triage. Agencies must be operating to these timelines by 7 December 2026. That is why three-day deadlines appear above and are not errors.
  • Absence from KEV is not a severity judgement. KEV entry requires evidence of exploitation, so a severe flaw with no confirmed exploitation carries no federal deadline at all.
