The Lead

Stocks closed lower on the Fed’s first hike since 2023 — Dow −631.21 to 51,461.90

Wednesday’s close is settled and was restated again by sources fetched for this edition. The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 631.21 points, or 1.21%, to 51,461.90. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,551.81. The Nasdaq Composite ended down 0.01% at 25,978.42 — effectively unchanged. Each level reconciles against Tuesday’s verified closes: 52,093.11 − 631.21 = 51,461.90 exactly.

The direction reversed during the session. All three indices were higher at a point during the day before the Federal Reserve raised its target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4.00% from 3.50%–3.75% — a unanimous decision and the first hike in three years — and before Chair Kevin Warsh spoke. It was the rate decision plus Warsh’s remarks on persistent inflation that turned the tape; the Fed said the action “will support a timelier return to the Committee’s 2 percent goal.” The Summary of Economic Projections points to one more hike in 2026, then steady through 2027.

A word on the round numbers still circulating. CNBC’s live blog is headlined “Dow drops 600 points as Fed rate hike and Warsh’s inflation talk unnerve investors”; Yahoo Finance’s reads “Dow sinks 600 points”. Earlier today a third rounding — 700 points — was in circulation as losses accelerated intraday. All three are restatements of the same slide at different moments in it. The settled figure is −631.21, which is what this page leads with and what the arithmetic above confirms.

The bond market did not flinch. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed back to the 5% mark and finished around flat at 5.008%; the 2-year was last up 5 basis points at 4.717%. Both were re-verified at source for this edition rather than carried forward.

After-Hours Movers

Wed 16 Sep · after the bell · Lennar / PR Newswire

Lennar reports Q3 FY2026: earnings per share less than half of last year’s

Third-quarter net earnings attributable to Lennar were $284 million, or $1.19 per diluted share, against $591 million, or $2.29, a year earlier. Excluding $53 million of mark-to-market losses on technology investments and $39 million of net one-time items in Financial Services, the figure was $294 million, or $1.23 — against $516 million, or $2.00, excluding $99 million of mark-to-market gains in the year-ago quarter. Financial Services operating earnings fell to $129 million from $177 million on lower profit per locked loan and lower lock volume; the Multifamily operating loss narrowed to $3 million from $16 million. Management hosts its call Thursday 17 September at 11:00 a.m. ET. No post-close price move for LEN is stated by any source fetched this run, so none is printed.

On the Radar

  • Lennar’s earnings call is tomorrow, Thursday 17 September, at 11:00 a.m. ET — the first detailed commentary from a major homebuilder since the Fed moved, and the read most directly exposed to a higher policy rate.
  • The Fed’s own projections point to one more hike in 2026, then holding steady in 2027. That, rather than today’s quarter point, is what the curve has to digest.
  • Oil and rates are moving as one instrument. CNBC put the one-month rolling correlation between front-month WTI and the 10-year yield at 0.96 in a 15 September piece — dated here because that is the date it carries — with the 10-year at its highest level since 2007.
  • Intel and SK Hynix are reported to be in talks over U.S. high-bandwidth-memory production. Sources describe the discussions as exploratory; nothing has been announced by either company.
  • Two freight names moved on the same worry in one session: J.B. Hunt warned on earnings and rising operating costs, and Old Dominion fell 4.34% on slowing trucking momentum, rising operating costs and elevated valuations.
