Top Story

CISA puts a federal clock on Chrome's exploited V8 zero-day

CISA added CVE-2026-85046 — a type-confusion flaw in Chromium's V8 JavaScript engine — to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on September 4, the only addition that day, citing evidence of active exploitation. Reporting on the entry gives federal civilian agencies a remediation due date of September 18, 2026. The flaw allows arbitrary code execution inside the browser sandbox by way of a crafted HTML page; Google's fix ships in Chrome 152.0.7977.82/.83. Because Chromium is the base for Edge, Brave, Opera and Electron applications, the exposure is far wider than Chrome installs alone.

Patch Priority

Update Chrome and every Chromium-derived browser to 152.0.7977.82 or later — due September 18

This is the single most urgent item on today's list: it is a browser flaw, it is being exploited now, and it is the one item on this page carrying a stated federal deadline. The callout is amber rather than red deliberately — the deadline is still away and the flaw is rated 8.8, not maximum severity, so this is urgent but not yet critical by this page's own rule. The date below in CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines is the same September 18 — the two sections are deliberately identical. Chromium-derived browsers inherit the same V8 engine and should be treated as in scope even though the KEV entry names Chromium.

Storm‑1175 — a former Medusa affiliate with new malware

Microsoft attributes the deployment of a new ransomware family, StormEncryptor, to Storm‑1175, a threat actor Microsoft assesses to be China-based. The group had previously been known for using Medusa ransomware against healthcare, professional-services and finance organisations in Australia, the United Kingdom and the United States; the StormEncryptor activity is the first Microsoft Threat Intelligence has observed from the actor since April 2026. StormEncryptor is written in C++ and appends .encrypted to the files it encrypts. Access is believed to come through the N‑able N‑central flaw below; post-compromise the group abuses the legitimate remote-management tools AnyDesk or SimpleHelp, runs Advanced IP Scanner for discovery, and dumps LSASS with Mimikatz.

Breaches & Incidents

Court recordsThird-party

Thomson Reuters C‑Track breach reaches sealed court files

Thomson Reuters disclosed a breach of C‑Track, the court case-management platform run by its subsidiaries. An unauthorised third party obtained C‑Track files in March 2026; the activity was not discovered until June 30, 2026 — roughly four months undetected. Named jurisdictions are Alabama, Pennsylvania, Kentucky, Montana, Nevada, North Dakota, South Carolina, Tennessee, Ohio, New Hampshire and Wyoming, plus the U.S. Virgin Islands and Canada. Exposed fields may include names with Social Security numbers, driver's licence numbers, dates of birth, medical information and health-insurance information; at some courts confidential, redacted or sealed material may also be affected. Twelve months of credit monitoring is being offered. ★ Note the count discrepancy: one aggregation headlines “at least 12 U.S. states” while the enumerated list runs to eleven. This page publishes the eleven it can name.

RansomwareMSP supply chain

StormEncryptor lands through N‑able N‑central

The delivery route for the ransomware above is CVE-2026-18577, an authentication bypass in N‑able N‑central that lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker take administrative control of a vulnerable server. It is assessed as a patch bypass for the earlier CVE-2026-18556. N‑able shipped a hotfix — 2026.3 HF1 / build 2026.3.1.7 — on August 2, and later a second N‑central hotfix as exploitation continued. Because N‑central is managed-service-provider tooling, one unpatched server can reach many downstream customer estates.

Vendor advisoryMax severity

ServiceNow patches three maximum-severity flaws

ServiceNow issued fixes for four vulnerabilities in its AI Platform, three of them rated CVSS 10.0. ServiceNow has patched its own hosted instances and released hotfixes for self-hosted deployments — so the residual risk sits with organisations running their own instances, which have to apply those hotfixes themselves.
