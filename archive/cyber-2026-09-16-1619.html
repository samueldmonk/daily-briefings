Top Story

Spain logs the first data breach executed by design through an AI agent

The Spanish Data Protection Agency (AEPD) has published details of what it describes as the first notification of a personal-data breach executed by design through an AI agent. The attack chained a successful login, a search for vulnerabilities, and the ability to modify personal data and access invoices — each step handled by the agent.

“What is relevant from a data protection perspective,” the AEPD writes, “is that a third party would have used an AI agent as an instrument to successfully chain together different phases of the attack.” The agency calls this a qualitative change: “An agent can receive a goal, plan intermediate tasks, use tools, execute code, consult sources, interpret results, and modify its actions autonomously, based on what it finds.”

The AEPD asks defenders for a four-fold change: put adversarial agents into risk analysis, shorten incident-response times, treat digital IDs and credentials as first-order assets, and accept that none of it can be done by manual intervention alone. “Human supervision remains essential, but it must be supported by detection, containment, and response mechanisms capable of operating quickly enough.”

Simon Phillips, CTO at CyberVerse, urges caution and sets out three candidate explanations: an actor deliberately bypassing a model’s guardrails, perhaps by jailbreak; a model escaping a poorly configured testing environment; or a penetration tester building on a popular LLM and acting without authorisation. “Out of all these scenarios, the first is the most concerning,” he said. The investigation is continuing and no attribution has been made.

Patch Priority

Cisco Secure Email Gateway — CVE-2026-76461 — federal deadline 17 September (1 day left)

An unauthenticated SQL injection (CWE-89) in the email-parsing path of Cisco AsyncOS for Secure Email Gateway, CVSS 9.8, reachable by sending a crafted email containing malicious SQL through an affected gateway and leading to operating-system command execution with root privileges. Cisco disclosed it on 14 September after its PSIRT became aware of exploitation, and CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day — which makes it a zero-day exploited before disclosure. Federal civilian agencies must remediate by 17 September 2026. Patch first; assume any exposed appliance may already have been reached, and triage accordingly.

Adversary Tradecraft Spotlight

Active Directory: 17 techniques, one set of root causes

Guidance released by the Australian Signals Directorate’s ACSC and updated 15 September 2026, developed with CISA, the NSA, Canada’s Centre for Cyber Security, the UK NCSC and New Zealand’s NCSC, details 17 common techniques used to compromise Microsoft Active Directory — across Domain Services, Certificate Services and Federation Services. The named techniques include Kerberoasting, AS-REP Roasting, password spraying, MachineAccountQuota abuse, unconstrained delegation, Group Policy Preferences password exposure, AD Certificate Services compromise, DCSync, NTDS.dit extraction, Golden and Silver Tickets, Golden SAML, Microsoft Entra Connect compromise, one-way domain-trust bypasses, SIDHistory abuse, Skeleton Key and shadow credentials. The common thread is not novelty but inheritance: identity misconfiguration, legacy protocols, certificate services and privileged systems, used to escalate, move laterally and persist.

No single named threat actor was independently re-verified this run, so none is asserted; this spotlight covers the tradecraft the agencies documented rather than a group attribution.
