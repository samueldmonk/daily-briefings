The Lead

A second straight lower close, as the 10-year Treasury yield reaches its highest level since 2007

All three major indexes settled lower for a second consecutive session on Tuesday. The S&P 500 fell 0.45% to 7,585.73; the Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 328.09 points, or 0.63%, to 52,093.11; and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.78% to 25,981.57. CNBC attributes the decline to traders looking ahead to the Federal Reserve's policy decision this week and to Treasury yields surging to multiyear highs.

The close is published here with levels, not percentages alone, because it clears this page's standing bar twice over. Two independent readings this run returned all seven figures identically, and each reconciles exactly against Monday's verified closes: 52,421.20 − 328.09 = 52,093.11; 7,619.98 − 34.25 = 7,585.73; 26,186.41 − 204.84 = 25,981.57.

The proportions differ more than the direction suggests. Measured against this morning's opening declines, the S&P widened by roughly 2.8× and the Nasdaq by 3.0×, while the Dow — the index that fell most in points — widened least in proportion, about 1.3×. Two readings are two points, not a path.

On the Radar

  • The Federal Reserve decision lands Wednesday and is the single event this tape is organised around. Futures pricing has moved decisively toward a quarter-point increase rather than a hold.
  • Goldman Sachs revised its forecast for this week from no change to a rate hike following the August inflation print, as market pricing of a hike pushed toward 90%. Earlier in September, markets were last pricing a 59% likelihood of a quarter-point increase — the repricing is the story in the rates market.
  • Crude is the transmission channel to watch. The tight relationship between oil and Treasurys could add further upward pressure on yields if crude stays elevated, as higher energy costs feed inflation expectations.
