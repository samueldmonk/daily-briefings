The Lead

Official close (4:00 PM ET): Dow +0.9%, S&P 500 +0.5%, Nasdaq +0.5% — the Dow snaps a three-week losing streak

Wall Street closed out a volatile week on a firmer note. The S&P 500 rose 39.28 points, or 0.5%; the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 478.64 points, or 0.9%; the Nasdaq Composite rose 129.34 points, or 0.5%; and the Russell 2000 edged up 0.1% (AP). Stocks got a boost after Brent crude dropped below $98 a barrel, AP said; the Dow snapped a three-week losing streak and the Nasdaq and S&P 500 also notched weekly wins. CNBC’s closing headline: “Dow jumps more than 470 points Friday; stocks notch winning week despite Treasury yield surge.” Official levels are in the Weekly Scorecard. That is little changed from the final hour: at 3 PM ET Yahoo Finance had the Dow up 0.78% and the S&P 500 and Nasdaq each up 0.36%, and at 2:33 PM ET the Dow was up 0.85%. Around 12:15 PM ET Yahoo Finance’s quote strip had shown the Dow up 0.86%, the Nasdaq up 0.63% and the S&P 500 up 0.53%, with the Cboe Volatility Index down 3.51% at 15.12. At 2:04 PM ET TheStreet said the small-cap Russell 2000 was barely positive, up 0.13%. Heading into Friday’s open the Nasdaq and S&P 500 were up 2.3% and 2% over the prior five days while the Dow was down 0.5% (Yahoo Finance). The lift came from oil: November WTI crude was down 2.80% at $91.96 at midday (Yahoo Finance), extending a 2.34% drop to $92.54 around the open (Schwab), after Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said the Strait of Hormuz could reopen and nuclear talks restart within seven days if Washington meets conditions he said are already in the collapsed June “Islamabad MOU” (CNBC via TheStreet), and as Reuters reported U.S. and Iranian negotiators in New York exploring a phased path out of the war.

Bonds remain the market’s pressure point. The 10-year Treasury yield hit 5.225% during Thursday’s session, its highest since 2007, and the 30-year reached 5.502%, its highest since 2004 (TheStreet); the 10-year stood at 5.17% heading into Friday’s open, below its overnight peak (Schwab). CME FedWatch put October rate-hike odds at 66% early Friday (Schwab). The University of Michigan’s final September consumer sentiment reading came in at 48.1, down 7% from August but above the 47.5 expected and the 47.8 preliminary reading (MarketScreener); year-ahead inflation expectations rose to 4.6%, the highest since June (CNN), and August durable goods orders were flat, up 0.3% excluding transportation (Schwab). Chinese President Xi Jinping concluded his White House visit today; Yahoo Finance said the U.S. and China seemed to agree to keep their trade relationship at the status quo for several months. Yahoo also reported the 10-year yield climbed to 5.2% on Friday, its highest level since the global financial crisis.

Movers & Drivers

+8% mid-morning

Akamai Technologies (AKAM)

Surged 15.31% in premarket trading (TheStreet) and was up almost 17% early (Schwab), then pared the gain to 8% at $119 by around 10 AM ET (Investopedia) after announcing a seven-year, $11.6 billion cloud services commitment from Anthropic to support its CPU workloads on Akamai Cloud (TheStreet). The companies can expand the arrangement by another $9 billion, to roughly $20 billion, and Akamai issued Anthropic a warrant equal to up to about 5% of its shares outstanding; Piper Sandler raised its price target to $158 from $125 (Yahoo Finance). JPMorgan lifted its target to $167 from $158 but stayed neutral, noting Anthropic accounts for some 93% of the $14.4 billion in new deals Akamai has signed this year; Oppenheimer reiterated outperform with a $180 target (Investopedia). Reported premarket gains varied widely across outlets.

+10.3% pre-mkt

People Inc. (PPLI)

Jumped 10.27% premarket after The Wall Street Journal reported MGM Resorts is weighing a takeover bid for Barry Diller’s media company — days after People withdrew its own $48.30-a-share offer for the rest of MGM (TheStreet).

+5.1% pre-mkt

Equinix (EQIX)

Gained 5.12% premarket amid accelerating demand for enterprise AI infrastructure and recent bullish analyst price-target updates (TheStreet).

+5% early

Arm Holdings (ARM) & AMD

Chip and AI-infrastructure stocks moved mostly higher early, led by Arm, up 5%; AMD climbed 2% after Bank of America raised its price target, citing the importance of CPUs in the agentic era (Schwab).

−4.4% pre-mkt

Twilio (TWLO)

Lost 4.41% premarket after HSBC downgraded the stock to reduce from hold with a $211 price target (TheStreet).

−4.0% pre-mkt

Zscaler (ZS)

Fell 3.98% premarket after naming Ross Tackett chief revenue officer, effective Oct. 1 (TheStreet).

+7.3% mid-morning

Datadog (DDOG)

Gained 7.28% by around 10:30 AM ET as traders positioned ahead of the monitoring and security platform’s next earnings release (TheStreet); it was also among the midday winners TheStreet listed at 1:29 PM ET, alongside Bloom Energy, Humana, Dell and Microsoft, as technology dominated the winning end of the market.

−8.6% mid-morning

Zscaler (ZS) & Twilio (TWLO)

The premarket losses deepened after the open: by around 10:30 AM ET Zscaler was down 8.64% after naming Ross Tackett chief revenue officer, and Twilio was down 6.22% after HSBC’s downgrade to reduce (TheStreet).

−3% midday

Meta Platforms (META)

Slipped about 3% around 12:30 PM ET, a sluggish end to a week in which it rose nearly 10% on its new AI feature (TheStreet); it was on TheStreet’s 12:54 PM ET midday losers list with Cboe Global Markets, Gen Digital, Palo Alto Networks and Devon Energy.

−2% early

Nike (NKE)

Fell 2% early after Bank of America downgraded it to underperform from neutral, seeing downside risk to earnings estimates and saying the turnaround is taking longer than expected (Schwab).

Lower pre-mkt

Costco (COST)

Slipped ahead of the open despite fiscal fourth-quarter earnings that topped estimates, as investors focused on a further slowdown in membership revenue growth (Schwab).
