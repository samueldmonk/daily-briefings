Top Story

SonicWall found two SMA1000 zero-days by finding the attacks first

SonicWall is urging customers of its SMA1000 secure remote access gateway and SSL-VPN appliance to patch two zero-day vulnerabilities that are already being exploited in the wild. In an advisory published Tuesday, the company says both the flaws and their exploitation were discovered internally — the vendor found the intrusions before it found the bugs, which is a different sequence from the usual disclosure story and a worse one for anyone still unpatched.

CVE-2026-83548 carries a CVSS score of 10.0 and is a pre-authentication SSRF in the Appliance Work Place interface, exploitable remotely with no credentials to reach sensitive functionality and perform unauthorised operations. CVE-2026-83549, scored 7.8, is an OS command injection in the Appliance Management Console that an authenticated attacker can use to run arbitrary commands. SonicWall says it has observed exploitation of both, which points to the pair being chained — the first supplying the access the second requires, ending in unauthenticated remote code execution.

Affected models are the 6210, 7210 and 8200v; hotfixes 12.4.3-03526 and 12.5.0-02952 and later versions carry the fix. SSL-VPN on SonicWall firewalls and the SMA100 series are not affected — a scoping line worth reading carefully before anyone patches the wrong estate. No details of the attacks have been published and the advisory carries no indicators of compromise, so defenders cannot currently check whether they were hit; patching is the only available action. CISA’s KEV catalog holds 17 SonicWall flaws, and these two are not among them yet.

A trap this page is naming before it falls into it. There is a second, older SonicWall SMA1000 pair in circulation — CVE-2026-15409 and CVE-2026-15410, patched in mid-July, added to KEV on July 14 with a three-day remediation window, and confirmed by CISA in August as exploited by ransomware gangs, with Resecurity linking the activity to an INC Ransomware affiliate and Volexity attributing earlier zero-day use to UTA0533. Same vendor, same product line, same “SMA1000 zero-days exploited” headline shape, different CVEs and a three-week gap. Nothing on this page conflates them, and any coverage that cites “the SonicWall SMA1000 flaws” without a CVE number should be read twice.

Patch Priority

This item is five days old and new to this page, not new to the world — the second patch shipped Friday, August 28. It is promoted here because “we patched PaperCut last week” is exactly the belief it invalidates.

PaperCut NG/MF — install Emergency Patch Release 2 today, even if you already installed the first one. CVE-2026-81578 (8.8) and CVE-2026-82078 (9.4), exploited in the wild, KEV deadline September 14.

The reason this displaces the usual ordering is that the failure mode is new to this page. PaperCut disclosed the two flaws as zero-days under active exploitation and shipped an emergency patch. watchTowr then fully reproduced the vulnerabilities, found multiple ways around that patch, and identified an additional authentication bypass; Huntress independently discovered multiple bypasses of the original patches too. PaperCut released Emergency Patch Release 2 on Friday, August 28, with hardening developed with both firms, and is urging every customer to install it even if the first patch is already in place. Release 2 covers versions 24, 25 and 26 on Windows, Linux and macOS; anyone on version 23 or earlier is told to upgrade rather than wait for a backport. Site Servers and secondary/print servers need it as well; Print Deploy and Mobility Print are not affected.

What the two bugs actually do. CVE-2026-81578 (8.8) is an authentication bypass in the NG/MF web management interface — unauthenticated remote requests aimed at administrative functions can trigger backend actions before access validation completes. CVE-2026-82078 (9.4) is unsafe dynamic class loading in the database connection utilities: driver classes are instantiated from configurable driver names without validation against an allowlist, so an attacker who can reach configuration can execute arbitrary Java bytecode as the PaperCut server process. Chained, that is pre-authentication remote code execution.

Detection, since indicators are thin. Huntress observed exploitation in two customer environments and reproduced the full pre-auth RCE chain; PaperCut logs captured hex-encoded Java .class files acting as a bridge from PaperCut to the underlying operating system, plus commands that looked like reconnaissance rather than malware deployment or persistence. Look for anomalous activity from pc-app.exe, for missing or truncated server.log files, and for the errors No suitable driver found for jdbc:no:x and DatabaseUtils - Database error looking up cardID: VALUES CAST. PaperCut says the attacks appear limited and targeted, has not named the actor, and is withholding post-exploitation detail while its investigation runs. Restrict the web interfaces to trusted IPs regardless.

Neither standing item is downgraded, and the rule that usually decides this box did not decide it. That rule — an elapsed deadline outranks a live one — ranks deadlines, and it assumes the remediation behind a deadline works. Here the published fix was shown insufficient, which is a different question from which clock is further along, so the rule is extended rather than applied, and that is said out loud. Still outstanding: Citrix CVE-2026-8452, added to KEV August 26, due August 29 under BOD 26-04 and now four days past due, with pre-auth root RCE proven August 14. And MLflow CVE-2026-64849, whose federal deadline is today, September 2. If your estate has all three, all three are today’s work.

Threat Actor Spotlight

RaaSMost active, Q2 2026

The Gentlemen

Emerged in mid-2025 and rose to become the most active threat actor of the second quarter of 2026, outpacing established rivals as it scaled. Its leak site listed more than 200 victim organisations across over 50 countries and 20-plus industries — energy, government and healthcare services among them — as of early 2026, with more than 320 claimed victims by April. In the first half of 2026 its strongest activity was in Europe and the U.K., where it was credited with 144 ransomware attacks.

The operation advertises openly on criminal forums and offers affiliates a 90% cut. It runs double extortion — affiliates exfiltrate, and the data is published on a Tor leak site if the ransom goes unpaid — with negotiations conducted over Tox and Session. It is led by a Russian-speaking actor using the alias “hastalamuerte”, also tracked as LARVA-368, who previously ran an affiliate crew called ArmCorp inside the Qilin programme before splitting off over a payment dispute in July 2025.

Aesto Health

The Birmingham, Alabama health-technology firm — which handles secure data migration, EHR exchange and legacy data archiving for providers and practices — has notified HHS that 9,540,683 individuals are affected. The breach was discovered December 18, 2025 in portions of its AWS infrastructure, with exfiltration determined to have occurred between December 2 and December 18. Compromised fields include names, Social Security numbers, driver’s licence and other ID numbers, dates of birth, financial account numbers, medical and health-insurance information and taxpayer identification numbers.

What the company has not said is as notable as what it has: Aesto has not publicly identified the threat actor, has not explained how the AWS environment was compromised, and its notices do not state whether ransomware was deployed or whether extortion was attempted.

The 23-year-old Sality botnet is sinkholed

A coordinated international operation carried out on August 31 and led by United States authorities disrupted the Sality peer-to-peer botnet, active for more than two decades. Authorities from Bulgaria, Hungary, Romania and the U.S. took part with support from Europol and private-sector partners CrowdStrike and the Shadowserver Foundation. At its peak the botnet gave its operator access to up to one million infected machines; more than 11 million unique IP addresses have been linked to the infrastructure to date. The method fits the target: rather than seizing a central server there was none to seize, so a peer-to-peer sinkholing operation redirected infected machines away from the criminal network, isolating them and rendering the operator’s command channel inoperable. Sinkholed is not remediated — the machines are still infected.

WatchGuard patches five criticals, all scored 9.3

WatchGuard released fixes for more than two dozen vulnerabilities, five of them critical and every one of the five scored CVSS 9.3. Three sit in the iked process of Fireware OS — the IKE daemon handling key establishment and IPsec VPN negotiation — and are exploitable without authentication for remote code execution via crafted network traffic: a heap buffer overflow (CVE-2026-19313), a stack-based buffer overflow (CVE-2026-19318) and a type confusion (CVE-2026-19315). A fourth, CVE-2026-13086, is a stack overflow in the Endpoint Protection Manager service used by the deprecated Mobile Security feature. The fifth, CVE-2026-78174 in WatchGuard Dimension, lets a low-privileged administrator extract a super admin’s session ID and CSRF tokens and take the account over. Fixes ship in Fireware OS 2026.2.2, 12.12.2 and 12.5.20 and Dimension 2.3.1. WatchGuard says it is not aware of any of these being exploited in the wild — the sentence that separates this card from the one at the top of the page.

MLflow’s webhook tester is handing out cloud credentials

MLflow is the Linux Foundation-backed open-source AI engineering platform, used to debug, evaluate and monitor LLM and agent applications, with over 30 million downloads a month. CVE-2026-64849 is a DNS-rebinding SSRF bypass in its outbound webhook delivery. The mechanism is unusually direct: a default Tracking Server — mlflow server, no authentication, default SQLite backend — exposes the model-registry webhooks API unauthenticated, including a synchronous POST /api/2.0/mlflow/webhooks/{id}/test endpoint that returns the upstream response status and body to the caller. An unauthenticated attacker who can reach the server makes it fetch arbitrary internal, loopback or cloud-metadata endpoints and simply reads the answers back — AWS instance-metadata IAM credentials among them. watchTowr says scanning began within hours of the CVE ID being assigned and that attackers are exfiltrating cloud credentials and secrets. Fixed in version 3.15.0.

~22,000 Microsoft Exchange servers still exposed

Nearly 22,000 Exchange servers worldwide remain unpatched for CVE-2026-62911, with roughly 6,200 in the United States and 5,100 in Germany, plus several hundred each in the U.K., Russia, Canada, Austria and France. Public exploit code exists — Orange Tsai demonstrated the flaw at Pwn2Own Berlin 2026 as one link in a three-bug chain. As of publication no confirmed in-the-wild exploitation has been reported, which is the only reassuring sentence in this card and the one most likely to expire first.

  • CVE-2026-64849 — MLflow. Added August 19, due September 2, 2026 under BOD 26-04 — CISA gave FCEB agencies two weeks. (0 days left — the deadline is today) The window is fourteen days as CISA assigned it, not derived from any formula here.
  • CVE-2026-66384 — JFrog Artifactory. Added August 27, due September 10, 2026 under BOD 26-04. (8 days left) Path traversal, CVSS 5.3, reported exploited in a campaign that used it for internet egress and lateral movement through Kubernetes service accounts and IAM/Key Vault credentials.
  • CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 — PaperCut NG/MF. Added August 31, due September 14, 2026. (12 days left) Note the window is fourteen days, not the twenty-one BOD 22-01 normally allows; the stated date governs, and no countdown here is derived from a formula.
  • Not on the catalog and still today’s priority: SonicWall CVE-2026-83548 and CVE-2026-83549 are exploited in the wild but had not been added to KEV as of publication. The catalog is a record of what CISA has processed, not of what is being attacked — there is no federal deadline to count down to here, and that is not a reason to wait.
  • One KEV item was researched this run and refused. CVE-2026-53362 surfaced in two reads that disagree with each other on both dates: one gives added August 20 / due September 3, the other gives an August 27 alert / due August 30, describing a Linux kernel IPv6 flaw at CVSS 7.8. An item whose add date and due date are both disputed cannot carry a countdown, and a countdown is most of what this section is for. Excluded on provenance, not on substance.
  • The August 26 batch of six also carried CVE-2015-3246, CVE-2015-5287, CVE-2019-1068, CVE-2021-23758 and CVE-2022-0995 — four of them a decade or more old, which is its own comment on what is actually being exploited.
