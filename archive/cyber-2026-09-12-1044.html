Top Story

NewData exposure

IDScan confirms a breach after 153 million driver’s licence scans surface for sale

Identity-verification company IDScan has confirmed that attackers accessed customer data held on its cloud platform, days after reporting tied the firm to a dark-web database of more than 153 million driver’s licence scans. The company published a notice on 4 September and says it became aware of the incident on or around 1 September — the same day a journalist reported the dark-web activity.

A dark-web identity-theft service called Nexus advertised access to more than 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver’s licence scans, 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents and 579,000 medical cards. Data at risk includes names and driver’s licence or other government-issued identification numbers, alongside the scans themselves.

IDScan says it moved to secure its systems, engaged third-party specialists and is notifying affected people “in an abundance of caution” with free credit monitoring and identity protection. The FBI has opened an inquiry, and the company has already been sued over the alleged breach.

Patch Priority

Deadline today — 0 days left

Federal civilian agencies must remediate CVE-2026-20079 in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center (CVSS 10.0 authentication bypass — an unauthenticated remote attacker executes script files and obtains root) by 12 September 2026, alongside Citrix CVE-2026-19490 (CVSS 9.3, NetScaler ADC/Gateway) and Fortinet CVE-2025-25249 (CVSS 7.3, FortiOS/FortiSwitchManager/FortiSASE heap buffer overflow). Cisco updated its advisory to note active exploitation in August 2026, identifying three clusters of post-compromise activity on FMC instances used to deploy web shells and malware. That deadline is today.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NewRansomware

Rhysida

Rhysida hit Berlin’s state government and, after a ransom demand of 30 bitcoin was refused, published roughly 6 terabytes of stolen data including state administration and national-defence records. The ransom deadline ran out on 4 September before the group dumped the cache online; Security Affairs reported the leak on 11 September, and threat-intelligence outlet Cyberverso identified the actor.

Breaches & Incidents

NewExtortion

FulcrumSec turns hardcoded credentials into an extortion line

Reported on 11 September: the cyber-extortion group FulcrumSec is exploiting hardcoded credentials in public-facing IT infrastructure, with Novo Nordisk named among its victims.

NewRansomware

Rhysida dumps 6 TB of Berlin government data

Berlin’s state government refused a 30 BTC demand; the group published approximately 6 TB covering state administration and national-defence records. Multiple further incidents were logged the same day, with Nightspire responsible for several.

Supply chainPhishing

Trezor customers phished through Brevo

Trezor’s marketing provider Brevo was breached, exposing about 347,000 customer email addresses that were used immediately for a phishing run. Trezor says 2,500 users clicked the embedded malicious link before the destination was taken down.

Patch regression

September Windows Server updates break Remote Desktop Services

Administrators report the September 2026 security updates causing RDS failures on Windows Server 2019, 2022 and 2025, preventing connections and in some cases requiring a hard reset to restore functionality.

Mobile malware

Mantax Otax fuses ransomware with spyware on Android

A new Android strain combining ransomware and spyware capabilities: it encrypts files, steals sensitive data, and spams and harasses victims.

Healthcare

INC Ransom claims a 5.7 TB healthcare theft

The INC Ransom group claimed responsibility in mid-September, ahead of a Department of Health and Human Services investigation, and alleged it stole 5.7 terabytes of data.
