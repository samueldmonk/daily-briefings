Top Story

Attackers are emptying AI development servers of their API keys through a critical Langflow flaw

CVE-2026-0768 carries a CVSS 9.8 and allows unauthenticated arbitrary code execution as root: Langflow, an open-source low-code platform for building AI applications, fails to validate the code parameter of its validate endpoint before executing it as Python. The endpoint sits in the code validator behind the custom component editor.

VulnCheck reports continuous exploitation beginning August 29 — at least 50 attempts over the weekend against honeypots in the U.K., and more than 360 by Monday, with attack traffic originating primarily from Russia. The behaviour is reconnaissance and credential harvesting rather than ransomware: reading Langflow's secret_key, grepping environment variables for API keys and cloud credentials, hunting SSH keys and .env files, then moving laterally and exfiltrating source code.

The stated objective is OpenAI and Anthropic API keys, AWS, GCP and Azure credentials, and database connection strings. Guidance from the reporting is blunt: any organisation running Langflow should assume existing credentials are compromised and rotate every configured secret. One outlet counts this as Langflow’s twelfth exploited CVE.

CVE-2026-0768 does not appear among the vulnerabilities CISA added on August 31 or September 2, so no federal remediation deadline attaches to it. One aggregated return put roughly 7,000 servers in scope; no source consulted for this edition states it directly, so it is not asserted here.

SonicWall SMA1000 — CVE-2026-83548 (CVSS 10.0), chained with CVE-2026-83549

SonicWall disclosed both flaws on September 1 as actively exploited. CVE-2026-83548 is a critical pre-authentication SSRF; leveraging it, an attacker can reach CVE-2026-83549, an OS command injection in the Appliance Management Console, and execute arbitrary OS commands without prior authentication. Kestra OSS CVE-2026-49869 is the other 10.0 in the same batch. Both were added to CISA KEV on September 2 with a remediation date of Saturday, September 5, 2026 — the same date carried in the federal-deadline list below.

MRXISBACK — routing as an attack surface

Between August 28 and 30 a threat actor Softaculous links to the handle MRXISBACK hijacked a block of the company's IP addresses. AS62390 (NexonHost) began announcing a more specific slice of Hetzner address space at roughly 20:57 UTC on August 28, overriding normal routing. The attacker then routed domain validation through the hijacked network to obtain a technically valid Let's Encrypt certificate covering virtualizor.com, api.virtualizor.com and files.virtualizor.com — so diverted clients saw no TLS warning. A malicious Virtualizor update package was delivered to a small number of installations that checked for updates during the window. The incident ran roughly 33 hours and touched update services, client billing areas and other Softaculous systems.

Breaches & Incidents

RansomwareGovernmentNew

Berlin refuses to pay after Rhysida hits the city-state

Rhysida attacked Berlin's state government weeks before an election. The group says it took 5.79 terabytes — 46,500 contracts plus emails, phone numbers, passwords and classified information — and is auctioning it from a starting price of 30 bitcoin ($77,622). The leak occurred August 7–12 and was discovered internally in mid-August. Officials cut home-office access on Monday: staff can send and receive email but cannot open VPN connections to internal networks, so they must work from office machines. Mayor Kai Wegner and interior senator Iris Spranger said jointly that “the state of Berlin will not submit to extortion.” Election infrastructure was not affected and no election data was compromised.

Account takeoverIdentityNew

A legacy Lenovo sign-in opened 5,000 Dropbox accounts

From August 4 to 21, attackers reached at least 5,000 Dropbox accounts holding nothing but the victims' email addresses. Lenovo's authentication let anyone register a Lenovo ID against any email address without verifying control of the inbox; because Dropbox accepted Lenovo ID as a sign-in path, that new identity bypassed the password entirely, and victims did not need a pre-existing Lenovo ID. About a third of affected users had files viewed or downloaded. Only accounts without Dropbox's own 2FA were exposed. Dropbox has terminated every Lenovo-ID session, disabled the integration outright, and now requires a native Dropbox password.

TakedownBotnetNew

A 23-year-old botnet is sinkholed out of existence

On August 31 the U.S. Department of Justice, FBI, Defense Criminal Investigative Service, CrowdStrike, the Shadowserver Foundation and Europol disrupted Sality, a peer-to-peer botnet running since 2003 and used to deliver malware to more than 15,000 machines worldwide — credential theft, spam, proxy services, network exploitation and DDoS. CrowdStrike's Counter Adversary Operations team turned the botnet's own peer-list mechanism against it, stripping legitimate peers and substituting controlled sinkholes so bots can no longer receive payload instructions. Shadowserver is working with ISPs and national CERTs to notify owners of infected devices.

ExploitedKEV

PaperCut is on its third emergency patch

PaperCut published an urgent advisory on August 27 confirming it was investigating active exploitation of PaperCut NG and MF, and had shipped a third emergency patch by September 1 after earlier fixes were bypassed. The two flaws chain: CVE-2026-81578 (missing authentication for a critical function, CVSS v4.0 8.8) gives an unauthenticated attacker the ability to alter system configuration, and CVE-2026-82078 (unsafe reflection, CVSS v4.0 9.4) uses that access to execute arbitrary Java bytecode on the application classpath under the PaperCut server process. Patches cover v24, v25 and v26. Both entered CISA KEV on August 31, due September 14.

Refused this run. A search for current breaches returned the IDMerit identity-verification leak — roughly a billion KYC records — as though it were today’s news. It is not: researchers found the unprotected MongoDB instance on November 11, 2025, it was secured the next day, and public disclosure came on February 18, 2026. It is not published here as current. The standing exclusion on the Pennsylvania Attorney General / INC Ransom incident (September 2025 coverage) also held for a third consecutive edition.
