Top Story

Michael Page wins on Saturday, is off the rankings by Monday, and is now reported to have been released outright

Michael “Venom” Page, a 39-year-old welterweight, beat Nursulton Ruziboev by unanimous decision at UFC Paris on 5 September. He was initially eligible to be ranked after that win. Then, according to longtime MMA journalist John Morgan, the weekly UFC rankings email sent on Monday 7 September told voters that Page had been removed from the rankings and would not be eligible for this week's selection.

What has changed since this briefing's 1:50 p.m. edition: two outlets now report the release as having happened rather than as looming, under the headline that the UFC has released him following the Paris win, and a third report says he has been removed from the UFC roster. This is still reporting, not a promotion announcement — one of the same reads describes the release as “reportedly” done — and the rankings removal remains the action with the most specific evidence behind it, namely an email sent to ranking voters and described by the journalist who received it. The page states the reports and their status rather than treating a headline as a confirmation.

The context makes it plausible: Page had not re-signed with the promotion before the bout and was fighting out his existing contract at UFC Paris; days earlier he had said publicly he doubted he would re-sign. He had been No. 15 in the UFC's media rankings and No. 8 in the recently formed Meta rankings, which the promotion adopted this summer. He signed with the UFC in December 2023 and went 5-1 across six appearances, without a single finish inside the Octagon. Correction to this briefing's earlier editions today: they called Page a middleweight. Sources read this run identify him as a welterweight, and the earlier description was wrong.

Prospect Watch

New5 contracts

Week 4 signed five, and all five now have names and provenance

Dana White's Contender Series Season 10 has passed its midway point, and Week 4 added five athletes to the UFC roster, taking the season total to 20 contracts awarded across eight divisions. The five: Adam Darby, a Cage Warriors contender — detailed in his own card below, and the only one of the five this briefing had previously named; Modestino Rodrigues, a former K-1 kickboxing ace; Silvestre Sanchez of Lux Fight League; Gabriel Lorenco, a Chute Boxe-trained heavyweight; and Adam Livingston, an LFA veteran. The other four are new to this page.

The season has been unusually violent. Of the 20 bouts so far, only five have gone the distance and eight have ended in the opening round — five of those in under a minute. Note the coincidence rather than reading anything into it: 20 bouts and 20 contracts are the same number by chance, not because every fight earned a deal. Remaining episodes: Week 6 on Tuesday 15 September, Week 7 on 22 September, Week 8 on 29 September.

Around the Sport

  • The women's flyweight title is vacant, and the reporting uses two different words for how it got there. Shevchenko is injured and sidelined for the next year, and the belt goes up at UFC 332 on 3 October, where Natalia Silva faces Wang Cong. Four reads this run say she vacated it, one of them framing that as forced to vacate; an earlier edition today carried a read that said she was stripped. Read-through correction: this page had adopted “stripped” in one place and “vacated” in another, which is a contradiction rather than a nuance. It now says vacated throughout, because that is where the weight of this run's sourcing sits and because “stripped” implies a promotional sanction that no source actually alleges — but the other wording is recorded here rather than deleted, since a fighter who cannot defend for a year and a promotion that removes a belt are describable both ways. The UFC has said Shevchenko is guaranteed a title shot when she is cleared, though one read notes the Silva–Cong winner may well defend once before that happens.
  • Carlos Ulberg will not defend the light heavyweight title this year, and an interim belt is looking likelier. He tore the ACL in his right knee during the opening of the UFC 327 fight in which he won the belt, rallied to finish Jiří Procházka anyway, and had reconstructive surgery a week later on what he has called a full rupture of the ligament. He now hopes to return early next year. This run's reads state that the UFC has not yet indicated an interim light heavyweight title will be created, while describing that outcome as increasingly likely given the timeline. His likeliest first challenger is named as Magomed Ankalaev, who finished Bogdan Guskov in July, or Paulo Costa, who has won back-to-back fights since his 2024 loss to Sean Strickland.

Rankings & Business

Rankings movement

  • Michael Page removed from the rankings on 7 September, having been No. 15 in the media rankings and No. 8 in the Meta rankings. See the Top Story.
  • The Meta rankings are new. The UFC adopted the Meta-branded ranking board this summer; it now runs alongside the long-standing media panel, and Page held a materially higher position on the newer board.

Business & broadcast

  • UFC Freedom 250 averaged 8.2 million viewers across the U.S. and Latin America on Paramount+, the streamer's biggest live exclusive event ever, and reached 17 million total viewers — unique people who watched for at least a minute. Including international audiences in Australia, China, India, South Korea, New Zealand and the U.K., TKO puts total global reach at 34 million.
  • UFC 324 drew a live average-minute audience of 4.96 million for its main card, the largest exclusive live event on Paramount+ at the time.
  • Paramount is in year one of a seven-year, $7.7 billion media-rights deal with the UFC, which is owned by TKO Group. Paramount says the arrangement is putting UFC events in front of an audience as much as 20 times larger than the pay-per-view average of the previous two years.
  • UFC 332 extends that reach to broadcast television, with the first numbered-event main card to air live and free on CBS.
