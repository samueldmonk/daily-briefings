Top Story

First of its kind: hackers crossed a private cellular network into a Polish heat plant’s OT — and shut its turbine down

CERT Polska has disclosed a December 29 attack on a combined heat-and-power plant that supplies heat to about 50,000 residents — the first observed case of attackers reaching an OT network through a private APN (cellular access point). The intrusion began at a wind farm, where the attackers compromised a Fortinet VPN and firewall device, then used a Teltonika cellular router to open an SSH tunnel into the distribution system operator’s private APN — and from there crossed into the plant’s OT network, going after a Wago PLC (CERT Polska via BleepingComputer, The Hacker News, Help Net Security).

The plant’s steam turbine and water-treatment system were shut down; recovery began around 7:30 a.m. — while the intruders were still active inside the network — and customers never lost heat or electricity. CERT Polska says a misconfiguration that let arbitrary devices on the private APN talk to one another helped make the attack possible. The same day, coordinated attacks hit some 30 renewable-energy facilities and a second CHP plant; the disclosure came August 8, after an investigation of more than three months (The Record, SC Media).

ESET has attributed the late-2025 attack wave on Poland’s energy system to the Russia-aligned Sandworm APT with medium confidence, citing “a strong overlap with numerous previous Sandworm wiper activity we analyzed” (Security Affairs, Help Net Security). The Metabase zero-day — still the most urgent patch on the board — moves to the Patch Priority box and incident cards below.

Breaches & Incidents

NewBreach

n8n joins the Metabase victim list

Workflow-automation platform n8n disclosed that an attacker used the Metabase zero-day to obtain 136 customer records — names and email addresses of self-hosted and Cloud users (BleepingComputer). Framework has told customers names, login IPs, addresses, phone numbers and emails were accessed, with no order or payment data taken (Engadget via THN); Help Net Security also names Tally and Kilo Code.

NewRansomware

CISA and the FBI map Gunra ransomware (AA26-222a)

A joint advisory from CISA, the FBI, NSA, the DoD Cyber Crime Center, the Secret Service and South Korea’s national police details Gunra, a double-extortion RaaS built from leaked Conti source code, active since April 2025 against healthcare, financial, government and professional-services targets. Initial access comes via exploited CVEs on internet-facing devices (including CVE-2024-5559 and CVE-2025-24472); agencies urge patching exposed VPNs and segmenting networks (CISA, The Record, GBHackers). One break for defenders: a weakness in Gunra’s Linux variant lets victims reconstruct keys from file timestamps and recover files without paying (CISA advisory).

ICS

Iran-linked attacks on U.S. water utilities keep spreading

Hackers targeted municipal water systems in seven states in a single week, the FBI says (NBC News), with SecurityWeek reporting ICS attacks now touching at least a dozen states. Attackers hit internet-exposed PLCs; the FBI and EPA have warned utilities nationwide. Water systems are reported operating safely, and the U.S. government has not formally attributed the activity, which intelligence agencies assess as likely Iranian (Time, CNN).

Malware

"Solidity Pro" VS Code extensions steal wallets and credentials

Two malicious extensions (helper-beeps.solidity-pro and web3devtoolsx.solidity-pro) evolved from beaconing to Cloudflare Workers endpoints into a full information stealer that collects browser profiles, crypto wallets, source-control tokens, API keys, SSH keys and Telegram bot tokens, exfiltrating via a Telegram bot. Neither remains on Open VSX, though the GitHub repo was still accessible at press time (Yeeth Security via The Hacker News).

AI

Atlassian Rovo tricked into exfiltrating Jira and Confluence data

Two firms independently showed attacker-controlled instructions can make Atlassian’s Rovo assistant gather data a signed-in user can access and send it to an outside server: PromptArmor hid instructions in an uploaded file (working as of its Aug 5 report, even with web search off), while Varonis Threat Labs preloaded instructions through the rovoChatPrompt URL parameter so one click was enough. Only one of the two routes is confirmed closed (The Hacker News).
