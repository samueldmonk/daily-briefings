The Lead

The 60-day clock ran out, and the market spent the session pricing what comes next

U.S. stocks closed lower on Monday, August 17. The June 17 memorandum of understanding between Washington and Tehran hit its 60-day deadline with nothing signed. A senior Iranian official told Reuters that Tehran is going "fully offensive" and would use its military to break the U.S. naval blockade if talks fail. President Trump, for his part, told Fox News he is in no rush, and threatened to bomb Oman — which has been trying to mediate — if it gets in the way of negotiations.

Shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has essentially stopped: three vessels crossed on Sunday, against roughly 130 daily before the war began in February. Brent crude rose 0.7% to $89.13 at midday, a quiet day by recent standards, though Yahoo Finance reported the international benchmark touching $90 a barrel during the session. The sharper move was in rates — the 30-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.31%, its highest level since June 2007, on concerns about growing national debt.

On the closing levels. Direction is unambiguous: the Dow was the weakest of the three at roughly −0.5%, the S&P 500 fell about 0.4% to 0.5%, and the Nasdaq Composite lost around 0.3%. Exact closing index levels are not published in this edition because the sources checked at press time did not agree on them — one report gave an S&P close of 7,745.06, while delayed quote feeds read between 7,754 and 7,758. Per this briefing's standing rule, a level is only printed when the points change, percentage change and level corroborate across sources. Movers & Drivers

Up 15%New

Cerebras Systems (CBRS)

Shares surged roughly 15% after a Wedbush note tied the AI chip designer more tightly to OpenAI's newest inference tier. Cerebras is serving as the exclusive compute backbone for OpenAI's GPT-5.6 Sol "Ultrafast" mode — a tier delivering up to 750 output tokens per second, roughly 14 times faster than standard processing, running on the company's Wafer-Scale Engine — backed by a 750-megawatt capacity purchase commitment through 2028. Wedbush lifted its price target to $290 from $280. The rally follows a roughly 17% drop on August 13 on a second-quarter revenue miss.

Up 6.5%New

SK Hynix (SKHY)

Rose 6.5% and added roughly $83 billion in market value, leading a memory complex that has been on a remarkable run. Micron Technology climbed about 5.9% and moved back above $1,000 a share. The memory trade absorbed a Morgan Stanley note on hyperscaler capital spending as a bullish signal rather than a warning.

Down 2.4%New

Microsoft (MSFT)

The single biggest drag on the S&P 500, down 2.4%, following a high-profile Morgan Stanley note observing that hyperscalers are spending heavily upfront on AI data-center builds while the payoff may take years to materialise. Microsoft weighed on all three indexes more than any other stock through the morning session.

Down 3.3%New

Meta Platforms (META)

Fell 3.3% ahead of Tuesday's opening arguments in a federal trial in Oakland, where 29 state attorneys general allege the company designed Facebook and Instagram to get children addicted.

Up 0.9%

Nvidia (NVDA)

Gained 0.9% on news it will provide up to $105 billion in credit support for a large AI data-center campus being built for OpenAI in Pike County, Ohio, and invest $1.5 billion in project developer SB Energy. SoftBank-backed SB Energy will build, own and operate the campus with OpenAI as primary tenant under a 20-year lease; the first 4.25 gigawatts arrive in 2028.

Up 1.5%New

Intel (INTC)

Up about 1.5% after disclosure that chief executive Lip-Bu Tan purchased more than 105,000 shares last week at $95 apiece.

On the Radar

  • FOMC minutes, Wednesday. Minutes from the July 28–29 meeting are released by the Federal Reserve and could offer more detail on officials' thinking.
  • Retail earnings all week. Home Depot, Target, Lowe's and Walmart report. It matters more than usual: July retail sales fell for the first time in nine months and consumer sentiment dropped to 51.0.
  • Reddit joins the S&P 500. The stock enters the index before the open on Tuesday, August 18, replacing AvalonBay.
  • September rate-hike odds have collapsed. Traders now put the probability at roughly a third — about 31% by one read, roughly 33% by another — down from close to 50/50 a week ago.
  • Meta's Oakland trial opens Tuesday. Opening arguments begin in the federal case brought by 29 state attorneys general.
  • Bank of Japan. Sources familiar with BOJ policy thinking say a rate rise could come as early as the September 17–18 meeting, with a more aggressive path thereafter than the current pace of roughly two hikes a year.
  • The bull case, for balance. The S&P 500 sits within a whisper of last Thursday's record and is up 13.6% on the year, with second-quarter earnings tracking near 50% growth.
