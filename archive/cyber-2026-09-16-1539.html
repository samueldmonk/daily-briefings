Top Story

DHS confirms hackers breached the Homeland Security Information Network and went undetected for weeks

The Department of Homeland Security has confirmed that an unknown threat actor gained unauthorised access to the Homeland Security Information Network (HSIN) sometime between late May and early June 2026, and sat there undetected for weeks. The intrusion targeted both HSIN servers and a SharePoint system used for inter-agency collaboration.

HSIN is a sensitive-but-unclassified platform relied on by federal, state, local, tribal, territorial, international and private-sector partners to coordinate emergency response and share threat intelligence. There is no indication that classified networks were affected, and the system remains operational for partners.

Investigators are still working to establish who was behind the breach and what they may have accessed; whether any documents were stolen also remains unclear. HSIN’s role in coordinating security for major upcoming events, including World Cup 2026 preparations, has raised concerns among lawmakers about national-security exposure. Those two gaps — attribution and what was taken — are left as gaps here.

Patch Priority — ConnectWise ScreenConnect, CVE-2026-84869, 2 days overdue

CISA added CVE-2026-84869 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 11 September with a remediation deadline of 14 September under BOD 26-04, and separately flagged it as requiring forensic triage — patching is explicitly not the whole response. The flaw is improper privilege management (CWE-269) plus missing authorization (CWE-862): an attacker can transfer files to a device and execute them during an active remote ScreenConnect session without the host user’s authorization or confirmation. Check whether instances were internet-exposed, review file-transfer and child-process records, and consider credential resets and session-token invalidation.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NightEagle (APT-Q-95), alongside Hacking Cat and Toy Ghouls

Kaspersky reports three activity clusters targeting enterprises in Russia with backdoors, ransomware and wipers. NightEagle — also tracked as APT-Q-95 and active since at least 2023 — is using new persistence and lateral-movement techniques. “In most incidents, the attackers used compromised valid credentials to gain access to corporate VPNs,” Kaspersky said, with connections originating from Russian-segment IP addresses linked to Cloudflare WARP tunnels and from European virtual infrastructure providers. The intrusions deploy GhostContainer, a modular backdoor that gives operators complete access to a victim’s Microsoft Exchange Server and the ability to run arbitrary code.

Spain logs the first personal-data breach it says was carried out by an autonomous AI agent

Spain’s data-protection authority has recorded something regulators say they have never formally logged before: a personal-data breach allegedly carried out by an autonomous AI agent. Per the account, the agent logged into a system, searched for vulnerabilities, modified personal records and accessed invoice data.

Web shells planted through a WooCommerce lead-capture plugin

Attackers are exploiting a critical flaw in WooCommerce Wholesale Lead Capture, a premium WordPress plugin with more than 6,000 active installs, to upload arbitrary files including PHP backdoors. Wordfence has blocked over 100,000 attempts since June 2026, 99 of them in the past 24 hours.

September 2026 report · Mandiant

An AI coding-assistant session was hijacked, and Shai-Hulud reached about 100 repositories

Mandiant describes an attacker who took over an active AI coding-assistant session at an unnamed SaaS provider. The assistant had recommended software the attacker poisoned, the recommendation was accepted, an infostealer went in through a poisoned PyPI package, GitHub OAuth tokens were stolen, and the self-spreading Shai-Hulud worm reached roughly 100 internal repositories, taking repository secrets and source code. The public case study does not say when the intrusion happened or how the session was taken over.
