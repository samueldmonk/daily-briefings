Top Story

Shevchenko vacates the women’s flyweight title

Valentina Shevchenko has vacated the UFC women’s flyweight championship. The announcement was made on the UFC Paris broadcast, and the reason given is injury — reporting the 12:16 edition describes ligament damage to the shoulder and back, with an absence expected to run about a year. She had been booked to defend against Natália Silva on 3 October; instead Silva now meets Wang Cong for the vacant belt in the UFC 332 main event at the Delta Center, Salt Lake City. The promotion is reported to have said Shevchenko will receive a title shot on her return. The vacant-title fight itself gained detail the 12:45 edition. Natália Silva carries a 14-fight win streak into it, with wins over former champions Rose Namajunas, Alexa Grasso and Jessica Andrade in her last three outings; Wang Cong has won four straight in the Octagon, most recently beating Tracy Cortez at UFC 329 in July. The card is the UFC’s fifth visit to Salt Lake City and its first since UFC 307 in October 2024, and its main card is the first for a numbered event to air on CBS. Corroborated the 12:45 edition across UFC.com’s own announcement, CBS Sports, FIGHTMAG and the Wikipedia event page; none was fetched first-hand. One framing difference is noted rather than adopted: MMA Mania’s headline says Shevchenko was stripped of the title, while UFC.com, CBS Sports and Yahoo Sports describe her as having vacated it after telling the promotion she would be unable to compete for at least a year. This page follows the promotion’s own wording.

Corroboration and its limits. UFC.com’s own Noche UFC fight-by-fight preview, fetched directly in the 10:22 edition, refers twice to “the forthcoming bout for the vacant flyweight title at UFC 332” and to Silva facing “Wang Cong for the vacant title next month,” and separately says Shevchenko is “said to be out for the next year” — that is the promotion’s own site, and it is what this page follows. Search returns in the 11:17 edition add the date and the medical detail. One divergence is printed rather than resolved: two headlines (Yahoo Sports, Sherdog) describe UFC 332 as an interim title fight, while UFC.com and the body text of the other returns describe the belt as vacant and the bout as being for the undisputed championship. Because she vacated rather than being kept as an injured champion, “vacant” is what this desk carries; the interim wording is recorded here so a later run does not mistake it for a correction.

Why this is the lead, and what it cost. This board has gone nineteen consecutive editions without a champions regression, and the discipline that produced that streak nearly caused the opposite error today. The previous edition promoted the Shevchenko row from “carried” to fully verified on the strength of an ESPN-sourced list that supplied her date, method, opponent, event and defence count — every field correct, and the row wrong, because the list predated the vacancy. An ESPN-sourced return the 12:16 edition still names her champion. ★ The standing rule this adds: a belt can change hands without a card being contested. Every prior version of the reasoning on this page said some form of “no event has been held since Paris, so no belt could have moved” — true of fights, false of titles, and a title can change hands without a punch being thrown. Vacancies, strippings and retirements move belts on a press release.

Parnasse arrives: a first-round finish of Dan Hooker, on debut, in a main event, at home

Salahdine Parnasse made his UFC debut in the UFC Paris main event on Saturday 5 September at Accor Arena and stopped Dan Hooker inside a round. About halfway through the opening round Parnasse landed a body kick that, in UFC.com’s account, “got Hooker’s attention,” then followed with a flurry against the fence to send Hooker crashing to the canvas. Yahoo Sports’ account of the same sequence, sourced in the 9:48 edition, is more specific about the weapons: a clean kick to the liver and hard left hands to put Hooker out. The two descriptions agree — body shot first, hands to finish — and the UFC.com wording is kept as the primary because it is the promotion’s own. Also sourced in the 9:48 edition: Parnasse called out Max Holloway afterwards. UFC.com’s results page, read directly in the 10:22 edition, does not mention the callout; it reports instead that Losene Keita called out Lerone Murphy and that Daniil Donchenko asked for Daniel Rodriguez before year-end. No bout has been announced, and none is implied here. The 28-year-old has now won six straight, all by stoppage, and UFC.com says he “instantly enters the highly touted lightweight Top 15.” Post-fight records are sourced the 12:16 edition for the first time: Parnasse moves to 24–2, Hooker falls to 24–15 (Yahoo Sports / MMA Mania, sourced in an earlier edition; editions before that carried Parnasse’s pre-fight 23–2). A record that settled on Saturday night is a newly-sourced detail rather than a new development, so it is not tagged New.

Parnasse is a former two-time KSW featherweight champion and one-time KSW lightweight champion — UFC.com calls him “a two-division champion outside of the UFC” and, in its main-card report, “the former KSW two-division champion” — who signed with the promotion in late July 2026 having previously turned it down, and was handed a five-round main event on debut. He is not a Dana White’s Contender Series signee.
