The Lead

Closing Bell: yields at 2007 highs pull stocks lower — S&P 500 −0.77%, Nasdaq −0.92%, Dow −347; Boeing sinks nearly 7%

Official close, Monday Sept. 28: the S&P 500 slid 0.77% to 7,683.69, the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.92% to 26,820.38 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 347.11 points, or 0.67%, to 51,481.51 (CNBC). AP’s closing report had the same picture in round numbers — S&P 500 down 0.8%, Dow down 0.7%, Nasdaq down 0.9% — as yields in the U.S. bond market “again reached their highest levels in roughly two decades.” In AP’s closing report the 10-year Treasury yield had jumped to 5.23% and touched its highest level since 2007 (AP); CNBC put it up 6 basis points at 5.24%, with the 30-year at 5.56%, its highest since 2004. TheStreet’s wrap said more than 65% of U.S. issues fell. Brent crude, which briefly topped $101 in the morning, pared its gains as U.S. officials said mediators were still working with Iran and the United States on a deal, and settled at $97.83, up 0.4% (AP). A climb for Nvidia after its historic buyback was more than offset by losses elsewhere (AP). Sources: CNBC · AP closing report (4:11 PM) · TheStreet (4 PM wrap)

Among the day’s decliners, Boeing closed nearly 7% lower after the Federal Aviation Administration said it would not certify the 737 MAX 10 until it assesses a new software glitch (CNBC); TheStreet had it down 6.2% at a 2026 low at 2:56 PM ET. Around midday stocks had pared losses after CNN and Axios, citing White House officials, reported that Trump is open to sanctions relief for Iran on nuclear matters; CNBC’s midday update had the Dow down 188 points (0.4%). With about an hour of trading left, AP had MongoDB down 16.9%, Newmont down 4.1% as gold sank 3.5%, American Airlines down 2.1% and United down 1.6%, against a 2.1% rise for Nvidia. Fed Governor Lisa Cook, speaking at Oakland Tech Week, said “AI-driven investment” is one factor behind inflation (TheStreet, 3:21 PM).

How the session unfolded. Rising oil prices knocked the U.S. stock market further from its record high on Monday. As of 11:45 a.m. Eastern the S&P 500 was down 0.8%, giving back most of last week’s gains, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 372 points, or 0.7%, and the Nasdaq composite was 1% lower (AP). The slide deepened through the morning: at the 9:30 open TheStreet had the S&P 500 down 0.48%, the Dow down 0.71% and the Nasdaq down 0.55%, with the Russell 2000 up 0.07%, and at 10 a.m. AP had the S&P off 0.4% and the Dow down 274 points.

The pressure came from oil and bonds. The most actively traded Brent contract rose 2.8% to $100.19 a barrel by late morning, after climbing above $101 before the open (AP). President Trump on Saturday rejected Iran’s latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, and told Axios on Sunday he expects more talks this week: “They want to make a deal, but it is not the deal that I want to make” (CNN; TheStreet). On Monday a U.S. official told CNN that Trump is willing to give Iran sanctions relief and release frozen funds for “concrete progress” on a nuclear deal, but that there will be no deal unless nuclear issues are addressed. By late morning the 10-year Treasury yield had jumped to 5.26% from 5.17% late Friday — back to its 2007 level — and the 30-year yield leapt to 5.58% from 5.49%, back to where it was in 2004 (AP). AAA puts regular gasoline at nearly $4.48 a gallon, up from $3.13 a year ago (AP).

AI stocks were broadly weaker after OpenAI said it paused training of its most capable models until it is “confident” additional safeguards are in place (Schwab), a rare exception being Nvidia, which rose after adding a record $150 billion to its buyback. Airlines sank on fuel costs — American Airlines down 3.8% and United down 3.6% — and gold miners fell as gold dropped 3.9%, with Newmont down 4.4% (AP). Early Monday CME FedWatch put October rate-hike odds at 70% (Schwab). Abroad, European indexes were mixed while Seoul fell 2.7% and Shanghai 1.7% (AP).

Official closes are CNBC’s, corroborated by AP’s closing report; they are also in the Weekly Scorecard. Nvidia (NVDA)

Up 2.1% late in the session (AP) after adding a record $150 billion to its share repurchase authorization, lifting remaining capacity to $235 billion, which it expects to use through fiscal 2028. Nvidia also unveiled a security platform meant to stop AI agents from going rogue (AP; TheStreet).

Fell more than 2% early after OpenAI paused training of its most capable models pending additional safeguards; Meta Platforms fell 2.7%, extending Friday’s losses (Schwab).
