Top Story

CISA adds the MikroTik router flaws to KEV — a week after the vendor shipped the patch without saying what it fixed

CISA added two MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog today, 10 September: CVE-2026-67277, a missing-authentication flaw, and CVE-2026-86060, an improper-neutralisation-of-argument-delimiters flaw. Both belong to a set of six disclosed by CERT Polska on 5 September, two of which the Polish national CERT says are being chained to take full control of devices without authentication where SSH is reachable from the internet. It named the chain MikroTrick.

The sequence is the story. On 3 September MikroTik posted a short notice announcing RouterOS 7.25beta3, 7.24.2, 7.23.4 and 6.49.21. It named no CVE and described no vulnerability, saying plainly: “To give time to update your systems, we are not currently publishing detailed information.” It added that “most configurations are not at risk.” On 4 September researcher Nick Pratley published a binary diff of 7.23.3 against 7.23.4, reconstructed three of the fixes and released working Python proof-of-concept code. The embargo held for roughly twenty-four hours.

CERT Polska's advisories arrived the next day with hard indicators: successful attacks — including creation of a privileged account — from 82.192.72.4, occurring since at least 2 September, a day before the patch shipped, with 103.102.31.18 used in further attempts. Its stated indicator of compromise is the presence of a highly privileged user named “ops”, with the caution that absence of these traces does not rule out compromise.

All six records list the same affected ranges: RouterOS 6.0.0 to before 6.49.21, 7.0.0 to before 7.23.4, and 7.24 to before 7.24.2. Restricting SSH to a management network mitigates the takeover chain but does not address CVE-2026-67278, which sits in outbound X.509 certificate validation, or CVE-2026-67281, reachable through WebFig. One detail worth its own line: CERT Polska credits the discovery to its own team working with OpenAI models under a government access programme, with human verification still required — a national CERT finding six flaws in a decade-old router OS, and a one-day gap between silent fix and public working exploit.

Patch Priority

Due tomorrow — 1 day left

CVE-2026-86218 — N-able N-central, CVSS 10.0, static code injection leading to unauthenticated remote code execution. CISA added it on 8 September with a Federal Civilian Executive Branch remediation deadline of 11 September 2026. It affects all on-premises N-central builds prior to 2026.3.1.14, spanning the 2025.4, 2026.1, 2026.2 and 2026.3 release lines; N-able shipped Hotfix 4 for N-central 2026.3 on 5–6 September. N-able initially said it had no confirmation of exploitation in production; CISA's listing and independent research from Huntress indicate otherwise, with at least one customer instance reportedly compromised on 4 September, two days before the patch shipped.

This is remote monitoring and management software — the tool that manages other people's infrastructure — which is why it outranks everything else on the board today. CVE-2026-75650 (Adobe Commerce and Magento, CVSS 10.0) carries the same 11 September deadline from the same batch.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ExtortionSupply chain

ShinyHunters, via a Metabase zero-day at a logistics provider

The Trezor customer data now in circulation was not taken from Trezor. It came out of ShipMonk, its third-party logistics provider, through exploitation of a critical zero-day SQL injection in the Metabase analytics platform — CVE-2026-72898, CVSS 10.0 — with the attack attributed to the ShinyHunters extortion group.

The pattern defenders should take from it: an analytics console sitting inside a fulfilment vendor became the route to a hardware-wallet maker's customer list, years after the orders themselves were placed. The exposed records date from November 2019 through August 2021 — data Trezor had understood to be deleted.

Breaches & Incidents

New67,000

Trezor: the ShipMonk breach is far larger than first disclosed

Trezor disclosed on 4 September that the breach at ShipMonk exposed order data for approximately 67,000 additional U.S. customers, on top of the 13,689 disclosed in August — roughly 80,700 in total. Exposed fields include names, email addresses, phone numbers, shipping addresses and order numbers. No wallet seeds, private keys or funds were exposed, and Trezor's own systems were not compromised. Trezor was first notified on 10 August, disclosed publicly on 13 August, and was told on 2 September that the incident was significantly larger.

NewIdentity

IDScan confirms unauthorised cloud access after 153 million ID scans were advertised

Identity-verification vendor IDScan confirmed that an unauthorised third party may have accessed and copied customer information stored in customer accounts on the IDScan.net cloud, including full names and driver's licence or other government-issued ID numbers. The company disclosed it in a 4 September notice, saying it learned on or around 1 September that data may have been accessed. The trigger was a 1 September report that a dark-web platform called Nexus was advertising more than 153 million U.S. and Canadian driver's licence scans, alongside claims of 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents and 579,000 medical cards. Nine class actions were filed between 2 and 4 September.

IDScan's own notice names no figure. Every count in circulation — 153 million, and a higher 170 million in one outlet's headline — originates with the marketplace selling the data, not with the company, and this page treats them as claims rather than confirmed totals.

Carried966 fixes

Microsoft's record Patch Tuesday

Microsoft's September release fixes 966 flaws, including two actively exploited zero-days and 105 rated Critical — 81 of them remote code execution, 20 elevation of privilege, two information disclosure and one security-feature bypass. Among the critical entries: CVE-2026-69676, a Windows Kerberos RCE at CVSS 8.8 via authentication bypass through capture-replay, and CVE-2026-69845 and CVE-2026-72979, Windows DHCP Server RCE flaws at CVSS 9.8 exploitable without authentication.

Counts differ across outlets — this page uses BleepingComputer's 966; other reads this week gave 974, and a separate figure of 204 fixed earlier in the month explains part of the spread.

CarriedRansomware

WatchGuard Firebox: ransomware gangs move in

CISA confirms ransomware operators are now exploiting CVE-2025-14733, the out-of-bounds-write unauthenticated RCE in WatchGuard Firebox flagged in December. Affected: Fireware OS 11.x (including 11.12.4_Update1), 12.x (including 12.11.5) and 2025.1–2025.1.3. Exploitation requires IKEv2 VPN configured — but a device can remain compromisable after the configuration is deleted if a branch-office VPN to a static gateway peer remains. Shadowserver counted more than 115,000 exposed in December and roughly 9,000 still unpatched nine months on. No CVSS appears in any read; none is printed.

CISA's alert page states the addition and cites BOD 26-04; no due date for either record appeared in the reads available at the time of this edition, so no countdown is asserted for these two. Patch anyway: RouterOS 6.49.21 (long-term), 7.23.4 (long-term), 7.24.2 (stable) or 7.25beta3 carry all six fixes.

The old BOD 22-01 fixed-window rule — 21 days from listing — does not apply to 2026 additions. CISA now assigns deadlines per CVE under BOD 26-04, which prioritises remediation by risk with short tiers. The 8 September batch carries two different due dates: three days from listing for two entries and fourteen for the other two. Every countdown on this page is derived from a CISA-stated due date, never from a fixed window.
