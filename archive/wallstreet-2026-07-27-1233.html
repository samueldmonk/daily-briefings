The Lead

Midday, ~12:30 PM ET: an oil-driven rally hands the tape back to the chip sellers as Fed week opens

As of the 12:30 PM ET check, CNBC's live blog had the S&P 500 down 0.2% and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.4%, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average held a gain of 107 points, or 0.2% — the broad index falling into the red as a plunge in chip stocks negated the earlier rally sparked by a decline in global oil prices (CNBC). It is a round trip from the open: 24/7 Wall St., publishing at 10:21 AM ET, had stocks opening higher with the Dow up 452 points, or 0.9%, and the S&P 500 up 0.4%; its early-morning tally read Dow +0.80%, Nasdaq Composite +0.20% and S&P 500 +0.26%.

Energy did the early lifting. Brent crude for September delivery slid 6.4% to roughly $90.60 a barrel and WTI dropped 5.7% to about $84.10 in early trade (24/7 Wall St.), after the United States and Iran halted strikes against each other over the weekend amid efforts to revive diplomacy — the U.S. paused its attacks late Friday without an official announcement, and Tehran said it had stopped its retaliatory strikes and opened talks with Oman over the Strait of Hormuz (CNBC/TradingEconomics). Futures had pointed the same way before the bell: Yahoo Finance had Dow and S&P 500 contracts up 0.8% and Nasdaq-100 futures up 1.4% at 4:04 AM ET.

Then the semis turned. Chipmakers rallied early on Chinese memory maker CXMT's blockbuster Shanghai debut — shares soared more than fivefold, instantly making it mainland China's most valuable company — before reversing on reports that a state-backed Chinese firm has begun mass production of domestic DUV lithography machines, pressuring ASML, Intel, Nvidia and Micron (24/7 Wall St./CNBC). AMD and Teradyne dropped 7% and 5.9% respectively to lead the declines, Micron shed 4%, and the VanEck Semiconductor ETF traded more than 3% lower (CNBC). It is the busiest week of the quarter: the FOMC decides Wednesday at 2 PM ET, with Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon all reporting (Yahoo Finance).

On the Radar

  • FOMC, Tue–Wed Jul 28–29: the decision lands Wednesday at 2 PM ET. A hold at 3.50–3.75% is still broadly expected, but this is one of the least-telegraphed Fed meetings in years and a hike is "not off the table" (Yahoo Finance). Bloomberg reported Sunday that bond traders entered the week seeing a better-than-one-in-three chance of a hike Wednesday after Middle East tensions lifted oil and yields; per Bloomberg, CME FedWatch had roughly 82% odds of a hike at the September meeting, up from below 53% a week earlier. Caveat: those readings predate Monday's ~6% oil slide, which pushes the inflation trade the other way — and CNBC's Jul 24 week-ahead put July hike odds below 17%. Treat the pricing as a moving target.
  • Waller's framing: Fed Governor Christopher Waller has said the balance of risks has tilted "more toward high inflation than labor market weakness" — a reversal from the Fed's posture a year ago (Bloomberg).
  • Megacap earnings gauntlet: Microsoft, Meta, Apple and Amazon all report this week, with capex guidance and AI monetization the focus after Alphabet's and Tesla's capex "caused a meltdown in the tech sector." AstraZeneca and Nucor open the week's calendar, which also features SK Hynix, Coca-Cola, Starbucks, Procter & Gamble and Arm Holdings, and closes with ExxonMobil and Chevron (Yahoo Finance).
  • China's chip push is now a market factor: CXMT's Shanghai IPO soared more than fivefold to make it mainland China's most valuable company, and a state-backed firm has reportedly begun mass-producing domestic DUV lithography machines — the trigger for Monday's reversal in ASML, Intel, Nvidia and Micron (24/7 Wall St.).
  • Credit is the tell to watch: HYG down nearly 1% in July and on pace for a third straight down month; Wolfe Research says "the bond market looks worried" even as equities rotate near highs (24/7 Wall St.).
  • Oil diplomacy watch: the pause is informal — no formal peace agreement, and Houthi threats in the Red Sea persist — so the reprieve in Brent and WTI remains fragile (TradingEconomics/CNBC).
