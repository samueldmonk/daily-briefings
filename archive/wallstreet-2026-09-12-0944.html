The Lead

Markets are closed — Wall Street ended a losing week on a high note

There is no session today. The next opening bell is Monday, 14 September, and the figures below are Friday’s official closes.

Friday was a rebound after four straight days of declines: the S&P 500 rose 0.86%, the Nasdaq Composite 0.96% and the Dow 0.98%, the last of those worth 509.19 points (Investrade’s recap gives 508.71; both reconcile to the same percentage and both are printed rather than picked). Oil retreating and an August CPI print close to expectations did the work.

The week itself was another story. All three averages fell over the five sessions — the S&P 500 −0.8%, the Dow −1.6% and the Nasdaq about −0.7%, the Nasdaq’s first negative week in three. The day and the week point in opposite directions, and it was a losing week.

On the inflation print itself: headline CPI came in at +0.4% month-on-month and 3.4% year-on-year, roughly in line, but core ran +0.3% against a +0.2% estimate, with core annual at 2.4%. That tenth is why rate-hike bets jumped rather than settled — as of 11 September, CME FedWatch implied roughly a 90% probability of a hike at Wednesday’s FOMC decision, up from about 70% before the print.

Movers & Drivers

NASDAQ:ACVAM&A

ACV Auctions — the session’s biggest move

Copart agreed to buy ACV Auctions for $10.50 a share in cash, equity valued at roughly $1.9 billion, with unanimous approval from both boards and completion targeted for the end of 2026 subject to customary conditions including antitrust review. Reads of the session move span +40% to +45%; 44.8% is the regular-session figure carried here, with the spread disclosed.

NYSE:DELLAI capex

Dell closes at a record

Oracle reiterated a fiscal-2027 capital-expenditure plan of $90–95 billion for AI data centres and named Dell and HPE as principal recipients. Dell finished at a record, with reads spanning “almost 11%” (CNBC, Seeking Alpha) to +11.98%, and a market capitalisation above $360 billion. RBC initiating coverage at Outperform helped.

NYSE:HPE

HPE rides the same supply-chain story

Up roughly 11% on the Oracle capex read-through, with one report putting the close at $61.49.

NYSE:ORCL

Oracle supplies the catalyst, keeps none of it

The quarter underneath it beat: adjusted $1.92 a share on revenue of $19.35 billion in the fiscal first quarter, against the $1.74 and $19.14 billion analysts polled by LSEG had sought, on cloud and AI-infrastructure growth. The Friday move is where reads diverge. One set has the company that moved its suppliers rising as much as 10.3% intraday and finishing about +0.3%; a movers roundup read this run instead puts it up 6%. Both are printed rather than reconciled, because no source read this run states an official closing change. On Friday the named beneficiaries of the spending plan kept the move; the company doing the spending did not.

NASDAQ:CSCODow leader

Cisco leads the Dow

Reads give +4.1% — the biggest percentage gainer in the Dow — and +3.43%; both are printed rather than reconciled. Sourced reasons: AI-infrastructure demand and a quantum-networking partnership.

Adobe, the other side of the earnings tape

Not every reporter was rewarded. Adobe (NASDAQ:ADBE) slid 2% after issuing current-quarter guidance that a movers roundup read this run describes as roughly in line with estimates — the same session in which Oracle’s beat carried its suppliers to records. Also named among the day’s biggest movers, without figures attached in the read: KR, CPRT, EXEL, ZUMZ and CHWY.

Sector Heat — live

On Friday nine of eleven S&P 500 sectors finished higher, with technology, industrials and communication services each up more than 1% and health care and utilities lagging; an earlier intraday board had ten of eleven with communication services +1.9%, and the session review is carried here as the later measurement. Breadth backed the move: advancing issues beat decliners inside the index by roughly 2.1 to 1, and the Russell 2000 added 0.45% — a narrower gain than the large-cap averages. Across the full week, energy and communication services were up while industrials, materials and real estate lagged.

On the Radar

  • Wednesday 16 September — the FOMC decision, with economic projections, closing a two-day meeting that begins Tuesday. This is the week’s event; everything else is positioning around it.
  • Tuesday brings the Empire State manufacturing index and ADP weekly employment change.
  • Wednesday also brings August retail sales, the import and export price indexes, July business inventories and the NAHB housing market index.
  • Earnings are thin. Second-quarter reporting has largely wrapped, with Lennar expected during the week. Attention has shifted from backward-looking results to the growth, inflation and policy outlook.
  • Oil is the swing factor. Both benchmarks gained more than 8% on the week on Middle East supply risk, then gave some back Friday on news of Iran–Gulf talks in Oman. Energy prices are feeding directly into the inflation print the Fed is now responding to.
