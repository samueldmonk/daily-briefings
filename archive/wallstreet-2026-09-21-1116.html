The Lead

Stocks start the week higher as oil retreats and diplomacy dominates the calendar — index figures as of the 9:31 AM ET open

The U.S. market opened Monday with the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 0.61%, the S&P 500 up 0.67% and the Nasdaq up 0.77%, while the Russell 2000 slipped 0.50% — TheStreet’s opening-bell entry, timestamped 9:31 AM ET. No index reading later than that open appeared in anything read this run. TheStreet’s live blog was last updated at 10:28 AM ET, and that update carried a market-movers block rather than a fresh index print. The page says so rather than implying the open is live.

The driver is oil. Crude fell as diplomatic efforts around the U.S.–Iran conflict gathered pace and Saudi supply recovered, easing the inflation anxiety that has dogged the month. “Markets are starting the week on a more constructive footing, with falling oil prices helping revive risk appetite after several weeks dominated by inflation and interest-rate concerns,” said Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com, who called oil “the biggest source of relief.”

Sourced this run and attributed: the PHLX Semiconductor Index rose more than 2% to its highest price since 9 September; QQQ, tracking the Nasdaq-100, was the morning’s standout at +1.5%; the advance erased the S&P 500’s decline for the month (no monthly percentage was stated, so none is printed); Treasury 10-year yields fell below 5% (no level stated, so the H.15 17-September row below stands); and Brent extended its drop into a fourth straight session, which would be its longest losing streak since June. On sectors, the account read this run says most sectors were in the green, led by technology and financial stocks, while oil and gas shares retreated.

On the Radar

  • The Trump–Xi summit is Thursday, 24 September, in Washington. Talks are expected to cover tariffs, critical minerals and artificial intelligence. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent met Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng for hours ahead of the visit and proposed a US–China AI safety notification mechanism; Xinhua acknowledged only that AI was discussed. The South China Morning Post reports the two sides are discussing possible announcements to strengthen military-to-military ties.
  • AI chief executives are attending a dinner with Xi — Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, OpenAI’s Sam Altman, former Apple CEO Tim Cook, Qualcomm’s Cristiano Amon and Microsoft’s Satya Nadella. The summit lands as an essay by Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei has reopened the debate over whether frontier labs should pace model development. Bessent told CNBC that AI developers “need to take responsibility for themselves” rather than expect a federal liability shield: “It is humans who are responsible, not the AI.”
  • Oura is going public. The Finnish smart-ring maker and some backers are seeking to raise as much as $2.2 billion, marketing 50 million shares at $40–$44, valuing the company at up to $14.1 billion at the top of the range against about $11 billion in its 2025 Series E. Oura will sell 13.5 million shares and selling shareholders 36.5 million; the ticker is OURA. It sold about 3.6 million rings in the past year at $399–$499. Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, JPMorgan, Allen & Company and Jefferies are leading.
  • The Paramount–Warner settlement has holdouts. California AG Rob Bonta, who leads the 12-state coalition, has been pressing to strike a deal; New York AG Letitia James is resisting the terms and seeking additional protections for workers, and Connecticut plus at least two other states have reservations (CNN).
  • The oil supply story behind the move. Saudi Arabia expects to restore about half of its East-West pipeline capacity within days; its crude exports have surged past 4 million barrels a day this month, up from a 13-year low of 2.4 million in August. Shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have climbed to a six-month high, and JPMorgan estimates total Gulf oil flows averaged 17.1 million barrels a day over the past 10 days.
  • Today’s calendar is thin. Economic data: Chicago Fed National Activity Index, August (−0.06 expected, −0.08 previously). Yahoo’s Monday entry reads “no notable earnings”; a Kiplinger week-ahead count carried from 20 September said 14. Both are printed; neither is reconciled.
  • Iran and the UN General Assembly. President Masoud Pezeshkian leads an Iranian delegation to the UNGA in New York on Tuesday amid renewed hope of a diplomatic track. Iranian state agency IRNA says the U.S. has refused visas for some delegation members. Separately, Trump told Fox News correspondent Trey Yingst the question was “if and when” it would be necessary to “blow the entire nation up” should Tehran not meet his demands.
  • The White House press pool has fractured. After CNN was barred from serving as the designated TV pooler on Monday, the four other members — NBC, ABC, CBS and Fox News — will stop covering the President’s events. CNN, MS NOW and Politico are suing the administration to overturn the access ban.
