Top Story

New this edition

Four spy groups, one exploit chain: “BlueMoon” turns two Chrome zero-days and a Windows flaw into a shared weapon

Proofpoint has documented a previously undocumented exploit kit, BlueMoon, that chains CVE-2026-85046 (a type-confusion bug in Chrome’s V8 JavaScript engine), CVE-2026-87491 (a V8 sandbox escape) and CVE-2026-85880 (a heap-based buffer overflow in Windows ALPC) to go from a clicked link to elevated privileges on a fully updated machine. The China-linked actor TA412, also tracked as APT31, was first seen using it on 28 August 2026 against NGOs and mining and commodity traders. Within days, at least three further espionage clusters had adopted the same chain: UNK_LateNight against US aerospace, UNK_DoubleCheck against Vietnamese manufacturing, and UNK_QuietRacket against government and finance targets in Indonesia and Singapore.

Google shipped fixes for the two Chrome flaws in the Stable channel on 3 and 8 September; the first was already under active exploitation when the update went out. The Windows link in the chain was patched in the September Patch Tuesday release described below. The speed of adoption — four unrelated state-aligned groups on one chain inside a week — is the part defenders should read twice: it implies a shared supplier rather than four independent discoveries.

What this resolves

Two open questions on this site close with it. (1) CVE-2026-87491 is real and is the V8 sandbox escape. A previous edition dropped it as unsupported after it failed to appear in an alert enumeration; Proofpoint’s chain names it explicitly, so it is restored here. (2) The naming split on CVE-2026-85880 was never a contradiction. This site had been carrying “heap-based buffer overflow” and “Windows ALPC” as two incompatible descriptions of the same CVE and printing both without merging. They are the same flaw — this run’s BlueMoon coverage describes it as a heap-based buffer overflow in Windows ALPC. Likewise CVE-2026-81963 is the Windows Update Stack flaw and the “link following” one — Tenable’s advisory reading states both.

Patch Priority

Deadline today — 0 days left

MikroTik RouterOS — CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060. Both were added to CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 10 September 2026 with a federal remediation date of 13 September 2026 — today. CVE-2026-67277 is a missing-authentication flaw on a critical function; CVE-2026-86060 is improper neutralisation of argument delimiters, and CISA flagged it as requiring mandatory forensic triage under BOD 26-04 rather than a patch alone. If you run RouterOS at a network edge, this is the item that cannot wait for Monday.

Breaches & Incidents

Newsupply chain

Trezor — 347,000 addresses phished through its email vendor

The hardware-wallet maker’s newsletter provider Brevo was breached on 9 September, an incident that touched 120 Brevo accounts. The attacker used that access to send mail that genuinely originated from Trezor’s own campaigns, with the subject line “Critical Security Alert: STM32 Entropy Vulnerability”, steering readers to a site built to harvest wallet backup seeds. 347,000 addresses were targeted and 2,500 people clicked before Trezor killed the domain within 20 minutes. Only the newsletter database was exposed; no Trezor system was compromised.

Newtakedown

DoJ moves against the Xinbi Guarantee marketplace

On 9 September 2026 the US Department of Justice announced coordinated action against the Xinbi Guarantee scam marketplace, seizing its Telegram channels and confiscating cryptocurrency wallets. A takedown rather than a breach — included because guarantee marketplaces are the settlement layer that makes large-scale fraud operations bankable.

How these deadlines are set

BOD 22-01 is revoked. CISA replaced it on 10 June 2026 with BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk”, which drops the old flat three-week window for a 16-tier matrix keyed to whether the asset is publicly exposed, whether the CVE is in KEV, and whether exploitation can be fully automated. The worst tier must be remediated within three days, with forensic triage; the mildest can wait for the next upgrade cycle. Agencies must be operating to these timelines by 7 December 2026. Two consequences: a same-week deadline is now normal and should not be “corrected” to three weeks, and CVEs in the same bulletin can carry different dates — take the stated date per CVE, never derive it. A corollary: KEV entry requires evidence of exploitation, so a severe flaw nobody is exploiting carries no federal deadline at all. Absence from KEV is not a severity judgement.

Provenance, stated plainly: cisa.gov returned empty on direct fetch for a seventh consecutive run, so the KEV catalog itself was not read this edition.
