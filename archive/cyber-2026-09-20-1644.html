Top Story

ShinyHunters rooted Clop’s leak site through its own CMS — and now wants to extort the extortionists

On Friday 19 September, the extortion crew ShinyHunters compromised and defaced the Clop ransomware gang’s dark-web data-leak site, exploiting an unauthenticated file-upload vulnerability in Grav CMS — the flat-file content management system Clop was running its public site on.

The first sign was a single text file dropped onto the site reading “THIS SITE HAS BEEN PWN3D BY SHINYHUNTERES #Skids10p — Maybe don’t try to threaten us next time”, with a link back to ShinyHunters’ own leak site. Hours later the site was gone entirely, replaced by ASCII art of Umbreon — the Pokémon ShinyHunters uses as a logo — above the line “rooting your systems since ’19 ;)”.

ShinyHunters claims it took the leak-site source code and Grav plugins, system logs from /var/log, and the private cryptographic keys for Clop’s Tor onion service. It has set a 72-hour deadline and stated it intends to extort Clop. Reported motive: a dispute over Clop’s 2025 Oracle E-Business Suite campaign, in which a Clop representative allegedly threatened violence and doxxing.

Caveat, stated plainly: the defacement itself is confirmed by multiple outlets, but the Tor private-key theft is ShinyHunters’ claim, not a verified fact — at least one write-up read this run is headlined “Tor Key Theft Unproven.” Treat the data-theft scope as alleged.

Patch Priority

CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco Identity Services Engine / ISE-PIC · CVSS 10.0 · federal deadline was 19 September (1 day overdue)

Insufficient authentication control on an API endpoint lets an unauthenticated, remote attacker bypass the web-based management interface and reach root-level command execution. Cisco disclosed the flaw and shipped fixes on 16 September, at which point active exploitation was already known — the bug surfaced while support engineers worked a customer TAC case, meaning at least one enterprise was breached before anyone knew the flaw existed. CISA added it to the KEV catalog the same day with a 19 September remediation date for federal civilian agencies, which has now passed.

Fixed releases: ISE 3.1 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.4 Patch 7, 3.5 Patch 4. There is no complete workaround. Until patching finishes, restrict management and control-plane access with infrastructure ACLs.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Ransomware-as-a-serviceActive

Qilin

On 17 September Qilin publicly claimed a cyberattack on Techwise (techwise.com.ar), an Argentine business-services provider, posting an extortion notice threatening to leak data absent negotiation. Reporting links recent Qilin deployments to exploitation of CVE-2026-50751, a critical Check Point VPN authentication-bypass flaw — part of a broader pattern of affiliates entering through vulnerable edge infrastructure. Qilin held the top position by volume across Q2 2026 even as its monthly output fell by roughly a third and softening payment rates squeezed affiliate economics. Victims have spanned healthcare, multinationals and government.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco Identity Services Engine. Added 16 September, federal remediation due 19 September 2026. (1 day overdue)
  • CVE-2026-87886 — Acronis Backup. Added 16 September. No remediation date appeared in anything read this run, so none is printed.
  • CVE-2026-76461 — Cisco Secure Email Gateway SQL injection. Added 14 September, due 17 September 2026. (3 days overdue)
  • CVE-2025-39682, CVE-2026-53266, CVE-2025-39964 — the Linux kernel trio. Added 18 September, due 21 September 2026. (1 day left) CISA additionally flagged all three as requiring forensic triage: agencies must investigate exposed assets for evidence of compromise, not merely patch. Guidance is to install vendor kernel updates, reboot into the corrected kernel and verify the running version; where no patch exists, apply vendor mitigations, follow cloud-specific BOD 26-04 guidance, or discontinue the unsupported product.
  • 11 September: three additions — CVE-2026-42016 and CVE-2026-42018 (JFrog Artifactory) and CVE-2026-84869 (ConnectWise ScreenConnect).
  • 8 September: four additions — CVE-2026-75650 (Adobe Commerce / Magento), CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880 (Microsoft Windows), CVE-2026-86218 (N-able N-central).
  • 2 September: seven additions — CVE-2026-9586, CVE-2026-48710, CVE-2026-49869, CVE-2026-59822, CVE-2026-82329, CVE-2026-83548, CVE-2026-83549.
A directive change worth naming. The three Linux kernel additions are issued under BOD 26-04, not the older BOD 22-01, and carry a three-day remediation window rather than three weeks. The recent Cisco windows fit the same pattern — added 14 September, due the 17th; added 16 September, due the 19th. Short federal deadlines are now normal for 2026 additions, and this desk publishes them as sourced rather than assuming a three-week rule. BOD 26-04 requires covered agencies to accelerate remediation based on operational risk. Every countdown above is computed from today’s date against the deadline stated in the cited source.
