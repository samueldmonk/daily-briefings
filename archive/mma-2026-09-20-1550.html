Top Story

Gane gets the belt after all — and a November date at Madison Square Garden

The UFC announced during the UFC 331 main-card broadcast on Saturday 19 September that Ciryl Gane has been promoted to undisputed heavyweight champion, and that he will defend against Josh Hokit in the headliner of UFC 334 on 14 November at Madison Square Garden.

The reversal is the story. The announcement arrived less than 24 hours after UFC CEO Dana White told Sportsnet on Friday that Gane would not be elevated without fighting again. Tom Aspinall announced on Monday 14 September that he was relinquishing the title because of ongoing complications from the eye injuries he suffered against Gane in October 2025 — a title fight that ended in a no contest after Gane poked Aspinall in both eyes in the opening round. Aspinall suffered a further setback to his right eye while preparing for a 14 November rematch with Gane; sources told Uncrowned the injury is back to “the same as day one.” He remains sidelined with no timetable.

Gane had taken the interim title in Aspinall's absence in June, defeating Alex Pereira at UFC Freedom 250. Hokit had publicly called for the shot after Aspinall vacated. The UFC also announced that the long-awaited superfight between women's bantamweight champion Kayla Harrison and Hall of Famer Amanda Nunes has been rebooked as the UFC 334 co-main event.

Bonuses

Over $500,000 paid out

Fight of the Night: Joshua Van and Alexandre Pantoja, $100,000 each. Performance of the Night: Arman Tsarukyan ($100,000) and Casey O'Neill ($100,000). Sean Sharaf took a Proper bonus plus an additional special bonus from Dana White, $100,000 in total. $25,000 finish bonuses went to Patricio Pitbull, Marlon Vera, Ryan Gandra and Joanderson Brito.

One claim is attributed rather than asserted: the Forbes account calls Sharaf's knockout of Steveson “the biggest upset in UFC history” and “the third-fastest KO in UFC heavyweight history.” Nothing else read this run corroborates either superlative, so both are carried as that outlet's characterisation, not as fact.

Around the Sport

  • Tsarukyan's case just got loud. Two elbows put Mauricio Ruffy out inside a round in a five-round co-main, with a lightweight title shot widely seen as the stake. Reporting notes competing talk of Justin Gaethje's first defence being a rematch with Ilia Topuria instead.
  • Aspinall is still nowhere near a return. “I don't [know when he's going to be back],” White told Sportsnet on Friday. “Whenever his eyes are healed, then we're ready for him.”
  • A women's flyweight belt is genuinely open. Shevchenko's vacating turned the UFC 332 main event into an undisputed title fight between Natália Silva and Wang Cong on 3 October.
  • Casey O'Neill is back. The women's flyweight returned from injury with a first-round armbar over Eduarda Moura on the UFC 331 early prelims and took a $100,000 Performance of the Night bonus for it.
