The Lead

At the close: Dow +1.08%, S&P 500 +0.33%, Nasdaq +0.08% — stocks muscle higher as yields sink and money rotates out of AI

Official close (AP; TradingKey). The S&P 500 rose 25.12 points to 7,744.06 (+0.33%) after erasing an early-morning loss, pulling closer to the all-time high it set last week; the Dow Jones Industrial Average jumped 556.21 to 52,177.69 (+1.08%) and the Nasdaq Composite added 20.16 to 26,713.03 (+0.08%) (AP; TradingKey). AP said companies delivering bigger profits led the way even as AI stocks kept swinging: Nvidia fell 1.8% and was the heaviest weight on the S&P 500 after more than halving an early loss that had reached 3.8%, and TSMC’s U.S.-traded shares finished down 1.8% after dropping as much as 5.3% in the morning. The 10-year Treasury yield sank to 5.09% from 5.19% late Wednesday (AP). TradingKey said defensive consumer and health-care stocks led, and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell 0.88% to 11,581.22, with 19 of its 30 members declining (TradingKey).

Abroad. South Korea’s Kospi fell 7.6% and Japan’s Nikkei 225 4.9% in the AI sell-off that began on Wall Street Wednesday; the Kospi has dropped 39% since its June record, while London’s FTSE 100 rallied 1.1% (AP).

Last read before the bell (Yahoo Finance Dow quote, “As of 3:52:08 PM EDT”; quote strip “U.S. markets close in 8m”). The Dow was up 571.97 points (+1.11%) at 52,193.45, inside a day’s range of 51,681.37–52,250.18; the S&P 500 was up 23.98 (+0.31%) and the Nasdaq Composite had turned positive, up 14.08 (+0.05%) after trading as much as 1.3% lower at the open. The Russell 2000 rose 1.35%, the 10-year yield was 5.09% (−0.10), the VIX eased 2.37% to 16.47, Nov. WTI crude was up 2.57% at $96.31 and gold up 1.94% at $4,187.60 (Yahoo Finance). These were pre-close readings; see the official close above.

Earlier final-hour read (Yahoo Finance quote strip, “U.S. markets close in 57m”; Nasdaq quote “As of 3:04:11 PM EDT”). The Dow was up 530.62 points (+1.03%), the S&P 500 up 19.26 (+0.25%) and the Nasdaq Composite down 22.13 (−0.08%), within a Nasdaq day range of 26,308.85–26,697.96; the Russell 2000 rose 0.83%, the 10-year yield was 5.08% and the VIX eased 1.18% to 16.67 (Yahoo Finance). Yahoo’s market sidebars showed Nike up 6.32%, CoreWeave up 4.19%, ManpowerGroup down 16.53% and Rocket Lab down 8.11% at the time of the read (Yahoo Finance).

Treasury curve at 3:19 PM ET (TheStreet). TheStreet said yields “finally dip in reprieve for equities”: the 10-year Treasury was at 5.085%, down 10.5 basis points, the 30-year at 5.426% (−9.8 bp), the 7-year at 4.983% (−10.7 bp) and the 2-year at 4.749% (−5.3 bp), while the front end was little changed — the biggest moves came in the 7- and 10-year maturities (TheStreet).

2 PM read (Yahoo Finance quote page, “As of 2:01:11 PM EDT”). The S&P 500 was up 18.69 points (+0.24%), off a day’s range of 7,669.66–7,745.15; the Dow was up 0.99% (+512.46) and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.20% (−52.46), while the Russell 2000 rose 0.90% and the VIX eased 1.78% to 16.57 (Yahoo Finance). The Motley Fool’s midday report said the chip sell-off was driving a rotation into safer assets that boosted the Dow, with health care the top sector and energy and technology falling; Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang told Bloomberg he is optimistic and that CoreWeave’s cost of capital is decreasing (Motley Fool).

Yields drive the rotation. Yahoo’s live blog said the 10- and 30-year Treasury yields were each falling more than 10 basis points, on track for their biggest daily declines since February, lifting homebuilders and mortgage lender Rocket Companies (Yahoo Finance). TheStreet’s 1:47 PM ET check had the S&P 500 up 0.30% and the Dow up 1.04%, with tech and energy declining “while everything else looks fine” (TheStreet).

Earlier. At 12:57 PM ET the Dow was up 0.81%, the S&P 500 up 0.03% and the Nasdaq down 0.43% (Yahoo Finance); at the ~11:56 AM read the Dow had been up 0.86%, the S&P 500 up 0.07% and the Nasdaq down 0.38%; TheStreet’s 12:54 PM ET note said the market was “in better shape midway through today than it has been throughout this tumultuous week” (TheStreet). Yahoo’s live blog said the S&P 500 had erased its losses and the Nasdaq had pared a decline of more than 1% earlier in the session, as a two-day tech sell-off sparked a flight to Treasuries that sent the 10-year yield down as much as 14 basis points to 5.05% (Yahoo Finance). TheStreet’s 11:32 AM ET check-in had the Dow up about 1%, with 22 of its 30 members advancing on strength in consumer, health-care and financial stocks (TheStreet).

The open (9:33 AM ET). Stocks opened mixed: the S&P 500 slipped 0.49%, the Dow edged up 0.43% and the Nasdaq fell 1.34%, per TheStreet (TheStreet). Yahoo said the Nasdaq 100 and S&P 500 were coming off their worst sessions since July (Yahoo Finance).

Inflation pressure, part two. The September producer price index rose 1.3%, up from 0.5% in August and the largest increase since March 2022 (Yahoo Finance); TheStreet’s headline put wholesale prices up 6.3% from a year earlier. Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn said Wednesday’s CPI “all but confirms” another Fed hike, possibly at the meeting in two weeks, which she said markets now see as a coin flip (TheStreet).

Where the pressure is. The chip rout went global overnight: South Korea’s Kospi ended 8% lower, with Samsung Electronics down 9% and SK Hynix 11%; Japan’s Nikkei 225 fell 5% and Taiwan’s Taiex 3% (Business Insider via TheStreet). Yahoo said the PHLX Semiconductor Index was on track for an eighth straight down day — the longest such streak since October 2008 — and that worries over financing costs for debt-heavy AI names such as CoreWeave are weighing on the trade (Yahoo Finance). TheStreet’s 12:13 PM ET midday-losers list was headed by tickers MAN, RKLB, PENG, DELL and MTSI, with AI-exposed trades still “taking on water” (TheStreet).

TSMC: record profit, lower stock. Taiwan Semiconductor posted a record net profit for a sixth straight quarter, up 68.7% from a year earlier (CNBC), and guided fourth-quarter revenue to $52.3 billion–$54.3 billion, but its gross margin slipped to 66.4% from 67.7% on 2-nanometer ramp costs; shares fell 4.14% early, per TheStreet (TheStreet), and were down 2% at Yahoo’s later read (Yahoo Finance).
