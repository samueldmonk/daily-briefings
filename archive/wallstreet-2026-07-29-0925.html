The Lead

Fed decision at 2 PM ET, oil up more than 6% as U.S.–Iran strikes resume, and Microsoft & Meta tonight — a loaded Wednesday

Heading into the open, as of roughly 9:15 AM ET. Stock futures found a footing Wednesday morning on one of the most loaded days of the year: S&P 500 futures were up about 0.2%, Dow futures hovered just above the flat line, and Nasdaq-100 futures were flat to slightly lower after investors in Asia again dumped chip stocks, sending South Korea’s Kospi to a nearly 6% loss (Yahoo Finance live blog, fetched this run; Reuters counted S&P and Nasdaq futures inching up as chip stocks wobbled). Yahoo’s ~5 AM ET futures board read S&P 7,482 (+0.22%), Dow 52,978 (+0.06%), Nasdaq 27,928 (+0.02%), with the VIX flat at 18.21. Benzinga notes the July 29 Polymarket contract implied about a 70% chance the S&P 500 opens higher — this after Tuesday’s split close: Dow +537.45 points (+1.03%) to 52,747.53, S&P 500 +0.22% to 7,429.22, Nasdaq −0.22% to 24,876.91 (Yahoo Finance closing board/CNBC).

The war premium is back. Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched what U.S. Central Command called an “attempted surprise attack” — multiple ballistic missiles fired at U.S. forces in the Middle East on Tuesday, all intercepted, the first exchange since a pause in hostilities took hold on Friday (CNN/Yahoo Finance). President Trump told Fox News the U.S. “will hit Iran hard,” saying Iran “is going to get a beating,” and U.S. and Saudi forces struck what CNBC’s report described as “multiple terrorist logistics and weapons sites” in eastern Iraq after more than 30 drone attacks in three days by Iran-aligned groups (CNBC). Crude ripped higher on the re-escalation: Brent jumped 6.6% to $89.61 and WTI rose 6.4% to $84.31 in the latest readings (CNBC) — snapping what Bloomberg’s wrap called a three-day drop — a slide this morning’s wire coverage put at more than 14% for Brent. Gold sat near $4,097 on Yahoo’s early board, little changed.

Into that tape drops the Federal Reserve decision at 2 PM ET, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s 2:30 press conference — the moment the central bank either holds at 3.50–3.75% or delivers its first rate hike in three years (Yahoo Finance). CME FedWatch data show markets largely anticipate a hold (Benzinga), but the odds are unusually live: Yahoo’s live blog put the priced-in probability of a hike near 35%, and TradingEconomics notes markets carry “about a one-third chance” of a quarter-point move — persistently high inflation and Warsh’s clampdown on Fed communications have kept the door open. The 10-year Treasury yield edged up to 4.63% early Wednesday (TradingEconomics) after three straight down sessions.

Earnings pile on top. Visa beat overnight — fiscal Q3 EPS of $3.32 versus the $3.23 consensus on revenue up 14% to $11.63 billion, with quarterly payments volume topping $4 trillion for the first time — but the stock slipped about 1.6% after hours as the company moderated full-year growth guidance and disclosed roughly 2,600 job cuts (about 7% of its workforce) with $563 million in related costs (Seeking Alpha/Benzinga/Yahoo Finance). SK Hynix posted the biggest quarter in its history — revenue of ₩79.3 trillion with operating profit up 557% year over year — yet still came in under elevated consensus, feeding the “AI peak” anxiety that has Seoul selling chips (SK hynix newsroom/Korea Times/TechTimes). In this morning’s premarket, CNBC’s movers board had Ford up 6% on a beat-and-raise, Generac +5.5%, Manhattan Associates +11%, Deutsche Bank +2% and General Dynamics higher — against Procter & Gamble down about 3% on a revenue miss and CoStar off 15%. Starbucks and Chipotle report today; after the bell it’s the main event: Microsoft and Meta, a make-or-break moment for the AI trade after Alphabet’s capex guidance spooked markets last week, plus Qualcomm and Arm (Yahoo Finance). Apple and Amazon follow Thursday.
