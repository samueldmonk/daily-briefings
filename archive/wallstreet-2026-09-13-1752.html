The Lead

Markets are shut for the weekend; Friday’s relief rally is the last word before the Fed

US equity markets are closed — it is Sunday. The most recent session is Friday 11 September, when all three major indexes snapped four-day losing streaks: the S&P 500 rose 0.86% to 7,656.98, the Dow gained 509.19 points, or 0.98%, to 52,573.29, and the Nasdaq Composite added 0.96% to 26,333.04. Two things did the work. Oil fell hard — Brent dropped about 3% after Iranian state media said Tehran would meet Gulf states in Oman to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, pausing a rally that had carried crude to triple digits. August CPI was more mixed than the rally suggests: headline came in at +0.4% month over month, as expected, but core ran hot at +0.3% against a +0.2% consensus — and it was that core reading that pushed rate-hike expectations up, not down.

The relief is narrower than it looks. That same combination pushed bond-market expectations for a rate increase higher, not lower, and Wednesday’s FOMC decision is now the week. How heavily a hike is priced is genuinely disputed in what was read this run: one reading of CME FedWatch as of 11 September implies roughly a 90% probability of a hike, a second gives 80–90%, and a third puts it at about 71% for a 25 basis-point increase. The 71% reading appears to predate the CPI print — the same source says the probability “jumped to around 90% following a hotter-than-expected monthly core CPI reading.” All three are printed; none is averaged. Volatility has already repriced — the VIX broke decisively above its 50-day moving average to just below 18, which implies a daily move of about 1.13% in the S&P 500.

Unreconciled, both printed: this site has carried the week’s moves as S&P −0.8%, Dow −1.6%, Nasdaq −0.7%. A week-ahead summary read on a previous run instead gives S&P −0.6%, Nasdaq −0.7%, Dow −426 points. Only the Nasdaq figure agrees across both; neither set has been retired or averaged, and neither was re-confirmed this run.
