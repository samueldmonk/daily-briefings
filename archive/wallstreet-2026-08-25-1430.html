The lead

Chips lead a broad afternoon advance — and DICK’S Sporting Goods is having the worst day in its history

The current read is timed, and it reconciles. A live quote strip on The Motley Fool’s August 25 market page, fetched by this desk at about 1:40 p.m. ET, prints the S&P 500 up 0.3% on a change of +22.33, the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 0.2% on +105.40, and the Nasdaq Composite up 0.6% on +161.37. Every one of those three point changes reconciles exactly against Monday’s verified closes of 7,652.86, 53,417.16 and 25,980.19 — the arithmetic test this desk applies before any board is published. The ranking has not moved all day: Nasdaq first, S&P second, Dow last. Per this page’s standing practice the precise index levels stay out of the editorial and live in the tables below.

The same page carries an earlier, explicitly timed editorial read, and it shows the tape firming rather than fading. “As of 11:36 a.m. ET,” the Motley Fool had the Nasdaq Composite up 0.58%, the S&P 500 up 0.23% and the Dow up 0.07% — smaller gains than the 1:40 p.m. strip on all three. On the same 11:36 read the 10-year Treasury yield had fallen to 4.64%, down about seven basis points from Monday’s 4.704% finish and retreating further from an almost two-decade high, with utilities and financials green and industrials and energy lagging.

Schaeffer’s midday check, published 12:13 p.m. ET, agrees on shape. It has the Nasdaq Composite up triple digits, the Dow up 74 points, the S&P 500 “confidently higher,” and the 10-year at 4.65% at last look, crediting falling Treasury yields and a chip rally. One caution attaches to that piece: it writes that “Nvidia’s heavily anticipated earnings have sparked a chip rally.” Nvidia does not report until Wednesday after the close. This desk reads that as anticipation rather than results and publishes it as such — no earnings figures from Nvidia exist yet.

The drivers. Canada has announced retaliatory tariffs answering the 50% US duties Washington set on Canadian cars, trucks, automotive parts and steel — duties that take effect January 1, 2027 — and the tape brushed it aside. Crude fell for a second session, easing inflation worries. Bitcoin pushed above $80,000 for the first time in three months, in a move traders tie to the Treasury’s expanded bond-buyback plan and renewed dollar-debasement talk, before slipping back. The week’s two events remain Nvidia’s Wednesday report and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole speech on Friday.

DICK’S Sporting Goods is having the worst day in its history

The live quote card on the Motley Fool page at about 1:40 p.m. ET has DICK’S Sporting Goods at $127.27, down $52.06 or 29.03% from Monday’s $179.33 close — far and away the largest move on the tape. Schaeffer’s Investment Research, writing at 10:49 a.m. ET, had it down 25.3% at $134.03 and called it the stock’s worst single-session percentage loss on record and its tenth loss in eleven sessions, with shares at two-year lows and down 32.6% year to date.

The numbers behind it: adjusted second-quarter earnings of $3.53 a share against the $3.78 analysts expected, and a full-year adjusted EPS outlook cut to $11.00–$12.00 against a $14.20 consensus — a midpoint roughly 19% below the Street. Management pointed at the Foot Locker business, where proforma comparable sales fell 3.6%; executive chairman Ed Stack said conditions across portions of the athletic footwear and apparel marketplace became increasingly promotional as the quarter progressed.

Positioning is stretched in both directions. Schaeffer’s puts the stock’s RSI at 19, deep in oversold territory, with 7.48 million shares sold short — 11.88% of the float, about six days’ worth of covering. Options were frantic: 28,000 calls and 33,000 puts by late morning, roughly 18 times the stock’s average daily volume, with the September 160 put most popular and new positions opening in the September 130.

The reported decline has widened through the session and is disclosed rather than smoothed: about 12% on an untimed early screen, 25.3% at 10:49 a.m., “more than 27%” at 11:36 a.m., and 29.03% at about 1:40 p.m. Only the timed reads are asserted. Separately, Schaeffer’s describes the $21.9–$22.2 billion range as “adjusted per-share earnings”; that is the company’s net-sales guidance, not EPS, and this page does not repeat the mislabel.

Super Micro is the day’s standout gainer on a new Cisco partnership

Super Micro Computer was up 7.6% at $37.84 as of Schaeffer’s 12:13 p.m. ET midday check, one of the best performers on the Nasdaq, after Cisco Systems announced a formal expansion of its AI infrastructure portfolio through a new partnership with Super Micro. The stock is up 29.5% year to date. It is the clearest single-name expression of the chip and AI-hardware rebound that is doing most of the index work today.

lululemon leads the Nasdaq decliners after a UBS price-target cut

lululemon athletica was down 4.2% at $117.69 on the same 12:13 p.m. read, one of the Nasdaq’s top decliners, after UBS trimmed its price target to $120 from $124 while maintaining a neutral rating. The athletic-apparel complex is moving together: 24/7 Wall St. reported Nike down 3% and Lululemon down 4% alongside the DICK’S collapse, which is exactly the promotional-market read-across management described this morning.
