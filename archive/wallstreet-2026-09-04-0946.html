The Lead

Payrolls at 162,000 Meet a Chair Who Won’t Guide the Market (as of ~9:35 AM ET, minutes after the open)

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the August employment report at 8:30 AM ET and it came in far above forecast: nonfarm payrolls rose 162,000. The consensus it beat is quoted four different ways in the sources fetched this edition and all four are printed rather than averaged: +53,000 (CNBC’s Dow Jones poll), +55,000 (TheStreet, and Yahoo Finance’s article text), +56,000 (a Reuters poll) and +58,000 (Yahoo Finance’s own economic-data calendar). The unemployment rate held at 4.1%, which is also what was expected. July’s reading was a decline of 23,000.

The rest of the release is a gap in the record this page will not paper over. Yahoo’s calendar gives expectations for the other components — private payrolls +50,000 (against +30,000 previously), manufacturing payrolls +5,000 (+5,000 previously), a labour force participation rate of 61.4% (61.4% previously), and average hourly earnings of +0.3% month-on-month (+0.1% previously) and +3.0% year-on-year (+3.2% previously). Those are forecasts. No source fetched this edition states the actual August figure for any of them, so none is published as a result.

The opening bell rang five minutes before this edition was compiled, and no source fetched this run states a post-open level or percentage move for the S&P 500, the Nasdaq Composite or the Dow. This page therefore publishes none, and the live quote widgets above are the arbiter for where the session actually is. What can be stated is the sequence of futures readings that led into it, each with the time it was taken. At roughly 6:10 AM ET, before the release, Yahoo Finance had S&P 500 futures at 7,758.25, up 3.50 or 0.05%, Dow futures at 53,687.00, down 58.00 or 0.11%, Nasdaq-100 futures at 29,658.75, up 134.00 or 0.45% (the NQ contract, not the Composite), Russell 2000 futures at 2,967.60, down 2.10 or 0.07%, and the VIX at 14.23, down 0.63%. After the release, at roughly 8:38 AM ET, CNBC had Dow futures down 151 points, or 0.3%, S&P 500 futures down 0.2% and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.1%. At 8:47 AM ET TheStreet’s live blog was headlined “S&P 500 futures edge higher after key jobs report” and called futures mixed. The consistent thread across all three snapshots is that the Nasdaq contract was the firm leg and the Dow contract the weak one; the S&P contract sat close to unchanged throughout.

The framing that has changed this morning is who the number is being read for. Yahoo Finance’s Jared Blikre points out that Wall Street will obsess over the payroll figure while Fed Chair Kevin Warsh may barely flinch — because in his first Jackson Hole speech as chair, one week ago on August 28, Warsh spent much of his time arguing against the Fed guiding investors toward its next rate decision, then set out a framework for how he reads inflation, jobs, demand, corporate activity and markets. Blikre characterises Warsh’s current diagnosis as leaning hawkish: jobs look solid, inflation pressures remain too high, and broad financial conditions do not look restrictive. That is Yahoo’s reading of the speech, attributed as such. Its practical consequence is that a blowout payrolls print buys markets less forward guidance than it once would have.

The policy arithmetic still matters. This was the last employment report before the FOMC meets September 15–16, with the decision on the 16th, and the funds rate at 3.50–3.75%; the live directional question this year has been whether the Fed hikes, not whether it cuts. Governor Christopher Waller said he would support holding steady if inflationary pressure abates, and Kyle Rodda of Capital.com, quoted by TheStreet, says Waller’s remarks plus a falling dollar had pushed pricing for a September hike back to roughly 50% before the print. A hiring number roughly three times consensus pulls the other way. August CPI is now the swing factor.

The currency leg is moving further than the equity leg. Bloomberg reports the Bloomberg Dollar Spot Index on track to end the week 0.7% lower after touching its lowest level since May on Thursday, while the yen is on course for its best week since July, up 2.7% against the dollar, on expectations the Bank of Japan may raise its benchmark rate a quarter point this month. “The dollar took a step back this week as Fed speak leaned dovish and a yen rally spilled over to the broader USD complex,” said Noah Buffam of CIBC Capital Markets.

One scheduling note that changes the shape of the week: U.S. stock and bond markets are closed Monday, September 7, for Labor Day. Today is the last session before a three-day weekend, which tends to thin liquidity into the close.

Volkswagen — up 6.1% on 50,000 more job cuts

Europe's biggest automaker said it will cut a further 50,000 positions, including management roles, under its Future Plan 2030 — twelve initiatives its supervisory board approved Thursday and which the company calls the most strategically profound transformation programme in its 89-year history. The new cuts come on top of 50,000 already approved, for a total of 100,000. Cited drivers: tariff pressure and competition from China.

NewUpEarnings ahead

GameStop — the one quote taken after the print

Up 1.30% to $19.48 as of TheStreet’s 9:10 AM ET update — the only single-name figure on this page drawn from after the 8:30 release. The video-game retailer reports second-quarter earnings on Tuesday, September 8, and guides to quarterly net income of $290 million to $310 million against $168.6 million in the same quarter last year.

Separately, GameStop amended its $1.4 billion convertible-notes exchange agreement. It was originally structured to settle entirely in Class A common stock, with the share count based partly on a 35-trading-day reference period that began August 3. Under the amended terms the company will pay roughly $358.4 million in cash and issue about 55.5 million shares, fixing the total number of shares so that no additional shares are issued.

On the Radar

  • Labor Day, Monday September 7 — markets closed. U.S. stock and bond markets do not open. Today is the last session before a three-day weekend.
  • FOMC, September 15–16. Today's payrolls report was the last employment reading before the decision. The funds rate sits at 3.50–3.75%.
  • August CPI. Waller has explicitly tied his policy bias to it, saying he would back holding rates if inflationary pressures are abating. With hiring this strong, the inflation print becomes the swing factor.
  • Warsh has changed what a data print buys you. In his first Jackson Hole speech as chair, on August 28, the Fed chair argued against steering investors toward the next rate decision, wanting to say less about the future path and have markets do more of the interpreting. Expect less of a guidance response to strong data than the reflex assumes.
  • The Bank of Japan, and the yen. The yen is heading for its best week since July, up 2.7% against the dollar, on expectations of a quarter-point BoJ hike this month. A surging yen has already spilled into the broader dollar complex and was one of the two drivers cited for Thursday’s rally on Wall Street.
  • Rate-path positioning. Market pricing for a September hike was back to roughly 50% before this morning's release, after Waller's remarks and a falling dollar; the direction of travel in the debate this year has been toward a hike, not a cut.
  • Iran and the oil complex. The EU has formally joined the U.S.-led sanctions campaign on Tehran — “Operation Economic Outcast” — and South Korea is weighing a military role in reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Vice President JD Vance declined to call the six-month-old conflict a war, saying “right now, there is no active shooting”. Crude is the transmission line from that story into equities.
