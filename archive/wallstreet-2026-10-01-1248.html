The Lead

As of ~11:40 AM ET Thursday: stocks stay lower as yields hold at 2002 highs and ISM prices paid jump to 77.9; Accenture surges

Morning session, Thursday Oct. 1. Stocks opened higher — at 9:38 AM TheStreet had the S&P 500 up 0.20%, the Dow 0.16% and the Nasdaq 0.44%, with the Russell 2000 down 0.39% — but turned lower within half an hour. Yahoo Finance’s quote strip at about 10:01 AM ET had the S&P 500 down 0.15%, the Dow down 0.40% (202 points) and the Nasdaq Composite essentially flat (−0.01%), with the Russell 2000 down 0.34% and the VIX up 2.7% to 16.78; by 10:26 AM TheStreet’s live blog read “stocks were falling as Treasury yields surged to their highest levels in more than two decades.” By about 11:05 AM ET the selling had broadened: Trading Economics’ CFD-based references had the S&P 500 down 0.37%, the Dow down 0.54%, the Russell 2000 down 0.80% and the Nasdaq 100 down 0.15%, with the VIX at 17.57 and Micron down 2.79% — reference prices, not official index levels (Trading Economics). TheStreet’s live blog, updated at 11:17 AM ET under the headline “Nasdaq slips on rising Treasury yields,” still read “Stocks were falling as Treasury yields surged to their highest levels in more than two decades” (TheStreet); the blog’s latest market item, at 11:39 AM ET, had the Nasdaq-100-tracking Invesco QQQ Trust down 0.33% at $737.34 in late-morning trading (TheStreet). Yahoo Finance’s live blog has since retitled its coverage “Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq fall as fresh manufacturing spurs cost pressure worries,” but no index read later than Trading Economics’ ~11:05 AM figures carried a verifiable timestamp this edition, so none is printed as current. The live quote widgets above stream current prices.

The bond market is the story. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 4 basis points to 5.3338% on Thursday, its highest since April 2002, and the 30-year rose 3 basis points to 5.6702%, its highest since July 2002 (TheStreet, citing LSEG). Yahoo Finance said the 10-year briefly touched 5.34% earlier Thursday after the bond market suffered its worst quarter in decades. Minneapolis Fed President Neel Kashkari said Wednesday that “inflation is still too high” despite cooler August PCE data (TheStreet, citing CNBC), and Capital.com’s Kyle Rodda said methodological changes behind the 3% core PCE reading made it “more noise than signal” (TheStreet).

Data. Initial jobless claims fell 1,000 to 197,000 in the week ended Sept. 26, below the 200,000 expected and the fourth straight weekly decline; continuing claims fell 11,000 to 1.7 million (Labor Department via Yahoo Finance). Challenger, Gray & Christmas counted 43,281 announced job cuts in September, down 18% from August, with tech responsible for 10,799 (TheStreet; Yahoo Finance). At 10 AM the ISM manufacturing PMI registered 54.5% for September, 0.1 point below August and a ninth straight month of expansion, but the prices index jumped 6.8 points to 77.9% — which ISM said returned it to its level at the start of the Iran War — while new orders rose to 55.3% and employment to 52.7% (ISM); Yahoo Finance’s calendar had expected 55 for the headline and 72 for prices. The September jobs report is due Friday, with consensus at 84,000 and unemployment seen steady at 4.1% (Schwab, citing Briefing.com).

Oil and metals. West Texas Intermediate rose 2.06% to $92.28 and Brent 2.61% to $100.60 early after Reuters reported Chinese refiners suspended October fuel exports outside Hong Kong and Macau (TheStreet, 7:20 AM). Gold futures rose 0.62% to $4,212.50 and silver 1.62% to $61.55 early (TheStreet).

Micron after its beat. Yahoo Finance’s live blog said Micron shares were little changed at the open after the memory maker beat estimates and raised its outlook; its quote chip around 10 AM showed MU down 2.45%. Nike reports after the bell; TheStreet had it down 0.42% early and 44% lower year to date. Sources: TheStreet live blog (Oct 1, to 10:26 AM) · Yahoo Finance live (Oct 1, quotes ~10:01 AM)

Wednesday recap (official close). The S&P 500 fell 19.30 points, or 0.25%, to 7,651.54; the Dow fell 443.87 points, or 0.86%, to 50,906.05; and the Nasdaq Composite rose 63.52 points, or 0.24%, to 26,861.06, as stocks erased a morning gain after the cooler August PCE report (AP). For September the S&P 500 declined 0.4% and the Nasdaq gained 1.8%, while the Dow fell about 4.3% and the Russell 2000 5.4% — “It’s AI or bust,” said Interactive Brokers’ Steve Sosnick (Yahoo Finance). After Wednesday’s bell Micron reported fiscal Q4 revenue of $54.229 billion and adjusted EPS of $33.42, both above estimates, and guided fiscal Q1 2027 revenue to $61.5 billion ± $1.5 billion (company release; TheStreet). Sources: AP via KRMG (Sept 30 closes) · Micron release (8-K) · TheStreet Micron live blog
