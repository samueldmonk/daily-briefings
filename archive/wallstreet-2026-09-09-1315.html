The Lead

Oil back over $100 broadens the selling — Dow −413 points at 12:07 p.m. ET, 78% of the S&P 500 red

Brent crude futures passed $100 a barrel for the first time since July on Wednesday, and Wall Street went with it. The Associated Press put the Dow down 413 points, or 0.8%, as of 12:07 p.m. Eastern, with crude the proximate cause.

The damage widened as the morning went on. TheStreet’s 11:40 a.m. ET update had the S&P 500 off 0.52%, with declines accelerating after about 11 a.m. as Brent cleared $100 — 68.6% of all U.S. issues were falling and 78% of the S&P 500 was in the red. That is a breadth problem, not a mega-cap problem.

The escalation is military. The U.S. struck five Iranian tankers on Tuesday, sinking one, after Iran fired ballistic missiles at a U.S. Navy warship. Iran retaliated against a U.S.-used base in Jordan — Jordan says it intercepted 18 Iranian missiles with no casualties — and said it had targeted vessels crossing the Strait of Hormuz. The U.K. Maritime Trade Operations centre reported several merchant vessels in the Northern Arabian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman hit by “disabling fire.”

An earlier opening-bell read gave the S&P 500 −0.24%, the Dow −0.58%, the Nasdaq −0.35% and the Russell 2000 −0.52% at 9:35 a.m. ET. All three snapshots are shown with their times rather than blended; the 12:07 p.m. Dow figure is the latest timed number found this run. Against Tuesday’s close of 52,786.07, 413 points is 0.78% — self-consistent with the reported 0.8%.

Movers & Drivers

NewGuidance
Marvell Technology · MRVL

A sweeping revenue-target raise, and the stock follows

Up 6.4% in the session after chief executive Matt Murphy lifted the fiscal-2027 revenue outlook to about $12 billion and the fiscal-2028 target to roughly $18 billion, against prior expectations of about $10 billion and $13.5 billion — a combined two-year forecast near $30 billion. Astera Labs (ALAB) rose 6.1% alongside it on the same AI build-out theme.

NewProduct
Meta Platforms · META

Muse launch lifts Meta against a falling tape

Up 5.4% in the session after the Facebook and Instagram parent launched Muse, a personal AI agent for users 18 and over aimed at scheduling, shopping and turning long-term goals into plans. It runs in a dedicated app and directly inside WhatsApp. Meta had climbed 4.9% premarket on the same news.

NewEarnings
Casey’s General Stores · CASY

The biggest single-name drop of the session

Down 16.5% in the session — steeper than the 12.2% premarket slide — after the convenience-store chain posted disappointing same-store sales growth and left its full-year outlook unchanged. It is the largest verified move in either direction on the day.

NewLegal
Booking Holdings · BKNG · UnitedHealth · UNH

A court loss and a valuation de-rating

Booking Holdings fell 4.3% after the EU General Court in Luxembourg fully dismissed its challenge to the European Commission’s 2023 decision blocking its roughly $1.9 billion acquisition of eTraveli. UnitedHealth fell 4.24% on what was described as valuation concerns, macroeconomic pressure and dividend-related factors.

CarriedEarnings
Signet Jewelers · SIG

Guidance raise sends the jeweller sharply higher

Signet raised its full-year profit outlook on better-than-expected second-quarter earnings. The premarket move was read at +10% in one account and +17% in another, with Reuters/Yahoo Finance describing shares soaring roughly 20% in the session. The spread is shown rather than resolved.

On the Radar

  • Treasury triples its buyback — announced at ~11 a.m. ET today. The Treasury Department said it will buy back up to $6 billion of already-issued longer-term government debt, specifically 10- and 20-year notes — three times the normal operation size, and above the “at least $4 billion” Secretary Scott Bessent flagged on 19 August. Officially it is about liquidity; it is also widely read as an attempt to put a lid on Treasury yields. Bessent’s line to currency traders on Tuesday: “I am the house now.”
  • Apple event, today. 10:00 a.m. Pacific. First product event under CEO John Ternus, who took over on 1 September. The iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max are expected, with a rumoured foldable. Apple slipped nearly 1% to $313.12 as the event got underway.
  • Chewy (CHWY) and American Eagle Outfitters (AEO) report today — two consumer-spending reads on an otherwise thin earnings day.
  • A new trade front with Canada. The White House said it will ban imports of Canadian motorbikes and a slew of other products starting later this month as relations with Ottawa fray.
  • August Producer Price Index — Thursday 10 September, 8:30 a.m. ET (Bureau of Labor Statistics). PPI for final demand was unchanged in July. A CPI release is also flagged for Thursday; the two dates could not be separated cleanly this run, so only the BLS-stated PPI date is asserted.
  • Oil is the macro variable. Higher crude is feeding straight into the inflation debate and into a Fed whose last vote had three members pushing for a hike — which is why a supply story is moving the whole tape rather than just energy. Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn: upcoming inflation data will be “particularly important” in determining whether $100 oil is feeding into the Fed outlook.
