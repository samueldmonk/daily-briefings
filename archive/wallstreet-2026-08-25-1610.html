Carried · 11:18 edition−29.03%DKS

DICK’S Sporting Goods is having the worst day in its history

The live quote card on the Motley Fool page at about 1:40 p.m. ET has DICK’S Sporting Goods at $127.27, down $52.06 or 29.03% from Monday’s $179.33 close — far and away the largest move on the tape. Schaeffer’s Investment Research, writing at 10:49 a.m. ET, had it down 25.3% at $134.03 and called it the stock’s worst single-session percentage loss on record and its tenth loss in eleven sessions, with shares at two-year lows and down 32.6% year to date.

The numbers behind it: adjusted second-quarter earnings of $3.53 a share against the $3.78 analysts expected, and a full-year adjusted EPS outlook cut to $11.00–$12.00 against a $14.20 consensus — a midpoint roughly 19% below the Street. Management pointed at the Foot Locker business, where proforma comparable sales fell 3.6%; executive chairman Ed Stack said conditions across portions of the athletic footwear and apparel marketplace became increasingly promotional as the quarter progressed.

Positioning is stretched in both directions. Schaeffer’s puts the stock’s RSI at 19, deep in oversold territory, with 7.48 million shares sold short — 11.88% of the float, about six days’ worth of covering. Options were frantic: 28,000 calls and 33,000 puts by late morning, roughly 18 times the stock’s average daily volume, with the September 160 put most popular and new positions opening in the September 130.

The reported decline has widened through the session and is disclosed rather than smoothed: about 12% on an untimed early screen, 25.3% at 10:49 a.m., “more than 27%” at 11:36 a.m., and 29.03% at about 1:40 p.m. Reporting fetched this run adds two more points on the same curve: the stock dropped more than 22% at the open, and an aggregator recorded the day’s move as −25.48% without stating a time. The Motley Fool quote card still read $127.27, −29.03% when this desk re-fetched the page at about 2:35 p.m., but that card sits on the same rail that returned cached index figures, so it is carried as the 1:40 read rather than asserted as the 2:35 price. Only the timed reads are asserted. Separately, Schaeffer’s describes the $21.9–$22.2 billion range as “adjusted per-share earnings”; that is the company’s net-sales guidance, not EPS, and this page does not repeat the mislabel.
