Top Story

Attackers are emptying AI pipelines of their cloud keys through a critical Langflow flaw

CVE-2026-0768 is a remote code execution flaw in Langflow, the low-code platform used to build AI agents. It carries a CVSS score of 9.8, and it needs no authentication: the flaw sits in the handling of the code parameter passed to the validate endpoint, where a user-supplied string is executed as Python without proper validation. Code runs in the context of root. Every Langflow release up to and including 1.4.2 is affected. Trend Micro's Zero Day Initiative disclosed it back in January.

What changed is that it is now being used. Continuous exploitation of internet-facing instances was observed starting August 29, 2026, and VulnCheck's VP of security research reported 50+ Canary detections — a mix of automated scanning, exploitation for initial access, and then a methodical sweep for secrets: reading Langflow's own secret_key, grepping environment variables for OpenAI and AWS keys and other cloud credentials, hunting SSH keys and .env files, moving laterally, and exfiltrating Langflow source code.

It is not alone. The same reporting pairs it with CVE-2026-66066 — nicknamed "KindaRails2Shell" — a CVSS 9.5 flaw in Ruby on Rails' Active Storage and libvips handling, patched July 29 but exploited through August 31 for the same purpose: reading /proc/self/environ, extracting the Rails credentials store, and pulling AWS and GCS keys, database passwords and third-party API tokens. VulnCheck's telemetry put Canary hits in Singapore, Israel and the United Kingdom, traced to a single source IP in France. Rails fixes shipped in 7.2.3.2, 8.0.5.1 and 8.1.3.1.

Reader caveat: the sources fetched for this edition confirm 1.4.2 as vulnerable but do not state a confirmed fixed Langflow version, so none is given here. Neither CVE-2026-0768 nor CVE-2026-66066 appears in the CISA KEV catalog.

Patch Priority

SonicWall SMA1000 — CVE-2026-83548 (CVSS 10.0), federal deadline Saturday

Two zero-days in SonicWall's SMA1000 appliances are being chained for unauthenticated remote code execution. CVE-2026-83548 is a pre-authentication SSRF in the Appliance Work Place, rated CVSS 10.0. CVE-2026-83549 is an OS command injection in the Appliance Management Console, requiring authenticated admin — which the first flaw supplies.

Affected models: SMA1000 6210, 7210 and 8200v. Hotfixes are 12.4.3-03526 and 12.5.0-02952 and higher. There is no workaround. If you find indicators of compromise, SonicWall's guidance is to re-image the appliance, rotate all user and admin passwords, and reset TOTP tokens.

Both are in the CISA KEV catalog with a remediation due date of Saturday, September 5, 2026 — the same deadline that applies to three other flaws listed below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

RansomwareDouble extortion

Rhysida

The group posted an entry on its dark-web portal on August 28, 2026 claiming 5.79 terabytes taken from the German city-state of Berlin, with a demand of 30 bitcoin. Its leverage came from a gap in the victim's response rather than anything exotic in the intrusion: Berlin's state government waited roughly seven days after first detecting suspicious data movement before disconnecting the compromised departments from its central network. Berlin has refused to pay, temporarily cancelled remote work, and is auditing all of its systems.

Breaches & Incidents

NewSupply chainBGP

A 33-hour BGP hijack delivered a malicious Virtualizor update

Attackers diverted Softaculous traffic by having AS62390 (NexonHost) announce part of Hetzner's address space, including the IPs behind Softaculous systems. The window ran from roughly Aug 28, 20:57 UTC to Aug 30, 06:10 UTC. Because Let's Encrypt's automated domain-ownership validation was routed through the same hijack, the actor obtained a valid TLS certificate for Softaculous domains. Any Virtualizor install that checked for updates during a diverted interval could receive the modified package — and the update client had no cryptographic package verification to reject it. Virtualizor says a handful of servers were affected rather than the general user base, and shipped Patch 9 with a Security Analyzer on Sept 1; package signing remains future work. Operators should run the official scanner, rotate and restrict API credentials, and audit each server for persistence.

RansomwareGovernment

Berlin refuses to pay Rhysida

Rhysida claims 5.79 TB from Berlin's state network — reported elsewhere as 46,500 contracts, 16,389 email addresses, 11,963 phone numbers, 12,076 individuals and 148 IBANs. Two valuations of the 30 BTC demand are in circulation, ≈$2.3M and ≈$2.5M; both are printed here and neither is adopted. Mayor Kai Wegner and interior senator Iris Spranger have refused to pay. The September 20 state election is unaffected.

Account takeoverSSO

Dropbox: ~5,000 accounts taken over through a Lenovo ID flaw

An email-verification weakness let attackers create Lenovo IDs on victims' addresses; legacy SSO then made Dropbox treat those as verified logins. Activity was detected Aug 4–21, 2026. None of the affected accounts had MFA enabled, and about one third had files viewed or downloaded. Dropbox has disabled the integration and expired Lenovo-ID sessions.

TakedownBotnet

Sality's peer-to-peer network turned against itself

The U.S. Department of Justice announced a coordinated disruption on August 31, 2026 involving authorities in the U.S., Bulgaria, Hungary and Romania alongside CrowdStrike and the Shadowserver Foundation. Investigators abused the botnet's own trusted-peer protocol to poison peer lists, so more than 15,000 infected hosts now beacon to sinkholes instead of the operator. Coverage splits on its age — "23-year-old" and "20-year-old" both appear; neither is adopted here.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • Due Saturday, September 5
    CVE-2026-83548 and CVE-2026-83549 (SonicWall SMA1000 appliances), CVE-2026-9586 (Sangoma Switchvox), CVE-2026-82329 (JFrog Artifactory) and CVE-2026-49869 (Kestra OSS). That is the two SonicWall flaws plus three others — five CVEs in total.
  • Due Wednesday, September 16
    CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette) and CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM).
  • All seven were added in a single tranche on September 2, 2026, confirmed against CISA's own alert page with product names. Remediation windows fall under BOD 26-04, which assigns risk-based per-CVE due dates — the older flat three-week window of BOD 22-01 does not apply here, which is why two dates sit in one tranche.
