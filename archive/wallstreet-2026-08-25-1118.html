The lead

A quiet green tape as of about 10:40 a.m. ET — chips lead the rebound, and one retailer is having its worst day on record

Markets are open and the session is roughly an hour old at this edition. Yahoo Finance’s live market bar, timed by its own “U.S. markets close in 5h 19m” countdown to about 10:41 a.m. ET, has the Nasdaq Composite up 0.46%, the S&P 500 up 0.16% and the Dow Jones Industrial Average up 0.06% — the mirror image of Monday, when a memory-chip selloff dragged the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq down while the Dow held green. The Russell 2000 is up 0.28%, back above 3,000. Yahoo Finance’s own live blog, whose headline this run reads “Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq gain as oil, Treasury yields fall,” puts the Dow and the S&P 500 up about 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite up 0.7% on a slightly later read, and a separate midday summary this run had the S&P 500 up 0.41%, the Dow up 0.22% and the Nasdaq up 0.68%. The three reads agree on direction and on the ranking — Nasdaq first, S&P second, Dow last — and differ only in how much of the morning they capture, so this page publishes the timed 10:41 a.m. figures as current and the later ones as attributed.

The arithmetic test passes cleanly this run. Every one of the three index reads on that 10:41 a.m. strip reconciles exactly against Monday’s verified closes: 7,652.86 plus 11.91 is 7,664.77; 53,417.16 plus 33.52 is 53,450.68; 25,980.19 plus 119.33 is 26,099.52. The market bar on a different Yahoo page was live, dated and internally consistent, and it is what the figures above come from. Per this page’s standing practice, precise index levels stay out of the editorial and live in the tables below; the moves above are percentages.

What is driving it. Semiconductors are rebounding into Nvidia’s print: Advanced Micro Devices and Micron Technology have each added about 3%, recovering part of Monday’s damage — Micron fell 5.8% and SanDisk and Seagate Technology each fell 6.5% in that session on weekend reports that Washington may let Apple source memory from Chinese suppliers. Nvidia itself closed lower on Monday for a seventh consecutive session, and TheStreet reports the company has told customers it will raise server prices. Kyle Rodda, senior financial market analyst at Capital.com, said that news “weighed on sentiment towards tech firms as niggling doubts persist about rising costs, eroded margins, weaker profitability and lower return on investment,” and that it “narrowed the focus and sobered the minds of market participants looking for a good story about the AI trade in Nvidia’s quarterly results.”

Bonds are helping. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.66%, roughly four basis points lower on the session per Trading Economics, and Yahoo Finance’s live blog put it at 4.658%. That extends Monday’s move, when the 10-year fell more than three basis points to finish at 4.704% and the 30-year fell more than four to 5.234% after a report that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent could tap the department’s near-$1 trillion General Account to help finance bond buybacks rather than issue short-term bills. The same report is why the dollar-debasement trade is back in the conversation: bitcoin pushed above $80,000 early Tuesday for the first time since mid-May, per TheStreet, before easing back.

Oil is falling for a second day, and the reason is a de-escalation read. West Texas Intermediate was down 3.14% at $82.34 a barrel and Brent down 3% at $89.41 in early trading, per TheStreet; the Yahoo market bar had the October WTI contract off 2.67% at 10:41 a.m. Ole Hansen, head of commodity strategy at Saxo Bank, told Reuters that the shift from military action to economic pressure in the US–Iran conflict “has reduced some of the oil market’s anxiety,” adding that Monday’s sanctions announcement was not as forceful as some traders had expected. Bessent unveiled the measures on Monday but declined to name the countries that would be targeted or say when the penalties take effect. Iran has vowed to retaliate. Two counterweights on the supply side: the Novoshakhtinsk refinery in Russia’s Rostov region was damaged by a Ukrainian drone overnight and suspended operations, and a fire broke out at KazMunayGas’s Atyrau refinery in western Kazakhstan on Tuesday.

Movers & drivers — late morning
New−27.29%DKS

DICK’S Sporting Goods is having the worst day in its history

Yahoo Finance’s live trending rail, on the same 10:41 a.m. ET read, has DICK’S Sporting Goods at $130.39, down $48.94 or 27.29% — far and away the largest single move on the tape. The company missed on both lines for its fiscal second quarter: adjusted earnings of $3.53 a share against the $3.78 analysts expected, on revenue of $5.59 billion against a $5.65 billion forecast. The guidance cut was the larger problem. Full-year non-GAAP earnings guidance came down to $11 to $12 a share from $13.50 to $14.50, against an analyst consensus of $14.20, and the net-sales outlook was trimmed to $21.9–$22.2 billion from $22.1–$22.4 billion. Management pointed at the Foot Locker business, where proforma comparable sales fell 3.6%, and said conditions across parts of the athletic footwear and apparel market became increasingly promotional as the quarter progressed. Schaeffer’s Investment Research headlined the stock as “on track for worst day ever.”

Reported percentages for this move vary widely by the minute and by vendor — CNBC had it down more than 22% at the open, one wire put it at 17%, another at 16%, a quote service at 25.48%. The figure published above is the timed Yahoo live quote, because it comes with both a price and a point change that are mutually consistent.

New+11.0%KURA

Kura Oncology’s chief executive bought the stock twice in a week, and it is at a 52-week high

Kura Oncology rose 11.0% in premarket trading and went on to set a new 52-week high, trading as high as $13.50 and last changing hands near $13.38. The catalyst is an insider filing: President and chief executive Troy Edward Wilson acquired 100,000 shares on August 24 at a weighted average price of $12.39 — about $1.24 million, executed across multiple trades between $11.47 and $12.85. It was his second large open-market purchase in roughly a week, following $1.11 million for 100,000 shares on August 17. After the latest buy, his indirect holding through the One Fish Two Fish Revocable Trust stands at 479,194 shares, alongside 435,456 held directly and 300,000 more indirectly through the Lorax Charitable Remainder Unitrust.

New+3% eachAMD · MU

The chips that led Monday’s selloff are leading Tuesday’s rebound

Advanced Micro Devices and Micron Technology have each added about 3%, and the semiconductor bid is the main reason the Nasdaq Composite is outrunning the other two indexes this morning. It is a partial retracement rather than a reversal: Micron fell 5.8% on Monday, SanDisk and Seagate Technology each fell 6.5% and Broadcom fell 2.6%, on weekend reports that Washington may permit Apple to source DRAM from CXMT and NAND from YMTC. No policy decision has been announced. Nvidia, which closed lower for a seventh straight session on Monday, reports fiscal second-quarter results after Wednesday’s bell — the event the whole complex is positioning around.
