The Lead

As of midday: the tape tilts lower at the highs while earnings do violent sorting — Honeywell Aerospace craters on its first solo report, and oil firms on an Iran–Oman corridor

Thursday is a stock-picker's tape, and it softened into lunch. As of the latest reads (~midday ET), the Dow Jones Industrial Average had fallen about 0.5% — putting the blue-chip index's record-breaking win streak at risk — while the S&P 500 fell about 0.2% and the Nasdaq Composite ticked just below the flat line (Yahoo Finance live blog, via two independent reads this edition). The late morning was firmer: Trading Economics' late-morning board — unchanged on this edition's re-fetch — showed the S&P 500 CFD +0.04%, the Dow CFD −0.16% and the Nasdaq-100 CFD −0.24%, with its session note calling US stocks "mixed" — the S&P flat for a second session — as chip producers extended their volatile momentum (TheStreet's Thursday headline still has the S&P 500 rising; MarketScreener's: "S&P 500 flat as chip, software weakness offsets broader gains"). The morning was a whipsaw: Yahoo Finance's 10:36 AM read had the Nasdaq up 0.2% as Broadcom rose nearly 2%, Marvell Technology climbed 3% and Nvidia traded green, before the complex faded again — Trading Economics' board has Nvidia at just +0.2%, with Microsoft +1.9% and J&J and Goldman Sachs up around 1% carrying the traditional wing. Breadth still tilts constructive: small- and mid-caps outperformed (Russell 2000 CFD +0.41%, S&P 400 +0.59% — Trading Economics), and early in the session 8 of 11 S&P 500 sectors traded higher, materials leading at +2.87% with energy lagging at −0.96% (StockTitan sector board).

Underneath the flat indexes, earnings are sorting winners from losers at industrial scale. The session's wreck: Honeywell Aerospace, the engine maker spun off from Honeywell in June, plunged 20.8% through 9:50 AM ET after its first earnings report as a standalone company — pro forma EPS of $1.87 missed the $2.13 analysts expected (The Motley Fool), and the company cut its 2026 organic sales growth forecast to 4–5% from 7–9% as supply-chain constraints capped its ability to meet aftermarket demand (Investing.com). The winners were just as emphatic: Motorola Solutions climbed about 11% in early action — the S&P 500's top early performer — on an earnings beat and raised outlook (Investing.com; MarketScreener), with TheStreet's later read at +8.5%, alongside Albemarle up 7.7% on a Q2 beat (TheStreet), and Parker-Hannifin rose about 8.5% after forecasting full-year profit above estimates (MarketScreener). Memory stayed routed — Western Digital down about 17% and Sandisk down nearly 10% on Trading Economics' latest session note, after Yahoo's late-morning read had them −18% and −13% — while Datadog fell 16% in early trade on weaker forward revenue guidance (StockMarketWatch), roughly the move options had priced (Market Rebellion). And SpaceX's IPO lockup is now live: insiders got their first chance today to sell up to 911.5 million shares, though another 455.8 million remain restricted because of the stock's weak performance (MarketWatch via TS2); Trading Economics' session note has the stock down as much as ~1% intraday — near all-time lows after Wednesday's 13.6% slide to $108.27, 19.8% below its $135 June IPO price (Benzinga; TS2/TheStreet).

Geopolitics turned supportive again: oil steadied near $80 a barrel after Iran said it reached an agreement with Oman for a temporary shipping route through the Strait of Hormuz — Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmail Baghaei: a deal will come "if certain third parties do not obstruct this process" (Yahoo Finance) — with WTI up 1.3% near $76 and Brent +1.8% near $81 on Trading Economics' live board. The labor data leaned reassuring ahead of Friday's main event. First-time jobless claims ticked up slightly to 199,000 in the week of August 1 (Yahoo Finance) — still below the ~203,000 forecast (Investing.com) — and Challenger, Gray & Christmas reported U.S. employers announced just 33,429 job cuts in July, the lowest monthly tally in two years (Challenger via Trading Economics/Investing.com). That sets up Friday's July payrolls report, where consensus runs ~83,000 jobs with a 4.3% unemployment rate per Yahoo-cited forecasts (Schwab cites 86,000) after June's +57,000 and Wednesday's soft +44,000 ADP print. The Fed backdrop sharpened too: Governor Lisa Cook said Wednesday she is "prepared to act" on a rate hike unless disinflation resumes (CNBC), and JPMorgan CEO Jamie Dimon warned that leverage across markets is elevated, with "margin debt being the highest it has ever been" (Yahoo Finance). Wednesday's context: the Dow rose 263.24 points (+0.49%) to a record 54,349.12 — its fifth straight positive day — while the S&P 500 (−0.17%) and Nasdaq (−0.83%) pulled back, and Nvidia rose over 3% on SpaceX's exclusive-chips commitment (CNBC; Yahoo Finance).

Movers & Drivers

▼ Spinoff stumble

Honeywell Aerospace (HONA)

The session's biggest wreck: the jet-engine and avionics maker, spun off from Honeywell in June, plunged 20.8% through 9:50 AM ET after missing on its first earnings report as a standalone company — pro forma Q2 EPS of $1.87 vs. the $2.13 expected (The Motley Fool). It also cut 2026 organic sales growth guidance to 4–5% from 7–9%, blaming supply-chain constraints that limited its ability to keep pace with surging aftermarket demand (Investing.com). Seeking Alpha's earlier read had the stock down 14%; the losses deepened from there.

▲ Industrial beats

Motorola Solutions (MSI), Parker-Hannifin (PH) & Albemarle (ALB)

The other side of earnings day: Motorola Solutions climbed about 11% — the S&P 500's top performer in early action — after an earnings beat and a raised outlook (Investing.com; MarketScreener; TheStreet's later read has it +8.5%), Parker-Hannifin rose about 8.5% after the motion-and-control technology firm forecast full-year profit above estimates on its Aug 6 report (MarketScreener), and Albemarle gained 7.7% after beating Wall Street's Q2 earnings expectations (TheStreet). Old-economy industrials and materials, not AI, are carrying Thursday's leaderboard.

New◆ Media

Warner Bros. Discovery (WBD)

The morning's marquee report landed mixed: Q2 EPS of $0.06 beat the expected 15-cent loss by 21 cents, but revenue of $8.72 billion fell 11.2% year over year and missed the ~$9.22 billion consensus (StockStory; Investing.com). Shares rose 0.5% in premarket trading after the profit beat (Investing.com); no settled intraday move surfaced in this edition's reads. The company says it remains focused on delivering strategic priorities while planning for the closing of its merger with Paramount (company statement via StockStory).

▼ Memory

Western Digital (WDC) & Sandisk (SNDK)

The premarket dip became an intraday rout: Western Digital plummeted about 17% after posting disappointing guidance, and Sandisk lost nearly 10% as results "failed to meet lofty expectations for memory producers" (Trading Economics session note, the latest read) — Yahoo's late-morning read had them as deep as −18% and −13% after both showed only ~5.4% declines premarket. Schwab had flagged the setup going in: with the global memory shortage lifting valuations, "winning the telephone game" of whisper numbers was the bar to clear — neither cleared it.

◆ Lockup day

SpaceX (SPCX)

The IPO lockup is live: insiders got their first chance today to sell as many as 911.5 million shares — roughly $99 billion worth — though another 455.8 million shares stay restricted because of the stock's weak performance (MarketWatch via TS2). The stock was up 1.7% premarket (Benzinga) and has traded down as much as ~1% intraday per Trading Economics' session note — near all-time lows after Wednesday's 13.6% slide to $108.27, 19.8% below its $135 June IPO price and far off the June 16 record of $225.64 (TS2; TheStreet). Behind the slide: Q2 capex of $18.37 billion — 86% of it AI infrastructure — against 92% revenue growth to $7.81 billion, a $541 million net loss and 12 million Starlink subscriptions (TS2).

▼ Guidance

Datadog (DDOG)

The software casualty: the cloud-monitoring and security platform fell 16% after issuing weaker forward revenue guidance, with investors also weighing broader software-sector AI concerns and insider selling pressure (StockMarketWatch live read). The move roughly matched what the options market had braced for — the Aug 7 weekly straddle was priced for about a 15% swing into the results (Market Rebellion pre-market IV report).

◆ Whipsaw

Chips flip the Nasdaq back and forth

The Nasdaq began the session lower, rose about 0.2% by 10:36 AM as chip stocks reversed early declines — Broadcom up nearly 2%, Marvell Technology up 3%, Nvidia in the green (Yahoo Finance live blog) — then drifted back to modestly lower as the complex faded again: Trading Economics' latest session note has chip producers "sharply down," pressuring the Nasdaq 100, with its live board showing the index CFD −0.24% and Nvidia trimmed to +0.2%. Micron dropped 5.6% in early trade and the memory names stayed routed, so the "AI capital requirements and monetization" sorting continues inside the complex, not just around it.

▼ Earnings pain

Figma (FIG) & Duolingo (DUOL)

The AI-cost theme reached software's growth names in premarket action (TS2 live-blog reads, ~6 AM ET): Figma fell 15.1% to $23.90 even as revenue grew 48% to $370.1 million and beat expectations — a 117% jump in cost of revenue, mainly AI infrastructure, slowed gross-profit growth and strained margins despite a $40 million full-year revenue-guide raise — and Duolingo dropped 9.3% to $122.68 despite Q2 revenue of $298.5 million topping forecasts, as bookings per daily active user fell 12% while the company prioritizes growing its 58.7 million DAUs (+23%) over monetization.

Chart of the Day — Honeywell Aerospace (HONA)

The session's biggest sourced mover: down 20.8% through 9:50 AM ET after its first post-spinoff earnings missed and the company cut its 2026 outlook (The Motley Fool; Investing.com).

Sector Heat — live

Early Thursday, 8 of 11 S&P 500 sectors traded higher — materials led at +2.87% while energy lagged at −0.96% (StockTitan sector board). By late morning the VIX had eased to 15.55 on Trading Economics' live board (Yahoo's pre-open read was 15.93), with small- and mid-caps outperforming (Russell 2000 CFD +0.41%, S&P 400 +0.59% — TE). Breadth backdrop: about 71% of S&P 500 stocks were above their 200-day moving averages as of Tuesday, up from 55% at the June peak (Schwab).
