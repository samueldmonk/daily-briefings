Top Story

PaperCut exploitation escalates from scanning to hands-on intrusions — and the first patch did not hold

Attacks against two PaperCut NG/MF vulnerabilities disclosed on August 27 have escalated, with threat actors shifting from reconnaissance to hands-on-keyboard activity (SecurityWeek). PaperCut’s updated indicators of compromise point specifically to the deployment of remote access tools on targeted systems (Help Net Security), and Cybersecurity Dive reports the flaws are being exploited by multiple threat actors.

The chain has an order, and the order is the whole story. CVE-2026-81578 comes first — missing authentication for a critical function lets an unauthenticated remote attacker modify certain system configuration settings. CVE-2026-82078 then turns those manipulated parameters into execution of arbitrary Java bytecode already on the application classpath, in the security context of the PaperCut server process. Read separately, neither flaw sounds like a takeover; read in sequence, the first supplies the input the second trusts. Because the bytecode is already present, nothing is uploaded — which is why file-drop detection sees nothing until the remote-access tooling arrives.

Two operational points. First, the vendor now considers all versions of PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF potentially impacted — a list of patched versions is not a list of affected ones, and an estate that used the version list as a triage filter filtered on the wrong thing. Second, PaperCut has issued a second emergency patch after researchers broke the first fix: an estate that patched once, promptly, and moved on is not covered. The September 14 clock is measured against the current build, not against the act of patching.

Patch Priority — PaperCut NG/MF, deadline September 14 (13 days left)

Patch or isolate every PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF server today, then verify you are on the second emergency build, not the first. CISA added CVE-2026-81578 (CVSS 8.8, missing authentication for a critical function) and CVE-2026-82078 (CVSS 9.4, unsafe reflection) to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 31, 2026 with a remediation deadline of September 14, 2026 for federal civilian executive branch agencies. Hunt for unexplained remote-access tooling on print servers — that is the escalation marker, and it appears after the exploitation that precedes it leaves no dropped file.

Threat Actor Spotlight

RansomwareOpportunistic

Medusa — 500+ organisations, and a method that names its own next victim

CISA reports Medusa affiliates have breached more than 500 organisations across critical infrastructure sectors, working opportunistically: monitoring vulnerability announcements and targeting organisations that have not yet patched. That is precisely the population a partially-patched PaperCut estate falls into. The group does not need a novel capability to reach an environment whose exposure was published on its behalf — which is the argument for treating the KEV clock as a floor rather than a target.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 (PaperCut NG/MF) — added August 31, 2026, remediation due September 14, 2026 (13 days left). This is the same date carried in the Patch Priority box above; there is one deadline for this pair and it is that one.
  • Six vulnerabilities added August 26, including CVE-2026-8452 (Citrix NetScaler), CVE-2019-1068 (Microsoft SQL Server remote code execution) and CVE-2022-0995 (Linux kernel out-of-bounds write). Their assigned due dates span August 29 to September 9 — some already elapsed, the latest (8 days left).
  • Deadlines are per-CVE and risk-based. Under BOD 26-04, CISA assigns a remediation date to each entry; the old flat “three weeks from the add date” heuristic from BOD 22-01 no longer applies and is not used to compute anything on this page. Every countdown here is measured against a date CISA published.
