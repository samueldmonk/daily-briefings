The Lead

Oil slides below $100 and the week opens green — as of ~9:58 AM ET

Stocks rose at the opening bell on Monday as crude retreated. At the open the Dow Jones Industrial Average was up 0.61%, the S&P 500 up 0.67% and the Nasdaq up 0.77%, while the Russell 2000 slipped 0.50% (TheStreet opening-bell entry, 9:31 AM ET).

Oil is the relief valve. West Texas Intermediate fell 2.50% to $97.79 a barrel and Brent sank 2.49% to $101.30 as of 7:42 AM ET, on easing supply fears: Saudi Arabia expects to restore about half of its East-West pipeline capacity within days, its crude exports have surged past 4 million barrels a day this month against a 13-year low of 2.4 million in August, and shipments through the Strait of Hormuz have reached a six-month high. JPMorgan analysts put total Gulf oil flows at 17.1 million barrels a day over the past 10 days.

“Markets are starting the week on a more constructive footing, with falling oil prices helping revive risk appetite after several weeks dominated by inflation and interest-rate concerns,” said Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com, who added that Asian equities advanced overnight led by technology and semiconductor stocks. She cautioned that none of it resolves the Middle East conflict and that Houthi attacks on Saudi infrastructure remain an obvious risk.

The backdrop is last week’s Federal Reserve rate hike — a 25-basis-point move to a 3.75%–4.00% target range on 16 September, the first increase since July 2023, passed 12–0, with the Fed signalling at least one more this year. Stocks finished mixed on Friday and the Dow booked its third straight weekly loss. Ahead this week: a Trump–Xi summit in Washington on Thursday covering tariffs, critical minerals and artificial intelligence.

What this desk would not print: the pre-open futures readings did not agree — one account had S&P futures +0.58%, Dow +0.60% and Nasdaq +0.86% at around 4 AM ET, another the same morning said +0.5% / +0.5% / +0.8%, a third +0.6% / +0.8%, and a fourth +0.7% / +0.7% / +1.0%. Rather than pick one, this page leads with the opening-bell figures, which are a single sourced reading.

Movers & Drivers

New+7.36%

Strategy (MSTR) rides the bitcoin bounce

The world’s largest corporate holder of bitcoin surged as the coin itself rose 5.13% to $84,402.56 as of 7:16 AM ET. A quote strip read at around 4:05 AM ET had bitcoin at $81,840.98, +2.01%; both readings of the same morning are printed here rather than one smoothed number. Bitcoin’s move followed the CLARITY Act stalling in the Senate on a 49–50 cloture vote, short of the 60 needed, and a short squeeze: CoinDesk, citing CoinGlass, reported just over $300 million of roughly $313 million in crypto liquidations belonged to short sellers, about 96% of the total.

New+7.12%

Warner Bros. Discovery on merger progress

WBD jumped after The Wall Street Journal reported progress in negotiations aimed at resolving legal opposition to its proposed $81 billion merger with Paramount Skydance, which rose 6%. Separately, CNN reports Paramount is in advanced talks with state attorneys general to settle the antitrust suit, though New York, Connecticut and at least two other states are not yet on board.

New+5.76%

Intel partners with AUO Optronics

The chipmaker rose after news it entered a partnership with the Taiwanese display-panel manufacturer to jointly develop Micro LED advanced packaging technology.

New−4.75%

HP warns on the 2027 PC market

HP fell after warning that it expects global PC unit volumes to shrink by mid-single digits in calendar 2027 compared with 2026.

−4.35%

Novo Nordisk fails to reassure

The drugmaker retreated after its long-term strategy and obesity ambitions failed to ease investor concerns about its ability to compete in an increasingly crowded weight-loss market.

−3.60%

Hasbro drifts with consumer discretionary

The toy and game maker gave up ground, attributed to broad macroeconomic pressure, rising interest rates and a general selloff hitting the consumer discretionary sector.

On the Radar

  • Trump–Xi summit, Thursday, Washington. Talks are expected to cover tariffs, critical minerals and artificial intelligence. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent met Chinese Vice Premier He Lifeng ahead of the visit and called the preliminary talks “very successful”; the South China Morning Post reports the two sides are discussing announcements on military-to-military ties. Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, OpenAI’s Sam Altman, former Apple CEO Tim Cook, Qualcomm’s Cristiano Amon and Microsoft’s Satya Nadella are set to attend a dinner with Xi.
  • UN General Assembly, Tuesday. Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian will lead a delegation to New York amid renewed hopes for a diplomatic track. Iranian state agency IRNA says the US has refused visas to certain members of that delegation, including its communications team.
  • Oura files to go public. The smart-ring maker and some backers are seeking as much as $2.2 billion, marketing 50 million shares at $40–$44; at the top of the range that is a $14.1 billion market value, against about $11 billion at its 2025 Series E. The company sold about 3.6 million rings in the past year.
  • AI liability. Bessent told CNBC that AI developers “need to take responsibility for themselves” rather than expect a federal liability shield: “It is humans who are responsible, not the AI.”
  • Press access litigation. CNN, MS NOW and Politico have sued to overturn their White House ban; the four other members of the TV press pool say they will stop covering the President’s events after CNN was barred from serving as designated pooler.
