Top story

GitLab’s GraphQL flaw goes from disclosure to in-the-wild exploitation in days — and watchTowr says AI is why

A newly disclosed flaw in GitLab has come under active exploitation within days of public disclosure, exposure-management firm watchTowr told The Hacker News on August 21, 2026. The bug, CVE-2026-19478 (CVSS 9.4), is a code-injection issue that lets an unauthenticated attacker modify or delete publicly accessible GitLab projects and rewrite their data — with no credentials, no user interaction and no obscure configuration required.

GitLab said in an alert earlier in the week that the issue is triggered through a GraphQL directive. The affected releases are GitLab Community Edition and Enterprise Edition 18.2 before 18.11.11, 19.0 before 19.0.8, 19.1 before 19.1.6 and 19.2 before 19.2.4; fixes shipped in 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8 and 18.11.11.

watchTowr said it reproduced the vulnerability within minutes of disclosure and then observed in-the-wild exploitation against its honeypot network. “This is the new reality of vulnerability reproduction and exploitation, where AI-enabled attackers are able to compress the time from disclosure to exploitation and ‘waiting until the next patch cycle’ is often too late,” said Jake Knott, principal security researcher at watchTowr.

The blast radius is worse than “defacement.” watchTowr noted the impact goes beyond modifying or deleting public projects: an attacker can delete entire repositories, forge merge records to make it appear as if a fix landed when it didn’t, and ban project maintainers. For anyone running an internet-facing self-hosted instance, the guidance is to upgrade now; if that is not immediately possible, restrict unauthenticated access to /api/graphql or remove public repository access entirely. Defenders who have not yet patched should hunt through web logs for requests containing @gl_introduced.

TrueConf Server — CVE-2026-72529, missing authentication for a critical function. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Thursday, August 20, 2026 on evidence of active exploitation, and set the federal remediation deadline at Saturday, August 23, 2026 — tomorrow, a three-day window. Its companion, CVE-2026-72530 (code injection), carries a longer September 3 due date. Both were fixed back in June 2026 in TrueConf Server 5.3.9, 5.4.9 and 5.5.5 — so this is a patch-application problem, not a patch-availability one. No CVSS score for either CVE was stated by any source fetched this run, so none is printed here.

Threat actor spotlight

The CameraSwarm operator — one person, 14,530 cameras, 35 days

Researchers at Hunt.io reconstructed an entire IoT campaign from the operator’s own mistake: a 407 MB exposed working directory holding 2,616 files across 234 subdirectories — tooling, logs, shell history and campaign records. Codenamed Operation CameraSwarm, it compromised more than 14,530 Dahua devices between June 17 and July 22, 2026.

Three attack paths ran in parallel. An asyncio-based scanning engine hit 12,324 unique IP addresses on port 37777, Dahua’s Easy4IP protocol port. Two authentication-bypass flaws — CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045, both five years old — were used to plant persistent backdoor accounts on 1,923 cameras. And 283 cameras were reached by serial number alone through Dahua’s own cloud relay infrastructure, bypassing the network perimeter entirely.

Confirmed compromises concentrate in Ukraine and Russia. Hunt.io described the operator as Russian-speaking based on language artifacts recovered from the directory, but was explicit that the activity has not been attributed to a named threat actor, a Russian government entity or any other known group.

Entra ID CVSS 10.0 — but not exploited

Microsoft’s bulletin for CVE-2026-69836, a maximum-severity deserialization RCE in Entra ID, originally read “Exploited: Yes.” On August 21 Microsoft corrected it to No and told The Hacker News the flaw “was not exploited in the wild.” It is fully mitigated; no customer action is required.

vCenter path traversal tied to China-nexus APT

A suspected China-nexus APT is assessed to be behind exploitation of CVE-2026-59310, the VMware vCenter Server directory-traversal flaw scored CVSS 9.8 that CISA added to KEV on August 18.
