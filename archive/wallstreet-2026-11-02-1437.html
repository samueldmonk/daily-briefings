The Lead

Rebound sputters: Dow +0.83%, S&P +0.08%, Nasdaq −0.16% (as of ~2:08 PM ET) as oil slides 7.5%

Monday’s rebound from Friday’s AI-driven rout is narrowing to a Dow-only rally. Yahoo Finance’s market strip at about 2:08 p.m. ET (“markets close in 1h 52m”; Dow “As of 2:08:34 PM EST”) showed the Dow up 428.31 points, or 0.83%, the S&P 500 up 6.29 points, or 0.08%, the Nasdaq Composite down 40.53 points, or 0.16%, and the Russell 2000 up 0.31% (Yahoo Finance). That is a steady fade from the late-morning highs — Yahoo’s text had the S&P and Nasdaq each up 0.6% and the Dow up 1.1%, and at ~1:37 p.m. the Nasdaq was still up 0.18% — while TheStreet noted at 12:55 p.m. that the S&P “continues to give up gains,” leaning on consumer defensive and industrial names as energy, technology and healthcare lag (TheStreet). The VIX was up 3.71% at 20.14 on the strip. Yahoo has since retitled its live blog “S&P 500, Nasdaq rebound sputters, Dow edges higher as oil prices fall”; a later Yahoo update surfaced by search (around 2:30 p.m.; the page itself still served the 2:08 strip when fetched) put the Dow up about 0.7% and the Nasdaq down 0.4%, with the S&P 500 wavering (Yahoo Finance). StockTitan, citing CNBC, logged the same slide: S&P 500 up 1% at 10:42 a.m., then 0.3% at 12:55 p.m. (StockTitan).

The driver is oil. December WTI was down 7.49% to about $96.32 on the Yahoo strip (~2:08 p.m.), and Brent traded near $105, on reports that the U.S. and Iran are seeking a diplomatic end to the war (Yahoo Finance); TheStreet cites Reuters on talks between Iranian and Qatari officials and a draft proposal being circulated (TheStreet).

The backdrop is still fragile. On Friday the Nasdaq fell 2.86% — its worst day since June — after Intel’s outlook sent the stock down about 28.6% and Amazon disappointed; the Nasdaq lost more than 5% in October (Reuters via Investing.com; Yahoo Finance). Apple reports after the bell (consensus EPS $2 on revenue of $113.46 billion, per Bloomberg, cited by Yahoo), Tuesday brings the U.S. midterm elections, and Friday the October jobs report (Yahoo Finance).
