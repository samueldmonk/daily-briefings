Top Story

Cisco Secure Email Gateway flaw is being exploited in the wild, and it hands attackers root

Cisco has warned that CVE-2026-76461, a CVSS 9.8 flaw in AsyncOS Software for Cisco Secure Email Gateway, is under active exploitation. The company describes it as insufficient validation in the email parsing logic: an unauthenticated, remote attacker can send a crafted message containing malicious SQL statements through an affected device and end up executing arbitrary commands with root privileges on the underlying operating system.

Both the physical and virtual appliance are affected, regardless of configuration. Cisco says Secure Email and Web Manager and Secure Web Appliance are not impacted. There are no workarounds — the fixed releases are 15.5.5-0141 (for 15.5 and earlier), 16.0.4-302 (16.0) and 16.5.0-780 (16.5).

The hunting guidance matters as much as the patch. Cisco warns that because exploitation yields root, "evidence of exploitation and indicators of compromise may be removed or hidden by the threat actors," and advises corroborating against network and firewall logs outside the appliance. Its own IoC check is to review mail_logs (and every device in a cluster) and run grep -i "COPY.*TO PROGRAM" against the IronPort text mail logs; any output may indicate malicious activity. Cisco has directly contacted Secure Email Cloud customers on whose devices malicious activity was detected, and has not disclosed the scale of the attacks.

Patch Priority

Patch Cisco Secure Email Gateway today — CVE-2026-76461, KEV deadline 17 September 2026 (1 day left)

The single most urgent item for defenders this morning. Maximum-severity-class (9.8), unauthenticated, exploited in the wild, no workaround, and a federal deadline that lands tomorrow. Upgrade to 15.5.5-0141, 16.0.4-302 or 16.5.0-780, then hunt for the COPY … TO PROGRAM pattern in mail logs across every cluster member. This is the same date used in the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Red Heron · Acronis Threat Research Unit

A suspected Chinese actor turning a fresh Gitea RCE into source-code theft and lateral movement

Acronis TRU attributes rapid exploitation of a recently disclosed Gitea vulnerability to a cluster it tracks as Red Heron. The group scanned 1,386 Gitea instances across seven countries and kept a separate dataset of 477 Taiwan-based systems. Confirmed compromises reached 13 organisations in six countries — Taiwan (4), the U.S. (4), Canada (2), Argentina (1), Qatar (1) and Sri Lanka (1) — spanning defence, election, energy, aerospace, telecommunications, government, public safety and research. The activity progressed from source-code theft to persistent access, credential collection and lateral movement, including root-level access to a three-node Proxmox cluster. Targets were classified using Simplified Chinese labels.

Arctic Wolf · late August 2026

Tens of millions of failed logins against internet-facing Fortinet VPNs

Arctic Wolf detected large-scale credential attacks against Fortinet VPN appliances in two sustained waves from 26 to 28 August 2026, across multiple U.S. customer environments, generating tens of millions of authentication failures. Researcher Kyle Siddall notes the actor used organisation-specific usernames, corporate email addresses, affiliate accounts and common administrative identities rather than generic spraying — evidence of previously collected or enumerated identity information. In one case a successful VPN authentication from 158.94.211[.]14 was followed by malicious activity in the environment.

Lumen Black Lotus Labs · 15 Sep

BambooToken uses MQTT as its command channel

A previously undocumented multi-platform malware family, found on VirusTotal in early 2026, that controls Windows and Linux systems over the MQTT messaging protocol. Assessed active since at least February 2023, used against organisations in Asia and South America, with activity detected as recently as July 2026. The operator sideloaded agents using Tendyron’s "OnKey" software — a vendor whose hardware tokens are used to verify user identities for workstation access in high-security settings.

The Hacker News · 14 Sep

Malicious Twitch extension leaked OAuth tokens for nearly 31,000 users

"Twitch Enhanced Viewer | JeetBot" forwarded users’ OAuth tokens to proxy servers run by a Russian commercial bot service. Listed with roughly 30,000 users on the Chrome Web Store (published 26 June 2025) and 604 on Firefox Add-Ons (7 July 2025); current builds forward the token inline as an &auth= query parameter on a network-layer redirect. Both listings were still downloadable at the time of writing.

ExPatch writeup · 12 Sep

Telegram Desktop HTML exports can still carry hidden JavaScript

A bot could plant hidden JavaScript in an inline-keyboard button label; the script ran when a user opened a chat exported to HTML in a browser, and could copy every message in the file to an attacker-controlled server or rewrite the page. Telegram shipped a fix in July, but the app update does not rewrite files exported with earlier versions — so old exports remain live carriers.
