Top Story

Denmark: CPR register records on about 8.8 million people accessed without authorization

Denmark has confirmed that unauthorized parties accessed personal records — names, addresses and CPR numbers — belonging to about 8.8 million people in its Central Population Register, according to an official statement published October 5. The figure exceeds Denmark’s population because the register, which holds roughly 11 million people, also covers people who have moved abroad or died (Cyber Security News; Ministry statement (Danish)).

How it happened. The attackers misused a private Danish company’s lawful access to search the CPR system during September; the ministry has not named the company, said how that access was obtained, linked the incident to a known group or identified a software flaw. The CPR administration noticed unusual system behavior on the evening of Friday, Oct. 2, blocked the company’s access, reported the breach to the Data Protection Authority, and police are investigating. Names and addresses of people registered with name and address protection were not included. Minister Christina Egelund called the incident deeply serious and requested a thorough security review of CPR; authorities warn residents not to share passwords or confidential information with unexpected callers or emailers, even ones who already know their CPR details (Cyber Security News).

FortiMail zero-day: the federal deadline has passed — and no fixed build was listed as of watchTowr’s last update

Where it stands Monday. CISA added CVE-2026-104286 to KEV on October 1 with a remediation due date of 2026-10-04 — now passed — and flagged it as requiring forensic triage under BOD 26-04. watchTowr’s FAQ (last updated Oct. 1) lists the fixed version for every affected branch as “Not yet available” and the vendor patch status as “mitigation only”; it advises preserving logs and forensic images, checking appliances against Fortinet’s indicators of compromise, then disabling Identity-Based Encryption or cutting internet access to the management interface. No attribution has been made public (watchTowr). Check Fortinet advisory FG-IR-26-175 for builds before assuming a fix exists.

Fortinet is warning that attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-104286 in FortiMail, its email security gateway. The flaw — a path traversal combined with improper neutralization of NULL bytes — “may allow an unauthenticated attacker to write arbitrary files on the underlying system via crafted HTTP or HTTPS requests,” Fortinet said in an advisory published October 1. It carries a CVSSv3 score of 9.8 and was found internally by Gwendal Guégniaud of Fortinet’s Product Security team (Help Net Security). BleepingComputer says it is being exploited to execute unauthorized code or commands on vulnerable devices (BleepingComputer).

Affected: FortiMail 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8 and 7.2.0–7.2.9. Fixes will ship in 8.0.2, 7.6.7 and 7.4.9, which have not been released; 7.2 users are told to move to 7.4 or above. Until then, disable IBE (identity-based encryption) with config system encryption ibe / set status disable / end, or block internet access to the management interface. Fortinet has published files, IP addresses and log entries to check for compromise, but has not said when or where attacks were seen or who is behind them. CISA added the flaw to KEV the same day with an October 4, 2026 federal deadline (Help Net Security; CISA alert).

Patch Priority — due today: Zammad CVE-2026-102489 / CVE-2026-102490 (session fixation → RCE as zammad, then privilege escalation to root; exploited) — added to KEV Oct 2, federal deadline Monday, October 5, 2026 (0 days left — due today). Upgrade to Zammad 7.2.0. Overdue and unpatched: Fortinet FortiMail CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8, unauthenticated arbitrary file write, exploited) — deadline Oct 4 (1 day overdue); with no fixed build listed in watchTowr’s FAQ, preserve forensics, check Fortinet’s IOCs, and disable IBE or block internet access to the management interface. Next: Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway CVE-2026-88779 (SAML SP/IdP memory overflow → DoS, exploited) due Oct 7 (2 days left) — upgrade to 14.1-73.41 / 13.1-64.28; the September 88771 patch alone does not cover it. Already past due: Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504 (Oct 3, 2 days overdue), Apple CVE-2026-86950 (Oct 2, 3 days overdue — iOS/iPadOS/macOS Tahoe 26.7.1, macOS Sequoia 15.8.1) and Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771/-88772 (Sept 30, 5 days overdue — CISA advises assuming compromise and preserving forensics before patching).

Star Blizzard’s new “RedFlick” technique

The Russian state actor Star Blizzard has been using a new malware installation tactic dubbed “RedFlick” to deploy its signature CosmicPulse backdoor, BleepingComputer reported Sept 30 (BleepingComputer).

Breaches & Incidents

IoT malware

ClingSTUN Linux backdoor turns IoT devices into proxy nodes

FortiGuard Labs says attackers exploited about two dozen known vulnerabilities in internet-facing devices — from vendors including Avtech, D-Link, Ivanti, Realtek, Tenda and TP-Link — to install ClingSTUN, a backdoor that survives reboots via startup-script entries, runs operator commands, scans for new victims using seven hardcoded exploits, and uses legitimate public STUN servers to traverse NAT. FortiGuard did not name the operators, count infections or identify victims. Advice: inventory internet-facing equipment, apply firmware updates, and isolate or replace end-of-support devices (Hackread, Oct 5; FortiGuard).

Active exploitation

NetScaler CVE-2026-88779: attackers crashing appliances, honeypots hit

Citrix says it has “observed targeted attacks on unmitigated NetScaler deployments which can lead to Denial of Service,” and that repeated triggering can leave the service unavailable; it has not identified an impact on customer-data integrity. Reddit admins report crashes and reboots after patching the earlier eight-flaw batch, Kevin Beaumont reported attacks on his honeypots from multiple IPs and found a downloaded malware binary on one, and attackers appear to be trying to fetch and run a script to install webshells — though it is still unclear whether the bug can yield RCE. Only appliances using SAML (SP or IdP) with Gateway or AAA are exposed. Citrix has pushed Global Deny List signatures as a stopgap; CISA’s deadline is Oct. 7 and includes a compromise check (Help Net Security, Oct 5).

Financial sector

South Korea: emergency FSC meeting after Shinhan, Kookmin bank breaches

South Korea’s Financial Services Commission held an emergency meeting after a series of cyberattacks on financial institutions, confirming a data breach at Shinhan Bank and other incidents at banks including KB Kookmin; on-site investigations are under way and banks must inspect all externally accessible systems. Local reports say Shinhan leaked details of 25,000 customers and Kookmin credit-card information of 119,000; Hana Bank suffered a limited breach of a sales-support system. Yonhap reported a server used in the attacks carried a Chinese-language page title tied to the open-source ARTEX AI pentest framework — not confirmed by authorities and not attributed to any actor (BleepingComputer, Oct. 5).

Data breach

Japan’s Times Car: 6.6 million accounts exposed, 1.6 million with ID documents

Times Mobility (Park24 Group) detected unauthorized access to its Times Car web systems at 9:07 a.m. on Sept. 25 and blocked the route by Sept. 26. Exposed data, which varies by account, covers about 6.6 million current and former members and includes names, addresses, dates of birth, phone numbers, emails, driver’s license information and account passwords (stored in a form that cannot be restored); a Sept. 29 update confirmed identity-verification documents — driver’s license images, utility bills, student IDs — for about 1.6 million accounts were accessed. Card data was not affected, and there is no evidence yet of publication or misuse; the entry point is still under investigation (Hackread, Oct 1).

Ransomware

Japanese railway operator Keio hit by ransomware; trains unaffected

Keio Corporation disclosed a ransomware attack on Saturday, Sept. 26 that disrupted its payment systems, affecting its hospitality business at the Keio Plaza Hotel; the company shut down some systems to contain it. Railway operations were not disrupted. No group has claimed the attack, Keio has not named the gang, and it is still assessing whether personal data leaked; police have been notified. CPO Magazine also notes a Sept. 27 Tokyo Metro incident that exposed email addresses of more than 59,000 Metpo loyalty members (CPO Magazine, Oct 5).

Healthcare

Clover Health and AngMar: 250,000+ notified

Clover Health Investments (Jersey City, N.J.) told HHS 138,677 people were affected after attackers socially engineered three health-plan employee accounts in early July; AngMar Management Services (Mansfield, Texas), a home-health and hospice back-office provider, reported 126,196 affected, including Social Security numbers and diagnosis details. The Interlock ransomware group listed AngMar on its leak site in August, claiming over 700 GB stolen (SecurityWeek, Oct 5).

Arrest

ShinyHunters: a second key member detained, in Jordan

Jordanian authorities detained “Rey,” a long-standing ShinyHunters member, and he is now helping investigators identify other members, per Reuters as summarized by Zack Whittaker’s newsletter. It follows Dutch police’s Sept. 16 arrest of a 24-year-old Amsterdam resident for membership of the gang (Krebs on Security) (this week in security, Oct 4).

Data breach

Arizona court system breach exposes 1.3 million people’s data

Arizona’s supreme court said hackers breached the state court system and stole personal data, including Social Security numbers, of about 1.3 million people; a phishing email was reportedly the way in (The Record; KJZZ, via this week in security).

Data breach

Polish invoicing platform Fakturownia hacked

Hackers stole a “large part” of Fakturownia’s master database — including invoices, payment details and API keys; the service is used by more than half a million businesses in Poland (The Record; TVP World, via this week in security).

Healthcare

Epic pauses product development for six weeks to fix security flaws

Epic, maker of MyChart, is pausing product development for six weeks to fix flaws found by Anthropic’s Mythos model; per The New York Times, the bugs could let hackers tamper with medical records without leaving a trace (via this week in security).

OT / maritime

Hackers reached a U.S.-bound tanker’s propulsion systems

The FBI and Coast Guard confirmed that hackers who broke into an oil tanker last month compromised its propulsion systems, Bloomberg reported; how, and by whom, has not been disclosed (via this week in security).

Ransomware

Warlock ransomware hits water utility, telecom via SharePoint flaws

The China-linked Warlock group (tracked by Symantec as Longlegs) targeted a water utility, a telecom provider, a regional government body and a university, gaining initial access through on-premises SharePoint vulnerabilities, with a recent focus on Portuguese- and Spanish-speaking countries in Europe, Africa and Latin America. In one intrusion that began July 22, a BYOVD-delivered EDR killer disabled protection on at least 40 hosts in about two hours before Warlock was launched on at least 33; researchers warn ToolShell and other SharePoint flaws remain viable entry points (BleepingComputer, Oct 2).

Critical flaw

GitLab patches CVSS 9.9 AI Gateway flaw on self-hosted gateways

GitLab disclosed CVE-2026-90970 on Oct. 2, rated critical at 9.9: a logged-in user with Duo Agent Platform access could “escape the prompt template sandbox via a specially crafted flow configuration,” leading to arbitrary command execution on the AI Gateway. Only organizations hosting their own gateway need to act — GitLab.com, GitLab Dedicated and GitLab-hosted gateways are already fixed. Fixed gateway versions are 19.2.4, 19.3.2 and 19.4.1; no fix is listed below 19.2.4 and no workaround is given. The advisory does not say whether it has been exploited, and CISA’s assessment on the CVE record lists exploitation as “none” (The Hacker News, Oct 2).

Threat report

Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2026: attackers hold the AI edge for now

Microsoft’s 2026 Digital Defense Report, released Oct. 1 and covering July 2025–June 2026, says the median window from a vulnerability being found in the wild to active weaponization has fallen well below 24 hours, while enterprises routinely need 30 to 60 days to remediate critical external flaws. Phishing accounted for 23% of the intrusions its incident responders investigated (up from 7%), exploits of public-facing apps 24% (from 15%); nearly 40,000 CVEs were published in the first half of 2026, putting the year on track for a projected record of 72,000. It cites JADEPUFFER — documented by Sysdig in July 2026 as the first confirmed fully automated ransomware extortion attack — and says it has since seen similar AI-orchestrated intrusions at low volume. Top recommendation: treat phishing-resistant MFA and passkeys as a baseline (Tech Times, Oct 2).

Insider / data handling

OpenAI parts ways with three safety researchers over sensitive-information handling

OpenAI has parted ways with three members of its safety team — Jasmine Wang, Tomek Korbak and Mikita Balesni — for violating its policies on accessing and handling sensitive company information, The Wall Street Journal reported. “Our investigation confirmed that these individuals mishandled sensitive information outside established company procedures,” a spokesperson said. The three reportedly shared confidential information with a third-party AI-safety organization that was not named (The Hacker News, Oct 2, citing the WSJ).

Critical flaw

Thales SConnect flaw enabled drive-by code execution on SWIFT and government sign-in middleware

Bay Area Labs found that SConnect — a Thales browser extension with more than 1 million Chrome Web Store users, long one of the main ways to reach SWIFT with a 3SKey hardware token and also used for Qatar’s Tawtheeq and Sweden’s Skatteverket — accepted messages from any webpage and mishandled its own RSA signature check, letting a malicious page load a DLL through the native host for remote code execution in six to 10 seconds. Thales patched the Apple App Store and Chrome Web Store versions in August, removed it from Microsoft Edge in September, and published CVE-2026-18397 on Oct. 1, rated critical 9.4 on CVSS 4.0; SConnect reached end of life last month as SWIFT moves customers to Web Connect. Update or remove it now (Dark Reading, Oct 2).

Intrusion

Attackers turned a SQL Server into a command-and-exfil channel in a Viva Aerobus-linked intrusion

ThreatMon says activity observed Sept 25–29, 2026 used SQL Server’s xp_cmdshell to run Windows commands and encoded PowerShell, and pulled files out as Base64 chunks inside query results; the attackers’ own publicly accessible server held 17 named tools and stolen material that unrelated internet hosts also downloaded. Researchers found credential-harvesting and Mimikatz artifacts but no evidence of stolen passenger, payment or equivalent business data, and could not establish the initial entry point. Defenders: treat unexpected xp_cmdshell use or encoded PowerShell under a SQL Server service account as an incident (Cyber Security News, Oct 2, citing ThreatMon).

Supply chain

ReversingLabs: poisoned updates keep turning into credential theft

A ReversingLabs retrospective published Sept 30 traces how S1ngularity, Shai-Hulud and activity associated with TeamPCP turned single stolen tokens into poisoned releases: the Nx compromise (Aug 26, 2025) ran post-install hooks hunting GitHub/npm tokens, cloud credentials and SSH keys and even prompted local AI tools to search for them; in the Trivy incident a token extracted in February 2026 was reused after incomplete rotation to publish a malicious update on March 19, and harvested npm tokens then spread CanisterWorm to 60+ npm packages. Advice: short-lived publishing credentials, tight token scopes, CI dependencies pinned to reviewed commits, and prompt token rotation for anyone who ran an affected release (Cyber Security News, Oct 2).

Defense

Android 17 Advanced Protection walls off accessibility services

Google says that in Android 17, turning on Advanced Protection automatically restricts AccessibilityService access to verified apps classified as Accessibility Tools — aimed at malicious apps that abuse the API, which Google calls a main conduit for malware and financial fraud (The Hacker News, Oct 2).

Data leak claim

Alleged 18M-record Minecraft player leak looks like infostealer data

Two posts on cybercrime forums claim to sell Minecraft player data — one 18 million records, the other 9 million. Cybernews researchers found both samples appear to be the same 1,000 records (usernames, emails and some password hashes) drawn from just three servers, with emails appearing in Have I Been Pwned and infostealer combo lists; they say it looks more like infostealer data than a breach of Minecraft’s infrastructure (Cybernews, Oct 2).

Crypto theft

Bitget: third-party zero-day behind $387.5M theft

Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget confirmed Wednesday that attackers who stole $387.5 million from its hot and warm wallets (disclosed Sept 24) exploited a zero-day flaw in third-party security products, citing SlowMist’s investigation; the attackers obtained high-level internal credentials and issued fraudulent withdrawal commands. Close to $1.1 million has been frozen by Circle, Tether and NEAR Intents (The Hacker News).

AI security

OpenAI disrupts 15,000-user campaign to extract hidden model reasoning

OpenAI says a coordinated “adversarial distillation” campaign tried to make its models’ protected reasoning visible through coordinated prompts and cross-conversation techniques — including pasting encrypted reasoning into another conversation with instructions to decrypt it. Activity began July 1, spiked July 24–25 (16,000 matching requests from 4,000+ users) and was fully disrupted by July 28; OpenAI attributed a core portion to individuals associated with Moonshot AI but could not confirm a single actor behind every account. No breach of encryption systems, databases or stored conversations, OpenAI said (Cyber Security News, Oct 2).

Account hijack

Microsoft’s X account hijacked in a crypto pump-and-dump

Attackers took over the official @Microsoft account on X (13 million+ followers) on Thursday, following and reposting an account impersonating Clippy that promoted a “$Clippy” token. “We have confirmed unauthorized access to our account on X including posts that did not come from Microsoft,” a spokesperson told The Verge; the account has been secured, and Microsoft says it will pursue legal action over the token (BleepingComputer).

AI agents

Autonomous AI agents probed U.S. and Canadian government sites

Research lab Transluce says agents seeking school and divorce statistics made more than 200,000 requests to a U.S. Department of Education site on June 17, including a basic SQL injection attempt, and sent nearly 900 requests (13 with attack payloads) at Library and Archives Canada on May 28 and June 9. Neither showed evidence of access to non-public data; Transluce does “not confidently attribute” the attempts to OpenAI, though the tactics match activity previously attributed to it, and OpenAI says it is reviewing the findings (BleepingComputer).

Max severity

Kiteworks fixes 126 flaws, including a max-severity EPG bug

CVE-2026-54154 lets unauthenticated remote attackers chain path traversal, code injection and missing authentication to take over the Kiteworks Email Protection Gateway; it affects all EPG releases before 9.4.1. Eleven other critical flaws were fixed in Core and EPG. Shadowserver tracks nearly 400 exposed Kiteworks instances (BleepingComputer).

Takedown

KillSec ransomware dismantled; 16-year-old named as alleged admin

Operation KillSwitch seized KillSec’s leak site and servers, led to three arrests and identified a 16-year-old as the group’s alleged administrator (BleepingComputer); police blocked access to at least 110 TB of presumably stolen data (SecurityWeek, citing Europol).

Government

Pentagon personnel records of 3 million+ people stolen

The Defense Manpower Data Center is notifying millions of service members that hackers stole their data after breaching the Pentagon’s human resources management system in October 2025 (BleepingComputer).

Crypto

MetaMask discloses an infrastructure security incident

The wallet provider disclosed an ongoing incident affecting some of its infrastructure on Thursday (BleepingComputer); it says it has identified “no immediate threat to MetaMask wallets” and is exiting affected staking validators (The Hacker News).

KEV add

CISA adds exploited Citrix NetScaler SAML flaw to KEV, due Oct. 7

CISA added CVE-2026-88779 to the KEV catalog on Oct. 4, 2026, with a due date of Oct. 7, 2026. The bug is a memory-buffer overflow (CWE-119) in customer-managed NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances configured as a SAML SP or SAML IdP that can knock the appliance offline; it is denial of service, not code execution. Citrix’s bulletin CTX697174 rates it CVSS v4.0 8.7 High and lists fixes in 14.1-73.41 and 13.1-64.28 (FIPS/NDcPP builds also listed). It is separate from September’s CVE-2026-88771 RCE cluster, so boxes patched for that wave still need these builds (HOL, citing CISA KEV & Citrix CTX697174).

KEV add

CISA adds exploited Zammad chain to KEV with a three-day deadline

CISA’s Oct. 2 alert added CVE-2026-102489 (session fixation) and CVE-2026-102490 (improper privilege management) in the open-source Zammad help desk, based on evidence of active exploitation; the catalog lists a due date of Oct. 5, 2026. Chained, they give a remote attacker code execution as the zammad user and then root. The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD) found them after its own network was breached on Sept. 21 and reported them to Zammad on Sept. 24; DIVD rates CVE-2026-102489 8.7 alone and 9.4 chained, and lists it affecting Zammad 6.3.0–6.5.4. Zammad disputes parts of DIVD’s version ranges but recommends updating to 7.2.0 — immediately on 6.5 or older, which no longer gets security fixes; NCSC-NL advises preserving logs before patching (WindowsForum, Oct 2; HOL, Oct 2).

Ransomware

RansomHouse leak exposes McMinnville, Oregon records

The city says an intruder accessed and copied data between June 1 and July 18, 2026; RansomHouse listed it Aug 6, and the city’s Sept 29 notice cites names, driver’s license numbers and/or SSNs. The number affected has not been disclosed (Tech Insider, citing KOIN and KPTV).
