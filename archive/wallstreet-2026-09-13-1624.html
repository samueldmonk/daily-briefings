The Lead

Stocks closed the week with a rebound they may not get to keep — because the bond market never joined in

Markets are shut for the weekend, so the most recent session is Friday 11 September. That day the Dow, S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite all snapped four-session losing streaks, rebounding after oil prices eased off their recent spurt and August CPI landed close to expectations — consumer prices +3.4% year over year, matching both economists’ forecasts and the July figure (Yahoo Finance, CNBC, TheStreet).

The bond market read the same data differently. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed almost 20 basis points on the week to 4.97%, finishing on the cusp of 5%, as inflation fears from crude well above $100 collided with rising odds of a near-term rate hike (Bloomberg; Reuters via Investing.com). Rate-futures pricing for a 25 bp hike at Wednesday’s FOMC jumped to roughly 90%, from about 70% beforehand, after the hotter-than-expected monthly core CPI reading — and Bloomberg today (13 September) headlines the next question directly: a 5% Treasury yield “is raising new risks for markets, economy.”
