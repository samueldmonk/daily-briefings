Top Story

The Cisco ISE maximum-severity bypass is now a federal problem — CISA added it to KEV today

This morning’s story has changed category. CVE-2026-76460, the CVSS 10.0 authentication bypass in Cisco Identity Services Engine, was added to CISA’s Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 16 September — catalogued as a Cisco Identity Services Engine Incorrect Use of Privileged APIs Vulnerability. Earlier editions today reported, correctly at the time, that no KEV entry could be confirmed for it. That is now superseded: it is listed.

The technical shape of the flaw explains the score. The vulnerability is due to insufficient authentication control on an API endpoint. An attacker sends crafted network requests directly to an exposed endpoint and the request bypasses the web management interface entirely — no credentials, no session, no interface to log it. From there an attacker can execute commands with root privileges and erase log entries to conceal their actions.

That last capability is what makes absence of evidence worthless here. An intrusion through this endpoint can remove its own traces, so “we checked the logs and found nothing” is not a clean bill of health on an unpatched box.

It was found the worst way. Cisco has said the flaw was discovered during the resolution of a Cisco TAC support case — meaning at least one enterprise was already compromised before anyone knew the bug existed. Affected: Cisco ISE and ISE-PIC, releases 3.1 through 3.5, across all device configurations. Cisco has released patches. There are no workarounds.

The strategic point is the one worth carrying: ISE is the system that decides what is allowed onto the network. An attacker who owns it can manipulate access rules and pivot deeper into private corporate subnets. The gatekeeper is the door.

Patch Priority

Patch Cisco ISE / ISE-PIC 3.1–3.5 for CVE-2026-76460 — today

Maximum severity (CVSS 10.0), confirmed exploited in the wild, no workaround, and added to CISA KEV today, 16 September. Cisco has shipped fixed releases. If you cannot patch immediately, get the API endpoint off any untrusted network path — the attack arrives as a crafted request straight to an exposed endpoint, bypassing the management interface.

Assume nothing from your logs. The flaw permits root command execution and deletion of log entries, so an unpatched, internet-reachable ISE with a clean log is not evidence of a clean system. Treat hunting as a separate exercise from patching.

On the federal deadline: the KEV addition is confirmed for 16 September 2026. The specific remediation due date CISA assigned to this entry was not stated by any source fetched this run, and the CISA alert pages returned empty bodies on direct fetch, so no countdown is asserted here. Check the KEV catalog entry directly for your binding date.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Tracked since June 2026 · figures as of 14 September

SETTRA — first seen in June, already at 64 claimed victims

SETTRA is a ransomware and data-extortion group first publicly observed in June 2026. As of 14 September 2026 it had publicly claimed 64 victims on its leak site, ten of them in the preceding 30 days — a rate that has not slowed as the brand matured.

Its victim concentration is unusually legible: Technology 22%, Professional Services 16%, Manufacturing 14%, spanning 18 countries. That mix — technology and professional services first — describes a group hunting organisations that hold other organisations’ data, where the extortion leverage is somebody else’s breach notification.

Context for the pace: the ransomware ecosystem reached 127 active groups by the close of the April 2025–March 2026 period and 146 by June 2026, with August 2026 the highest month on record for active reported groups.
