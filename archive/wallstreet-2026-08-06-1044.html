The Lead

As of late morning: the tape flips — the Dow slips and risks its record streak while chip stocks reverse and pull the Nasdaq green, with Friday's jobs report looming

Thursday keeps changing its mind at the highs. After opening with the Dow up and the Nasdaq down, the latest reads flipped: as of late morning, roughly 10:30 AM ET, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down about 0.2% — putting its record-breaking win streak at risk — while the S&P 500 held just above the flat line and the Nasdaq Composite rose about 0.2% as chip stocks reversed early declines (Yahoo Finance live coverage; TheStreet's Thursday headline has the S&P 500 rising as investors weigh jobs data). Semis did the lifting: Broadcom rose nearly 2%, Marvell Technology climbed 3% and Nvidia traded in the green (Yahoo Finance). Early in the session, 8 of 11 S&P 500 sectors traded higher, with materials leading at +2.87% and energy lagging at −0.96% (StockTitan sector board). Investors are absorbing what Yahoo called "a wall of earnings reports, prospects of an imminent Strait of Hormuz deal, and fresh labor market data."

The labor data leaned reassuring ahead of Friday's main event. First-time jobless claims ticked up slightly to 199,000 in the week of August 1 (Yahoo Finance) — still below the ~203,000 forecast (Investing.com) — and Challenger, Gray & Christmas reported U.S. employers announced just 33,429 job cuts in July, the lowest monthly tally in two years, down 27% from June's 45,849 and 46% below a year earlier (Challenger via Trading Economics/Investing.com). That sets up Friday's July payrolls report, where consensus sits in the low-to-mid 80,000s — Yahoo-cited forecasts run ~83,000 with a 4.3% unemployment rate, Schwab cites 86,000 — after June's +57,000 and Wednesday's soft +44,000 ADP print.

Earnings kept doing the sorting — brutally, in memory. Western Digital shed 18% after its first-quarter forecast disappointed and Sandisk dropped 13% on fiscal fourth-quarter results that failed to impress (Yahoo Finance) — far deeper than their premarket slides — while Datadog fell 16% in early trade on weaker forward revenue guidance (StockMarketWatch), roughly the move the options market had priced in (Market Rebellion). SpaceX faces its next test today as its IPO lockup expires, with more than 900 million shares — about $101 billion worth — becoming eligible for sale; the stock, still near all-time lows after Wednesday's slide below its $135 June IPO price, was up 1.7% premarket (Benzinga; Yahoo Finance; StockMarketWatch; TheStreet Aug 5). For context on Wednesday's close: the Dow rose 263.24 points (+0.49%) to a third straight record at 54,349.12 while the S&P 500 (−0.17%) and Nasdaq (−0.83%) snapped four-day streaks, and Nvidia rose over 3% after SpaceX said it would exclusively use Nvidia chips (Yahoo Finance).

Movers & Drivers

▼ Guidance

Datadog (DDOG)

The session's headline casualty: the cloud-monitoring and security platform fell 16% after issuing weaker forward revenue guidance, with investors also weighing broader software-sector AI concerns and insider selling pressure (StockMarketWatch live read). The move roughly matched what the options market had braced for — the Aug 7 weekly straddle was priced for about a 15% swing into the results (Market Rebellion pre-market IV report).

◆ Lockup day

SpaceX (SPCX)

Today's test: the IPO lockup expires, making more than 900 million shares — about $101 billion worth — eligible for sale (Benzinga; StockMarketWatch). The stock was up 1.7% in premarket trading but remains near all-time lows (Benzinga; Yahoo Finance) after Wednesday's double-digit slide took it below its $135 June IPO price, far off the $225.64 record from June 16 (TheStreet, Aug 5). Yahoo frames the risk simply: pressure "should insiders sell their shares."

New▼ Memory

Western Digital (WDC) & Sandisk (SNDK)

The premarket dip became an intraday rout: Western Digital shed 18% after its first-quarter forecast disappointed investors, and Sandisk dropped 13% on fiscal fourth-quarter results that failed to impress (Yahoo Finance live blog). Both had shown only ~5.4% declines on Yahoo's premarket board. Schwab had flagged the setup going in: with the global memory shortage lifting valuations, "winning the telephone game" of whisper numbers was the bar to clear — neither cleared it.

New▲ Reversal

Chips turn the Nasdaq green

The Nasdaq began the session lower, then rose about 0.2% as chip stocks reversed early declines — Broadcom up nearly 2%, Marvell Technology up 3%, and Nvidia in the green (Yahoo Finance live blog). Nvidia, which rose over 3% Wednesday on SpaceX's exclusive-chips commitment, had been up another ~1% on an early live read (StockMarketWatch). The reversal is selective: Micron dropped 5.6% in early trade and the memory names stayed pinned, so the "AI capital requirements and monetization" sorting continues inside the complex, not just around it.

▲ Earnings

Eli Lilly (LLY)

Wednesday's earnings star, and the fuel under the Dow's record run: Lilly climbed nearly 5% after EPS of $8.38 beat the FactSet consensus by $2.37 on revenue up 47.7% to $22.97 billion vs. $20.69 billion expected — Mounjaro sales rose 91%, Zepbound's U.S. revenue rose 44%, and the company raised fiscal 2026 revenue guidance above consensus (Schwab Market Update).

Chart of the Day — Western Digital (WDC)

The session's biggest sourced mover: down 18% by late morning after a disappointing first-quarter forecast (Yahoo Finance).

Sector Heat — live

Early Thursday, 8 of 11 S&P 500 sectors traded higher — materials led at +2.87% while energy lagged at −0.96% (StockTitan sector board). Breadth backdrop: about 71% of S&P 500 stocks were above their 200-day moving averages as of Tuesday, up from 55% at the June peak (Schwab).
