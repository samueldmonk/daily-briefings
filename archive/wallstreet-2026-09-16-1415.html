The Lead

The Fed hikes for the first time since 2023, lifting the target range to 3.75%–4.00%

The Federal Open Market Committee raised the target range for the federal funds rate by a quarter point, to 3.75%–4.00%, at 2:00 p.m. ET on Wednesday — the central bank’s first rate increase since 2023, and the first policy change of Chair Kevin Warsh’s tenure. Rates had been held at 3.50%–3.75% since December.

Two live blogs carried the outcome as this edition was built. Yahoo Finance headlined its Fed live blog “Fed hikes interest rates for first time in 3 years in unanimous decision”, and CNN Business headlined its own “Fed raises interest rates for the first time since 2023”. Both outlets report a hike; the unanimous characterisation is Yahoo Finance’s. Reporting on the updated Summary of Economic Projections indicates the median official expects one further hike this year.

The market read, minutes after the decision. On a Yahoo Finance quote reading timed at about 2:06 p.m. ET, the Nasdaq Composite was +0.83% at 26,197.96, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was −0.03% at 52,079.90, and the Russell 2000 was +0.50% at 2,884.77. Those levels reconcile against Tuesday’s verified closes, which is why they are printed: 26,197.96 against a Nasdaq close of 25,981.57 is +0.83%, and 52,079.90 against a Dow close of 52,093.11 is a shade under flat. The hike landed without breaking the tape.

It arrived almost fully priced. CME FedWatch odds on a quarter-point move were cited at 92.5% in TheStreet’s live blog today, 92.7% on Yahoo Finance Wednesday morning and 93% by Kiplinger on Monday — each printed with its own attribution rather than averaged. Deutsche Bank strategists had written that a hold “would be the biggest dovish surprise at a scheduled FOMC meeting on record”, going back to 1994.

The morning’s one hard data point cut the same way. August retail sales rose 1.2%, per the Census Bureau, rebounding from July’s downwardly revised 0.6% drop — a rebound Kiplinger reported economists reading as strengthening the case for a hike. “We also expect the Fed will raise rates to address the inflationary pressures coming from the demand side of the economy,” said Jeffrey Roach, chief economist at LPL Financial.

Two things had leaned against the tape all week: a bond sell-off that carried the 10-year Treasury yield to a 19-year high on Tuesday, and crude above $100 a barrel. Both eased on Wednesday, and the chip complex supplied the bid.

Chair Warsh’s press conference was scheduled for 2:30 p.m. ET. Nothing from it is reported on this page — no remark, framing or guidance is attributed to him here. The vote breakdown beyond the word “unanimous” in Yahoo Finance’s headline, the dot-plot grid and the full statement text were not independently read for this edition and are not reproduced. The next edition carries the press conference.

Semis · up · 1:24 p.m. ET

Intel and AMD carry the Nasdaq to 26,151

The Nasdaq Composite’s 0.7% lead came “on strength in several semiconductor stocks”, Kiplinger reported, naming Intel (INTC) +4.6% and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) +3.8%. Intel was up 3.84% in premarket trade on the Reuters report that it is in exploratory talks with SK Hynix about making memory chips on U.S. soil for the first time — one scenario has SK Hynix leasing part of Intel’s long-planned Ohio facility, another a venture with cloud companies. One source stressed no decisions have been made, and opposition from Seoul is described as a major hurdle.

On the Radar

  • 2:30 p.m. ET — Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference, his first following a policy change. TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre framed the stakes before the decision: “Investors want to know whether this is the start of a series of hikes or just fast, preemptive action that ends here.” He added that a hike paired with reassuring language could calm the bond market, while one with hawkish or vague commentary could push yields higher and take stocks down with them.
  • Two more central banks this week. The Bank of England is widely expected to hold on Thursday, and the Bank of Japan is expected to raise by a quarter point on Friday, which would take its rate to the highest in 31 years, per Reuters. The ECB raised for the second time this year last Thursday.
  • The vote, as it was forecast. Deutsche Bank economists said the “only necessary change” to the statement is the move to 3.75–4.00%, and their base case was a unanimous decision — adding that if there were dissents, Governors Waller and Bowman were the candidates. In July the Fed held, with three dissents to hike: Cleveland’s Beth Hammack, Minneapolis’s Neel Kashkari and Dallas’s Lorie Logan.
  • The former officials are near-unanimous. In Jon Hilsenrath’s September survey run with Duke’s economics department, 29 of 32 former governors, regional bank presidents and staff said the Fed should raise this week; one said hold, two did not answer.
  • What a hike might do to stocks. Per JPMorgan analysis cited by CNBC, a 25-basis-point hike with no guidance — the consensus view — would contain the back end of the curve and could lift the S&P 500 0.25% to 0.75%. The longer-run history is less kind: across six tightening cycles over the last 30-plus years, the S&P 500 fell an average 3.4% in the month after the initial hike.
  • The inflation backdrop. August CPI ran at 3.4% year over year, well above the Fed’s 2% goal, while core CPI slowed to 2.4% from 2.5% in July — the one number Freedom Capital Markets’ Jay Woods suggests Warsh could cite if he wanted to hold.
  • The bond market is doing the arguing. “The Federal Reserve is under pressure from the bond market to hike rates, as it’s not customary for the Fed funds rate to remain this far below where bond yields are trading,” said Brent Wilsey of Wilsey Asset Management.
  • Overseas: European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed opening the door for Canada to become the EU’s first “associate member”; the status is new and its terms are undefined, and it would need approval from EU member states.
