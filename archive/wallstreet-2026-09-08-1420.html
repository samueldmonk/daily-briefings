  • A Tuesday session summary has the Dow down 1%, the S&P 500 down roughly 0.4% and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.1% as the trading day progressed. Movers & Drivers

Expanded−14%

Novartis is the day's biggest single-name move

Novartis (NVS) is down 14% to $137.63 in Tuesday afternoon trade after pelacarsen, an experimental heart drug developed with Ionis Pharmaceuticals, failed its pivotal Phase III study. The drug did lower lipoprotein(a) — the lipid particle it targets — but did not reduce the risk of cardiovascular death, heart attack or stroke against placebo. Novartis announced the result on 4 September.

This is Novartis's second failed trial in the run-up to today's session, not its only one. A sector note read this run describes health care weakness led by Novartis after “not one but two failed drug trials in different drugs”. The other, carried from this briefing's earlier edition today, is del-desiran, whose Phase 3 results showed no significant improvement in patients with myotonic dystrophy type 1. The fall has deepened through the day: this briefing's 11:44 a.m. ET snapshot carried −12%, against −14% in the read above.

NewHealth care

Health care is now the worst sector on the board

Amgen (AMGN) is down 10% to $394.38 at midday, interrupting a 23% year-to-date gain heading into the session; a second read has it down over 8% and on track for its worst single day since 2016. Amgen is developing olpasiran, an Lp(a)-lowering siRNA therapy in the Phase III OCEAN(a)-Outcomes study, and the Novartis miss forces investors to mark down its odds. BMO Capital Markets downgraded Amgen the same day. The whole sector followed: health care is down over 2%, with the Health Care Select Sector SPDR (XLV) off 2.46% and DYN and SRPT also pressured.

ExpandedEnergy

Houthi strikes on Saudi energy sites put Brent near $100

Saudi Arabia paused operations at some energy facilities near the Yemen border after attacks by Iran-aligned Houthis, who claimed the 400,000-barrel-a-day Jazan refinery and other domestic-market facilities. The Saudi Press Agency attributed the strikes to the Houthis; 73 civilians were injured. Brent rose 1% to $97.99 by 11:28 a.m. ET after a session high of $99.46; U.S. crude rose more than 2.5% to nearly $94, while a second read has WTI up 1.55% at $92.90. Both crude reads agree on direction and disagree on level, so both are shown.

ExpandedTrade

Canada's retaliatory tariffs took effect after midnight

Canada's counter-tariffs came into force just after midnight Tuesday as Prime Minister Mark Carney escalated an 18-month-old trade war. New detail this run: the measures cover $20 billion of U.S. goods at duties ranging from 15% to 50% — a range, where earlier editions of this briefing had only “up to 50%” — across products from steel and furniture to clothing and electronics. Talks collapsed last month; Carney has said Canada would match Washington's new tariffs dollar for dollar.
