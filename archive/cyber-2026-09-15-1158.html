Top Story

Software supply chain · Wiz

Attackers took administrator control of internet-facing Artifactory servers and left Rust backdoors behind

Cloud security company Wiz has documented attacks chaining flaws in JFrog Artifactory — the repository that software build pipelines pull from — to take administrator control of self-hosted servers and plant backdoors. Wiz observed the activity between 15 August and 8 September 2026. JFrog had fixed the flaws before that window opened, so only servers that had not been updated were exposed.

The chain is a token-handling failure rather than a memory-safety bug. CVE-2026-42018 makes Artifactory hand an internal anonymous-user token to a caller who has not logged in, even when anonymous access is turned off. CVE-2026-42016 then allows that low-privilege token to be swapped for one with administrator scope, because Artifactory checks a token’s signature and who issued it but not what the token is allowed to do. A third CVE, CVE-2026-82329, is named alongside them in part of the coverage.

The count is not settled and is printed as found. BleepingComputer and The Hacker News describe attackers chaining two flaws; SecurityWeek and CyberSecurityNews headline three Artifactory flaws exploited for backdoor deployment. Both framings are in circulation and neither is treated here as having superseded the other.

What the attackers did with the access is the part that outlasts the patch. Across the compromised servers they created administrator accounts and left them in place; they installed malicious Groovy plugins through Artifactory’s own plugin framework, which gives code execution on the server; and in multiple cases they dropped a custom Rust backdoor with command-and-control features. A patch closes the door; it does not remove an administrator account or a plugin that is already resident. JFrog Artifactory was among the seven vulnerabilities CISA added to the KEV catalog on 2 September, alongside Sangoma Switchvox, Kludex Starlette, Kestra OSS, BerriAI LiteLLM and SonicWall SMA1000.

Patch Priority

Cisco Secure Email Gateway — CVE-2026-76461, CVSS 9.8, federal deadline Wednesday 17 September (2 days left)

This remains the single most urgent item on the board, and it outranks the Artifactory story above on urgency even though that story is larger. An unauthenticated remote attacker sends a crafted email carrying malicious SQL and executes arbitrary operating-system commands as root, against physical and virtual appliances in any configuration. Secure Email and Web Manager and Secure Web Appliance are not affected. Cisco has published indicators of compromise while noting that an attacker with root can remove them — which is why its guidance for virtual appliances is a rebuild plus full credential and key rotation, not a patch. CISA added it on 14 September with a 17 September due date; that date is the one used in the KEV section below and in the banner above.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ExtortionNew

ShinyHunters — the help desk is the attack surface

The same name sits behind two separate items on this page. ShinyHunters told BleepingComputer it was behind the McKesson intrusion, saying it gained access by running voice phishing against multiple McKesson employees. Microsoft, separately, attributes an ongoing campaign against corporate Microsoft 365 accounts to threat actors linked to ShinyHunters, Helix and other extortion gangs. The tradecraft is consistent across both: research the target organisation and its staff, then call or message employees while impersonating the corporate IT help desk. The group has been observed registering .claims domains containing the names or abbreviations of targeted companies to make the impersonation land. No exploit is involved at the point of entry.

Breaches & Incidents

NewHealthcare

McKesson — third-party applications, data exfiltrated

McKesson discovered the incident on 25 August 2026 and filed a Form 8-K three days later disclosing unauthorised access to third-party applications and the exfiltration of data. On 29 August it narrowed the scope publicly to a subset of customers within its Oncology & Multispecialty and Medical-Surgical units — two segments accounting for roughly 15% of a company that reported $403.4 billion in fiscal 2026 revenue. ShinyHunters separately claims the theft of 284 million records; that figure is the attacker’s claim, not a company statement, and is reported as such.

NewIdentity

Passkey-themed phishing into Microsoft 365

Microsoft has disclosed campaigns using passkey- and SSO-themed social engineering to compromise corporate Microsoft accounts and steal data from Microsoft 365. Activity has been observed since May 2026. Employees are told they must urgently update a passkey, MFA or SSO configuration or lose access, then directed to phishing sites built to resemble Microsoft login pages — with links sometimes sent by SMS to personal phones, outside corporate filtering entirely. One campaign sent more than a million scam emails between 3 and 5 August 2026, masquerading as chief executives.

Education

Mathspace — more than a million people, via Metabase

Check Point reported on 14 September that the Australian and New Zealand education platform Mathspace was breached through CVE-2026-72898 in a self-hosted Metabase instance, reaching an internal reporting database. Names, emails, usernames and locations were exposed; passwords and academic records were not.

Operations

September updates break Remote Desktop Services

Microsoft has confirmed reports that the September 2026 security updates cause Remote Desktop Services failures on Windows Server systems. This is a patching-programme problem rather than an intrusion: with three Windows CVSS 9.8 remote-code-execution flaws in the same release, an update that breaks remote administration is the kind of side effect that stalls a rollout precisely when it should not stall.

AI risk

PaperCut: the campaign run by hundreds of AI agents

Carried from earlier editions and unchanged. GreyNoise’s “Agents Gone Wild” documents a likely Russian-speaking actor directing hundreds of AI agents against CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 in PaperCut NG/MF: 440 instances, 395 organisations, 48 countries, with 204 education-sector victims and, at one US high school, access to domain admin in seven minutes. Not tagged New — the report is several days old.

  • Added 11 September — due 14 September (overdue by 1 day). GitLab Community and Enterprise Edition path traversal, CVE-2026-85706. A deadline that passed yesterday is not “0 days left”; it is overdue, and the magnitude is stated.
  • Added 10 September — due 13 September (overdue by 2 days). The pair of MikroTik RouterOS vulnerabilities.
  • Added 9 September — due 12 September (overdue by 3 days). Four vulnerabilities: Fortinet multiple products, Citrix NetScaler, Google Chromium V8 and Cisco Firewall Management Center. The Hacker News reported this batch under a 12 September federal patch deadline, which is the date carried here.

No due date for this batch was returned by a source read this run, so none is asserted — but the Artifactory entry is the same product as the Top Story, and the attacks Wiz observed ran until 8 September.
