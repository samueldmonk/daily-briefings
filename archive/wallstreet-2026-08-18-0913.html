The lead

Housing starts fall off a cliff as an expired Iran ceasefire keeps crude bid into the open

Markets are closed as this edition is written; the regular session opens at 9:30 AM ET, so what follows is the pre-open picture, not index levels. US stock futures were pointing to a negative open on Tuesday as Wall Street digested heightened Middle East risk and weighed equity valuations against rising bond yields.

The morning's data was ugly. July housing starts, released at 8:30 AM ET, fell to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1.239 million against the 1.350 million expected — down 12.4% from June's revised 1.415 million and 13.5% below July 2025's 1.432 million. Single-family starts dropped 9.9% to 808,000. Permits, though, went the other way: 1.443 million, up 5.0% on the month and 3.1% on the year, with single-family permits up 2.5% to 894,000 and buildings of five units or more at 490,000. Planned construction is holding up; actual building is not.

On the futures board, Benzinga's pre-market read (article stamped 2:23 AM ET) has S&P 500 futures down 0.41%, Nasdaq 100 futures down 0.76%, Dow futures down 0.16% and Russell 2000 futures down 0.24%. The prediction-market crowd agrees: Polymarket's "S&P 500 (SPX) Up or Down on August 18?" contract was pricing a 27% chance of a higher open. Monday's equivalent contract resolved "Down."

The cause remains the energy complex, and the trigger has now changed. President Trump rejected extending the 60-day temporary ceasefire with Iran, which expired Monday, absent a broader peace agreement; in response a senior Iranian official threatened that Tehran would shift to a "fully offensive" military posture if diplomacy fails, escalating the risk around the Strait of Hormuz. Energy reacted accordingly: Brent crude futures rose to $91.27 a barrel and WTI to $84.20. Rising oil and concerns over government borrowing have lifted yields: the 30-year Treasury yield reached 5.31%, a 19-year high, with the 10-year near 4.7%.

Against that, earnings kept supporting equities. Home Depot reported before the open and beat on both lines while reaffirming full-year guidance; Baidu also reports Tuesday. Ed Yardeni's "Fed's Stock Valuation Model" still leaves the index marginally cheap on his maths: the S&P 500's forward P/E of 19.9 sits just under the 21.4 reciprocal of the bond yield, and with the 10-year at 4.68% last week the model's fair value came out at 8,300.

Movers & drivers

New1.239M vs 1.350M est.

July housing starts — a 12.4% drop

Privately owned housing starts fell to a 1.239 million annual rate in July, missing the 1.350 million consensus by a wide margin — 12.4% below June's revised 1.415 million and 13.5% below a year earlier. Single-family starts fell 9.9% to 808,000. The offsetting signal: building permits rose 5.0% to 1.443 million, single-family permits up 2.5% to 894,000, multifamily (5+ units) at 490,000. Released 8:30 AM ET by the Census Bureau.

Pre-market +1.5%

Home Depot (HD) — beat and reaffirm

Shares gained about 1.5% in pre-market trading after fiscal Q2 results topped expectations on both lines: adjusted EPS of $4.92 against $4.73 expected, revenue of $47.86 billion against $47.27 billion forecast. Comparable sales rose 1.7% and US comparable sales 1.3%. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed — adjusted diluted EPS growth of flat to 4.0% from fiscal 2025's $14.69, or $14.69 to $15.28. Earnings call at 9 AM ET.

Fabrinet dropped more than 9% in pre-market trading, per CNBC's morning movers list — the largest decline among the names it flagged ahead of the open, and it came despite fiscal fourth-quarter earnings and revenue that exceeded expectations alongside upbeat guidance.
