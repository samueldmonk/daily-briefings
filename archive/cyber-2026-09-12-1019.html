Top story

A breach at Trezor’s mail vendor turned into a phishing run on 347,000 wallet owners — and a 20-minute containment window held the damage to 2,500

Trezor’s third-party marketing provider Brevo was breached on 9 September, exposing roughly 347,000 customer email addresses. Attackers used them immediately, sending a campaign under the subject line “Critical Security Alert: STM32 Entropy Vulnerability” that claimed a hardware flaw in STM32 microcontrollers could let an attacker brute-force a user’s seed, and pushed recipients to click through and “check” whether their device was affected. The claimed flaw was not real. The destination was an app built to harvest wallet backup seeds.

The number that matters is the second one. Trezor took the phishing domain down within 20 minutes, which disabled the link and capped the campaign at the 2,500 customers who had already clicked. The company suspended the Brevo account to stop further distribution, and says no other Trezor system was touched.

The lesson is not about hardware wallets. A marketing-automation vendor holds a list that is, functionally, a target list of people who own crypto — and it sits well outside the security perimeter the vendor’s customer actually controls. Trezor could not prevent the breach; it could only shorten the window afterwards, and the gap between 347,000 targeted and 2,500 reached is entirely the value of having done that fast.

Patch priority

Due today — 0 days left

CVE-2026-20079, CVSS 10.0, in the web interface of Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center. An unauthenticated remote attacker can bypass authentication, execute script files on the device and obtain root on the underlying operating system — on the box that manages your firewall estate. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on 9 September with a remediation deadline of 12 September 2026, which is today. The same deadline covers CVE-2026-19490 (Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway, CVSS 9.3) and CVE-2025-25249 (Fortinet FortiOS, FortiSwitchManager and FortiSASE). If you run any of the three and have not patched, this is the only item on this page worth reading first.

Threat actor spotlight

Ransomware-as-a-serviceHealthcare

INC Ransom

Emerged in July 2023 and has grown from a nascent ransomware-as-a-service operation into one of 2026’s most prolific crews, with no fewer than 830 victims claimed since August 2023. It runs the standard double-extortion model — encrypt, exfiltrate, threaten to leak — and its targeting is unusually concentrated: healthcare organisations account for 29% of its attacks, with education close behind. It remains active into mid-2026 through the Lynx derivative, with documented affiliate relationships and steady output. Sector concentration is the actionable part: if you are a hospital system, you are not a bystander to this one.

Breaches & incidents

NewSupply chainPhishing

Trezor — 347,000 targeted, 2,500 reached

The Brevo breach of 9 September handed attackers Trezor’s newsletter list. A fake “STM32 entropy” security alert drove recipients toward a seed-stealing app; the domain was killed inside 20 minutes and the Brevo account suspended. No other Trezor system was touched.

NewPatch regression

September’s Windows Server updates break Remote Desktop

KB5122876 (Server 2019), KB5122882 (2022) and KB5122871 (2025), all released 8 September, are causing Remote Desktop Services failures. Servers run normally for a few hours, then connections begin to fail: existing sessions cannot disconnect or log off and new attempts hang before timing out, with hard reboots needed in the worst cases. Microsoft says it is investigating; as of 10 September there was no out-of-band fix and no timeline.

IdentityFBI investigating

IDScan.net — 153 million licence scans

Brian Krebs reported on 1 September that a dark-web identity-theft service called Nexus was advertising more than 153 million US and Canadian driver’s licence scans, 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents and 579,000 medical cards. IDScan disclosed in a 4 September notice that it learned on or around 1 September that data may have been accessed without authorisation from its cloud platform; exposed fields can include full names and government-issued identification numbers. The FBI’s New Orleans field office has opened an investigation and multiple lawsuits have been filed.

NewMobile malware

Mantax Otax blends ransomware with spyware on Android

A newly reported Android strain that combines both capabilities in one package: it encrypts files, steals sensitive data, and then spams and harasses the victim — extortion pressure applied through the victim’s own contact list rather than through a leak site.

Mass exploitationAI-assisted

PaperCut — 440 servers, but only 12 reached domain admin

GreyNoise dates the campaign’s start to 31 August, three days after the two flaws were published: CVE-2026-81578, an authentication bypass, and CVE-2026-82078, an unsafe-reflection RCE, which chain into unauthenticated arbitrary Java bytecode execution. Depth rather than breadth is the story: credentials harvested from 280 victims, OS and domain secrets from 147, administrator privileges at just 12 organisations — though one US high school went from initial access to domain admin in seven minutes. Fixed builds: 26.0.5, 25.0.13, 24.1.10.

  • Adobe Commerce CVE-2026-75650 — added 8 September, due 11 September (overdue by 1 day).
  • N-able N-central CVE-2026-86218 — added 8 September, due 11 September (overdue by 1 day). Both of the 11 September entries carry a CVSS of 10.0.
  • MikroTik RouterOS CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060 — added 10 September, due 13 September (1 day left).
  • GitLab CVE-2026-85706 — added 11 September, due 14 September (2 days left).
  • Google Chromium CVE-2026-85046 — added 4 September, due 18 September (6 days left).
  • Microsoft Windows CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880 — added 8 September, due 22 September (10 days left).
  • Google Chromium CVE-2026-87491 — added 9 September, due 23 September (11 days left). It shared a bulletin with the Cisco, Citrix and Fortinet entries whose deadline is today, and is the clearest illustration on this page that the window is assigned per CVE, not per batch: same bulletin, same day, deadlines eleven days apart.

Windows is the other proof of the same point: the 8 September bulletin carried four CVEs and split them, 11 September for Adobe Commerce and N-able, 22 September for the two Windows flaws. Do not derive a 2026 deadline from the three-week window of the older BOD 22-01 — the observed windows above run from three to fourteen days. Take the date CISA publishes against your own entry.
