Top Story

Parnasse arrives: a first-round finish of Dan Hooker, on debut, in a main event, at home

Salahdine Parnasse made his UFC debut in the UFC Paris main event on Saturday 5 September at Accor Arena and stopped Dan Hooker inside a round. About halfway through the opening round Parnasse landed a body kick that, in UFC.com’s account, “got Hooker’s attention,” then followed with a flurry against the fence to send Hooker crashing to the canvas. The 28-year-old has now won six straight, all by stoppage, and UFC.com says he “instantly enters the highly touted lightweight Top 15.” Post-fight records are sourced this edition for the first time: Parnasse moves to 24–2, Hooker falls to 24–15 (Yahoo Sports / MMA Mania this run; earlier editions carried Parnasse’s pre-fight 23–2). A record that settled on Saturday night is a newly-sourced detail rather than a new development, so it is not tagged New.

Parnasse is a former two-time KSW featherweight champion and one-time KSW lightweight champion — UFC.com calls him “a two-division champion outside of the UFC” and, in its main-card report, “the former KSW two-division champion” — who signed with the promotion in late July 2026 having previously turned it down, and was handed a five-round main event on debut. He is not a Dana White’s Contender Series signee.

One number this desk will not assert: the stoppage time. UFC.com’s main-card results page was fetched directly again this run and its main-event heading still reads “TKO, Round 1, 2:25”, while Yahoo Sports, Sherdog, LowKick and fightnews.com all give 2:35. That is four named outlets against the promotion’s own page, and the sixth consecutive edition in which the two have failed to converge. The previous edition set the test explicitly — adopt 2:35 only if UFC.com’s own page changes — and it has not: the page’s article:modified_time still reads 2026-09-05T18:40:35−0400, byte-for-byte the timestamp recorded three runs ago, so the promotion has not revised it. A stoppage time is a single number, so this desk still declines to overwrite the promotion’s own results page on a count of aggregators: both figures are noted, and the table below records the result as TKO, Round 1, with no stamp.

Fight Week — Upcoming Cards

Sat 12 Sep · Desert Diamond Arena, Glendale, AZ

Noche UFC: Silva vs. Delgado

The fourth annual Noche UFC, on Mexican Independence Day weekend. Featherweight Jean Silva meets Arizona’s Jose Miguel Delgado in the main event — former interim featherweight champion Yair Rodríguez was originally booked opposite Silva but withdrew injured and was replaced by Delgado, which is why some listings still carry the old billing. Also on the card: Brandon Moreno vs. Joseph Morales, the TUF season 33 winner, and Manon Fiorot vs. Alexa Grasso — a card bout, not the headliner. Prelims 2:00 PM ET, main card 5:00 PM ET on Paramount+. The main event is scheduled for five rounds at featherweight, and it is Delgado’s first chance to headline a UFC card. Silva is 17–3 and 6–1 in the UFC with five finishes, coming off a decision win over Arnold Allen; Delgado is 12–2 and 4–1 with a pair of knockouts. Officially UFC Fight Night 288, billed as Noche UFC 4.
Odds: Silva −425 / Delgado +355 (FightOdds.io, via Yahoo Sports and MMA Mania).

UFC 331: Van vs. Pantoja 2

Flyweight champion Joshua Van defends against Alexandre Pantoja in a rematch of their UFC 323 meeting in December 2025, which Van won by technical knockout 26 seconds into the first round after Pantoja suffered an arm injury. Thirteen fights. Early prelims about 5:00 PM ET, prelims 7:00 PM ET, main card 9:00 PM ET.

Prospect Watch

Prospect

Axel Sola — lightweight

The Nice native delivered what UFC.com called “the knockout of his career,” dropping Farès Ziam with a left hand after about 90 seconds of feeling out. It was his third Octagon appearance of 2026, a second straight first-round finish, and a second win in Paris in as many starts there; he opened the year with a Fight of the Night performance opposite Mason Jones.

Prospect

Losene Keita — featherweight, now 17–2

The former Oktagon MMA double champion took his first UFC win and moved to 17–2, dropping Muhammad Naimov with a jab midway through the round and finishing with a clean right hook before scaling the Octagon wall and taking a front-row seat in the stands. He called out Lerone Murphy in a bilingual post-fight interview, after a close debut against Nathaniel Wood earlier this year.

Prospect

Daniil Donchenko — 25, on an eight-fight run

The Ultimate Fighter winner swept the cards against Punahele Soriano for a third consecutive UFC win and an eight-fight winning streak, hurting Soriano early and managing the rest. He called for a matchup with Daniel Rodriguez toward the end of the year.

Roster move

Five DWCS contracts awarded, 1 September

Week 4 of Contender Series season 10 ran at the Meta Apex in Las Vegas with a five-bout card and ten athletes. UFC CEO Dana White announced contract offers for all five winners: Adam Darby, Modestino Rodrigues, Silvestre Sanchez, Gabriel Lorenço and Adam Livingston.

Around the Sport

  • Kurtis Campbell became the first fighter to finish Trevor Peek. “The Pink Panther” took his first UFC win in his sophomore appearance, battering Peek with elbows from mount before working to the rear-naked choke in the third — a recovery after faltering on debut earlier this year.
  • Michael “Venom” Page has now won four straight and all three of his middleweight appearances, though UFC.com’s own account described the Ruziboev fight as “a low-output jousting match” that left viewers “wanting more.”
  • Mario Pinto remains undefeated at 13–0, and 4–0 in the UFC, after overwhelming Ryan Spann with ground-and-pound 55 seconds into the second round, following a first round UFC.com described as back-and-forth with both men landing near fight-ending blows.
  • UFC 332 is being advertised for Salt Lake City on UFC.com. No date is asserted here — none appeared on any page fetched this run.
