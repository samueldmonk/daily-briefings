The Lead

No session today: the tape has been shut since Friday’s close

It is Sunday afternoon. US equity markets are closed and do not reopen until Monday 21 September, so nothing on this page is a live reading of a session in progress. The most recent print is Friday 18 September, and every figure below is from that close or earlier.

Friday finished a volatile week defined by the Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in more than three years, elevated oil and a 10-year Treasury yield pressing toward 5%. The S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50 and the Nasdaq Composite advanced 0.39% to 26,522.55, while the Dow shed 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64. Both the Dow and the S&P 500 posted weekly losses. The small-cap tape is disputed: this desk’s standing record carries the Russell 2000 down 0.53%, while a session note read this run has the Russell ETF (IWM) closing at the lows of the day but up 0.6%. Both are printed; neither is adopted.

Two things newly sourced this run sharpen the picture. Friday was a quadruple-witching expiration put at roughly $7 trillion in notional value — described as the second largest ever — which is the usual explanation for heavy volume and sharp swings into the final hour. And the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index rose 2.78%, the strongest sector-level number anyone published for the session, on a memory-chip rally. That is consistent with the wrap that credits technology with doing the heavy lifting into the close.
