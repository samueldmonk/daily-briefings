Top Story

Second time unlucky: N-able's incomplete N-central patch becomes an exploited zero-day with MSP supply-chain reach

CISA on Monday added CVE-2026-18577 — an authentication-bypass-via-alternate-path flaw in N-able's N-central remote monitoring and management platform — to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after attackers took over customer servers. The bug (CVSS 8.2) exists because the fix for an earlier flaw, CVE-2026-18556, proved incomplete: threat actors found a patch bypass and resumed exploitation in the wild (The Hacker News, GBHackers).

The stakes are bigger than one server. N-central is the console MSPs use to manage fleets of customer endpoints — successful exploitation yields administrative access, and attackers have abused the built-in Take Control remote-access feature to pivot from compromised servers into managed endpoints and plant persistence (GBHackers/The Hacker News). Exploitation has been observed in the wild since August 1, with attackers registering Cloudflare Tunnel (cloudflared) services on compromised hosts — outbound-only connections that survive reboots and persist even after access through the N-central server is revoked (Rapid7/The Hacker News). N-able shipped build 2026.3.1.7 on August 2 as the first unaffected version, and says it has identified a limited number of impacted customers (GBHackers).

Federal civilian agencies have until Wednesday, August 6 to remediate under the risk-based BOD 26-04 regime — one of the shortest windows CISA assigns, reflecting the supply-chain blast radius.

Patch Priority

Most urgent action today

Upgrade N-able N-central to 2026.3.1.7 — federal deadline Wednesday (2 days left)

CVE-2026-18577 (CVSS 8.2, actively exploited, KEV-listed Aug 3) affects all N-central builds prior to 2026.3.1.7. It is a bypass of a prior fix, so systems patched against CVE-2026-18556 are still vulnerable. Hosted environments are upgraded by the vendor; on-premise deployments need manual remediation (Rapid7).

Then hunt, don't just patch: N-able has published exploitation-linked IP indicators and a detection service template for Windows endpoints — review admin accounts, Take Control session logs, newly created Windows services and outbound Cloudflare Tunnel connections, and remember a clean template result does not rule out compromise (GBHackers).

Threat Actor Spotlight

INC Ransom — the SMA1000 landlord

The INC Ransomware operation has emerged as the "dominant threat actor" exploiting SonicWall's SMA 1000 VPN flaws (CVE-2026-15409/15410), with Resecurity observing its activity accelerating since the beginning of August and multiple victims listed on its data leak site (The Hacker News). The group has claimed 885 victims to date per Ransomware.Live, the most recent listed August 2. The two flaws were exploited as zero-days since June 22 — weeks before SonicWall's mid-July fixes (Help Net Security) — and Rapid7 says attackers used the foothold to extract high-value credentials and session data. Any appliance patched late should be treated as potentially already compromised and hunted, not just updated.

Breaches & Incidents

NewCrypto theft

Coldcard flaw behind a $70M, 41-minute Bitcoin sweep

An attacker drained 1,196 Bitcoin addresses in 41 minutes on July 30 — 1,082.65 BTC, ~$70.2 million — with Galaxy Research tying the sweep to a March-2021 firmware flaw in Coinkite's Coldcard hardware wallet that routed seed generation to a deterministic software PRNG instead of the hardware RNG. Coinkite shipped emergency firmware July 31, but patching does not repair an exposed seed: owners must generate a new one and move funds (The Hacker News).

NewEspionage

Storm-2945: fake updates over hijacked hotel Wi-Fi

Microsoft says the CaptiveCrunch operation — attributed to Storm-2945, an assessed sub-cluster of Midnight Blizzard (APT29/Cozy Bear, tied by the U.S. and U.K. to Russia's SVR) — hijacked hotel captive-portal gateways that doubled as DNS resolvers, forging DNS answers to redirect laptops' connectivity checks to fake browser updates delivering CornFlake, a RAT that captures webcam images, microphone audio and keystrokes (The Hacker News).

NewSupply chain

Adform ad script poisoned to swap crypto wallet addresses

Attackers modified a JavaScript file served by ad-tech firm Adform, turning it into a browser-side tool that rewrites cryptocurrency wallet addresses on customer sites. Adform detected and removed the code on July 27; anyone who copied a Bitcoin, Ethereum or Tron address on an affected page that day may have pasted an attacker's address instead — and the altered file may still sit in browser caches (The Hacker News).

NewGovernment

PNLD breach exposes UK police contact details

The UK's Police National Legal Database confirmed that names, organisations and work emails of police officers, staff, criminal-justice professionals and "Ask the Police" users were published on the dark web after an incident identified July 26. No passwords or credentials are believed compromised, but the data makes phishing against named officers far more convincing; PNLD notes it is not the Police National Computer and holds no confidential case data (The Hacker News).

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • CVE-2026-18577 — N-able N-central (auth bypass, exploited): added Aug 3, federal due date Aug 6 (CISA via GBHackers) 2 days left
  • CISA currently lists ransomware use of CVE-2026-18577 as unknown — but an RMM compromise hands attackers centralized access to fleets of customer devices, which is why the window is so short (GBHackers).
  • Deadlines are per-CVE and risk-based under BOD 26-04, which replaced the old flat three-week window in June — the N-central assignment is a three-day window, among the shortest CISA issues.
