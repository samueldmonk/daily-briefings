Top Story

UFC 333 lands in Abu Dhabi with two titles on one card — Volkanovski defends against the unbeaten Evloev

UFC 333: Volkanovski vs. Evloev is set for Saturday, October 24, 2026 at the Etihad Arena in Abu Dhabi, streaming on Paramount+ with no pay-per-view; the main card starts at 2 PM ET / 11 AM PT. In the main event, reigning two-time featherweight champion Alexander Volkanovski defends against undefeated contender Movsar Evloev — the fight the division has been pointing at since Evloev beat Lerone Murphy in London in March.

The co-main is a title fight in its own right: Petr Yan defends the bantamweight championship against Merab Dvalishvili, their third meeting. Two belts above one another on a card with no pay-per-view attached is the clearest statement yet of what the Paramount+ era looks like — the same inventory that would once have carried a $79.99 price tag, inside a subscription. Odds for both bouts have opened, but no numbers appeared in what was fetched this run and none are printed here.

Rankings & Business

Rankings movement. The most consequential result of the past week was at bantamweight: Umar Nurmagomedov, unbeaten at 20–0 going in, was knocked out by Song Yadong and takes the first loss of his career. Song has asked for a title shot; the belt is booked elsewhere, with Petr Yan defending against Merab Dvalishvili at UFC 333 on October 24. At light heavyweight the champion is out injured into next year.

Business & broadcast. The economics behind the no-pay-per-view era finally have numbers attached. Paramount is in year one of a seven-year, $7.7 billion rights deal with TKO Group, an average annual value of about $1.1 billion, under which Paramount+ exclusively carries 13 marquee numbered events and 30 Fight Nights a year in the U.S., with select numbered cards simulcast on CBS.

The proof of concept is UFC Freedom 250: TKO Group reported 34 million total global viewers and 17 million across the U.S. and Latin America. Domestically the figures reported differ by platform and are printed as they were found — ESPN reports a 7.0 million U.S. average, the most-watched UFC event ever domestically, while The Hollywood Reporter puts the Paramount+ audience at 8 million. The subsequent McGregor–Holloway rematch drew 6.5 million viewers across the U.S. and Latin America and peaked at 8.3 million concurrent streams, which Paramount says trails only Super Bowl LVIII among live events on the service. No gate figure was stated in anything fetched this run and none is printed.
