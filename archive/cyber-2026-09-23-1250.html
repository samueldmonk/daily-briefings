Top Story

ShinyHunters claims it breached the FBI through a new Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day

The extortion group ShinyHunters told BleepingComputer it used a previously unknown remote-code-execution flaw in Oracle PeopleSoft to access FBI systems Monday night and move laterally into FBI-managed AWS GovCloud infrastructure. The group claims to have stolen between 2 and 3 terabytes of data on current and former FBI employees and job applicants — including names, agent statuses, emails, phone numbers, home addresses and, in some cases, spouse information and Social Security numbers. Axios reports the FBI is investigating the claims. ShinyHunters says the FBI was targeted in retaliation for a May 2026 bureau advisory that detailed the group’s earlier targeting of the Canvas learning-management system and urged victims not to pay ransoms. The bureau has not confirmed the intrusion, and the claims come from the threat actor itself, not an independent forensic review.

Sources: BleepingComputer · Axios · The Hacker News

Patch Priority

Patch the Zyxel GS1900 switch flaw today — due tomorrow

CVE-2026-7273 (Zyxel GS1900 series switches, CVSS 8.8, stack-based buffer overflow enabling unauthenticated OS command execution) is actively exploited — a suspected Chinese-speaking actor has compromised 996 devices across 48 countries since August 17 — and carries a federal remediation deadline of September 24, 2026 (1 day left) under Binding Operational Directive 26-04’s new three-day window. One day later, on September 25 (2 days left), the deadline lands for three more actively-exploited, near-maximum-severity flaws: Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator (CVE-2026-93952, CVSS 10.0), F5 BIG-IP APM (CVE-2026-94127, CVSS 9.8) and twin Check Point Quantum Security Gateway bugs (CVE-2026-85102 / CVE-2026-93616, CVSS 9.8). All five deadlines match the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Suspected Chinese-speaking actor behind the Zyxel GS1900 campaign

Per Help Net Security, GreyNoise first spotted exploitation of CVE-2026-7273 on September 17. Since August 17, the actor has weaponized a stack-based buffer overflow in the switch’s CGI program to compromise and exfiltrate data from 996 Zyxel GS1900 switches across 48 countries, including Italy, the U.S., Taiwan, France and South Korea. CISA added the flaw to its KEV catalog on September 21 after confirming active exploitation.

Source: Help Net Security · SecurityWeek

Breaches & Incidents

153M records

IDScan.net — 153 million driver’s licenses

The ID-verification vendor, used by car-rental firms, retailers and cannabis dispensaries, confirmed unauthorized cloud-platform access after a dark-web database surfaced allowing lookups of over 150 million U.S. and Canadian driver’s licenses, including photos. Names and government ID numbers are exposed; the FBI’s New Orleans field office has opened an investigation.

Help Net Security · BleepingComputer · TechCrunch · Krebs on Security

New

Microsoft discloses two cloud phishing campaigns

Microsoft detailed a CEO-fraud campaign that blasted over one million AI-drafted emails (Aug 3–5) impersonating executives to trick accounts-payable teams into ACH transfers for a fake ServiceNow renewal, plus a separate, ongoing (since May 2026) passkey-themed social-engineering campaign in which callers pose as IT help desks to get victims to reset MFA/passkey settings, then harvest cloud identities via proxy infrastructure.

Microsoft Security Blog · The Hacker News

New

Japan’s Digital Agency — 246,000 records

Japan’s Digital Agency, which runs the Government Solution Service used by multiple ministries, confirmed a breach after attackers exploited a VPN appliance vulnerability. Roughly 246,000 records — names and contact details of government officials and contractors — were exposed; financial data was not affected.

Reported this run via security-news aggregation; agency advisory not independently fetched.

Maritime

Cyberattacks disrupt two Texas-bound oil tankers

Onboard systems on two tankers, including the VL Prosperity, were disrupted by cyberattacks during voyages to the U.S. The Coast Guard and FBI boarded the vessels and confirmed malicious cyber activity, underscoring continued targeting of maritime OT systems.
