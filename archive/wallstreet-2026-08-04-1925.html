The Lead

Official close: S&P 500 tops 7,700 and the Dow tops 54,000 — both for the first time ever — on Palantir's "otherworldly" quarter and a 5% oil slide

The rally that opened August compounded straight through the closing bell. The S&P 500 closed at a record 7,736.52, up 1.79% — its first-ever finish above the 7,700 line (CNBC/CNN); the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 907.47 points, or 1.71%, to 54,085.88, closing above 54,000 for the first time (CNN); and the Nasdaq Composite surged 2.59% to 26,584.99 (Motley Fool). It was the S&P 500's first record high in two months, supported by strong corporate earnings, a rebound in tech shares and traders embracing hopes for progress on reopening the Strait of Hormuz (CNN/Zacks). Each close reconciles to Monday's verified finish plus the quoted change — the Dow's to the penny (53,178.41 + 907.47).

Two engines are driving it. First, earnings: Palantir delivered what Motley Fool's wrap called "otherworldly" growth — the descriptor its own leadership used — and the stock, up nearly 17% before the open (TheStreet) and nearly 27% in early trading (Yahoo), closed up 29.45% at $162.66 (Motley Fool quote data). With roughly 85% of S&P 500 companies beating estimates and aggregate profit growth tracking above 47% this season, "investors have a genuine, bottom-up reason to stay invested," Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn told TheStreet. Second, oil: crude climbed overnight on fresh reports of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz (TheStreet), then reversed hard — WTI fell more than 6% to $75.32 by the U.S. close (Motley Fool; Trading Economics' board showed $76.14, −5.2%) — on hopes that traffic through the vital waterway might resume relatively soon. Lower oil, the Fool notes, could ease inflation and make the Fed less likely to raise rates this year.

Under the hood, most sectors finished green — technology up 4.20% and industrials up 3.42%, with only energy and utilities lagging (Motley Fool). Citadel Securities analysts argued August may prove less volatile than July, citing reduced leverage, less retail risk-taking and a strong earnings season. After the bell, the baton passed to AMD and SpaceX — and both stocks fell in extended trading despite beats (see After-Hours Movers below).

Movers & Drivers

▲ Earnings

Palantir (PLTR)

The session's engine. The AI-software firm posted blockbuster quarterly results, raised its full-year outlook (TheStreet), and reported growth its leadership described as "otherworldly" — reassuring investors that demand for AI tools remains strong (Motley Fool). Up nearly 17% premarket, nearly 27% in early trading, and closed +29.45% at $162.66 (Fool quote data), with U.S.-market growth called out as the standout (Zacks via Yahoo).

▲ Semis

Chip rally: Marvell, Micron, Nvidia

Semiconductors rallied broadly behind the AI-earnings signal: Marvell advanced nearly 13% and Micron gained more than 7% (Zacks via Yahoo), while Nvidia added about 2.6% to $211.94 (Fool quote data). The Nasdaq-100's tech comeback was the day's dominant trade.

▲ Dow leader

Caterpillar (CAT)

The biggest gainer in the Dow after better-than-expected Q2 numbers and raised revenue-growth guidance (Zacks via Yahoo), finishing the day up 5.60% on record quarterly revenue (Motley Fool). A fitting leader on a day industrials rose 3.42% as a sector.

▲ Ahead of earnings

SpaceX (SPCX)

Rose into its first earnings report as a public company — closing up 9.4% at $125.33 (Fool quote data) after trading up 1.3% premarket (TheStreet) — then gave the gain back in extended trading when the mixed numbers landed (see After-Hours Movers).

▼ Decliner

AdaptHealth (AHCO)

The day's ugliest post-earnings move: the home-medical-equipment company slumped almost 40% following an earnings miss and a reduction in its full-year earnings guidance (Motley Fool).

▼ Energy

Crude's round trip

Oil climbed overnight on fresh reports of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz (TheStreet), then reversed hard on diplomatic optimism: WTI fell over 6% to $75.32 by the U.S. equities close (Motley Fool); Trading Economics' board showed $76.14, −5.2%, after WTI opened the day at $80.00 (Forbes). Stocks treated the attacks as "an energy-market story rather than a growth-and-inflation story," Capital.com's Hathorn told TheStreet.

▲ Follow-through

Amazon (AMZN)

Monday's engine: Amazon eclipsed a $3 trillion market cap for the first time in Monday's record session (Yahoo Finance). Tuesday it cooled — off about 2.3% at $277.42 late (Fool quote data) — while Palantir's blowout carried the AI-demand story instead.

After-Hours Movers

▼ After hours

AMD (AMD)

Lost about 8% in after-hours trading after reporting what TheStreet's wrap called mixed second-quarter results — and what Motley Fool called a fall "despite solid results" (after-hours roundups/TS2). A bar-was-higher story on a day the stock rose into the print: "AI jitters have not gone away," as the Fool's wrap put it.

▼ After hours

SpaceX (SPCX)

Declined 7% in extended trading even though its first earnings report as a public company topped Q2 revenue forecasts at $7.81 billion (after-hours roundups/TS2). Motley Fool's wrap characterized the results as mixed — the stock had risen 9.4% in the regular session ahead of the release, so the after-hours give-back still leaves the June IPO's Aug-4 round trip roughly flat.

▲ After hours

Arista Networks (ANET), Wynn (WYNN) & Booking (BKNG)

The after-hours tape wasn't all red: Arista Networks advanced 11% after beating on both profit and sales, Wynn Resorts rose 7% on robust earnings, and Booking Holdings added more than 5% as gross bookings exceeded consensus (after-hours roundups/TS2).

▼ After hours

Pinterest (PINS)

Fell 8% in extended trading after guiding to third-quarter revenue that trailed Wall Street's expectations (after-hours roundups/TS2).
