The Lead

Just after the open (~9:45 AM ET): stocks slide again as the 30-year yield hits a 2004 high and Oracle drags tech

Stocks fell at Thursday’s open as Treasury yields kept marching higher and traders anticipated further rate hikes from the Federal Reserve. In early trading the Nasdaq Composite was down 0.8%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average had lost 182 points, or 0.4%, and the S&P 500 was off 0.5% (CNBC). That extends Wednesday’s losses, when the S&P 500 fell 0.75% and the Nasdaq 1.13% (official closes in the Weekly Scorecard).

The 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.446%, a level not seen since June 2004, and the benchmark 10-year surged to 5.15%, near levels last reached in July 2007 (CNBC). Oracle led Nasdaq names lower, sinking more than 5% after Bloomberg reported it sent a force-majeure notice over its “Project Jupiter” data-center campus in New Mexico, seeking to delay payments if the site fails to come online as expected in 2028; Oracle is not trying to leave the project and says it remains on schedule (CNBC). New York Fed President John Williams said market expectations of another hike before year-end are a “reasonable way of thinking” (TradingKey). The key event is President Trump’s meeting with Chinese leader Xi Jinping, scheduled for 11:30 AM ET, after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the two sides will extend their “Busan Agreement” trade truce from November 10 to January 10 (Yahoo Finance / TradingKey).

Earlier: before the bell, Dow futures were down 0.34%, S&P 500 futures 0.57% and Nasdaq-100 futures 1.03% (TradingKey). Sources: CNBC live updates (Sept 24) · CNBC (Oracle) · Yahoo Finance (Sept 24 live) · TradingKey (pre-market)

Movers & Drivers

New −5%+

Oracle (ORCL)

Sank more than 5% after Bloomberg reported it sent the developer of its New Mexico “Project Jupiter” data-center campus, a Blue Owl Capital unit, a letter citing force majeure; a key natural-gas pipeline needed to power the site has already been delayed (CNBC).

−9%+ pre-mkt

MGM Resorts (MGM)

Tumbled more than 9% premarket after Barry Diller’s People withdrew its proposal to buy the casino giant; Diller said “the mix” wasn’t coming together as hoped but still holds 66.8 million shares (CNBC).

−18% pre-mkt

Stitch Fix (SFIX)

Dipped 18.2% to $2.31 premarket after missing fourth-quarter revenue estimates and guiding fiscal 2027 revenue to $1.31–$1.36 billion, below the $1.41 billion analysts expected (Benzinga).

−6.6% pre-mkt

Darden Restaurants (DRI)

The Olive Garden and LongHorn parent fell 6.6% after fiscal first-quarter results: earnings of $2.05 a share matched estimates, while revenue of $3.20 billion was just shy of the $3.21 billion expected (FactSet, via CNBC).

New −2–3% pre-mkt

Chip & memory stocks

AI chip and memory names fell premarket as yields climbed: Nvidia about −1%, AMD about −2%, Intel and Marvell nearly −3%, Micron about −2.3% and SanDisk about −2.7% (TradingKey).

−2% pre-mkt

Meta Platforms (META)

Fell more than 2% premarket on profit-taking after a nearly 12% gain earlier this week; at its Connect event it unveiled Meta Charm, a small portable Muse AI device, and camera-less VR glasses (TradingKey / Yahoo Finance).
