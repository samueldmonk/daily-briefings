The Lead

A third straight down day, and the sell-off deepened into the bell — Dow closes −405.41 as Brent settles at $101

The market is closed. Official closes for Wednesday 9 September: Dow Jones Industrial Average 52,380.66, −405.41 (−0.77%); S&P 500 7,636.36 (−0.48%); Nasdaq Composite 26,253.34 (−0.64%). It was the third consecutive day of declines for all three indexes. Brent crude, having cleared $100 a barrel for the first time since July, went on to settle at $101, and Treasury yields in the middle of the curve set new 52-week highs — the 10-year reaching 4.857%, its highest since November 2023.

The last two hours went the wrong way. This page’s previous edition had the Dow at −319.27 on a 2:00 p.m. quote board, itself a recovery from a 12:07 p.m. low of −413. The close at −405.41 says the market gave all of that repair back and then some into the bell. The Russell 2000 was −1.24% at 3:03 p.m., down over one percent for a second day — small caps taking the worst of an oil-and-yields squeeze, as they usually do.

Breadth was the tell all day. TheStreet’s 12:19 p.m. ET update put advancers at just 27.1% of U.S. issues, with nearly 70% declining, per FinViz; at 11:40 a.m., 68.6% of all U.S. issues were falling and 78% of the S&P 500 was in the red, with declines accelerating after about 11 a.m. as Brent cleared $100. A tape where roughly one stock in four is up was never a mega-cap problem; it was everything at once, and the close confirmed it.

The escalation is military. The U.S. struck five Iranian tankers on Tuesday, sinking one, after Iran fired ballistic missiles at a U.S. Navy warship. Tehran said on Wednesday that its forces had struck two American vessels and eight oil tankers in the Gulf in retaliation for the destruction of the five Iranian crude carriers. Iran also retaliated against a U.S.-used base in Jordan — Jordan says it intercepted 18 Iranian missiles with no casualties — and said it had targeted vessels crossing the Strait of Hormuz. The U.K. Maritime Trade Operations centre reported several merchant vessels in the Northern Arabian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman hit by “disabling fire.”

The whole session, in timed snapshots, none blended. 9:35 a.m. ET — S&P −0.24%, Dow −0.58%, Nasdaq −0.35%, Russell 2000 −0.52%. 11:40 a.m. — S&P −0.52%, with 68.6% of all U.S. issues and 78% of the S&P 500 declining. 11:43 a.m. — S&P −0.48%, Dow −0.73% at 52,399.06. 12:07 p.m. — Dow −413, −0.8%. 12:19 p.m. — 27.1% of issues advancing. 2:00 p.m. — Dow −319.27, S&P −32.34, Nasdaq −158.58. 3:03 p.m. — Russell 2000 −1.24%. 4:00 p.m. close — the official figures above.

Two independent reads gave the same three numbers.

Those marks reconcile arithmetically to Tuesday’s closes, so they are not nonsense; they are stale. The same board simultaneously showed SpaceX at −0.8% and Apple at −0.1%, which are the early-morning readings for both, not their afternoon ones. A feed that agrees with itself can still be showing you nine o’clock at five o’clock, so the board was discarded and the reported closes used instead.

Movers & Drivers

NewLockup
SpaceX · SPCX

Unlock day arrives, and the slide widens through the session

Quoted at $146.27, −4.7% (−$7.20) on the 2:00 p.m. board — a marked deterioration from the −0.78% at $152.28 read in early trading. Roughly 319 million shares became eligible for sale on Wednesday, the 90th trading day after the IPO, with a further 59 million freeing up Thursday. The stock debuted at $135 on 12 June and hit a record $225.64 on 16 June. CarriedGuidance
Marvell Technology · MRVL

A sweeping revenue-target raise, and the stock follows

Up 6.4% in the session after chief executive Matt Murphy lifted the fiscal-2027 revenue outlook to about $12 billion and the fiscal-2028 target to roughly $18 billion, against prior expectations of about $10 billion and $13.5 billion — a combined two-year forecast near $30 billion. Astera Labs (ALAB) rose 6.1% alongside it on the same AI build-out theme.

UpdatedProduct
Meta Platforms · META

Muse launch lifts Meta against a falling tape, and the gain is holding

Quoted at $650.01, +6.0% (+$36.53) on the 2:00 p.m. board, after the Facebook and Instagram parent launched Muse, a personal AI agent for users 18 and over aimed at scheduling, shopping and turning long-term goals into plans. It runs in a dedicated app and directly inside WhatsApp. The move built through the morning — 4.9% premarket, 5.4% at 10:25 a.m., 6.04% at 1:35 p.m. — and has since flattened out rather than extended. It remains the standout gainer on a red tape.

CarriedEarnings
Casey’s General Stores · CASY

The biggest single-name drop of the session

Down 16.5% at 10:25 a.m. — steeper than the 12.2% premarket slide — after the convenience-store chain posted disappointing same-store sales growth and left its full-year outlook unchanged. A midday read describes the fall as “almost 15%” despite an earnings beat; both readings are shown, and the difference is consistent with the stock recovering some ground through the morning. It remains the largest verified single-name decline on the day.

CarriedLegal
Booking Holdings · BKNG · UnitedHealth · UNH

A court loss and a valuation de-rating

Booking Holdings fell 4.3% after the EU General Court in Luxembourg fully dismissed its challenge to the European Commission’s 2023 decision blocking its roughly $1.9 billion acquisition of eTraveli. UnitedHealth fell 4.24% on what was described as valuation concerns, macroeconomic pressure and dividend-related factors.

NewEvent
Apple · AAPL

The foldable is real — and the stock fell anyway

Apple announced its next-generation iPhones, including a new foldable iPhone, at the “Surprise and Shine” keynote — the first product event under chief executive John Ternus, who took over from Tim Cook on 1 September. TheStreet marked the stock at −1.30% at 2:20 p.m., just after the announcement, having been “nearly 1%” lower at $313.12 at 11:10 a.m. and −0.8% at $313.72 on the 2:00 p.m. board. So the reveal that had been the day’s most-anticipated corporate event coincided with the decline widening, not narrowing. Ahead of it, TheStreet Pro’s Ed Ponsi had flagged the risk as timing rather than hardware: a delay of the new phones into spring 2027 “could see the stock hit an air pocket.”

Guidance raise sends the jeweller sharply higher

Signet raised its full-year profit outlook on better-than-expected second-quarter earnings. The premarket move was read at +10% in one account and +17% in another, with Reuters/Yahoo Finance describing shares soaring roughly 20% in the session. The spread is shown rather than resolved.

On the Radar

  • Treasury triples its buyback — announced at ~11 a.m. ET today. The Treasury Department said it will buy back up to $6 billion of already-issued longer-term government debt, specifically 10- and 20-year notes — three times the normal operation size, and above the “at least $4 billion” Secretary Scott Bessent flagged on 19 August. Officially it is about liquidity; it is also widely read as an attempt to put a lid on Treasury yields. Bessent’s line to currency traders on Tuesday: “I am the house now.”
  • Chewy (CHWY) and American Eagle Outfitters (AEO) report today — two consumer-spending reads on an otherwise thin earnings day.
  • A new trade front with Canada. The White House said it will ban imports of Canadian motorbikes and a slew of other products starting later this month as relations with Ottawa fray.
  • August Producer Price Index — Thursday 10 September, 8:30 a.m. ET (Bureau of Labor Statistics). PPI for final demand was unchanged in July. A CPI release is also flagged for Thursday; the two dates could not be separated cleanly this run, so only the BLS-stated PPI date is asserted.
  • Gasoline — carried at headline depth only. A same-day headline seen this run states that gasoline prices have hit new records and are still rising. The article was not read in full, so nothing beyond that sentence is asserted — no price, no state, no year-on-year figure. It is noted because pump prices are the channel through which $100 Brent reaches households, which is why an oil supply shock is trading as an inflation story rather than an energy-sector story.
  • The threat has widened beyond Iranian barrels. Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn: “Houthi attacks on Saudi energy facilities have widened the threat further, raising concerns that disruption could spread beyond Iranian supply to the infrastructure and alternative routes that have helped keep Gulf crude flowing.” That is the difference between a sanctioned-supply story and a shipping-and-infrastructure story, and it is why the move is being priced as inflation rather than as an energy-sector rotation.
  • Oil traders are shortening their horizons. Morgan Stanley’s co-head of global oil trading, Brendan Ross, told the Asia Pacific Petroleum Conference in Singapore on Wednesday that war-weary traders are concentrating derivatives positions over shorter time horizons and becoming more “clinical” about risk, shifting from broad exposure toward specific instruments: “People have been more precise with their risk. They’ve decided what they really want and what’s an unexpected bleed.” Reported by Bloomberg.
  • Oil is the macro variable. Higher crude is feeding straight into the inflation debate and into a Fed whose last vote had three members pushing for a hike — which is why a supply story is moving the whole tape rather than just energy. Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn: upcoming inflation data will be “particularly important” in determining whether $100 oil is feeding into the Fed outlook.
