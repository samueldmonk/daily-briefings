The lead

Bessent doubles the buyback, and stocks claw back a piece of a bruising week — but the bond market still sets the terms

U.S. markets are closed for the weekend. The figures below are Friday’s official close, August 21, 2026.

Stocks rose on Friday and trimmed their losses from a shaky week — even though the bond market, which has been the centre of Wall Street’s action, remained jumpy and added more pressure, as the Associated Press put it. The Dow did the heavy lifting, adding 517.80 points, or 1%, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq composite each gained 0.4% and the Russell 2000 of smaller companies rose 0.9%.

The catalyst was the Treasury, not the Fed. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the department had doubled the size of its long-term debt buybacks, from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation — and told CNBC the figure “could be more than the 4 billion per issue,” declining to commit to a number because it depends on conditions. “All we’re trying to do is get people to focus on the fundamentals and not trade the headlines during a quiet period in a thin market,” he said. He also discussed currency interventions involving the yen and the peso, and new fiscal consolidation plans.

It only partly worked. Trading Economics reported the 10-year yield at 4.7% and the 30-year at about 5.25%, the long bond nearly erasing Wednesday’s decline. “Markets are ending the week on a softer tone after the relative calm of early August was disrupted by renewed pressure in global bond markets, another rise in oil prices and growing uncertainty around the Federal Reserve’s next move,” said Daniela Hathorn, senior market analyst at Capital.com. “U.S. equities have pulled back from record highs, with the Nasdaq among the weaker performers, as higher long-term borrowing costs once again challenge elevated valuations.”

Two other things did work on Friday: Ross Stores rallied after reporting stronger quarterly profit than analysts expected, and a report suggesting strong growth in U.S. business activity raised hopes for corporate profits broadly. Oil prices climbed. Underneath it, the geopolitics stayed loud — Iran said its response to new U.S. threats would be “devastating” after Washington pledged the toughest financial penalties in history, and Bessent argued that maximum economic pressure means “likely there will not be a large-scale kinetic restart.”

On the radar

  • Nvidia earnings. Morgan Stanley reset its Nvidia forecast ahead of the company’s Q2 FY27 report — the single largest scheduled catalyst for a tech tape that just shed more than 3% over five sessions.
  • Marvell Technology reports August 27. Friday’s 6% drop was explicitly framed as risk reduction into that print.
  • The long end. Whether Bessent’s doubled buybacks hold the 30-year below the 5.33% level it touched earlier in the week is the question equities are actually trading. Trading Economics also reported that long-term yields rose after Fed Chair Warsh signalled a rate hike may not be his preferred tool against higher inflation.
  • Iran. Iran finalized a preferential trade agreement with Oman on Friday as Trump threatened an “economic D-Day.” Vice President JD Vance called economic pressure “the most effective tool that we have.” Crude above $90 Brent is the transmission channel into U.S. inflation expectations.
  • Positioning. Pershing Square drew down its Alphabet investment this quarter, taking profits — disclosed in the filing that circulated this week.
