Top Story

Second time unlucky: N-able's incomplete N-central patch becomes an exploited zero-day with MSP supply-chain reach

CISA on Monday added CVE-2026-18577 — an authentication-bypass-via-alternate-path flaw in N-able's N-central remote monitoring and management platform — to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog after attackers took over customer servers. The bug (CVSS 8.2) exists because the fix for an earlier flaw, CVE-2026-18556, proved incomplete: threat actors found a patch bypass and resumed exploitation in the wild (The Hacker News, Help Net Security).

The stakes are bigger than one server. N-central is the console MSPs use to manage fleets of customer endpoints — successful exploitation yields administrative access, and attackers have abused the built-in Take Control remote-access feature to pivot from compromised servers into managed endpoints and plant persistence (Help Net Security). N-able says in-the-wild exploitation has been observed since August 1, with attackers deploying Cloudflare Tunnel (cloudflared) on compromised hosts for persistent access (Rapid7). N-able shipped build 2026.3.1.7 on August 2 as the first unaffected version.

Federal civilian agencies have until Wednesday, August 6 to remediate under the risk-based BOD 26-04 regime — one of the shortest windows CISA assigns, reflecting the supply-chain blast radius.

Patch Priority

Most urgent action today

Upgrade N-able N-central to 2026.3.1.7 — federal deadline Wednesday (2 days left)

CVE-2026-18577 (CVSS 8.2, actively exploited, KEV-listed Aug 3) affects all N-central builds prior to 2026.3.1.7. It is a bypass of a prior fix, so systems patched against CVE-2026-18556 are still vulnerable. Upgrade, then review admin accounts and Take Control session history for signs of pivot into managed endpoints.

Also overdue: the exploited Cisco Secure FMC static-credential flaw (CVE-2026-20316) went past its federal deadline on Aug 1, and Arista VeloCloud (CVE-2026-16812) on Jul 30 — see the KEV board below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

INC Ransom — the SMA1000 landlord

The INC Ransomware operation has emerged as the dominant actor exploiting SonicWall's recently disclosed SMA 1000 VPN flaws (CVE-2026-15409/15410), and its activity has accelerated since the beginning of August (The Hacker News). The group has claimed 885 victims to date, with the most recent listed on August 2. The two flaws were exploited as zero-days since at least June 22 before SonicWall shipped fixes in mid-July — meaning any appliance patched late should be treated as potentially already compromised and hunted, not just updated.

Breaches & Incidents

ExtortionManufacturing

Tata Electronics confirms June incident

The Indian electronics manufacturer confirmed a June 2026 cybersecurity incident after the World Leaks ransomware group published more than 200,000 alleged company files online (2026 breach trackers). The confirmation-after-leak pattern is now the standard extortion playbook.

RansomwarePharma

Inotiv notifies 9,542 people

The pharmaceutical research firm is notifying 9,542 individuals that their data was exposed in a ransomware attack, per 2026 breach roundups tracking the disclosure. Inotiv's notifications land amid a run of life-sciences sector incidents this year.

Third partyFinance

Deutsche Bank confirms third-party breach

A Deutsche Bank spokesperson confirmed a breach at a third-party provider after a ransomware gang claimed access to internal bank data (Cybernews). The bank characterizes the impact as limited to the external provider; the claim itself remains attacker-asserted.

Leak siteFood & beverage

Fairlife listed by Anubis

The Anubis ransomware group listed Coca-Cola dairy subsidiary Fairlife on its leak site on July 20, and Coca-Cola had notified the SEC of the initial breach (2026 breach trackers). A leak-site listing is an attacker claim; the SEC notification is the confirmed part.
