Top Story

Operation CameraSwarm: one operator, 14,530 cameras, and a backdoor that outlives a factory reset

Between 17 June and 22 July 2026 — a 35-day window — a single operator compromised at least 14,530 Dahua IP cameras and related surveillance devices, in a campaign researchers have named Operation CameraSwarm. Headline counts vary by outlet between 14,000, 14,500+ and the precise 14,530; all three are reported here rather than averaged.

Three access methods ran concurrently. A brute-forcing system scanned TCP port 37777 and compromised devices at 12,324 unique IP addresses. A further 1,923 cameras were reached through authentication bypass using CVE-2021-33044 and CVE-2021-33045 — flaws disclosed five years ago. And a cloud-relay attack reached 283 cameras sitting behind NAT using nothing but serial numbers and SDK credentials embedded in Dahua's own applications.

The part that should worry defenders is persistence. The operator planted a backdoor account named p2pwn with the password p2password, found on 1,923 cameras. Changing the admin password does not remove it, and on most affected firmware versions it also survives a factory reset — meaning the standard remediation playbook for a compromised camera does not work here.

The devices are concentrated in Ukraine and Russia. Researchers assess the operator as Russian-speaking based on language artifacts recovered from the working directory.

⚡ Patch Priority — SonicWall SMA1000, due tomorrow

CVE-2026-83548 — server-side request forgery in SonicWall SMA1000 appliances, CVSS 10.0, actively exploited and in CISA's catalogue with a federal remediation date of September 5, 2026 — one day left. Affected models are the 6210, 7210 and 8200v; fixed in 12.4.3-03526 and 12.5.0-02952. Its companion CVE-2026-83549 (OS command injection, same appliances) carries the same date. If you run SMA1000 anywhere, this is today's work.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NewActive

The CameraSwarm operator

Not a ransomware crew and not a named APT — a single operator, assessed as Russian-speaking from language artifacts left in the working directory, who built an industrial-scale access operation out of three cheap techniques: credential brute force, two five-year-old CVEs, and vendor SDK credentials shipped inside Dahua's own apps.

The tradecraft worth noting is the p2pwn backdoor account: it is cleared by neither of the two actions an operator would normally take to clean a device — a password change or a factory reset. The target set — surveillance cameras concentrated in Ukraine and Russia — points at collection rather than extortion.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • Seven vulnerabilities were added to the KEV catalogue on September 2, 2026, on evidence of active exploitation. They split across two remediation dates.
  • Due September 5, 2026 — 1 day left — five CVEs: CVE-2026-83548 and CVE-2026-83549 (SonicWall SMA1000), CVE-2026-9586 (Sangoma Switchvox), CVE-2026-82329 (JFrog Artifactory), CVE-2026-49869 (Kestra OSS).
  • Due September 16, 2026 — 12 days left — two CVEs: CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette) and CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM).
  • Five plus two is the full set of seven. The September 5 date was corroborated independently this edition by CISA's own alert page and by third-party trackers.
