The Lead

A global bond sell-off does the driving: Japan’s 10-year touches 3% for the first time in 30 years, the U.S. 30-year adds four basis points to 5.251% — and the session closes lower across the board — Dow −419.02 points, or 0.79%, S&P 500 −0.71%, Nasdaq Composite −1.03% — off the lows it set in the last hour (official close, September 1)

The United States and Iran returned to open hostilities over the weekend. U.S. forces struck an Iranian island in the Strait of Hormuz on Sunday and Tehran retaliated against U.S. military targets in the Middle East; President Trump told a Fox News reporter “We’re going to hit them hard… There will be a response.” After six months of war the conflict remains at an impasse, with Tehran keeping the Strait closed and Washington maintaining a counter-blockade of Iranian ports (AFP via Yahoo Finance). Schwab’s market-open note adds an overnight attack on a cargo ship transiting the Strait, and TheStreet reports that two oil tankers — one Saudi and one South Korean-owned — were hit by projectiles on Monday night as the two sides resumed hostilities. The bond move is global, and the record it is setting is a duration record, not a level record

A read fetched this run says U.S. 30-year bonds “reached levels not seen since 2006.” That is not what the underlying reporting says.
