The Lead

Oil Retreats, CPI Lands In Line — and Wall Street Snaps a Four-Day Slide (as of ~2:25 PM ET)

August CPI rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4% on the month and 3.4% from a year earlier, the Bureau of Labor Statistics reported Friday; both readings matched the Dow Jones consensus. Core CPI rose 0.3% for the month — a tenth of a point above forecast — with the core annual rate at 2.4%, in line.

The tape read it as relief rather than threat, because the other half of the week’s problem eased at the same time: crude fell back after four sessions of oil-and-yield pressure. TheStreet quoted WTI down 3.35% to $99.05 and Brent down 3.61% to $103.70 before the bell; CNBC put WTI at $99.44 (−3%) and Brent at $104.68 (−2.74%). Motley Fool quote modules read this run (~2:25 PM ET) had the S&P 500 at 7,671.67 (+1.1%, +79.97), the Dow at 52,642.65 (+1.1%, +578.55) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,391.88 (+1.2%, +310.16). At the opening bell TheStreet logged the Russell 2000 up 1.04%.

What did not ease was the rate path. CME FedWatch odds of a hike at next Wednesday’s FOMC decision stood at 85.6% at 10:03 AM ET per TheStreet, and the Motley Fool put the probability at 86%, up from 60% a week ago. “Friday’s CPI print was in-line with expectations, but inflation is still too hot, and the Federal Reserve’s hands are tied,” said Skyler Weinand, chief investment officer at Regan Capital. “A rate hike next week is all but assured.”

Stocks had fallen for four straight sessions into Thursday on rising oil and Treasury yields; August PPI came in at +0.4% on the month and +5.4% from a year earlier. Trading floors also paused Friday to mark the 25th anniversary of the September 11 attacks.
