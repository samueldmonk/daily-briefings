Top Story

CISA adds APT29-exploited Apple flaw to KEV; agencies have until Nov. 18

CISA on Wednesday added CVE-2024-27834 (CVSS 5.5), an improper-authentication flaw that lets an attacker with arbitrary read and write capability bypass Pointer Authentication across multiple Apple operating systems, to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog following reports of active exploitation (The Hacker News; CISA alert). Apple fixed it in May 2024 in iOS 16.7.8 and iPadOS 16.7.8, iOS 17.5 and iPadOS 17.5, macOS Sonoma 14.5, tvOS 17.5, watchOS 10.5 and Safari 17.5; it was first demonstrated as a zero-day at Pwn2Own Vancouver in March 2024 (The Hacker News).

The addition follows Sekoia’s report that actors possibly linked to Russia exploited previously patched Safari and Chrome flaws to deliver malware that harvests browser and device data. Sekoia attributes the activity to APT29 (Cozy Bear, Midnight Blizzard) with “moderate to high confidence”; compromised legitimate sites, look-alike sites and a supply-chain attack on Plerdy SEO Tracker served the malicious JavaScript that profiled visitors before triggering the chain. Federal civilian agencies are told to patch by Nov. 18, 2026 (The Hacker News).

Patch Priority

Federal deadline — TODAY, Friday, Nov. 6, 2026 (0 days left): WordPress Core CVE-2026-119977 (CVSS 8.1) — a time-of-check time-of-use race condition that CISA says lets unauthenticated attackers execute code on the server during the upgrade window, via predictable, web-accessible staging paths used in full-package core upgrades. Patches shipped late last month; Wordfence says it blocked 12,632 exploit attempts in 24 hours (The Hacker News).

OVERDUE since Tuesday, Nov. 3, 2026: Plex Media Server (CVE-2026-96652, -96655) and Siemens Software Center (CVE-2026-110936) — from CISA’s Oct. 20 KEV batch; that batch’s other entries, Plex CVE-2026-96654/-96656 and SiteGuard CVE-2026-106931, carried an Oct. 23 deadline and are overdue (dates per The Hacker News, Oct. 21; desk record). Overdue since Monday, Nov. 2: Ivanti Connect Secure CVE-2026-111357 (CVSS 8.8), which Ivanti says was exploited before disclosure when chained with auth-bypass CVE-2026-104935; Ivanti released fixes Oct. 29 (Rapid7; Help Net Security). SOCRadar warns a clean Integrity Checker result is not proof an appliance is safe (SOCRadar).

Not in KEV but urgent: Siemens SIMATIC IoT2050 Advanced CVE-2026-58115 (CVSS 10.0) — exploited since at least Oct. 30 to drop a Linux bot and command backdoors via Node-RED; fixed in 4.3.4.1 or later, or uninstall/harden Node-RED (The Hacker News). Also overdue: GitLab CVE-2026-97229 — BleepingComputer reports more than 1,000 internet-exposed GitLab servers still unpatched (BleepingComputer via Xloggs feed) — plus Cisco ASA/FTD CVE-2026-76418, Linux “Fragnesia” and PHP2Shell.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Russia

APT29 (Cozy Bear / Midnight Blizzard)

The state-sponsored group affiliated with Russia’s Foreign Intelligence Service is behind the “Poisoned Source” watering-hole campaign, per Sekoia (“moderate to high confidence”), whose iOS exploit chain prompted CISA to add Apple’s CVE-2024-27834 to the KEV catalog this week. The chain pairs a WebKit remote code execution flaw with a pointer-authentication bypass to run a JavaScript payload that steals browser cookies (The Hacker News).

Breaches & Incidents

New Financial

Crédit Mutuel Arkéa: attackers move bank funds through a service provider

Attackers got into French cooperative banking group Crédit Mutuel Arkéa’s internal IT network through a service provider’s platform and used the bank’s own account to transfer funds to other financial institutions; Arkéa confirmed the attack on Nov. 4. Blockchain investigator ZachXBT estimated more than €118 million (about $133 million) was stolen — Arkéa hasn’t confirmed a final figure — and the group says customer funds weren’t affected. Arkéa froze transfer channels, alerted supervisors and law enforcement, and some funds have been blocked; no attacker has been named (Security Boulevard, Nov. 6).

New Healthcare

Myriad Genetics intrusion halts genetic testing

Myriad Genetics detected unauthorized access within its IT network on Nov. 2 and disclosed it in an 8-K on Nov. 5, temporarily suspending testing operations and order-management systems and resetting certain credentials. It is assessing whether patient records were accessed; most systems had returned to normal by Nov. 5, and it doesn’t currently expect a material adverse effect (Security Boulevard, Nov. 6).

New Data breach

Revolve: saved payment cards taken “through automated means”

Online fashion retailer Revolve Group said an unauthorized actor may have obtained customers’ saved payment-card data from its live e-commerce platform; the incident occurred Sept. 11 and was discovered Sept. 29. Texas’s breach portal lists 127,200 affected Texans, and data behind that listing shows 1,515,073 people in total — Revolve hasn’t confirmed the total (Security Boulevard, Nov. 6).

New Hosting

DreamHost: months-long intrusion stole customer files and inboxes

Starting in August, hackers exploited an unspecified flaw in DreamHost’s file manager to obtain session tokens; the attack wasn’t detected until late October. DreamHost says “well under 10%” of its roughly 400,000 paying customers were affected; stolen data includes files, email inboxes, credentials and session tokens (Security Boulevard, Nov. 6, citing TechCrunch).

New Data breach

DigiCert: migration mistake exposed customer identity documents

A February 2025 server migration put a VPN-only internal DigiCert system on the internet; on Oct. 19 DigiCert confirmed one unauthorized self-registered account accessed archived customer records between June 27 and July 2, including some driver’s licenses, passports and utility bills. DigiCert says its certificate authority services, CertCentral and DigiCert ONE weren’t compromised (Security Boulevard, Nov. 6, citing BleepingComputer).

Ransomware

HYFLOCK ransomware flaw leaves parts of large files readable

In HYFLOCK’s Oct. 17 build, the malware encrypted only the first 4,096 bytes of larger files examined in testing, leaving the rest readable; researchers recovered selected contents (73 internal components from seven encrypted Office files) but did not restore complete documents or produce a general decryptor. An earlier October build could permanently discard file data. Check Point traced four generations from April through October 2026; the RaaS appeared in underground promotion in March and its leak site listed eight (unverified) victim claims by Oct. 13. Defenders are advised to preserve affected files and execution evidence before attempting restoration (Cyber Security News, Nov. 5).

ICS / OT

Second wave: hackers warm supermarket fridges across Europe

Attackers remotely changed the settings on refrigeration controls at supermarkets in Belgium, Sweden, Estonia and other European countries overnight into Monday, Nov. 2, warming fridges and freezers. Danfoss, the Danish maker of refrigeration controls, said its products were involved — as they were three weeks earlier, when attackers hit hundreds of French supermarkets. No one had claimed the attacks by Monday night, and it is not known whether they are linked to France (The Hacker News).

In Belgium attackers switched units into defrost mode; an ICA store in southern Sweden said its fridges and freezers had been set to 50°C. Affected chains include Delhaize (several dozen stores), Carrefour (15 of more than 700), Coop Sweden, Musgrave’s SuperValu in Ireland (13 of 223) and Intermarché in Belgium and Portugal. A Danfoss spokesperson said the Dutch systems hit were not on a secure network and still used default passwords; Belgium’s federal prosecutor has opened a criminal investigation. CERT-FR’s October alert — covering Danfoss AK-SM and AK-SC, Copeland xweb and Dixell xweb controllers — says to take devices off the internet, check defrost schedules and restore access only via VPN (The Hacker News).

Espionage

APT29-linked “Poisoned Source” watering hole hits iOS and Android

Sekoia ties a large watering-hole campaign to Russia-linked APT29: compromised sites, fake domains and a supply-chain compromise of Ukrainian analytics service Plerdy SEO Tracker served injected JavaScript that fingerprinted devices, then delivered platform-specific N-day exploit chains to steal browser cookies and, in some cases, Wi-Fi passwords or full device data. Discovered mid-October 2026. (The Hacker News)

Supply chain

cPGuard update channel hijacked to backdoor Linux servers

An attacker used compromised credentials to break into cPGuard’s update system on Nov. 2 and pushed a script that installed a backdoor and collected password hashes; it shipped in versions 5.88.12–5.88.19, and 5.88.25+ removes it. OPSSHIELD says copied credentials may still work — rotate passwords and keys. (The Hacker News)

Data breach

Chainstack breach exposes customer data and password hashes

The blockchain infrastructure provider says an attacker entered a development environment via a third-party component on Oct. 26, escalated into parts of production, and was cut off Oct. 31; it is assessed as part of a large AI-powered campaign that surfaced last week. (The Hacker News)

Incident

mSecure tells users to change passwords and reset 2FA

A Nov. 3 email from developer mSeven Software cites suspicious activity linked to the password manager and urges customers to change passwords, reset 2FA and remove passkeys; it does not say whether stored data was exposed. (The Hacker News)

Espionage

SlippyNunchucks hijacks Sogou Input Method DNS to drop DarkVigil

ESET attributes a DNS-hijacking surveillance campaign, observed since July 1, 2026, abusing insecure HTTP traffic in Windows versions of Sogou Input Method to deploy the DarkVigil (FDMTP) implant against targets in Hong Kong, Thailand, Japan, Vietnam, Oman and Sri Lanka. (The Hacker News)

Ransomware

3AM ransomware hits UK school estate — possibly AI-driven

An affiliate encrypted about 37 of nearly 50 machines reached from an unmanaged host; Bitdefender suspects AI-generated commands and an automated agentic loop, citing repeated, slightly modified commands issued every 40–100 seconds. Activity ran Sept. 10–19; the attacker pulled credentials from three Veeam backup servers and created backdoor domain-admin accounts “library” and “svc_bkpmon.” (BleepingComputer)

Industry

CrowdStrike to buy Fiddler AI and Dreadnode

CrowdStrike agreed to acquire Fiddler AI (agent observability) and Dreadnode (testing AI systems for security weaknesses) for $495 million combined, per the WSJ; it says Falcon will continuously scan AI models, applications and agents before deployment. Shares rose more than 2% early. (Yahoo Finance)

Crypto

Mercado Bitcoin pauses deposits and withdrawals

Brazil’s largest crypto exchange paused services after a breach that resulted in $49 million in stolen funds; it says $11 million has been blocked with Tether, Circle, Paxos, BRL1 and Chainalysis, and Tether froze more than 4.5 million USDT. (The Hacker News)

Crypto

BTCPay Server hot wallets drained

Bitcoin and Litecoin were stolen from instances running 2.4.1 or earlier; BTCPay urges moving to a fresh server with a new wallet and says there are no credible drain reports on 2.4.7. (The Hacker News)

Botnet

Google disrupts 1.3M-device x.cpe botnet

Google says it is highly confident TV-box maker Videostrong deliberately built in the backdoor and sold access to criminals; it banned the company from certified Android. The backdoor remains in firmware and owners cannot remove it. (The Hacker News)

Data breach

Florida student aid site hacked

SecurityWeek reports data of 4.9 million people at risk after a breach of the state’s financial aid website. (SecurityWeek, via search)

Data breach

Chainalysis breached via “offensive agentic techniques”

Attackers detected in October accessed customer user IDs, names, emails and role labels; the firm says it is unrelated to the Binance hack. (Help Net Security)

Supply chain

Trezor warns of seed-stealing implant

Buyers from reseller CryptoBilis since March 2026 are told to move funds to a new wallet with a new seed. (Help Net Security)

Supply chain

Claude Code extension on Cursor Marketplace backdoored

Two tampered versions installed a persistent SSH backdoor; Cursor says up to 5,000 users were affected. (The Hacker News)

Ransomware

Synology NAS targeted via patched DSM flaws

Ransomware actors are exploiting CVE-2024-10441 and related flaws, patched in December 2024, on outdated DSM. (The Hacker News)
