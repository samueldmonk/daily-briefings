The Lead

Monday 14 September 2026 · official close, 4:00 PM ET

Stocks close lower on an AI warning from the AI labs — but well off the lows

The S&P 500 fell 37.00 points, or 0.5%, to close at 7,619.98. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 152.09 points, or 0.3%, to 52,421.20. The Nasdaq Composite fell 146.62 points, or 0.6%, to 26,186.41 (Associated Press). Every one of those rows is internally consistent and reconciles against Friday’s closes, and CNBC’s wrap makes the same point the numbers do: the market traded off its worst levels of the session as rate and oil pressure eased a little into the afternoon. At the lows the S&P was down 0.8% and the Nasdaq 1.3%, with the Dow off almost 300 points.

The selling started with an essay. Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei called publicly for the industry to slow the development of frontier models on safety grounds; OpenAI’s Sam Altman agreed the industry needs to “pace the frontier” and warned the pace “could go very badly” with humans potentially losing control; Elon Musk, who runs xAI, backed Amodei over the weekend, and Altman suggested OpenAI may need to delay its much-anticipated IPO to focus on safety (Investrade’s closing recap). Amodei’s own claim is the one that moved the tape: that within six to twelve months AI agents “could be capable of taking over the entire internet potentially causing hundreds of billions of dollars in damage.” Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon had resigned last week saying both companies are “gambling with our lives.” President Trump publicly dismissed the call. Semiconductors took the hit: TheStreet had the VanEck Semiconductor ETF down over 4% at 1:36 PM, and Yahoo Finance’s live coverage put a key industry gauge down 5.7%. Schwab noted the PHLX Semiconductor Index came into the session already about 19% below its all-time high of three months ago.

The path there was a grind, not a straight line. At the 9:33 AM open (TheStreet) the S&P 500 was −0.75%, the Dow −0.28% and the Nasdaq Composite −1.17%, with the Russell 2000 +0.45%. By 11:26 AM TheStreet had the S&P at −0.65% with 233 holdings advancing. The Motley Fool’s 11:38 AM card had the S&P and Nasdaq Composite both −0.44%. By 2:52 PM TheStreet had the Russell 2000 at −0.07% — nearly flat — which is the day in miniature: this was a large-cap AI problem, not a broad one. The Russell 2000 finished −0.40% at 2,892 (Investrade’s closing recap) — a smaller decline than the S&P 500 or the Nasdaq Composite, though the Dow gave up less still.

The late-morning bounce had a second cause. Stocks pared their losses after President Trump posted that “The failing Nation of Iran wants to make a deal, quickly and badly” and that he would decide whether the United States engages; Iran’s state-linked Tasnim News later rejected the claim that Tehran wants a quick deal.

Two other things pressed all day. Oil rose again — Saudi Arabia shut its East-West pipeline after drone attacks and Oman postponed talks with Iran over the Strait of Hormuz — and the 10-year Treasury yield touched 5.014% — its highest since October 2023 — before reversing, and was last quoted up more than a basis point at 4.987% in late trading (CNBC). Brent ran past $109 and US crude came close to $105 at the intraday peaks, with diesel at an all-time high (NBC News); both pared back by the bell, Brent ending at $105.68 and WTI settling at $101.39 (Investrade). The settles, not the peaks, are what the commodities table below carries. Friday’s hotter CPI has markets pricing a rate hike on Wednesday somewhere between roughly 85% and better than 90% depending on which source and which hour — the full spread is broken out under On the Radar below — and Goldman Sachs switched its call from no change to a hike after the print.

Movers & Drivers

Up

Rum Group surges on a reported Anthropic compute deal

RUM jumped after The Information identified Anthropic as the unnamed counterparty on the six-year, $13.7 billion compute contract the company disclosed in an August securities filing. Reported moves differ by the hour and the outlet: Yahoo Finance and Proactive Investors have it +27% to $1.99, while several others reported roughly +20%. Anthropic would lease capacity at a data centre under construction in Maysville, Georgia, and can buy up to 51 million RUM shares at $0.01 each. Rum Group renamed itself from Rumble after buying German neocloud Northern Data in June for about $767 million.

UpCyber bid

Cybersecurity is the hedge against the AI scare

The same warnings that hit chips bid up security names. TheStreet at 10:29 AM had CrowdStrike +10.45% and Palo Alto Networks +8.97%; the Motley Fool’s midday card said CrowdStrike “soared 15%”; 24/7 Wall St. headlined CrowdStrike and Zscaler at +12% with Palo Alto +11%, and TECHi put CrowdStrike and Zscaler at +15%. All four readings are printed; none is averaged. CrowdStrike CEO George Kurtz had responded to Amodei that frontier labs will keep building regardless and security’s job is to make that safer. Palo Alto also beat on fiscal Q4.

DownSemis

Chips and memory lead the decline

Late in the session, at roughly 2:05 PM, Trading Economics had Micron −6.50%, Broadcom −3.97% and Nvidia −3.38%. Earlier readings: Micron −5.72% and Nvidia −3.0% on the Fool’s 11:38 AM strip; premarket, Marvell −7.59% and Lam Research −8%. Ellison scraps a $7.5 billion Oracle share sale

Oracle co-founder Larry Ellison has axed plans to sell up to $7.5 billion of stock in an about-face, as the company comes under pressure over its all-in bet on AI. Disclosures the previous day had shown he intended to sell as many as 50 million shares by the end of October; he has now elected not to sell any (Investrade’s closing recap). On the Radar

  • The Fed meets tomorrow and Wednesday. The FOMC’s September meeting runs 15–16 September, and a hike would be the first increase in borrowing costs since 2023. A reading taken this run puts the probability at nearly 89%, which sits inside — and does not resolve — the spread of readings listed below.
  • FOMC decision and press conference, Wednesday 16 September. Odds of a 25bp hike read differently by source: roughly 85% (Capital.com via TheStreet), 88% (CME FedWatch via Schwab), 88.5% (Yahoo Finance) a 83%–90% range (Motley Fool) and greater than 90% as of Monday (CNBC). Goldman Sachs revised its forecast for this week from no change to a hike after Friday’s August inflation print. Schwab notes this week is the last regularly scheduled chance to hike before late October, which narrowly precedes the November midterms.
  • Consumer sentiment is deteriorating. The University of Michigan’s preliminary September Index of Consumer Sentiment fell to 47.8, its second-lowest reading on record, against expectations of 51.5 and 51.7 in August. Year-ahead inflation expectations spiked to 4.6% from 4.0%. The final reading lands 25 September.
  • Wednesday also brings Lennar earnings plus August retail sales, export and import prices; housing starts and building permits follow Thursday, and the Bank of Japan decides Friday (Schwab’s week-ahead).
  • The Senate holds a key procedural vote Tuesday on the Clarity Act, which would establish a regulatory framework for cryptocurrencies and other digital assets (Schwab).
  • Friday is triple witching. Investrade’s closing recap relays a widely shared stat from @Bluekurtic on X: more than $2 trillion in delta notional options expire Friday, and since 2012 the S&P 500 has been negative on the event day 12 of 14 times. Printed as the recap relays it, an X post rather than an exchange figure.
  • Three central banks in four days. Beyond the Fed on Wednesday, the Bank of England is expected to hold at 3.75% on Thursday even with markets pricing about 100bp of tightening over the next year, and the Bank of Japan meets Friday with a 25bp hike described as close to locked in. On the FOMC itself, UBS expects a 10–2 vote to raise, with Governors Waller and Bowman dissenting in favour of a hold (Investrade).
  • Oil supply risk is the open variable. Saudi Arabia shut the East-West pipeline that bypasses the Strait of Hormuz after drone attacks; the Houthis claim strikes on King Khalid Air Base and the capture of the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands near Bab al-Mandeb; Oman has postponed Hormuz talks with Iran without setting a new date. The pipeline carries about 4 million barrels a day to the Red Sea port of Yanbu and is the key route for Saudi exports to bypass the war-disrupted Strait of Hormuz; industry sources cited by Reuters said repairs could take five to six weeks, though another suggested limited operations might resume sooner (Investrade).
