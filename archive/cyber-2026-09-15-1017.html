Top Story

Zero-day · actively exploited

The appliance that reads every email is now an unauthenticated root shell

Cisco warned customers on Monday that CVE-2026-76461, a CVSS 9.8 flaw in the email-parsing code of AsyncOS for Secure Email Gateway, has been exploited in the wild as a zero-day. An unauthenticated, remote attacker can send a specially crafted email containing malicious SQL statements and have arbitrary commands executed on the underlying operating system with root privileges. Both the physical and virtual appliances are affected, in any configuration; Secure Email and Web Manager and Secure Web Appliance are not.

Cisco says its PSIRT became aware of exploitation in September 2026 but has not described the attacks or attributed them. It published indicators of compromise while noting the obvious limit of those IoCs: an attacker with root on the box can remove or hide them. For virtual appliances Cisco’s guidance is not a patch but a rebuild — deploy a new VM on a fixed release, reconstruct the configuration from scratch, and renew every credential and cryptographic key that was installed on the appliance.

CISA added the CVE to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Monday 14 September and set a federal remediation deadline of 17 September. It is only the second Secure Email Gateway flaw ever to reach the KEV list, after CVE-2025-20393, which China-linked actors began exploiting in late 2025. It also lands days after Cisco and CISA warned that CVE-2026-20079 and CVE-2026-20316 in Secure Firewall Management Center were being exploited by both Russian state-sponsored hackers and profit-driven criminals.

Patch Priority

Do this first

CVE-2026-76461 — Cisco Secure Email Gateway (AsyncOS), CVSS 9.8. Unauthenticated root command execution by crafted email, exploited in the wild. CISA-stated federal remediation deadline: 17 September 2026 — 2 days left. Treat any internet-reachable appliance as suspect rather than merely unpatched: Cisco’s own remediation for virtual appliances is a rebuild plus full credential rotation, which is what you do when you cannot trust the IoCs.

Already overdue and higher-scored: CVE-2026-85706 (GitLab CE/EE, CVSS 10.0) was due 14 September and is one day past. If both are in your estate, GitLab is the older breach window; Cisco is the shorter clock.

Deadlines above are the dates CISA states per CVE under BOD 26-04, which superseded and revoked BOD 22-01 on 10 June 2026 and replaced the flat three-week window with a 16-tier, risk-based matrix — four factors (asset exposure, KEV status, exploit automation and post-exploitation impact) producing 24 tiers, the most dangerous remediated in three days with mandatory forensic triage. Agencies must be operating to it by 7 December 2026. A three-day window is normal now and is never widened to fit the retired rule.

Threat Actor Spotlight

China-linkedEspionageNew

UNC3569 — one click, one backdoor

Gen Threat Labs found CVE-2026-51990 while investigating a live intrusion by UNC3569, a group Google Threat Intelligence ties to China and places in the country’s hacker-for-hire scene, targeting government, education, technology and finance mostly across East and Southeast Asia. The flaw is in Tencent’s Sogou Input Method for Windows — an IME with hundreds of millions of installations — and chains three weaknesses (unvalidated command-line argument injection, unrestricted URL navigation, and an outdated un-sandboxed Chromium engine) so that a single crafted link silently opens a backdoor, with no user interaction beyond the click. The payload is GRAYRABBIT, a small implant the group has used for years: remote shell, file transfer both ways, and additional modules loaded from the attacker’s server on demand. Gen reported it to Tencent on 9 April 2026; Tencent confirmed a fix on 21 April, shipped by automatic update in version 16.3.0.3498.

Breaches & Incidents

NewGovernment

Japan’s Digital Agency: ~246,000 people, and the patch was known

Unauthorised access to servers at Japan’s Digital Agency may have leaked personal data on about 246,000 people — roughly 189,000 government employees and 57,000 private-sector workers engaged in government duties. Addresses and phone numbers were included; My Number identification numbers and bank account details were not. The intrusion hit the Government Solution Service, a system used across ministries and agencies, via an account belonging to a maintenance and operations contractor; investigators traced the entry point to a VPN device vulnerability the agency knew about but had not yet patched. The account was suspended and the equipment isolated. (Japan Times and Bloomberg give ~246,000; SecurityWeek headlines the figure as 240,000.)

NewSupply chain

A Twitch extension with 30,000 installs was shipping session tokens to a bot service

Socket’s Threat Research Team found that “Twitch Enhanced Viewer | JeetBot”, installed by more than 30,000 users across Chrome and Firefox, was forwarding live OAuth session tokens from nearly 31,000 users to proxy servers run by the Russian-language bot service JeetBot. Version 4.8, released in January 2026, POSTed captured tokens to a /set-token endpoint on JeetBot infrastructure with backups on deno.dev and deno.net. Anyone holding such a token can act on the account without the password and without two-factor. The behaviour contradicts the extension’s own disclosure that it does not collect, store or process user data. Remove it, then sign out of all devices to invalidate existing sessions.

Fintech

Revolut: the request came from a real government domain

Revolut has notified a subset of customers that personal and financial information was disclosed to an unauthorised third party impersonating a government agency. The requests arrived from a legitimate government-agency domain carrying valid domain authentication, so they were fulfilled in the belief they were genuine. Exposed material includes passport or driving-licence copies, identity-verification selfies, account statements and complete transaction histories including Bitcoin-related activity. Revolut blocked the address, alerted the agency, law enforcement and regulators, and says its core systems, mobile app and customer accounts were not compromised. The FT reports nearly 700 clients affected.

ExploitedDevOps

Three JFrog Artifactory flaws, chained into backdoors

Attackers chained CVE-2026-42018 (improper authentication, patched 12 August) to obtain an anonymous-user token and CVE-2026-42016 (insufficient token validation, patched 27 July) to escalate to administrator — activity running from mid-August through 8 September against self-hosted instances. Separately, multiple actors began exploiting CVE-2026-82329 (authentication bypass, patched 28 August) in the first week of September. Observed post-exploitation: persistent admin accounts, malicious plugins for arbitrary code execution, shell commands through the plugin endpoint, second-stage payloads, configuration and cluster-key exfiltration, token minting and asset enumeration.
