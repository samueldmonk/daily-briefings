The Lead

As of just after the opening bell, 15 September

A soft open into a Fed meeting nobody expects to be dovish

US stocks opened lower on Tuesday. Just after the bell the S&P 500 was off about 0.1%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 162 points or roughly 0.3%, and the Nasdaq Composite traded down about 0.1%. Futures had pointed the same way overnight, with Dow futures leading the downside at −0.6% and S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 futures each off 0.5%. A later intraday quote returned this run had the Dow at 52,139.44 (−281.76, −0.54%) and the Nasdaq Composite at 26,118.65 (−67.77, −0.26%) — both reconcile exactly against Monday’s closes, but they are readings from different points in the session and neither is averaged into the other.

The pressure is coming from rates, not equities. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.041% on Tuesday, its highest since July 2007, after briefly touching 5% intraday on Monday — a level it had not seen since 2023 — before easing back. The Federal Open Market Committee begins its September meeting today, and traders overwhelmingly expect a 25 basis-point hike on Wednesday, with pricing reported at roughly 90% to 92%. That would be the first increase since July 2023. Wednesday also brings the dot-plot and a press conference from Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh.

Underneath the index moves, the AI complex is doing the opposite of Monday. Losses in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq were mitigated by gains in AI-linked names that had been under pressure in the prior session: Nvidia and Micron Technology rose 1%, while Advanced Micro Devices and Intel jumped more than 2%. The proximate cause of Monday’s slide was an essay on AI safety by Anthropic chief executive Dario Amodei, and industry calls for a slowdown in AI development; SoftBank Group, a major OpenAI investor, jumped about 8% in Tokyo trading, recovering from its overnight losses.

Movers & Drivers

NewRebound

AI chips bounce off Monday’s hit

The names that took Monday’s AI-safety selling are leading the tape back. Nvidia and Micron each up 1%; AMD and Intel up more than 2%. It is those gains, not index strength, holding the S&P 500 and Nasdaq losses to about a tenth of a percent at the open.

NewRates

10-year yield at 5.041% — a nineteen-year high

The benchmark yield rose to 5.041% on Tuesday, the highest since July 2007, and was last up more than 3 basis points to around 5%. Monday it briefly topped 5% intraday for the first time since 2023 before reversing lower. The one-month rolling correlation between front-month WTI and the 10-year has climbed to 0.96 — the oil story and the rates story are now effectively one trade.

Energy

Oil stays elevated on a struck Saudi pipeline

Reuters priced US crude up 1.27% to $102.68 and Brent up 1.21% to $106.96 in the Asian session. Yemen’s Iran-aligned Houthis launched a new attack on Saudi Arabia on Monday, after Riyadh blamed Iran-backed fighters in Iraq for the strike on its east-west pipeline — which it said could disrupt as much as 4% of global oil supply. Gulf Arab states have postponed planned talks with Iran.

Overseas

SoftBank +8% in Tokyo

SoftBank Group, the Japanese giant and major OpenAI investor, jumped about 8% in Tokyo trading, recovering from overnight losses. Asian shares more broadly struggled, weighing Middle East tensions and the calls for an AI slowdown ahead of central bank meetings in both the US and Japan.

Chart of the Day — AMD

AMD is the session’s largest sourced single-name move on the page — up more than 2% at the open, alongside Intel, after Monday’s AI-safety selling.

On the Radar

  • Wednesday 16 September — FOMC decision. The dot-plot and Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference land with it. A hike would be the first since July 2023. Goldman Sachs chief economist David Mericle has framed the reasoning behind the call plainly: with pricing for a hike near 90%, the committee would likely want to avoid the market reaction that holding would provoke.
  • Central banks, plural. Reuters frames this week around key meetings in both the US and Japan, with the Bank of Japan also in view — part of why Asian shares struggled overnight.
  • Middle East supply risk. The Saudi east-west pipeline strike, fresh Houthi attacks on the kingdom, and Gulf Arab states postponing talks with Iran keep a bid under crude — and, through a 0.96 correlation, under yields.
  • A quiet tape by design. Little in the way of economic data or earnings Tuesday; Forgent Power Solutions (FPS) and Vera Bradley (VRA) report.
