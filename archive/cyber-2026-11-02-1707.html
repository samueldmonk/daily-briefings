Top Story

FBI attributes record $6.43B Binance theft to North Korea’s TraderTraitor

The FBI has officially attributed the theft of more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance to TraderTraitor, a North Korean hacking group. The haul eclipses the $1.46 billion taken from Bybit in February 2025, making it the largest cryptocurrency theft in history. The funds were stolen across 11 blockchains — 82% of them on Ethereum and Bitcoin — and $2.85 billion has been frozen on-chain (The Hacker News).

Binance restored withdrawals on Sunday, says it has secured about 95% of the total and has seen net withdrawals of $4.7 billion — less than 4% of the $128 billion it holds, per a spokesperson cited by Bloomberg (TheStreet). Yahoo Finance reports $4.649 billion in withdrawals against about $1.01 billion in deposits, and says the uncovered 5% sits in smaller, less liquid tokens (Yahoo Finance).

Patch Priority

Ivanti Connect Secure — CVE-2026-111357 (CVSS 8.8), privilege escalation, exploited. Federal deadline: TODAY, Monday Nov. 2, 2026 (0 days left). CISA added it and CVE-2026-118527 (CVSS 4.3, due Nov. 13) to KEV on Oct. 30. With auth-bypass CVE-2026-104935 (added to KEV Oct. 13), the flaws were chained by an actor tracked as UTA0576 to run code as root on VPN appliances without logging in (SOCRadar; SC World). Ivanti shipped fixes for the last two flaws on Oct. 29; prior reporting put the fixed builds at 25.1.1.3 and 22.8R2.6 (desk record, GBHackers). SOCRadar warns a clean Integrity Checker result is not proof an appliance is safe — memory and network forensics are needed.

Overdue: GitLab CVE-2026-97229 (CVSS 10.0) and Cisco ASA/FTD CVE-2026-76418 (due Nov. 1); Linux “Fragnesia” CVE-2026-46300 (due Oct. 31); PHP2Shell CVE-2026-109618/-109619 (due Oct. 29).

Breaches & Incidents

Supply chain

Claude Code extension on Cursor Marketplace backdoored

Two tampered versions of the extension installed a persistent SSH backdoor; Cursor says up to 5,000 users were affected, and it rotated keys and rebuilt the marketplace. (source)

Crypto

Polymarket pauses trading after DebugBear compromise

Attackers compromised third-party service DebugBear — whose DNS records were hijacked via its domain registrar — to serve crypto-draining JavaScript that took about $2.7 million from 33 deposit wallets in under an hour. (source)

Data breach

Keystone to destroy 18,835 hardware wallets

An attacker abused a third-party monitoring tool to obtain 25,964 customer records, 12,873 checkout records and a device-authenticity key; shipping of unshipped orders is suspended. (source)

Critical infrastructure

Fake alerts on Ukrainian and Russian warning systems

Ukraine’s air-raid app sent false nationwide red-level warnings; hours later Russia said its alert app was also attacked. Whether the incidents are connected is unclear. (source)

Ransomware

Aur0ra (Gold Ogre) leans on AI agents

Sophos says the group used Cursor to escalate from credential access to domain administrator, bypassing guardrails by framing intrusions as authorized red-team work. (source)

Ransomware

C3RB3R wave hits Synology NAS

Attackers are using old, patched Synology flaws to deploy C3RB3R ransomware; owners should update DSM. (source)
