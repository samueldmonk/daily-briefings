The lead

The close finally posted — a mixed finish to a week the Dow lost, with the 10-year back through 5%

Markets closed at 4:00 PM ET. The S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50; the Nasdaq Composite added 104.25 points, 0.39%, to 26,522.55; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average shed 95.40 points, 0.18%, to 51,682.64. On the week the Dow lost more than 1.5%, the S&P 500 eked out a loss, and the Nasdaq finished green.

The session was a triple witching — the quarterly simultaneous expiry of stock options, index futures and index options, which falls on the third Friday of March, June, September and December. TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre had flagged it before the open: “That tends to produce heavy volume and sharp swings, particularly in the final hour… This is mechanical action and should not be confused with a shift in the fundamental view of the market.”

Underneath it, rates did the work. The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.01%, up 0.07 percentage points on the session — back above a threshold it had crossed earlier in the week to reach its highest level since July 2007, before Thursday’s stabilisation let chips and big-cap technology catch a bid. The 30-year sits at 5.33% and the 2-year at 4.77%.

And the week’s marquee corporate story landed before the bell: Warren Buffett, 96, is stepping down as chairman of Berkshire Hathaway, becoming chairman emeritus effective immediately while remaining a director. His son Howard Buffett becomes chairman; Susan Decker continues as lead independent director. It comes a little more than nine months after Greg Abel, 64, took over as CEO. “Father Time always wins,” Buffett wrote to shareholders. “He has, however, been generous with me.” Berkshire stock declined modestly on the news; no percentage move for it was stated in anything read this run, so none is published here.

Provenance of the close, stated plainly. For four consecutive editions today this page refused a Friday close, because the only figures on offer were TheStreet’s 9:32 AM opening-bell print relabelled as a close. That trap fired again this run and was refused again (see Refused this run). The figures above come from a CNBC-attributed post-close rendering and are published because all three reconcile arithmetically against independently known prior closes: the Dow’s −95.40 on 51,682.64 computes to −0.18%; the S&P’s 7,650.50 against Thursday’s 7,637.72 computes to +0.17%; and the Nasdaq’s 26,522.55 against Thursday’s 26,418.30 computes to +0.39%. A second independent summary corroborated all three directions and magnitudes (Nasdaq +0.4%, S&P +0.2%, Dow −0.2%, Dow −1.5%+ on the week).

On the radar

  • SpaceX’s Nasdaq-100 weighting more than doubles at Friday’s close. As part of the index’s quarterly rebalance the weighting rises to about 2.82% from roughly 1.28%, triggering an estimated $15.5–22 billion in programmatic buying (figure attributed to Investing.com). The new weighting takes effect Monday 21 September. SPCX was +0.19% at $155.11 in the 9:14 AM premarket.
  • Sandisk joins the S&P 100 on 21 September. Same session as the Nasdaq-100 change takes effect.
  • The Fed’s own Silicon Valley Bank review landed Friday. Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman announced in a London speech that an outside review by Starling Advisory Group found Fed staff “knew, or should have known” the bank was vulnerable before its 2023 failure. The review found deposits were “94 percent uninsured and concentrated in venture capital-backed technology companies”.
  • Gulf leaders meet President Trump next week — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait and Oman — with the Iran conflict on the agenda. A tanker was hit by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz early Friday, the second vessel attacked there in hours; the fire was extinguished and the crew reported safe.
  • The Bank of Japan raised rates 25 basis points to 1.25%, a 31-year high, at the fastest pace of tightening since 1990; the Nikkei rose over 1%. RSM’s Joe Brusuelas has warned this sets up “another unwind of the globally important carry trade”.
  • The Trump administration approved a potential $24.3 billion sale of 48 Lockheed Martin F-35s and 49 Pratt & Whitney engines to Saudi Arabia. LMT was +0.17% at $539 premarket.
