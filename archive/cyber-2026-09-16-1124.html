Top Story

Forged admin tokens: WSO2 API Manager JWT bypass under active exploitation

A critical flaw in WSO2 API Manager, tracked as CVE-2026-5430 (CVSS 9.8), is being exploited in the wild, The Hacker News reported on 16 September, citing watchTowr. The bug is an improper verification of a cryptographic signature: per WSO2’s own advisory, “JWT authentication can be bypassed when a token is signed using an unsupported algorithm, allowing unauthorized access,” and successful exploitation “may lead to unauthorized access, including potential compromise of administrative accounts and full account takeover.”

watchTowr’s honeypot network captured JWT tokens arriving on 13 September 2026 with administrator privileges baked in. “The flaw exists in the service due to how JWT authentication accepts tokens signed with algorithms it does not support, then approves them anyway,” said Yordan Ganchev, principal threat intelligence specialist at watchTowr, who put the rating at CVSS 10.0 — WSO2 scores the flaw 10.0 in multi-tenant deployments and 9.8 in single-tenant ones.

Affected: WSO2 API Manager 4.1.0 through 4.6.0, plus API Control Plane, Traffic Manager and Universal Gateway (4.5.0 and 4.6.0). Fixes exist as community pull requests and as per-product update levels for support-subscription holders. Because the product sits in the path of API traffic to internal systems, Ganchev warned it offers attackers “lateral movement-as-a-service” — access to every API backend endpoint and to consumer keys and secrets for every registered application. The advisory was published in May 2026; the Hacktron Team is credited with the discovery.

Patch Priority

Today is the deadline: CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM) and CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette)

Federal civilian agencies have until 16 September 2026 — today, 0 days left — to remediate the LiteLLM improper-authentication flaw (CVSS 8.8, an MCP Streamable HTTP endpoint that accepts an arbitrary Bearer token) and the Starlette HTTP request/response smuggling flaw (CVSS 6.5). Wiz has observed exploitation attempts against CVE-2026-59822 on its honeypots, and links Qilin-associated actors to the LiteLLM chain. Running a close second: CVE-2026-76461, the actively exploited Cisco Secure Email Gateway (AsyncOS) flaw at CVSS 9.8 with no workarounds, due 17 September — 1 day left.

Launched in early 2025 and widely believed to be a rebrand of the Hunters International ransomware cartel, World Leaks has focused on stealing and publishing data rather than encrypting victims’ systems. 16 September 2026

WooCommerce plugin exploited to plant PHP web shells

Attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-27540 (CVSS 9.8) in WooCommerce Wholesale Lead Capture, a premium WordPress plugin with more than 6,000 active installs. The flaw is an arbitrary file upload caused by missing file-type validation in an AJAX action named wwlc_file_upload_handler, affecting all versions up to and including 2.0.3.1. Wordfence says unauthenticated attackers can upload PHP backdoors and achieve remote code execution, and that it has blocked over 100,000 exploit attempts since June 2026 — 99 of them in the past 24 hours.

Chrome 153 / Firefox

Browsers patch 115 flaws between them

Chrome 153 fixes 42 security defects, including three critical and 28 high-severity bugs: CVE-2026-91726, an out-of-bounds read in WebGL, and CVE-2026-91721 and CVE-2026-91749, use-after-free issues in Internals and Workers. It is rolling out as 153.0.8010.47/.48 on Windows and macOS and 153.0.8010.47 on Linux. SecurityWeek notes neither Google nor Mozilla says any of these defects have been exploited in the wild.

Five KEV entries now eleven days overdue

The 2 September KEV additions — SonicWall SMA 1000 (CVE-2026-83548, CVSS 10.0, and CVE-2026-83549, 7.8), Sangoma Switchvox (CVE-2026-9586, 9.3), JFrog Artifactory (CVE-2026-82329, 9.8) and Kestra OSS (CVE-2026-49869, 10.0) — carried a 5 September due date. Horizon3.ai and watchTowr reported actors weaponising the Switchvox and Artifactory bugs to deploy reverse shells and mint admin tokens; Microsoft tied the Kestra flaw to a reverse shell, Docker discovery and a cryptocurrency miner.

Ongoing

AI infrastructure is now a first-class target

Microsoft and Wiz report attackers breaking into LiteLLM gateways via CVE-2026-42271 and CVE-2026-48710 to deliver an XMRig miner, harvesting records from LiteLLM’s PostgreSQL tables (LiteLLM_ProxyModelTable, LiteLLM_VerificationToken) for provider key material and proxy-issued virtual keys, then persisting via ~/.ssh/authorized_keys. Microsoft’s advice: “Defenders should monitor AI workloads according to their control-plane role, not only as isolated applications.”

  • 11 September trio — Cisco FMC (10.0), Citrix NetScaler (9.3), Fortinet (7.3) — due 12 September 2026. (overdue by 4 days)
  • 2 September batch of five — SonicWall SMA 1000 ×2, Sangoma Switchvox, JFrog Artifactory, Kestra OSS — due 5 September 2026. (overdue by 11 days)
Deadlines run under BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk,” issued 10 June 2026, which supersedes and revokes BOD 19-02 and BOD 22-01. It replaces the old uniform three-week window with a 16-tier remediation matrix keyed to public exposure, KEV status and whether exploitation can be automated; the most dangerous tier must be remediated within three days with mandatory forensic triage. Same-week KEV deadlines are therefore normal, not errors. Agencies must be operating to these timelines by 7 December 2026. Countdowns computed from 16 September 2026 against each stated due date. The Citrix NetScaler flaw is held at the vendor’s official 9.3, not the 9.8 figure that circulated in blog coverage.
