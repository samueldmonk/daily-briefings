The Lead

Stocks slip as oil turns higher and the 10-year yield climbs back above 5% (as of ~10:32 AM ET)

Wall Street opened lower Wednesday — the S&P 500 down 0.10%, the Dow off 0.30% and the Nasdaq down 0.15% at the 9:32 AM ET bell, while the small-cap Russell 2000 bucked the trend, up 0.51% — as rising crude prices and climbing Treasury yields renewed inflation and rate-hike worries. By 10:32 AM ET the S&P 500’s slide had deepened to roughly −0.6% as oil reversed higher and the 10-year Treasury yield pushed back above 5%; TheStreet’s live blog attributed the pressure to declines in tech-sector holdings weighing on the index, with 224 S&P holdings still advancing. The session followed Tuesday’s mixed close, in which the Nasdaq Composite notched a fresh record (27,244.28) while the S&P 500 was essentially flat and the Dow slipped.

President Trump said U.S. and Iranian officials held a three-hour, Qatari-mediated meeting at the United Nations on Tuesday that he called “very good” and “very productive,” while Tehran’s foreign ministry confirmed the talks but gave no sign its negotiating position had changed. Trump is set to greet Chinese President Xi Jinping at Joint Base Andrews this week for Xi’s first U.S. state visit in 11 years, with a Thursday dinner bringing together Trump, Xi and tech leaders including Nvidia’s Jensen Huang, OpenAI’s Sam Altman and Google’s Sundar Pichai to discuss trade, AI and rare earths.
