Top Story

The lure is a passkey. The objective is your tenant.

Microsoft’s security team published on 9 September a breakdown of a social-engineering campaign in which attackers call or message an employee’s personal phone, claim to be the organisation’s IT help desk, and press them to immediately update their passkey, MFA or SSO configuration. An SMS to the personal device then lands them on a counterfeit page that mimics the legitimate Microsoft sign-in experience.

Microsoft’s own framing is the part worth reading twice: passkey enrolment is often not the actor’s real objective. The passkey story is simply a plausible reason to walk a victim through an adversary-in-the-middle phishing flow or a device-code authentication flow. Once MFA has been completed, the same session reaches My Apps, My Profile, My Sign-Ins, Microsoft Approval Management, SharePoint Online, OneDrive, Outlook Web and internal business applications — and from there enumerates available applications, users, groups, roles, authentication methods, cloud repositories, emails and attachments.

Microsoft describes the behaviour as “automated collection from compromised cloud identities using proxy-associated infrastructure” and dates the activity to May 2026. The clusters named are ShinyHunters, Helix, Storm-3121 and Storm-3032.

Patch Priority

Do this first — deadline already passed
CVE-2026-20079 — authentication bypass in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center, CVSS 10.0, letting an unauthenticated remote attacker run scripts as root. CISA set a federal patch deadline of 12 September 2026; today is 13 September, so it is one day overdue. Cisco Talos is tracking three distinct threat clusters — tied to both ransomware and state-sponsored activity — exploiting it alongside CVE-2026-20316 (CVSS 5.3). Next after that: CVE-2026-85706, the CVSS 10.0 GitLab path traversal, due tomorrow, 14 September.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NewIdentity

ShinyHunters · Helix · Storm-3121 · Storm-3032

Four clusters Microsoft links to the same playbook: passkey- and SSO-themed social engineering to hijack Microsoft 365 accounts and steal cloud data. The tradecraft is notable for what it does not need — no malware, no exploit, just a phone call to a personal number and a sign-in page that looks right. ShinyHunters is the only name on this list with a long public trail; the three Storm/Helix designations are Microsoft’s own tracking labels.

Breaches & Incidents

NewFraud

Microsoft: AI-assisted executive impersonation and invoice fraud

A second Microsoft write-up, dated 10 September, covers a campaign that pushed more than one million scam emails between 3 and 5 August 2026 through third-party email delivery infrastructure — not compromised Microsoft systems. The actor impersonated the CEOs of the target companies themselves, asking accounts payable to process an ACH payment of nearly $50,000 for a supposed ServiceNow annual subscription, and included a forwarded email thread and a fabricated invoice for texture. Microsoft says generative AI was used to build the templates and tailor drafts to recipients. Targeting skewed to US enterprises in IT services, consumer goods, real estate and discrete manufacturing.

NewExtortion

INC Ransom claims a 5.7 TB theft

The INC Ransom group claimed responsibility for an intrusion this month and alleges it exfiltrated 5.7 terabytes of data. Treat the volume as a claim by the attacker: it is the group’s own figure, and no independent confirmation of the total appeared in anything read this run.

CarriedCVSS 10.0

Three clusters, one Cisco firewall

Cisco disclosed that three distinct threat clusters, linked to ransomware and state-sponsored operations, have been exploiting two recently patched Secure Firewall Management Center flaws — CVE-2026-20079 (10.0, unauthenticated root script execution) and CVE-2026-20316 (5.3). The federal deadline for the first passed yesterday.

CarriedSupply chain

Trezor, by way of Brevo

Trezor confirmed that phishing against its customers reached 347,000 email addresses and that 2,500 users clicked the embedded malicious link. The mail went out through Brevo, Trezor’s marketing platform, under a fabricated hardware-flaw subject line — the product itself was not breached.
