The Lead

Warsh’s Fed hikes a quarter point — its first increase since 2023 — and the dot plot points to more (as of ~2:36 p.m. ET)

At 2:00 p.m. ET the Federal Open Market Committee raised the target range for the federal funds rate by a quarter percentage point to 3.75%–4.00%. Kiplinger’s live blog reported the move “in a unanimous decision”; search-returned coverage described the vote as 12–0. It is the first increase since 2023 and the first policy change of Chair Kevin Warsh’s tenure.

The tape held its gains. CNBC’s post-decision read had the S&P 500 up 0.3%, the Nasdaq Composite up 0.7% and the Dow down 44 points (−0.1%); a later read in the same coverage had the S&P +0.4% and the Nasdaq +0.8%. Both are printed as they were reported — no single post-decision index level was verified this run, so none is published.

The Summary of Economic Projections showed slightly higher GDP and inflation than June. Kiplinger’s 2:24 p.m. entry reads the dot plot as two additional hikes — one more this year and one in 2027 — before the funds rate starts coming down, with a long-run range of 3.0%–4.0% and PCE inflation reaching 2.0% by 2029 (June: 2028). TheStreet’s 2:05 p.m. bulletin instead described one more hike in 2026 “before holding steady in 2027.” The two agree on 2026 and differ on 2027; both are printed and neither is reconciled.

The statement was rewritten around the action. Via Kiplinger’s 2:10 p.m. comparison of the new and July texts: “Inflation remains elevated. Today’s policy action will support a timelier return to the Committee’s 2 percent goal. The Committee will deliver price stability.” The July version had attributed elevated inflation in part to supply shocks including energy.

Movers & Drivers

2:36 p.m. ET · Kiplinger

Warsh explains the hike

In his press conference the chair cited the “resilience of the U.S. economy,” saying the jobless rate remains low and that job openings and hours have been rising. With the labour market at full employment, he said it is time to focus on the price stability half of the mandate: “too many categories” of goods and services show increases that are not consistent with slowing inflation.
