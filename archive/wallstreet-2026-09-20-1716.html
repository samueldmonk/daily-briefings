The lead

No session today — the last print is Friday’s, and it closed the Fed’s hike week almost exactly flat

Markets are closed for the weekend. There is no intraday direction to report, no after-hours block, and nothing on the tape carries a 19 or 20 September stamp. Everything below is Friday 18 September unless it says otherwise.

Friday ended a volatile week mixed and very nearly unchanged: the S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 0.39% to 26,522.55, and the Dow shed 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64 — a session shaped by rising Treasury yields, elevated oil and the Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in three years. It was also a quadruple-witching expiration of roughly $7 trillion notional, described as the second largest on record.

Friday’s wrap credits technology with doing the heavy lifting, supported by Nvidia’s outlook and by regulatory progress on tokenised securities; the only sector-level figure this desk has sourced at all is the PHLX Semiconductor Index, up 2.78% on a memory-chip rally, carried from an earlier edition today and not restated in any source read this run.

A conflict worth naming. A separate account of the same session has the S&P 500 falling 0.17%, the Dow losing 0.40%, the Nasdaq 100 up 0.02% and the Russell 2000 down 0.75% — the same magnitude as the close, with the sign inverted on the S&P. That reading appears in a piece describing the session as it traded rather than where it settled. The closing figures above are corroborated by two independent outlets and are what this page publishes; the alternative is printed here rather than buried.

Coinbase (COIN)

Friday’s biggest single-name move, on the SEC’s five-year conditional “Innovation Exemption” announced 17 September, which allows tokenised US-listed securities to trade via automated market makers. Three readings this run — +11.77%, +12.07% and “jumps 12%” — all cluster at roughly 12%, so the rounded figure is published and the spread is named.
