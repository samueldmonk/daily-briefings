The rally re-accelerates after lunch: the Nasdaq Composite adds more than 400 points — as of ~12:38 PM ET

Into the early afternoon, US stocks are climbing again after results from Nvidia, Salesforce and CrowdStrike lifted the technology trade — and the advance has widened back out since noon. The latest read seen this run has the Nasdaq Composite up 400.29 points, or 1.53% and the Dow up 208.48 points, or 0.39%, with the S&P 500 still holding a 0.4% gain (Yahoo Finance, Aug 27). Both large-cap gauges are above the 12:05 tallies this page carried — the Dow at +147.67 (+0.28%) and the Nasdaq at +279.61 (+1.07%) — so the cooling this page reported at midday has reversed, and the page names that reversal rather than quietly overwriting it. No fresh Russell 2000 read was seen this run, so the small-cap line from 12:05 (+7.64, +0.25%) is not restated as current.

Four index reads have now been taken across this session, and all four reconcile to the same prior close. In order: +169.90 (+0.32%), +217.20 (+0.41%), +147.67 (+0.28%) and now +208.48 (+0.39%) on the Dow, and +300.97 (+1.15%), +327.22 (+1.25%), +279.61 (+1.07%) and now +400.29 (+1.53%) on the Nasdaq Composite. Each pair divides out to a prior close within the rounding band of Wednesday's, which is what makes them a sequence of intraday snapshots rather than competing claims about one moment — so the newest leads and the earlier ones are kept as history, not averaged away. No source stamped its figures with a time, so no level is asserted in this section; levels live in the Weekly Scorecard. Two other items were rejected outright. An aggregator page offered 7,673.04 / 53,195.36 / 26,168.46 as the close of August 27 — impossible before 4 PM, and its Dow figure is irreconcilable with its own stated percentage. And a "midday" snippet reported Nvidia up 39 cents to $163 with a $3.97 trillion market cap and the S&P 500 up 16 points to 6,279. Those are 2025 levels — the same late-August anniversary collision that has caught this page before, because Nvidia reports its second quarter in the last week of August every year. New at 12:38 — the same aggregator came back, and was rejected a second time. It now says US markets "ended the session slightly lower on August 27" — a claim that is both impossible more than three hours before the closing bell and the opposite direction to the close it asserted earlier today. A source that dates a close before the close is not repaired by changing which way it went. Not published.

The 8:30 data is in, and it is good. Initial jobless claims for the week ending August 22 came in at 203,000, down 4,000 from the prior week's upwardly revised 207,000 (first reported at 206,000). The four-week moving average rose 1,250 to 205,500. Trading Economics notes the print extends a run of unusually low claims since a near-60-year low of 189,000 in mid-July. This replaces the "no figure corroborated" line this page carried at 8:40 — and it is not the 232,000 number that circulated earlier, which traced to a 2022 report and was rejected.

The quarter itself, from Nvidia's own filing: revenue of $96.2 billion, up 106% year over year and 18% from the prior quarter; Data Center revenue of $89.0 billion, up 117%; GAAP and non-GAAP gross margins both 75.0%; and diluted EPS of $2.46 GAAP / $2.22 non-GAAP. Management guided third-quarter revenue to $108 billion, plus or minus 2%, with non-GAAP gross margin near 74% — a figure that excludes any Data Center compute revenue from China, where Hopper shipments were under 1% of Data Center revenue in the quarter. CNBC reported CEO Jensen Huang forecasting roughly 70% revenue growth in fiscal 2028, well above estimates.

Okta remains the largest single-stock move, and the breadth has widened beyond the four earnings names. The latest tally seen this run puts Okta up 26.17%, with a separate line in the same coverage describing the shares as having surged 19% — both printed and neither averaged — the first above the "more than 20%" carried at 9:35, the second below it. New at 12:38 — a fresh set of single-stock reads arrived with the fresh index set, and it is materially higher across the board. The Yahoo Finance coverage carrying the +400.29 Nasdaq figure quotes Nvidia up 9.48%, Salesforce up 21.04% and CrowdStrike up 17.93%. Each is the highest read this page has seen for that name today, and each is printed as a read rather than adopted as the number: Salesforce has now been quoted at 14.78% (9:35), 11.2% (11:35 and 12:05) and 21.04% (12:38); CrowdStrike at 8.9% pre-open, 14.34% (9:35), 9% (11:35), 9.4% (12:05) and 17.93% (12:38); Nvidia at 5.87% live just after the open, 6%–7% in pre-market coverage and 9.48% now. The Benzinga 11.2% / 9% pair resurfaced in a separate search this run and is not treated as a new competing read — it is the same coverage this page already carried at 12:05, returning again. None of these reads is averaged, and the spread is wide enough that this page asserts none of them as the move. Names added to the board during the late morning and carried: Zscaler up 8.7%, Palantir up 3.8% at $184.32 and Arm Holdings up 6.2% at $266.50.

That follows a flat-to-lower Wednesday session: the S&P 500 closed at 7,675.70, down 0.02%, with the Dow off 0.08% and the Nasdaq Composite down 0.16%.
