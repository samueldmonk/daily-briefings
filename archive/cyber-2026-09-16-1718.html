Top Story

Cisco ships a CVSS 10.0 ISE authentication bypass — and says it is already being exploited

Cisco published advisory cisco-sa-ISE-ABP-VNSW7Tn5 at 16:00 GMT today for CVE-2026-76460, an authentication bypass in an API of Cisco Identity Services Engine. The base score is 10.0 — CVSS:3.1/AV:N/AC:L/PR:N/UI:N/S:C/C:H/I:H/A:H, the maximum, with a changed scope — and the weakness is CWE-648, incorrect use of a privileged API. In Cisco’s own words the flaw “is due to insufficient authentication control on an API endpoint”, and an unauthenticated remote attacker exploits it by sending a crafted request that bypasses the web-based management interface entirely.

Two lines in the advisory carry the weight. Under Exploitation and Public Announcements: “The Cisco PSIRT is aware of active exploitation of this vulnerability.” Under Source: “This vulnerability was found during the resolution of a Cisco Technical Assistance Center (TAC) support case.” Read together, those say the bug surfaced because a customer was already in trouble — at least one production environment was compromised before anyone knew the flaw existed. It affects Cisco ISE and ISE-PIC regardless of device configuration.

There is no workaround. Cisco offers one mitigation — infrastructure access control lists (iACLs) permitting only required management and control-plane traffic to the appliance — and otherwise directs customers straight to fixed releases: 3.1 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.4 Patch 7 and 3.5 Patch 4. ISE 3.0 has reached End of Software Maintenance and gets no fix; those deployments must migrate.

The hunting guidance is unusually blunt about its own limits. Cisco tells administrators to review access.log on every node of a distributed deployment for suspicious usernames — its worked example greps the API gateway log for dummyuser — and warns that because successful exploitation yields command execution with root privileges, “evidence of exploitation and indicators of compromise may be removed or hidden by the threat actors.” It therefore recommends cross-checking network and firewall logs outside the appliance for unexpected uploads to external addresses, and, where malicious activity is suspected, re-imaging the affected nodes and restoring from configuration backup. That is the operative detail for defenders today: a clean local log is not evidence of a clean box.

CVE-2026-76460 did not arrive alone. Cisco’s advance notification for today lists it among a large scheduled batch — an ISE Hardening Release covering six CVEs at Critical 10.0, a further six-CVE ISE set also at 10.0, three ISE remote-code-execution flaws at 9.9, and command-injection pairs at 9.1 — plus separate Critical sets for Secure Firewall ASA/FTD/FMC and Nexus Dashboard. ISE is network access control: it decides which devices and users are admitted to the enterprise. A pre-authentication root bypass in that box is a bypass of the gate itself.

Patch Priority

Cisco Identity Services Engine — CVE-2026-76460 — CVSS 10.0, exploited now, no workaround

This displaces Monday’s email-gateway entry as the single most urgent item because it is maximum severity, vendor-confirmed as actively exploited, and has no workaround. Unauthenticated remote attackers bypass ISE’s API authentication and reach the device behind the management interface; successful exploitation yields command execution as root. Upgrade to 3.1 P12 / 3.2 P11 / 3.3 P12 / 3.4 P7 / 3.5 P4; ISE 3.0 is end of software maintenance and must be migrated. If patching cannot happen immediately, restrict management and control-plane reachability with iACLs. Then hunt: check access.log on every node for unexpected usernames, and corroborate with off-box network and firewall logs, because root access lets an attacker edit the local evidence. Still due tomorrow under a federal deadline: CVE-2026-76461 in Cisco Secure Email Gateway — CVSS 9.8, exploited as a zero-day, fixed in 15.5.5-014 / 16.0.4-302 / 16.5.0-780, remediation required by 17 September.

Threat Actor Spotlight

GENESIS — extortion without encryption

GENESIS emerged in October 2025 with a coordinated batch of nine simultaneous attack claims posted to its Tor v3 data-leak site. Tracking data puts it at more than 60 claims between October 2025 and late April 2026, with the tempo accelerating through Q1 2026 — on at least two posting dates it claimed more victims than any other tracked actor — and a running total somewhere in the 90-to-115 range as of early September 2026, concentrated in professional services, manufacturing and healthcare in the United States. Claimed breach volumes run from 200 GB to 2 TB per target. What separates it from a conventional ransomware crew is what it does not do: the group operates a data-exfiltration-only model with a structured extortion timeline and a centralised posting cadence, and no encryption capability has been substantiated. Among its listings is Interim HealthCare, posted on 10 August 2026 with a claim of roughly 1 TB of exfiltrated data including medical and clinical records; a second ransomware group has separately claimed the same victim.

Provenance: these are leak-site tracking figures compiled by threat-intelligence trackers, not victim confirmations, and the victim total is published as a range because the sources give one. Claims posted to a leak site are assertions by the actor until a victim confirms them.

Wed 16 Sep · Reuters / HIPAA Journal

Boston Scientific — a cyberattack that moved guidance

Unauthorised activity identified on 25 August caused a network outage that disrupted manufacturing and the processing and shipment of customer orders globally. The company now says it is unlikely to meet the 2026 guidance it issued in July — adjusted earnings of $3.28 to $3.32 per share on reported sales growth of 5.5% to 6.5% — and expects a material impact on both Q3 and the full year. Major distribution centres are shipping again and it expects to recover part of the lost revenue, but the full impact remains uncertain; an update is promised with Q3 results on 28 October. Stifel’s Rick Wise says investors increasingly treat 2026 as a “lost year” for the company. No group has claimed responsibility. This led the previous edition and is carried, not dropped, behind today’s Cisco disclosure.

Wed 16 Sep · WBAY / Identity Theft Resource Center

IDScan.net — FBI confirms it is looking into the ID-image leak

The identity-verification firm posted a security notification saying that around 1 September an unauthorised third party may have accessed or copied customer information, potentially including full names and driver’s licence or other government-issued ID numbers. ITRC president James Lee: “We’ve been told that there’s in excess of 150 million images of driver’s license, of state IDs, of medical dispensary cards.” The number of people affected, and which states are involved, are both still unknown.

Since May · Microsoft Security Blog

Passkey-themed social engineering into Microsoft cloud accounts

Attackers pose as IT helpdesk staff and call or text a user’s personal phone, manufacturing urgency around a passkey, MFA or SSO configuration that must be updated “immediately”, then steer the target to a fraudulent sign-in page or a device-code authorisation flow. Microsoft has seen unusual sign-ins followed by attacker-added authentication methods, high-volume Graph activity, SharePoint and OneDrive downloads and mailbox collection via REST APIs, and attributes the initial access to a range of actors including Storm-3121 and Storm-3032.

The utility disclosed the incident in a Form 8-K after a threat actor posted claims that a dataset containing millions of customer records had been stolen and offered for sale.
