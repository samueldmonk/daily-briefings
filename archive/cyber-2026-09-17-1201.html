Top Story

Cisco ISE: a maximum-severity authentication bypass, exploited in the wild, with a federal deadline on Saturday

CVE-2026-76460 carries a CVSS of 10.0 and affects Cisco Identity Services Engine and the ISE Passive Identity Connector. The mechanism is insufficient authentication control on an API endpoint: a crafted request bypasses the web management interface and yields remote, unauthenticated root. Cisco states the flaw is exploitable regardless of device configuration — there is no hardening posture that takes a system out of scope.

What root on an ISE appliance buys an attacker is the reason this ranks above the day's other maximum-severity bugs: policy modification, credential extraction, deletion of log entries, and lateral movement across every network segment the appliance governs. An identity appliance compromised is not one host lost; it is the control plane for everything that authenticates through it.

Cisco says it is aware of active exploitation. CISA added the CVE to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 16 September, setting a remediation deadline of 19 September 2026 for federal civilian agencies under Binding Operational Directive 26-04 — a three-day window from the add date. Fixed releases: ISE and ISE-PIC 3.1 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.4 Patch 7 and 3.5 Patch 4.

Patch Priority — today, 17 September

The single most urgent item on the calendar is CVE-2026-76461 in the Cisco Secure Email Gateway, a zero-day Cisco has confirmed under active exploitation. CISA added it on 14 September with a federal deadline of 17 September — that is today. If you run an internet-facing Secure Email Gateway and have not patched, the window closes at the end of the day. Immediately behind it: CVE-2026-76460 (Cisco ISE, CVSS 10.0), due 19 September, with the fixed releases listed above.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Ransomware · the denominator

The ecosystem, not a single crew

Black Kite's 2026 report counts 7,551 publicly disclosed ransomware victims between April 2025 and March 2026 — a 24.9% increase over the prior period — drawn from 127 active groups at the close of that window, rising to 146 by June 2026. The growth is in the number of operators as much as in the number of victims.

Ransomware
Espionage → extortion

Whoever is inside vCenter now has company

CISA confirmed on 15 September that ransomware operators have joined an espionage campaign already exploiting CVE-2026-59310, the critical VMware vCenter flaw. Earlier activity focused on establishing persistence; the crews arriving now monetise it. Researchers had confirmed 361 unique victim IP addresses across 47 countries by early September.
