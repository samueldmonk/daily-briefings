Top Story

Title vacated · 14 September

Aspinall gives up the heavyweight title, and the division is down to an interim belt

Tom Aspinall vacated the undisputed UFC heavyweight championship on 14 September 2026. The reason is his eyes: ongoing complications mean he cannot be medically cleared, after multiple surgeries, accidents and infections, and he suffered further damage to his right eye in an accident after he had already agreed a fight date and returned to sparring. He is not retiring, and intends to return once he is cleared.

The root cause traces back to the eye pokes in his no-contest with Ciryl Gane at UFC 321 in October 2025. The absence was approaching the year mark and had produced audible discontent inside the promotion. Gane, who won the interim heavyweight title by KO in round two over Alex Pereira at Freedom 250 on 14 June 2026, now holds the division’s only belt.

It is the second belt to come off the board this month: Valentina Shevchenko vacated the women’s flyweight title, sidelined roughly a year by injury, and Natália Silva vs. Wang Cong will contest the vacant championship at UFC 332 on 3 October.

Around the Sport

  • Michael “Venom” Page released. The promotion declined to renew his contract after his fight at UFC Paris. Jake Paul has since said he is targeting Page as a signing for MVP MMA.
  • Two vacant belts at once. Aspinall’s heavyweight title and Shevchenko’s women’s flyweight title are both open; only the latter has a scheduled fight to fill it (UFC 332, 3 October). Wording matters here — Shevchenko vacated; “stripped” implies a sanction no source alleges.
  • Both UFC 333 title fights are set. Volkanovski vs. Evloev at featherweight over Yan vs. Dvalishvili at bantamweight — a card with the reigning champion defending in both the main and the co-main.
  • Contender Series runs Tuesdays. Week 5 was 8 September; Week 6 is tonight, 15 September.

Rankings & Business

  • Freedom 250 is the ceiling. TKO Group reports the White House card delivered 34 million total global viewers, including 17 million on Paramount+ across the US and Latin America, and an average of 8.2 million across both markets — the biggest exclusive live event in Paramount+ history.
  • The rest of the year scales down from there. The McGregor–Holloway rematch (11 July) averaged 6.5 million viewers across the US and Latin America; UFC 324 drew 4.96 million views in the Paramount+ debut.
  • Platform-level numbers. Since the start of 2026, 20 million subscriber households have watched more than 200 million hours of UFC programming on Paramount+ — which the promotion frames as more than 23 times the average pay-per-view event of the past two years.
  • The deal underneath it. Paramount’s seven-year, $7.7 billion rights agreement with TKO Group made it the exclusive US streaming home of the UFC and effectively ended the sport’s domestic pay-per-view model. UFC 332 on 3 October is the first numbered-event main card on CBS.
  • Gate. Noche UFC set a Desert Diamond Arena record at $4,436,470.
