The Lead

Yields fall, and stocks rally more than 1% across the board (readings as of ~11:46 AM–2:10 PM ET)

The three main indexes are firmly higher on Thursday as Treasury yields pull back and traders reassess the odds of a rate rise at the September meeting. Fed Governor Christopher Waller said he would lean toward holding rates steady unless there is a major inflation surprise — a stance the Motley Fool describes as contrasting with Chair Kevin Warsh’s emphasis on reducing stubborn inflation. The 10-year Treasury yield fell 5 basis points to 4.75%.

Two readings, printed as two moments in the same session and neither adopted as the current print: at 11:46 AM ET the Motley Fool had the Nasdaq Composite +1.35% to 26,572, the Dow +1.20% to 53,699 and the S&P 500 +1.01% to 7,744; by roughly 2:10 PM ET a second reading had the Dow +1.20%, the S&P 500 +1.17% and the Nasdaq +1.62%. The Fool’s own quote panels on the same page showed the S&P at 7,747.68 (+1.06%, +81.08), the Nasdaq Composite at 26,586.15 (+1.40%, +368.32) and the Dow at 53,694.52 (+1.19%, +632.57).

The rally held through an escalation in the Gulf this week: Iran has fired missiles and drones at U.S. facilities in Jordan, Kuwait and Bahrain in retaliation for a renewed wave of American strikes on southern Iran. Jordan’s military said 13 Iranian missiles entered its airspace, 10 of which were intercepted and three of which fell in remote areas, with no casualties reported; Bahrain said its air defences intercepted and destroyed Iranian drones. The UAE condemned the attacks.

Nothing on this page is a live price. The streaming quotes above are the only real-time figures here; every number in the editorial is a reading from a stated moment.
