The Lead

Oil back over $100 keeps the tape red — Dow −321.52 points on a 1:35 p.m. ET quote board, after a 12:07 p.m. low of −413

Brent crude futures passed $100 a barrel for the first time since July on Wednesday, and Wall Street went with it. The latest reading this run comes from the live quote board on a Motley Fool market page fetched at about 1:35 p.m. ET: S&P 500 7,641.46, −32.06 (−0.42%); Nasdaq Composite 26,262.83, −158.58 (−0.60%); Dow Jones Industrial Average 52,464.55, −321.52 (−0.61%). Earlier, the Associated Press had put the Dow down 413 points, or 0.8%, as of 12:07 p.m. Eastern — so the index has clawed back part of the midday hole without turning positive.

Earlier in the session the damage had been widening. TheStreet’s 11:40 a.m. ET update had the S&P 500 off 0.52%, with declines accelerating after about 11 a.m. as Brent cleared $100 — 68.6% of all U.S. issues were falling and 78% of the S&P 500 was in the red. That is a breadth problem, not a mega-cap problem.

The escalation is military. The U.S. struck five Iranian tankers on Tuesday, sinking one, after Iran fired ballistic missiles at a U.S. Navy warship. Tehran said on Wednesday that its forces had struck two American vessels and eight oil tankers in the Gulf in retaliation for the destruction of the five Iranian crude carriers. Iran also retaliated against a U.S.-used base in Jordan — Jordan says it intercepted 18 Iranian missiles with no casualties — and said it had targeted vessels crossing the Strait of Hormuz. The U.K. Maritime Trade Operations centre reported several merchant vessels in the Northern Arabian Gulf and the Gulf of Oman hit by “disabling fire.”

Four timed snapshots, none blended. 9:35 a.m. ET — S&P −0.24%, Dow −0.58%, Nasdaq −0.35%, Russell 2000 −0.52%. 11:40 a.m. — S&P −0.52%, with 68.6% of all U.S. issues and 78% of the S&P 500 declining. 11:43 a.m. — S&P −0.48%, Dow −0.73% at 52,399.06. ~1:35 p.m. — the quote-board figures in the paragraph above. Why the 1:35 figures are published as levels: all three reproduce exactly against Tuesday’s verified closes — 52,786.07 − 321.52 = 52,464.55; 26,421.41 − 158.58 = 26,262.83; 7,673.52 − 32.06 = 7,641.46 — and each point change divides back to the stated percent. One earlier figure is refused: the 11:43 a.m. article gave the Nasdaq Composite a level identical to the S&P’s, plainly a copy error, so only its percentage is carried.

On the Radar

  • Treasury triples its buyback — announced at ~11 a.m. ET today. The Treasury Department said it will buy back up to $6 billion of already-issued longer-term government debt, specifically 10- and 20-year notes — three times the normal operation size, and above the “at least $4 billion” Secretary Scott Bessent flagged on 19 August. Officially it is about liquidity; it is also widely read as an attempt to put a lid on Treasury yields. Bessent’s line to currency traders on Tuesday: “I am the house now.”
  • Chewy (CHWY) and American Eagle Outfitters (AEO) report today — two consumer-spending reads on an otherwise thin earnings day.
  • A new trade front with Canada. The White House said it will ban imports of Canadian motorbikes and a slew of other products starting later this month as relations with Ottawa fray.
  • August Producer Price Index — Thursday 10 September, 8:30 a.m. ET (Bureau of Labor Statistics). PPI for final demand was unchanged in July. A CPI release is also flagged for Thursday; the two dates could not be separated cleanly this run, so only the BLS-stated PPI date is asserted.
  • Gasoline — carried at headline depth only. A same-day headline seen this run states that gasoline prices have hit new records and are still rising. The article was not read in full, so nothing beyond that sentence is asserted — no price, no state, no year-on-year figure. It is noted because pump prices are the channel through which $100 Brent reaches households, which is why an oil supply shock is trading as an inflation story rather than an energy-sector story.
  • Oil is the macro variable. Higher crude is feeding straight into the inflation debate and into a Fed whose last vote had three members pushing for a hike — which is why a supply story is moving the whole tape rather than just energy. Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn: upcoming inflation data will be “particularly important” in determining whether $100 oil is feeding into the Fed outlook.
