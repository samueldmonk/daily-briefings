Top story

ShinyHunters defaced Clop’s leak site — and is now threatening to extort the extortionists

On Friday 19 September, ShinyHunters compromised and defaced the Clop ransomware gang’s dark-web data-leak site. The first sign was a single text file dropped onto the site reading “THIS SITE HAS BEEN PWN3D BY SHINYHUNTERES #Skids10p - Maybe don’t try to threaten us next time”, alongside a link back to ShinyHunters’ own leak site. Hours later the whole site was gone, replaced with ASCII art of Umbreon — the Pokémon ShinyHunters uses as its logo — above the line “rooting your systems since ’19 ;)”.

ShinyHunters says it entered through an unauthenticated file-upload flaw in Grav CMS and took the leak-site source code and Grav plugins, system logs from /var/log, and the private cryptographic keys for Clop’s Tor onion service. It set a 72-hour deadline and said it intends to extort Clop. Reporting gives the motive as a dispute over Clop’s 2025 Oracle E-Business Suite campaign, in which a Clop representative allegedly threatened violence and doxxing.

What is actually established: BleepingComputer confirmed the defacement. The entry vector, the claim of full server access, and the theft of source code, logs and Tor private keys all come from ShinyHunters and are not independently verified — one write-up is headlined “Tor Key Theft Unproven”. This page treats the defacement as fact and the rest as a claim.

Patch priority

Patch first · deadline expired

CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco Identity Services Engine and ISE-PIC, CVSS 10.0. Insufficient authentication control on an API endpoint lets an unauthenticated remote attacker send a crafted request and execute commands as root, with the ability to wipe logs behind them. Cisco has confirmed active exploitation; the flaw surfaced while support engineers were working a customer TAC case, meaning at least one enterprise was already breached before the bug was known.

CISA added it to the KEV catalog on 16 September with federal remediation due 19 September 2026 — now past due. Fixed releases: ISE 3.1 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.4 Patch 7, 3.5 Patch 4. There is no workaround; upgrading is the only remediation.

Second in line: CVE-2026-76461 (Cisco Secure Email Gateway, CVSS 9.8), due 17 September and further past due — it is triggered simply by sending a crafted email through the gateway.

Threat actor spotlight

UAT-11988 and two more clusters inside Cisco firewall managers

Cisco Talos has confirmed three separate threat clusters exploiting two Secure Firewall Management Center flaws: CVE-2026-20079, an authentication bypass scored CVSS 10.0 giving unauthenticated remote root, and CVE-2026-20316, static credentials — for which no CVSS has been published, and none is asserted here.

One cluster, tracked as UAT-11988, enumerated the domain, stole credentials, deployed antivirus killers and dropped Qilin ransomware. Other observed activity overlaps with Sandworm and Cyclops Blink. Exploitation began in August 2026. The management plane is the target in every one of this week’s lead stories — ISE, FMC and the email gateway are all appliances that authenticate or filter for everything behind them.

Breaches & incidents

Ransomware

US water utilities under an OT-focused ransomware campaign

A ransomware group is running a campaign against water utilities across the United States, leveraging unpatched vulnerabilities in their operational-technology environments; the same report warns of multiple critical flaws in widely used SCADA systems that could let attackers manipulate control processes. Single-sourced — one OT security roundup dated 20 September.

Data theft

Florida DMV data listed by ShinyHunters

A breach of the State of Florida DMV was disclosed by ShinyHunters on 16 September, comprising 52 GB of compressed data. Single-sourced — one breach tracker; no scope, record count or state confirmation was found this run.

Exposure

Gyazo / Helpfeel — 23.6 million user records

Disclosed 18 September; entry was through a flaw in the image upload server. Carried from this desk’s 18–20 September ledger and not re-verified this run.

Patch Tuesday

Patch Tuesday landed at record size, with two flaws already exploited

Microsoft’s September release is the largest Patch Tuesday on record, more than double August. Two vulnerabilities were already being exploited: CVE-2026-81963 (Windows Update Stack) and CVE-2026-85880 (ALPC). Twenty-two Critical Office patches are exploitable via the Preview or Reading Pane — no click, no macro prompt — and at least 17 CVEs are unauthenticated network-reachable RCEs across DNS, DHCP, MSMQ, NFS and SSTP VPN, plus critical RCEs in Netlogon and Kerberos.

CISA KEV & federal deadlines

  • CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco ISE / ISE-PIC auth bypass, CVSS 10.0. Added 16 September, federal remediation due 19 September 2026.
  • CVE-2026-76461 — Cisco Secure Email Gateway SQL injection, CVSS 9.8. Added 14 September, due 17 September.
  • CVE-2025-39682, CVE-2026-53266 and CVE-2025-39964 — three Linux kernel flaws added 18 September under BOD 26-04, due 21 September. All three are additionally flagged as requiring forensic triage: agencies must investigate exposed assets for evidence of compromise, not simply install the patch.
  • CVE-2026-87886 (Acronis Backup) was added on 16 September in the same batch as the Cisco ISE flaw. No separate due date for it appeared in any source read this run, so none is published here.
  • Note on window length. Every one of these is a three-day window, not the three-week schedule this desk used to treat as standard. The 18 September Linux additions are issued under BOD 26-04, which directs accelerated remediation based on operational risk. Short deadlines are now normal and are published as sourced. CISA’s own alert pages were not fetched directly this run; these dates come from vendor advisories and from reporting on the additions.

Also on the wire

  • The exploited-this-week tracker contradicts itself three ways. The same Senserva page read this run is titled “15 New Exploited CVEs” for the week, says in its body that CISA confirmed 9 newly exploited vulnerabilities in the last seven days, and reports in its own counter that 7 were added in the last seven days. Three figures, one page, one run. None is adopted here — the direction is what the page supports, not the number. The stable figures on it are 1,315 exploited non-Microsoft CVEs across 282 vendors.
  • The Patch Tuesday count is disputed across six outlets. September’s release has been reported as 963 CVEs requiring customer action, 964, 972, 973, 974 and 1,169 depending on the source and on what each counts; Critical totals split between 106 and 113. No single figure is published here. What every account agrees on is that it is the largest Patch Tuesday on record and that exactly two flaws were already under exploitation.
  • CISA marked the Linux trio for forensic triage, not just patching — an escalation from the ordinary KEV instruction, and a signal that the agency expects compromised assets to already exist inside federal networks.
  • One nursery-sector attack was excluded again this run. It surfaces repeatedly in 2026-dated search results but the incident itself dates to September 2025, so it is not a current item and is not published here. It has been caught and dropped in every edition since it first appeared.
