The Lead

A hawkish hold: the Fed stands pat with three dissents for a hike — and the Dow’s 1,153-point dive is its worst day since April 2025

The close. The Federal Reserve held its benchmark rate at 3.50–3.75% Wednesday for a fifth consecutive meeting — but in a split decision, three officials dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike: Minneapolis’ Neel Kashkari, Dallas’ Lorie Logan and Cleveland’s Beth Hammack (CNBC/Bloomberg/CNN/US News). Chair Kevin Warsh shrugged off the division at his press conference — “I asked for a good family fight, and I got one” (Yahoo Finance/CNN) — but markets read the cliffhanger as a central bank at odds with itself over inflation, and the reaction was brutal: the Dow closed down 1,153.18 points (−2.19%) at 51,594.14, its worst decline since April 2025; the S&P 500 slid 1.52% to 7,316.15; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 1.74% to 24,442.94, leaving it more than 10% below its record (CNBC). The Nasdaq 100 finished in correction territory — down 1.8% on the day and more than 11% from its June peak, with a rout in chipmakers still pummeling the broader gauge (Bloomberg). CNBC’s wrap pins the selloff mostly on the bond market signaling the Fed “could be falling behind on the inflation fight”: the 10-year Treasury yield rose about 9 basis points to roughly 4.70% by the close (Motley Fool; CNBC had it above 4.67% just after the decision) — and the 30-year yield jumped 10.5 basis points to 5.201%, touching 5.244%, its highest level since July 2007 (CNBC). Yahoo Finance’s headline called it a “hawkish hold.” Sector-wise, industrials fell 3.42% and technology dropped 2.36%, with energy and consumer defensives among the few gainers (Motley Fool). The drop more than unwinds Tuesday’s split close: Dow +537.45 (+1.03%) to 52,747.53, S&P 500 +0.22% to 7,429.22, Nasdaq −0.22% to 24,876.91 (Yahoo Finance closing board/CNBC).

The war premium held through the close. Iran’s Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps launched what U.S. Central Command called an “attempted surprise attack” — ballistic missiles fired at U.S. forces, all intercepted, the first exchange since Friday’s pause in hostilities (CNN/Yahoo Finance) — and TheStreet’s Wednesday blog adds that Jordan’s military intercepted a fresh barrage of Iranian missiles Wednesday. President Trump told Fox News the U.S. “will hit Iran hard,” saying Iran “is going to get a beating,” and joint U.S.–Saudi strikes hit Iranian-backed forces in Iraq — killing at least 20, per CBS News reporting cited by TheStreet — after more than 30 drone attacks in three days by Iran-aligned groups (CNBC). Crude surged and stayed up: WTI settled up 6.6% at $84.46 (CNBC), and TradingEconomics’ evening reads ran hotter still — WTI at $84.62 (+6.7%) with a latest flash of “around $85” (+7%), and Brent at $88.01 (+4.66%) with its latest flash above $90, snapping a three-day losing streak, after morning prints of $88.49–$89.94 Brent and $83.18–$84.69 WTI across TheStreet, 24/7 Wall St and CNBC. TradingEconomics adds two fresh wrinkles: Iran rejected Oman’s proposal for shared 50-50 control of the Strait of Hormuz, and Yemen’s Houthis are reportedly weighing transit fees on Red Sea shipping. API data showed U.S. crude inventories down 3.3 million barrels last week. Gold firmed as the day went on — +0.27% to $4,048.99 at the U.S. close (Motley Fool), with TradingEconomics’ evening board at $4,066 (+0.94%), “extending a rebound from a 9-month low.”

After the bell, the AI trade got its verdict — split, and Meta’s side got worse as the evening wore on. Microsoft rose about 2% in extended trading after fiscal Q4 EPS of $4.74 (vs $4.24 expected) on revenue of $90.01 billion (vs $87.62 billion), with Azure growth of 43% at constant currency beating the ~40% estimate and Azure’s fiscal-2026 revenue topping $100 billion for the first time (CNBC/Investing.com). Meta went the other way — and kept going: Benzinga clocked the stock down 5.06% at $556 shortly after the print and its quote board read −5.2% at our ~6:15 PM check, while CNBC’s evening headline had Meta “plunging almost 10%” on disappointing guidance and “dwindling free cash flow.” The full print (Benzinga, fetched this run): EPS of $6.18 missed the ~$7.13 consensus by roughly a dollar on a $2.4 billion legal charge; revenue grew 28% to $60.80 billion (beating $59.50B); expenses jumped 55% to $42 billion and net income fell 14% to $15.8 billion; free cash flow shrank to $784 million against $31.86 billion in operating cash flow; Q3 revenue guidance of $61–64 billion carries a light low end vs the $62.68B–$63.15B consensus range; and Meta raised the low end of its 2026 capex guidance to $130–145 billion (from $125–145B) while lifting full-year expense guidance to $165–169 billion. The rest of the late tape leaned green — Starbucks jumped 11% on a top-and-bottom beat with global same-store sales up 7.9%, Fortinet surged 10% on a beat-and-raise, Chipotle rose 7% and Lam Research climbed 7% on a beat-and-raise of its own, while Qualcomm fell 4% on light Q4 guidance (Investing.com; see After-Hours Movers below).

Under the hood of the regular session, earnings and downgrades still mattered. Garmin was the day’s standout gainer — up roughly 16% in board readings through the session — after a Q2 beat on $2.02 billion in revenue, a fitness business up 25% and a raised full-year EPS forecast of $10.00 (Benzinga; TheStreet’s blog had it “soaring nearly 16%” early). In the Dow’s wreckage, Caterpillar tumbled 6.91% after an analyst downgrade and Deere dropped 4.52% on falling agricultural commodity prices and cautious guidance, while Exxon Mobil and Chevron rose with crude (Motley Fool). Humana fell 7.2% in the afternoon session after a solid Q2 beat was overshadowed by the insurer holding its full-year adjusted profit forecast unchanged for a second straight quarter (StockStory/Reuters via Investing.com). Ford faded with the tape but still closed up 2.14% at $15.28 on its beat-and-raise, well off its ~7.6% morning gain (StockStory/Motley Fool). And a caution flag flew over the whole tape: Berkshire Hathaway’s cash pile hit a record $397 billion, fueling speculation the firm is readying for a downturn (Motley Fool). Thursday brings the week’s last megacap test: Apple (consensus EPS $1.89), Amazon (consensus EPS $1.82) and Reddit (consensus EPS $0.97) all report after the close (TipRanks/Finance Calendar/indmoney).

The dissent

The hold came with a fight: Minneapolis Fed president Neel Kashkari, Dallas’ Lorie Logan and Cleveland’s Beth Hammack all dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike (CNBC/Bloomberg/US News/CNN). Warsh — who has argued the Fed should signal less and emphasize conditions instead — told reporters: “I asked for a good family fight, and I got one” (Yahoo Finance/CNN). The bond market’s answer: the 10-year rose to ~4.70% and the 30-year hit 5.244% intraday, its highest since July 2007 — which CNBC’s wrap reads as a signal the Fed “could be falling behind on the inflation fight.”

Caterpillar & Deere CAT −6.91% New

Two industrial bellwethers helped drag the Dow to its worst day since April 2025: Caterpillar tumbled 6.91% after an analyst downgrade — TradingEconomics’ board read −6.99% at $782.05 — and Deere dropped 4.52% amid falling agricultural commodity prices and cautious guidance (Motley Fool). The sector damage was broad: industrials fell 3.42% as a group, the day’s worst sector, per the Fool’s wrap.

Berkshire’s $397B war chest New

News that Berkshire Hathaway’s cash position reached a record $397 billion offered the market another reason for caution — the eye-watering pile has fueled speculation that the firm Warren Buffett transformed may be readying for a downturn (Motley Fool).

CBIZ (CBZ) +~17%

Grant Thornton Advisors will acquire CBIZ in a definitive all-cash deal: $55.00 per share, a $5 billion enterprise value and a ~54% premium to the 30-day volume-weighted average price, with the close expected in Q4 2026 and CBIZ shares to be delisted from the NYSE (Grant Thornton press release/Accounting Today/Investing.com). A live-blog reading had the stock up nearly 16.6% at $54.45, pinned just under the offer.

Vertiv (VRT) −~11% premarket

The data-center power-and-cooling maker fell 10.9% in premarket trade to $240.10 after second-quarter revenue of $3.274 billion came in about 3.1% below expectations (live-blog movers reading this run) — another data point for the AI-infrastructure nerves that have dogged the tape since Alphabet’s capex guidance.

SoFi (SOFI) −9.4%

Visa (V) −1.6% after hours

Beat and trimmed: fiscal Q3 EPS $3.32 vs $3.23 expected, revenue +14% to $11.63B vs $11.39B, processed transactions +10% to 71.7B and quarterly payments volume above $4 trillion for the first time — but shares slipped ~1.6% in late trading as Visa moderated full-year growth guidance and disclosed ~2,600 job cuts (~7% of staff) with $563M in related costs. CEO: consumer and business spending “remains resilient” (Seeking Alpha/Benzinga/Yahoo Finance).

SK Hynix / chips Kospi ~−6%

Stateside, the iShares Semiconductor ETF entered the day off four straight sessions with chip stocks down nearly 7% for the week, and SK Hynix’s U.S.-listed shares slipped again (24/7 Wall St).
