Top Story

Attackers target critical Atlassian Data Center flaw within hours of proof-of-concept publication

Threat actors have started targeting CVE-2026-21589, a critical vulnerability in Atlassian’s self-hosted Data Center products, SecurityWeek reported Thursday. Atlassian disclosed the bug on Oct. 5 with a CVSS score of 9.3; it affects Bitbucket, Confluence, Jira Software, Jira Service Management, Bamboo, Crowd, Crucible and Fisheye, and patches are out for all affected versions. The flaw lets remote, unauthenticated attackers access specific files in the web application’s root directory — “Exploitation requires prior knowledge of the target file’s exact name and path,” Atlassian notes — and cannot be used to list directory contents (SecurityWeek, Oct 8).

Why it matters. watchTowr, which published its analysis Oct. 6, traced the issue to a library the products share and found the bigger risk when Jira is integrated with Crowd: an attacker can read a configuration file storing Crowd application credentials in plaintext. watchTowr used those credentials to create a user and add it to the Jira administrators group, calling direct Crowd access with leaked credentials “basically game over.” Previdian’s honeypots began recording exploitation attempts on Oct. 6, hours after watchTowr’s write-up, and had logged 190 attempts from 32 IP addresses in 10 countries as of Oct. 8. CISA has not yet added the CVE to KEV. Update to the fixed versions; if that is not immediately possible, cut instances off from the internet or apply Atlassian’s firewall and rewrite rules (SecurityWeek).

FortiMail zero-day: the federal deadline has passed — and no fixed build was listed as of watchTowr’s last update

Where it stands. CISA added CVE-2026-104286 to KEV on October 1 with a remediation due date of 2026-10-04 — now passed — and flagged it as requiring forensic triage under BOD 26-04. watchTowr’s FAQ (last updated Oct. 1) lists the fixed version for every affected branch as “Not yet available” and the vendor patch status as “mitigation only”; it advises preserving logs and forensic images, checking appliances against Fortinet’s indicators of compromise, then disabling Identity-Based Encryption or cutting internet access to the management interface. No attribution has been made public (watchTowr). Check Fortinet advisory FG-IR-26-175 for builds before assuming a fix exists.

Fortinet is warning that attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-104286 in FortiMail, its email security gateway. The flaw — a path traversal combined with improper neutralization of NULL bytes — “may allow an unauthenticated attacker to write arbitrary files on the underlying system via crafted HTTP or HTTPS requests,” Fortinet said in an advisory published October 1. It carries a CVSSv3 score of 9.8 and was found internally by Gwendal Guégniaud of Fortinet’s Product Security team (Help Net Security). BleepingComputer says it is being exploited to execute unauthorized code or commands on vulnerable devices (BleepingComputer).

Affected: FortiMail 8.0.0–8.0.1, 7.6.0–7.6.6, 7.4.0–7.4.8 and 7.2.0–7.2.9. Fixes will ship in 8.0.2, 7.6.7 and 7.4.9, which have not been released; 7.2 users are told to move to 7.4 or above. Until then, disable IBE (identity-based encryption) with config system encryption ibe / set status disable / end, or block internet access to the management interface. Fortinet has published files, IP addresses and log entries to check for compromise, but has not said when or where attacks were seen or who is behind them. CISA added the flaw to KEV the same day with an October 4, 2026 federal deadline (Help Net Security; CISA alert).

Patch Priority — overdue: Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway CVE-2026-88779 (SAML SP/IdP memory overflow → DoS; exploited, with attackers crashing unmitigated appliances) — added to KEV Oct 4, federal deadline Wednesday, October 7, 2026 (2 days overdue). Upgrade to 14.1-73.41 / 13.1-64.28; the September 88771 patch alone does not cover it, and CISA’s action includes a compromise check. Patch now, no federal deadline yet: Atlassian Data Center CVE-2026-21589 (CVSS 9.3, unauthenticated file read; exploitation attempts since Oct. 6; not in KEV) — update, or take instances off the internet / apply Atlassian’s firewall and rewrite rules (SecurityWeek). Also overdue: Zammad CVE-2026-102489 / CVE-2026-102490 (exploited RCE-to-root chain) — due Oct 5 (4 days overdue), upgrade to 7.2.0; Fortinet FortiMail CVE-2026-104286 (CVSS 9.8, exploited) — due Oct 4 (5 days overdue), with no fixed build listed in watchTowr’s FAQ (last updated Oct 1) — preserve forensics, check Fortinet’s IOCs, and disable IBE or block internet access to the management interface; Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504 (Oct 3, 6 days overdue), Apple CVE-2026-86950 (Oct 2, 7 days overdue) and Citrix NetScaler CVE-2026-88771/-88772 (Sept 30, 9 days overdue — CISA advises assuming compromise and preserving forensics before patching).

Threat Actor Spotlight

Nation-state

Hafnium / Silk Typhoon: U.S. offers $10 million for alleged operator Zhang Yu

The State Department’s Rewards for Justice program announced Wednesday a reward of up to $10 million for information on Zhang Yu, a Chinese national accused of taking part in the Hafnium campaign against Microsoft Exchange servers. RFJ says Zhang is a director at Shanghai Firetech Information Science and Technology Company and allegedly worked on behalf of the Shanghai State Security Bureau, part of China’s Ministry of State Security. Critical patch New

Citrix urges immediate patching of new critical NetScaler flaw CVE-2026-107406

Citrix on Thursday warned of a critical-severity memory overflow in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway that could lead to remote code execution or denial of service on appliances configured as a SAML SP or SAML IdP under specific conditions; Secure Private Access hybrid deployments that use NetScaler must be updated too. “As of the publication of the bulletin, Citrix is not aware of any unmitigated exploits of this vulnerability,” the company said. Fixed builds are listed in Citrix bulletin CTX697191. It follows exploited NetScaler flaws CVE-2026-88779, -88771 and -88772 in recent weeks (SecurityWeek, Oct 9).

Government breach

Denmark: CPR register records on about 8.8 million people accessed without authorization

Denmark has confirmed that unauthorized parties accessed personal records — names, addresses and CPR numbers — belonging to about 8.8 million people in its Central Population Register, according to an official statement published October 5. The figure exceeds Denmark’s population because the register, which holds roughly 11 million people, also covers people who have moved abroad or died (Cyber Security News; Ministry statement (Danish)). The attackers misused a private Danish company’s lawful access to search the CPR system during September; the ministry has not named the company, said how that access was obtained, linked the incident to a known group or identified a software flaw. The CPR administration noticed unusual system behavior on the evening of Friday, Oct. 2, blocked the company’s access, reported the breach to the Data Protection Authority, and police are investigating. Names and addresses of people registered with name and address protection were not included. Minister Christina Egelund called the incident deeply serious and requested a thorough security review of CPR; authorities warn residents not to share passwords or confidential information with unexpected callers or emailers, even ones who already know their CPR details (Cyber Security News).

Active campaign

FBI and Secret Service: FortiBleed still hitting Fortinet firewalls, locking some admins out

The FBI and U.S. Secret Service warned Tuesday that the FortiBleed credential-harvesting campaign remains active against internet-facing Fortinet FortiGate firewalls and SSL VPN gateways. Per the agencies, it “exploits reused or leaked credentials and legacy SHA-256 password storage,” and attackers are still scanning exposed devices with previously stolen credentials; the Russian-speaking operation is estimated to have netted more than 86,644 working device credentials across 194 countries as of June 19, 2026. Intruders create new admin accounts for persistence and in some cases delete existing ones, so victims may be locked out; operator overlaps tie it to INC and Lynx ransomware. Affected organizations are told to isolate devices, collect logs and report to the FBI and USSS (The Hacker News, Oct 7).

DNS / PKI

Hijacked .gh, .sl and .as registries used to obtain HTTPS certificates for Google and others

Attackers compromised the country-code registries of Ghana, Sierra Leone and American Samoa, changed authoritative DNS records and used that control to pass certificate authorities’ domain-control checks, obtaining unauthorized HTTPS certificates for Google and other organizations. In an Oct. 6 disclosure summarized by Cyber Security News, Google said its own systems were not breached and the issuing CAs did nothing wrong; Chrome blocked the identified certificates via CRLSets and Google worked with CAs to revoke them. Google has not named the attackers, said how they got in, or published a full list of affected domains, and warned its review may not have found every one. Defender takeaway: watch Certificate Transparency logs across your whole domain portfolio and set restrictive CAA records (Cyber Security News, Oct 7).

Ransomware stats

Q3 2026 was the worst ransomware quarter on record: 2,627 attacks

A Comparitech study published Oct. 6 counted 2,627 ransomware attacks in Q3 2026 — nearly 29 a day, the highest quarterly figure to date — up 29% from Q2’s 2,030 and 61% from Q3 2025. Of 247 confirmed attacks, 138 hit businesses, 53 government entities, 36 healthcare companies and 20 educational institutions. The median ransom demand was $150,000 (average $602,400), and the most prolific gangs were Qilin and The Gentlemen — the same group behind the Azazel affiliate above. Comparitech’s Rebecca Moody flagged rising triple extortion, citing The Gentlemen adding clients of South African tech firm MIP Holdings to its leak site after MIP paid to have stolen data deleted (The IT Nerd, citing Comparitech, Oct 6).

IoT malware

ClingSTUN Linux backdoor turns IoT devices into proxy nodes

FortiGuard Labs says attackers exploited about two dozen known vulnerabilities in internet-facing devices — from vendors including Avtech, D-Link, Ivanti, Realtek, Tenda and TP-Link — to install ClingSTUN, a backdoor that survives reboots via startup-script entries, runs operator commands, scans for new victims using seven hardcoded exploits, and uses legitimate public STUN servers to traverse NAT. FortiGuard did not name the operators, count infections or identify victims. Advice: inventory internet-facing equipment, apply firmware updates, and isolate or replace end-of-support devices (Hackread, Oct 5; FortiGuard).

Active exploitation

NetScaler CVE-2026-88779: attackers crashing appliances, honeypots hit

Citrix says it has “observed targeted attacks on unmitigated NetScaler deployments which can lead to Denial of Service,” and that repeated triggering can leave the service unavailable; it has not identified an impact on customer-data integrity. Reddit admins report crashes and reboots after patching the earlier eight-flaw batch, Kevin Beaumont reported attacks on his honeypots from multiple IPs and found a downloaded malware binary on one, and attackers appear to be trying to fetch and run a script to install webshells — though it is still unclear whether the bug can yield RCE. Citrix has pushed Global Deny List signatures as a stopgap; CISA’s deadline is Oct. 7 and includes a compromise check (Help Net Security, Oct 5). watchTowr told SecurityWeek it reproduced the bug and determined it is a DoS flaw that can only crash systems, and suspects it was used to deliberately crash machines to speed exploitation of CVE-2026-88771 (SecurityWeek, updated Oct 5).

Critical flaw

GitLab patches CVSS 9.9 AI Gateway flaw on self-hosted gateways

Threat report

Microsoft Digital Defense Report 2026: attackers hold the AI edge for now

Microsoft’s 2026 Digital Defense Report, released Oct. 1 and covering July 2025–June 2026, says the median window from a vulnerability being found in the wild to active weaponization has fallen well below 24 hours, while enterprises routinely need 30 to 60 days to remediate critical external flaws. Phishing accounted for 23% of the intrusions its incident responders investigated (up from 7%), exploits of public-facing apps 24% (from 15%); nearly 40,000 CVEs were published in the first half of 2026, putting the year on track for a projected record of 72,000. It cites JADEPUFFER — documented by Sysdig in July 2026 as the first confirmed fully automated ransomware extortion attack — and says it has since seen similar AI-orchestrated intrusions at low volume. Top recommendation: treat phishing-resistant MFA and passkeys as a baseline (Tech Times, Oct 2).

KEV add

CISA adds exploited Citrix NetScaler SAML flaw to KEV, due Oct. 7

CISA added CVE-2026-88779 to the KEV catalog on Oct. 4, 2026, with a due date of Oct. 7, 2026. The bug is a memory-buffer overflow (CWE-119) in customer-managed NetScaler ADC and Gateway appliances configured as a SAML SP or SAML IdP that can knock the appliance offline; it is denial of service, not code execution. Citrix’s bulletin CTX697174 rates it CVSS v4.0 8.7 High and lists fixes in 14.1-73.41 and 13.1-64.28 (FIPS/NDcPP builds also listed). It is separate from September’s CVE-2026-88771 RCE cluster, so boxes patched for that wave still need these builds (HOL, citing CISA KEV & Citrix CTX697174).

KEV add

CISA adds exploited Zammad chain to KEV with a three-day deadline

CISA’s Oct. 2 alert added CVE-2026-102489 (session fixation) and CVE-2026-102490 (improper privilege management) in the open-source Zammad help desk, based on evidence of active exploitation; the catalog lists a due date of Oct. 5, 2026. Chained, they give a remote attacker code execution as the zammad user and then root. The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD) found them after its own network was breached on Sept. 21 and reported them to Zammad on Sept. 24; DIVD rates CVE-2026-102489 8.7 alone and 9.4 chained, and lists it affecting Zammad 6.3.0–6.5.4. Zammad disputes parts of DIVD’s version ranges but recommends updating to 7.2.0 — immediately on 6.5 or older, which no longer gets security fixes; NCSC-NL advises preserving logs before patching (WindowsForum, Oct 2; HOL, Oct 2).

Ransomware

RansomHouse leak exposes McMinnville, Oregon records

The city says an intruder accessed and copied data between June 1 and July 18, 2026; RansomHouse listed it Aug 6, and the city’s Sept 29 notice cites names, driver’s license numbers and/or SSNs. The number affected has not been disclosed (Tech Insider, citing KOIN and KPTV).
