The Lead

Closing bell: yields hit a 2007 high and stocks retreat on rate-hike fears (official close, 4:00 PM ET)

Wall Street finished lower Wednesday as Treasury yields marched higher on worries that more Federal Reserve rate hikes are coming. The S&P 500 fell 58.61 points, or 0.75%, to 7,706.03; the Nasdaq Composite lost 308.24 points, or 1.13%, to 26,936.04, breaking a four-day win streak after back-to-back record highs; and the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 352.10 points, or 0.68%, to 51,511.59. Small caps fared worst: the Russell 2000 slid 1.77%, and TheStreet counted more than 72% of U.S. issues lower on the day.

The benchmark 10-year yield climbed to 5.135%, its highest since July 2007, and the 2-year reached 4.947%, its highest since May 2024 (CNBC); the 5-year topped 5% for the first time since 2007 (TheStreet). The catalysts were S&P Global’s flash September PMI, which showed business activity expanding more than economists expected, higher oil, and Fed Governor Michael Barr, who said in Chicago that “further policy adjustments are likely to be needed” to bring inflation down (Yahoo Finance). Fed funds futures now imply a greater-than-68% chance of another hike in October (CNBC). Energy was the only S&P 500 sector advancing as of mid-afternoon, per TheStreet.

Earlier: at the 9:32 AM ET open the S&P 500 was down 0.10%, the Dow 0.30% and the Nasdaq 0.15% (TheStreet). Sources: Yahoo Finance (close) · CNBC (evening) · CNBC live blog (Sept 23) · TheStreet
