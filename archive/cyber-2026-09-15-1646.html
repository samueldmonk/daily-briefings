Top Story

Patch management · Microsoft

Microsoft has shipped emergency out-of-band updates because its own September patches broke Remote Desktop on Windows Server

Session hosts running Windows Server 2019, 2022 and 2025 began freezing hours after boot once the September 2026 Patch Tuesday cumulative updates went on, leaving RDP connections stuck at “Connecting…” and preventing logged-in users from disconnecting or logging off cleanly. Affected environments can look normal at first and then start failing connections after several minutes; servers may sit indefinitely at the “Please wait for the Remote Desktop Configuration” screen.

Three cumulative updates are named: KB5122876 (Server 2019), KB5122882 (Server 2022) and KB5122871 (Server 2025). Microsoft acknowledged the problem, issued temporary Group Policy workarounds, and then published emergency out-of-band updates on 14 September.

The reason this leads rather than sits in a footnote is the bind it puts defenders in, stated plainly by the reporting: rolling back the update restores stability but strips away the critical security fixes shipped in the same package — a package that, per the Zero Day Initiative and vendor trackers, closed two already-exploited Windows zero-days and a CVSS 9.8 Remote Desktop Services RCE. Several of the KEV entries below are edge and remote-access devices, so “defer the September updates” is not a neutral choice this month.

The scale of the release is itself disputed and both readings are printed rather than averaged: Senserva puts Microsoft’s official September count at 1,169 CVEs across 60 KBs with 118 rated Critical, while Tenable — the only tracker this site has retrieved in full — counted 964 (104 critical / 860 important). Other trackers have said 973 and 974+.

Patch Priority — do this firstCVE-2026-76461 — Cisco Secure Email Gateway (AsyncOS). CVSS 9.8. Federal deadline 17 September — 2 days left. A SQL-injection flaw in the email parsing of Cisco AsyncOS Software, exploited in the wild as a zero-day. An unauthenticated, remote attacker can execute arbitrary commands with root privileges on the underlying operating system by sending a crafted email message containing malicious SQL statements. It affects virtual and physical appliances regardless of device configuration — there is no configuration that takes you out of scope. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on Monday 14 September and ordered Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies to patch within three days, by 17 September. This is the same date carried in the banner above and in the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

China-linkedEspionage

UNC3569 — one click, a Chromium 80 engine, and a fileless backdoor

The China-linked group UNC3569 exploited CVE-2026-51990 in Tencent Sogou Input Method for Windows — a Chinese-language input method editor with hundreds of millions of installations — to deploy the GRAYRABBIT backdoor against government, education, technology and finance targets, predominantly in East and Southeast Asia. The chain stacks three weaknesses: unvalidated command-line argument injection in the proprietary sgbiz: URI handler, unrestricted URL navigation in a CEF-based webview, and an outdated, unsandboxed Chromium 80 engine lacking modern web security protections. A single crafted link silently opens a backdoor; no user interaction beyond the click is required. GrayRabbit itself is a modular, fileless implant offering remote shell access, file transfer and dynamic module loading, talking to command-and-control over RC4-encrypted TCP on port 443 — dressed as HTTPS but often without a proper TLS handshake, which is the detection opportunity. Gen reported the flaw to Tencent on 9 April 2026; Tencent confirmed a fix on 21 April, shipped to users by automatic update in version 16.3.0.3498.

Breaches & Incidents

FintechExtortion

Revolut handed customer passports and transaction histories to a government impostor

Revolut disclosed that an unauthorised third party submitted fraudulent requests for information from a legitimate government agency email domain, carrying valid domain authentication, and that the requests were fulfilled under the reasonable belief they were authentic. The exposed data covered identity and contact details — birth date, postal and email addresses, phone numbers — plus copies of passports and driver’s licences, and may have included verification selfies, account statements and transaction histories. Revolut says a limited number of customers were affected, that systems and customer funds are unaffected, and that it blocked the address and alerted the agency, law enforcement and regulators. It has not named the agency or its domain. A threat actor claiming to hold the data has threatened to release more unless Revolut pays.

Supply chain

CISA warns the max-severity GitLab flaw is now being exploited

CISA states that attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-85706, the CVSS 10.0 GitLab path traversal, in the wild. Because GitLab is where build pipelines pull from, an unauthenticated arbitrary file read on a self-managed instance is a credential and source-code problem before it is a server problem. Patched in 19.1.8, 19.2.6 and 19.3.2 on 10 September; added to KEV on 11 September.

BOD 22-01 is revoked — do not read these as “three weeks from the add date”. CISA issued BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk”, on 10 June 2026, superseding BOD 19-02 and BOD 22-01. It replaces the uniform window with a 16-tier remediation matrix keyed to whether the asset is publicly exposed, whether the CVE is in KEV, and whether exploitation can be fully automated; the most dangerous tier must be remediated within three days, with mandatory forensic triage. Agencies must be operating to these timelines by 7 December 2026. A same-week deadline is therefore normal, not an error. And note the corollary: KEV entry requires evidence of exploitation, so a severe flaw with no confirmed exploitation carries no federal deadline at all. Absence from KEV is not a severity judgement.
