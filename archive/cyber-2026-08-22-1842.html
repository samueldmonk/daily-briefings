Top Story

GitLab CVE-2026-19478 is being exploited in the wild days after disclosure

A code-injection flaw in GitLab rated CVSS 9.4 came under active exploitation within days of public disclosure, exposure-management firm watchTowr told The Hacker News on August 21. The bug lets an unauthenticated attacker modify or delete publicly accessible GitLab projects and rewrite their data — no credentials, no user interaction and no unusual configuration required. GitLab said the issue is reachable through a GraphQL directive.

Affected builds are GitLab Community and Enterprise Edition 18.2 before 18.11.11, 19.0 before 19.0.8, 19.1 before 19.1.6 and 19.2 before 19.2.4. Fixes shipped in 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8 and 18.11.11. watchTowr said it reproduced the vulnerability within minutes of reading the advisory and subsequently observed exploitation against its own honeypots.

Defenders should hunt web logs for the @gl_introduced directive and, where patching lags, restrict unauthenticated access to /api/graphql or remove public repository access.

It is one of four August 18 additions that all came due yesterday. The longest-overdue entry is now CVE-2025-62593 in the Ray distributed-computing framework — CVSS 9.4, added August 17, due August 20 and 2 days past due. The nearest deadline still ahead is CVE-2026-72529 in TrueConf Server — CVSS 9.3, due August 23, tomorrow.

The TrueConf figure is new to this edition: earlier runs could not source a score. CISA and SecurityWeek now describe CVE-2026-72529 as a missing-authentication flaw that lets an unprivileged remote attacker run arbitrary scripts by invoking an undocumented function over port 4307/TCP, and it is being chained with CVE-2026-72530 (due September 3) to replace TrueConf client distribution files and push PhantomCore malware to meeting participants.

TrueConf fixed both flaws in June 2026 in Server versions 5.3.9, 5.4.9 and 5.5.5. The required action falls under CISA's risk-based BOD 26-04 guidance rather than a fixed three-week window.

Head Mare — turning a video-conferencing server into a malware delivery channel

The group behind the two TrueConf Server flaws added to CISA's KEV catalog on August 20 is the hacktivist crew tracked as Head Mare. Rather than stopping at server compromise, the operators chained CVE-2026-72529 (CVSS 9.3, missing authentication for a critical function) with CVE-2026-72530 (code injection) against on-premises TrueConf instances, then replaced the TrueConf client distribution files hosted on those servers.

That turns a trusted internal download into a delivery mechanism: anyone joining a meeting who pulled the client from the compromised server received PhantomCore malware instead. It is a supply-chain pattern executed inside the victim's own perimeter, which is why CISA set an unusually short remediation window — three days — on the first of the two CVEs.

Reported by SecurityWeek, SC Media, BleepingComputer and Security Affairs following CISA's August 20 KEV addition. Head Mare is described in that reporting as a hacktivist group; no state sponsor is asserted here. Previous spotlight: the suspected-Russian UNC6293 / UNC7005 / UNC5976 clusters abusing Google OAuth and WhatsApp device linking.

ShinyHunters claims 7.1 million Baxter International records

The extortion crew ShinyHunters claims on its dark-web leak site to have published 7.1 million Salesforce records taken from medical-products maker Baxter International, according to Information Security Media Group’s GovInfoSecurity. It is the group’s latest move against the healthcare sector after earlier claims involving DentaQuest.

ExtortionFinancial services

US Bank investigating LockBit claim, with a September 3 leak deadline

US Bank says it is investigating claims by the LockBit ransomware group that the gang breached the bank and stole data, The Register reported on August 20. LockBit added the bank to its leak site and set a 14-day deadline, expiring September 3, threatening to publish unless it is paid. The bank confirmed it is aware of the claims but declined to discuss any contact with the extortionists or the sum demanded, and said there is no current indication of impact to its internal systems or of unauthorised network access.

No victim count, record total or data category is published here — none has been confirmed by the bank, and LockBit's own claims are unverified. The group re-emerged in 2025 with its LockBit 5.0 variant after earlier law-enforcement disruption.

NewCloudCredentials

768 leaked AWS keys are still live — and still hold full admin

Truffle Security, analysing publicly exposed AWS credentials from August 2022 to August 2026, found 768 keys that remain active and carry full administrative privileges over corporate cloud accounts: 526 root access keys plus 242 IAM user keys attached to the AdministratorAccess managed policy. Re-validating 10,616 key pairs on August 10, the researchers found 88% still authenticated.

The exposure skews old rather than new. The oldest working credential was 17.4 years old, and only 25 enumerable keys had been created in the preceding 30 days — so the dominant risk is forgotten credentials, not fresh developer mistakes. Of 2,903 keys examined, just 398 had a newer credential on the same IAM user, implying roughly 86% were never rotated or revoked. Sources of leakage included public Git histories, Hugging Face datasets, Docker images, package registries and CI/CD logs.

Critical infrastructure

Five US agencies warn of AI-generated exploit scripts hitting Siemens S7 PLCs

A joint advisory from the NSA, CISA, FBI, Department of Energy and EPA, published August 19, warns of an "active threat" against US critical infrastructure using AI-generated exploit scripts disguised as legitimate monitoring tools. The activity targets Siemens S7 series programmable logic controllers for reconnaissance and capability development, though the agencies assess the wider PLC targeting is broader than Siemens alone. Actors use internet scanning services such as Censys and ZoomEye to find exposed PLCs running outdated or poorly protected software. Named target sectors include critical manufacturing, energy and water.

Supply chain

Malicious Rust crates published to crates.io, then pulled within two hours

The Rust Project deleted malicious versions of three widely used crates after a compromised maintainer account published releases that added a typosquatted dependency whose build script downloaded and executed a remote payload during compilation. The affected releases — arrayref 0.3.10, internment 0.8.7 and append-only-vec 0.1.9 — all came from the same owner account on August 20 and were removed within 86 to 107 minutes. Because the code sat in a build script, merely resolving the dependency was enough to run the payload. There is no patched version and no CVE; developers are advised to check ~/.cargo/registry/cache and pin arrayref at 0.3.9 or earlier.

Supply chain

14 trojanized npm packages drop an AI-assisted Linux backdoor

TrendAI, Trend Micro's enterprise security business, reported on August 21 that a set of npm packages masquerading as calendar and streak utilities quietly deliver a Linux implant dubbed RedC2 4.0. "When the module loads, it locates the bundled binary, marks it executable, and launches it as a detached background process," the researchers wrote. No install hook is required — a single import anywhere in the dependency graph, "even a transitive one," is enough to execute the payload. Named packages include streak-metrics-math, kit-map-vim, streak-map-cache and streak-calc-metrics.

Endpoint

Microsoft Defender's own signed driver can be turned against security software

Check Point Research disclosed a technique that uses Defender's legitimately signed boot-time remediation driver, BTR.sys, to perform arbitrary kernel-level file and registry operations on Windows 7 through Windows 11 25H2 — exploiting no software flaw and importing no outside driver. Because BTR.sys is a required Windows component, it cannot be added to Microsoft's Vulnerable Driver Blocklist or blocked by WDAC without breaking Defender itself. Researcher Jiří Vinopal presented the work at Black Hat USA 2026 and DEF CON 34 and published a proof-of-concept tool, BTR_CLI, on August 20. Check Point said it found no evidence the technique has been used in real-world attacks.

Automotive

First documented malware infection of an Android car head unit

Kaspersky, which discovered the threat in June 2026, flagged a malware family built to infect Android-based vehicle head-unit firmware developed by DoFun, with the goal of delivering a multi-stage downloader for ad fraud and a proxy botnet. "The malware spread through the built-in updaters," researcher Dmitry Kalinin said, calling it the first documented case of malware on a car head unit with an infection chain specific to that device type. Kaspersky attributes the activity with high confidence to the MoYu Group, previously named by HUMAN's Satori team as part of the BADBOX ad-fraud and residential-proxy scheme.

EmailExploited

Zimbra SNMP flaw exploited for unauthenticated remote code execution

CERT Polska reports active exploitation of CVE-2026-73570 (CVSS 8.9), a command-injection issue in Zimbra Collaboration. Per NIST's NVD entry, the flaw exists in ZCS before 10.1.20 when the optional zimbra-snmp package is installed and SNMP notifications are enabled; improper sanitisation during notification processing lets an unauthenticated attacker send crafted SMTP requests that can execute arbitrary OS commands as the Zimbra user. Zimbra patched it last month in 10.1.20.

Breach-tracker aggregators circulated further victim claims this weekend beyond the two added above. None were corroborated by a primary source or victim statement fetched this run, so no victim counts or data categories are published for them.
