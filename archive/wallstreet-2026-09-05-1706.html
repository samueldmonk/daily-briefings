The Lead

A jobs report nobody expected turned good news into bad news — and the front end of the curve took the hit

The Labor Department reported 162,000 nonfarm payrolls added in August, against a consensus TheStreet puts at 55,000 and CNBC’s Dow Jones survey at 53,000. The unemployment rate was unchanged at 4.1%. One return this run describes it as the strongest monthly gain since March, with average hourly earnings up 0.3% on the month to $37.75 and 3.1% over the year, food services and drinking places adding 59,000 and local government education 42,000 while the information industry shed jobs.

Stocks read it as a rate-hike signal. The Dow took the worst of it at −0.51%, with the S&P 500 −0.38% and the Nasdaq −0.29%. The one exception among the major indexes was the Russell 2000, which finished +0.25% — TheStreet calls it “the one exception” to the day’s declines, and it is notable precisely because small caps are the yield-sensitive corner of the market. TheStreet’s 4:01 p.m. wrap offers the explanation that some of the September repricing may already have been in the small-cap price; the same wrap records the Cboe Volatility Index rising about 1.47% into the close.

Chris Zaccarelli of Northlight Asset Management captured the mechanic: “a great Jobs report – 162k jobs created vs 55k expected – had an instant reaction of stocks and bonds selling off.” Bill Adams of Fifth Third Commercial Bank put the consequence on the calendar: “Next week’s releases of the CPI and PPI reports have the power to decide whether the Fed hikes or holds.”

On the Radar

  • Markets closed Monday, September 7. Stock and bond markets both shut for Labor Day; regular hours ran on Friday with no early close. The week after Labor Day is conventionally one of the busiest on the calendar as institutional desks return.
  • CPI and PPI next week are, on Bill Adams’s framing, the releases with “the power to decide whether the Fed hikes or holds.” This edition does not assert an FOMC meeting date from a primary source: two lower-tier returns this run place the meeting on September 15–16, and that is recorded here as reporting rather than as an established date.
  • Rate-hike pricing moved twice this week, and the two moves had different authors. Sourced this run: after Fed Governor Chris Waller indicated on Thursday that he would support holding rates steady this month barring an inflation surprise, market pricing for a September hike fell back to roughly 50%. Then Friday’s payrolls reversed it — one account this run puts the odds at about 59% (from 52% before the report), another at about 62% (from ~55%). Both current figures are printed rather than averaged. Earlier readings held in this desk’s file — 63%, a drop to ~50% after Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s first major speech on August 28, then 57–58% and ~52% — are recorded there, not re-established this run, and are deliberately not strung into a single chain: the August ~50% follows Warsh, the Thursday ~50% follows Waller, and they are different events with different speakers.
  • NewGameStop (GME) reports second-quarter earnings on Tuesday, September 8. The company has guided to quarterly net income of $290–310 million, close to double the $168.6 million a year earlier, and has amended its $1.4 billion convertible-notes exchange to pay roughly $358.4 million in cash and issue about 55.5 million shares, fixing the share count.
  • NewNorway’s $2.3 trillion sovereign wealth fund has proposed deep cuts to its U.S. Treasury holdings. Norges Bank Investment Management recommends cutting government bonds in its benchmark bond index from 70% to 50%; Reuters calculates that as roughly $80 billion off about $215 billion of Treasuries held at end-June. Any implementation is unlikely before well into 2027 — but the fund owns an average 1.5% of every listed company globally, so its benchmark decisions move flows.
  • Political pressure on the Fed is now explicit. President Trump posted on Truth Social demanding the central bank and Chair Kevin Warsh cut rates, threatening to “STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE HAVE A DEFICIT” otherwise.
  • Volkswagen rose on Friday after approving a plan to cut a further 50,000 jobs, bringing approved reductions to 100,000. The move is reported at +6.1% by TheStreet; this desk’s file records other coverage putting it as high as 7%, so the figure is given as a 6.1–7% range rather than a single number.
