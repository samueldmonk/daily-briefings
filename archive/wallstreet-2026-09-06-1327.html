The Lead

Markets are shut for the long weekend, and the last thing they did was flinch at a hot jobs number

It is Sunday. U.S. stock and bond markets are closed on Monday, 7 September for Labor Day, and the next session opens Tuesday, 8 September. Nothing has traded since Friday afternoon, so everything below is Friday’s tape and what is queued behind it — there is no current session to report and this page does not pretend otherwise.

Friday closed lower across the three major averages. The Dow fell 271.86 points, or 0.51%, to 53,414.25; the S&P 500 slid 0.38% to 7,718.60; the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.29% to 26,506.99. The Russell 2000 was the one exception among the major indexes, finishing +0.25%. The arithmetic ties: 53,686.11 − 271.86 = 53,414.25 exactly.

The driver was the August employment report. Nonfarm payrolls grew 162,000 against the 53,000 expected by economists polled by Dow Jones — a beat wide enough to reframe the September Fed meeting. Treasury yields jumped, and a labour market that will not cool made a rate increase, not a cut, the live question. Unemployment held at 4.1%; average hourly earnings rose 0.3% month-over-month to $37.75, +3.1% year-over-year.

A word on a trap this desk has logged repeatedly: searches for “stock market news for Sept 4” have several times returned 7,747.71 / 53,686.11 / 26,584.06, all up 1.1–1.4% as Friday’s close. Those are Thursday 3 September’s levels, and they appear in the Thursday row of the scorecard only. This run the first search returned the correct Friday figures, so there was no catch to make.

On the Radar

  • Monday 7 September — Labor Day. U.S. stock and bond markets are closed. Next open Tuesday 8 September.
  • Thursday 10 September, 8:30 AM ET — PPI. Friday 11 September, 8:30 AM ET — CPI. Both feed directly into the following week’s Fed decision. The framing in this run’s coverage is blunt: if both prints come in hotter than expected, a September hike moves from a coin flip to the base case; if they come in cooler, the Fed can discount the hot labour-market signal.
  • Wednesday 16 September, 2:00 PM ET — FOMC decision, with the meeting running 15–16 September and the press conference at 2:30. The pre-meeting quiet period began Saturday 5 September and runs through Thursday 17 September.
  • NewThe rate-hike odds are genuinely contested, and this page will not pick a winner. This run returned, side by side: Goldman Sachs calling a September hike “very unlikely” on softer retail sales, a slowing labour market and cooling inflation; Kalshi traders at 48% for a 25 bp hike; and a broader 2026 measure putting the probability of at least one hike this year at 71.5%, with nine of nineteen participants seeing at least one increase by December. This desk’s own record has the September reading wandering from 63% to roughly 50% after Chair Warsh’s late-August remarks, to 57–58%, to about 52%. No arrow-chain is drawn through those numbers, because they were established on different days by different events.
  • NewEarnings week. GameStop reports Tuesday 8 September — guidance of $290–310M net income against $168.6M a year earlier, with the $1.4B convertible-notes exchange amended to roughly $358.4M cash plus about 55.5M shares. Chewy follows Wednesday 9 September, and Oracle, Macy’s and Adobe Thursday 10 September.
  • Norway’s $2.3T sovereign fund has proposed cutting government bonds in its benchmark from 70% to 50% — roughly $80B off about $215B of Treasuries on a Reuters calculation — though it is unlikely to take effect before well into 2027. The fund owns around 1.5% of every listed company globally.

The two New tags were each grepped against archive/wallstreet-2026-09-05-2205.html before being written; neither the contested-odds set nor the Chewy/Oracle/Macy’s/Adobe schedule appeared there. GameStop did, which is why its tag sits on the earnings-week bullet as a whole rather than on GameStop alone.
