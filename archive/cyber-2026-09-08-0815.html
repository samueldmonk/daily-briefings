Top Story

Adobe patches StyleSmuggler — a CVSS 10.0 Magento zero-day that had already been backdooring stores for three days

Adobe published APSB26-146 on 7 September at 20:20 UTC with a priority rating of 1, its highest, closing the flaw the Dutch e-commerce security firm Sansec named StyleSmuggler. It is now tracked as CVE-2026-75650 and scored CVSS 10.0. Adobe states it is aware of the vulnerability being exploited in the wild: an unauthenticated attacker can execute arbitrary code on an affected installation.

The mechanism is the store’s own template system. The chain abuses Magento’s templating through PHP code injection to generate a “Payment Transaction Failed Reminder” email, triggering code execution in the process. Sansec reproduced the full unauthenticated chain on clean Magento Open Source 2.4.7, 2.4.8 and 2.4.9 installations; it reports all current versions affected, including 2.4.9.

What is landing on compromised stores. Threat actors are using CVE-2026-75650 to deploy a Rust-based Linux backdoor that connects out to an external server and waits for instructions, and to deliver a PHP dropper that writes a web shell capable of executing arbitrary PHP.

The timeline is the reason this sits at the top of the page. Sansec dates exploitation to 4 September and published an early advisory on 5 September, saying it was going out ahead of a fix because stores were being compromised at that moment. The Netherlands-based firm Disrex says a Magento server it manages was compromised 50 minutes after the first confirmed exploitation was reported, on 4 September at 10:20 p.m. The patch arrived on the evening of 7 September — three days into active exploitation.

Applying it is not a normal upgrade. The fix ships as a hotfix, not a full release: VULN-39341-composer-patches.zip, downloaded from repo.magento.com and applied as a composer patch. Note for readers of earlier editions of this page: StyleSmuggler previously appeared here as a named exploitation campaign with no CVE assigned. That is no longer true — the identifier and the score both exist now, and the entry has been corrected rather than carried.

Patch Priority

Patch Adobe Commerce / Magento Open Source against CVE-2026-75650 today. It is scored CVSS 10.0, it is unauthenticated, Adobe confirms exploitation in the wild, and the fix has existed for only a matter of hours. Apply the APSB26-146 hotfix (VULN-39341-composer-patches.zip from repo.magento.com, as a composer patch), then hunt: a Rust Linux backdoor and a PHP web shell are the payloads that have been observed.

No federal remediation deadline for CVE-2026-75650 was confirmed at this desk this run, so none is printed. The nearest federal deadline still open belongs to PaperCut NG/MF — Monday 14 September, six days out — and it matches the KEV section below exactly.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Ransomware-as-a-serviceDouble extortion

INC Ransom

INC operates as a ransomware-as-a-service business and uses double extortion — data is stolen first and leaked on its site if the demand is not paid. Reporting in June 2026 put it past 830 victims since 2023; as of 17 August 2026 its leak site claimed 900. It has been described mounting a rapid campaign against law firms. In September it claimed responsibility for an attack in which it says it stole 5.7 terabytes of data. The 5.7 TB claim is the group’s own and the returns read here do not name the victim, so no organisation is identified with it. A leak-site count is a claim by an extortion group, not a verified victim tally.

Breaches & Incidents

NewExploitedE-commerce

A managed Magento store fell 50 minutes after the first StyleSmuggler report

Disrex, a Netherlands-based e-commerce development platform, says a Magento server under its management was compromised 50 minutes after the first confirmed StyleSmuggler exploitation was reported on 4 September at 10:20 p.m. That is the interval between a public warning and a real intrusion on a professionally administered store — a useful number for anyone estimating how long an unpatched cart has.

Hands-on intrusionEducation

PaperCut servers are being worked by hand, not just scanned

Attackers are chaining authentication bypass and remote code execution flaws in PaperCut servers to run commands and perform reconnaissance, and to create privileged accounts. Post-exploitation activity reported includes delivery of registry collection tools and Metasploit payloads, with vulnerable servers across the education sector among the targets. This is the activity behind the 14 September federal deadline below.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • PaperCut NG/MF — CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078. Federal remediation due Monday 14 September — (6 days left). The nearest open deadline on this page, and the one the Patch Priority box points at.
  • Google Chrome V8 — CVE-2026-85046. Added to KEV on 4 September; FCEB agencies must apply the patches by 18 September — (10 days left).
  • The 2 September batch — seven vulnerabilities, including CVE-2026-9586 (Sangoma Switchvox), CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette) and CVE-2026-49869 (Kestra OSS). Due 16 September — (8 days left).
  • SonicWall — CVE-2026-83548 → CVE-2026-83549. CISA set a 5 September federal remediation deadline. That date has passed: overdue by 3 days.
  • Volume check. CISA flagged 10 actively exploited vulnerabilities in the single week of 31 August to 6 September 2026 — consistent with the 31 August, 2 September and 4 September alerts this page tracks.
  • A note on the clock itself, because this page has stated it wrongly before. The uniform “three weeks from the add date” shorthand is not the text of BOD 22-01, and it is not what governs now: BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk,” issued 10 June 2026, scores each asset–vulnerability pair into one of five remediation tiers with windows of 3, 14 or 60 calendar days. Every date printed above is the date CISA or the reporting attached to that specific entry, not a window this desk computed.

Day counts are measured from today, 8 September 2026, to the stated due date; the arithmetic is this desk’s and the dates are the sources’.
