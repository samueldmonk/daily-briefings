The Lead

Cheaper oil and an in-line CPI ended a four-day slide — but the week still finished red

U.S. markets are closed for the weekend. The last completed session was Friday, 11 September, when all three major indexes rose about 1% and snapped a four-session losing streak. Crude pulled back from its recent spurt — a drop of nearly 3% in Brent — which took pressure off the inflation outlook, and August CPI came in close to economists’ expectations. Breadth was genuinely broad: advancing stocks outnumbered decliners within the S&P 500 by roughly 2.1 to 1.

The rally was concentrated in AI infrastructure. Oracle’s fiscal first-quarter results and capital-spending guidance — CFO Hilary Maxson confirmed full-year capex of $90–95 billion, with Oracle naming HPE as a supplier of AI racks, cooling and networking gear — sent that spending straight through to the hardware complex. Hewlett Packard Enterprise and Dell both surged about 11–12% to lead the S&P 500; Super Micro and ON Semiconductor each finished up more than 7%.

One good day did not rescue the week. The S&P 500 fell 0.8%, the Dow fell 1.6% and the Nasdaq fell 0.7% across the five sessions. One read this run gives the weekly S&P decline as 0.68% rather than 0.8%; both are printed, and the same read’s weekly closing level of “7,666” is not published here, because it cannot be reconciled with the points-and-percent pair every wire report gives for Friday.

Attention now turns to the FOMC meeting on 15–16 September, with the decision Wednesday 16 September at 2 PM ET and a Summary of Economic Projections alongside it. Futures moved to roughly a 90% probability of a quarter-point increase after the core CPI print — an increase, which is what makes the projections the more consequential half of the release.

Movers & Drivers

Top S&P gainer

Hewlett Packard Enterprise +12.4%

HPE gained 12.44% to close at $62.09 on one tally, with other reads putting the move at 12.2% or "about 11%". The trigger was Oracle reiterating its fiscal 2027 capex outlook and naming HPE as a supplier of AI racks, cooling and networking equipment, on top of record quarterly revenue and its recently raised guidance.

AI infrastructure

Dell +11.5%

Dell closed at $567.13, an all-time high, newly sourced this run. The size of the move is reported two ways — 11.5% on one tally, "roughly 12%" on another — and both are printed. It ran alongside HPE as Oracle’s capex plan lifted the AI-server complex, with RBC Capital’s recent initiation at Outperform, price target $640, adding to the bid. The two names led S&P 500 gains on the day.

On the Radar

  • FOMC, 15–16 September. Futures price roughly a 90% chance of a quarter-point hike after hotter-than-expected core CPI — an increase, not a cut, as energy-driven inflation risk persists.
  • The oil driver is geopolitical, not seasonal. The week’s spike is attributed to strikes between the US and Iran prolonging the blockade of tankers in the Persian Gulf — which is why the pullback, not the level, is what equities traded on.
  • Oil is still the swing factor. Friday’s near-3% Brent pullback was the proximate cause of the equity rebound, so any renewed spike reverses the trade and re-prices the front end.
  • The AI capex cycle has a named reference number. Oracle’s $90–95 billion full-year capex guidance is now the anchor for server, networking and cooling demand — the mechanism behind Friday’s HPE, Dell, Super Micro and ON Semiconductor moves.
  • August CPI, in full. Headline +0.4% m/m, in line with forecast and up from July’s +0.1%; 3.4% y/y, unchanged from July. Core came in at +0.3% m/m against a 0.2% consensus and a 0.2% July reading — a tenth above forecast, and the reason the hike odds jumped rather than the headline. Yields retreated after the print, ending a week-long global bond selloff.
  • The week ahead, 14–18 September. Empire State and ADP weekly employment on Tuesday; import and export prices, retail sales, business inventories, the NAHB housing index and the FOMC decision with projections at 2 PM ET on Wednesday; jobless claims, housing starts, building permits and pending home sales on Thursday. No market holiday falls in the week.
