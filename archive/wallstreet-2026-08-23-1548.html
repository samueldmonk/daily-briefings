The lead

A Friday bounce that didn’t save the week

US markets are closed for the weekend, so the most recent session is Friday, August 21. Stocks rose across the board to cap a volatile week: the Dow added 0.98%, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite each gained 0.43%, and the Russell 2000 rose 0.85%. The advance was broad rather than tech-led — TheStreet counted 341 S&P 500 members higher at midmorning, anchored by materials, health care and financials — and it followed a report showing US business activity growing at its fastest pace in more than four years.

It wasn’t enough. All four indexes still finished the week lower — the S&P 500 down about 1.4% — after a Treasury sell-off put pressure on tech and AI stocks. Relief from Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent’s Thursday plan to expand bond buybacks beyond $4 billion per issue lasted only briefly; markets read it as a limited fix and the 10- and 30-year yields snapped back to their previously held higher levels. “Part of it is signaling here to show that we believe yields don’t reflect the underlying fundamentals,” Bessent told CNBC.

Bitcoin ran the other way, climbing 7.04% on Friday to about $76,927 and extending a surge that began Wednesday when Bessent said the Treasury would double its long-term debt buybacks from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation. The Treasury also disclosed last week that the US national debt had passed $40 trillion. Ahead: Bessent details the plan to economically isolate Iran on Monday, then July PCE and Nvidia’s fiscal Q2 land on the same day, Wednesday, before Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s first Jackson Hole keynote on Friday. Correction to earlier editions: July PCE is released Wednesday, August 26 — not Friday alongside Warsh.

On the radar
  • Monday, August 24 — Bessent’s Iran press conference. The Treasury Secretary unveils details of the US plan to economically isolate Iran, the latest phase of the Middle East war. Earlier in the week Trump threatened “TREMENDOUS Economic Consequences” for any country trading with Iran, putting the focus on China, which sources oil from the Gulf. Also Monday: the Chicago Fed National Activity Index and the Dallas Fed Manufacturing Survey.
  • Tuesday, August 25. The Conference Board’s Consumer Confidence Index (90.8 previously), July new home sales (+1.6% previously), the Richmond Fed manufacturing index and the FHFA house price index, plus earnings from Intuit, Zoom, Bank of Montreal and DICK’S Sporting Goods.
  • Wednesday, August 26 — PCE and Nvidia. The Fed’s preferred inflation gauge lands in the morning: July PCE (−0.1% m/m previously, +3.7% y/y) and core PCE (+0.1% m/m, +3.3% y/y), alongside the second estimate of Q2 GDP and July durable goods orders. Nvidia then reports fiscal Q2 after the bell — the last of the Magnificent Seven and the next major test of the AI trade. Corrections to earlier editions: Nvidia reports Wednesday the 26th, not Thursday the 27th; and July PCE is also Wednesday, not Friday.
  • Thursday, August 27. Jackson Hole opens, and Marvell Technology, Workday, Autodesk, Dollar General, Dollar Tree and Ulta Beauty report. Also: initial jobless claims (206,000 previously) and the advance July goods trade balance.
  • Friday, August 28 — Warsh at Jackson Hole. Fed Chair Kevin Warsh delivers his first Jackson Hole keynote as chair. The data slate is lighter: the MNI Chicago PMI and the final August University of Michigan sentiment and inflation-expectations readings.
