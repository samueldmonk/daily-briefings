The Lead

A 1% rally on Friday, and a much worse week underneath it

U.S. markets are closed for the weekend. The last completed session was Friday, 11 September, when all three major indexes rose about 1% and snapped a four-session losing streak. Crude pulled back from its recent spurt — a drop of nearly 3% in Brent — which took pressure off the inflation outlook, and August CPI came in close to economists’ expectations.

Newly sourced this run, and the reason the headline number misleads: across the full week the S&P 500 fell 0.68% — but the equal-weight S&P 500 fell 1.87%, the Dow fell 1.51% and small caps fell 2.38%. The cap-weighted index was held up by a handful of very large stocks while the average constituent did considerably worse. A one-day rally of 1% does not change that; it is what the week looked like underneath.

Newly sourced this run, and it retires a carried line: Friday’s advance was led by Communication Services, Consumer Discretionary and Information Technology, with Communication Services up 1.6% to pace the S&P 500 sector table and T-Mobile up more than 3.5% at the front of it. Previous editions carried a “nine of eleven sectors higher, health care and utilities lagging” line that was never re-sourced; it is dropped in favour of the sector detail above, which was.

Friday itself was concentrated in AI infrastructure. Oracle’s capital-spending guidance — CFO Hilary Maxson confirmed full-year capex of $90–95 billion — sent that spending straight through to the hardware complex. Hewlett Packard Enterprise added 12.2% and Dell rose 11.5% to lead the S&P 500; Super Micro and ON Semiconductor each finished up more than 7%.

Attention now turns to the FOMC meeting on 15–16 September, with the decision Wednesday 16 September at 2 PM ET and a Summary of Economic Projections alongside it. The Wednesday timing is independently re-confirmed this run by a second week-ahead source, which strengthens the previous edition’s refusal of a preview that had put the Fed on Thursday. The market is pricing an increase rather than a cut — which is what makes the projections the more consequential half of the release — but how large a probability is genuinely in dispute, and both readings are printed below rather than reconciled here.

Movers & Drivers

Top S&P gainer

Hewlett Packard Enterprise +12.2%

HPE led the S&P 500 — a description re-confirmed by a fresh source this run. The 12.2% figure is carried; other reads put the move at 12.44% (to a close of $62.09), “about 11%”, and, newly this run, “up nearly 11%”. All four are printed rather than averaged; what every source agrees on is that HPE was the index’s single biggest gainer. The trigger was Oracle’s capex outlook and its naming of HPE as a supplier of AI racks, cooling and networking equipment.

AI infrastructure

Dell +11.5%

Dell closed at $567.13, an all-time high. The size of the move is reported two ways — 11.5% on one tally, “roughly 12%” on another — and both are printed. It ran alongside HPE as Oracle’s capex plan lifted the AI-server complex, with RBC Capital’s initiation at Outperform, price target $640, adding to the bid.

CarriedSemis

ON Semiconductor +7%

ON Semiconductor finished up more than 7%, joining Super Micro in the second tier of the AI-hardware move.

AI infrastructure

Super Micro +7%

Super Micro Computer climbed more than 7% on the same read-through from Oracle’s capital-spending guidance. The smaller gain is described as a partly priced-in story, because SVP Mike Staiger had already flagged a $60 billion order book at a Goldman Sachs conference on Thursday — a day before the Oracle read-through moved the rest of the group.

Disputed

Oracle — a mixed session

Oracle leapt 6% after beating Street expectations in its fiscal first quarter, but sources disagree on where it finished: one describes shares declining almost 2% as the day progressed, while carried editions had it up 10.3% intraday and closing near flat. No source read this run states an official closing change, so none is asserted.

NewBreadth

The average stock had a much worse week

Newly sourced this run: over the five sessions the equal-weight S&P 500 fell 1.87% and small caps fell 2.38%, against just 0.68% for the cap-weighted S&P 500. That gap — roughly 1.2 percentage points between the index and its own equal-weight version — is the week’s real story, and it is invisible in the headline figure.

On the Radar

  • A week-ahead preview put the Fed on the wrong day, and this page is not following it. One preview read this run schedules the Federal Reserve decision for Thursday 17 September, alongside the Bank of England. The Federal Reserve’s own meeting calendar puts the September FOMC on 15–16 September with the announcement Wednesday 16 September at 2 PM ET. The Wednesday date is published; the Thursday one is named here only to retire it.
  • Three central banks decide next week, not one. The Federal Reserve on Wednesday, the Bank of England on Thursday 17 September and the Bank of Japan on Friday 18 September. Only the Fed’s has a projections round attached.
  • The diplomacy has a date now. Gulf Cooperation Council diplomats are expected to meet their Iranian counterpart on Monday to discuss a possible temporary arrangement for managing shipping through the Strait of Hormuz. That meeting, not the level of crude, is the event risk for Monday’s open.
  • Friday’s intraday oil quotes are still circulating as if they were closes. A preview written during Friday’s session had oil extending gains, quoting Brent November at $108.21 (+0.54%) and WTI October at $102.96 (+0.47%). Both benchmarks settled lower than that. The pairs appear here together because the higher one is an intraday quote, not a close.
  • Inflation is still the binding constraint, but the core reading cuts both ways. Headline CPI ran 3.4% year on year against the Fed’s 2% target, and core came in a tenth above forecast at +0.3% m/m, up from +0.2% in July — which is why hike odds jumped rather than the headline. Newly sourced this run, and pointing the other way: the annual core rate slowed to 2.4%, its lowest since 2021. A firming monthly print inside a cooling annual trend is exactly the ambiguity the Summary of Economic Projections will have to resolve.
  • Wednesday is a stacked day beyond the Fed. The same week-ahead source puts UK CPI, US retail sales and New Zealand GDP on the Wednesday alongside the FOMC decision.
  • The Treasury is buying bonds back into the yield spike. Coverage this run notes the ten-year touching its highest since 2023 despite a $6 billion bond buyback plan attributed to Bessent (the source gives no title, so none is given here) — the failure of the buyback to hold yields down is the point, not the buyback itself.
  • Diesel is the quiet part of the oil story. One report on the week’s crude gains carries US diesel at a record high. It is reported alongside the week’s crude gains and is carried at that weight — a headline figure, with no supporting series in the sources read.
  • Oil is still the swing factor. Friday’s near-3% Brent pullback was the proximate cause of the equity rebound, so any renewed spike reverses the trade and re-prices the front end — and despite that pullback the week was crude’s biggest since July.
  • The AI capex cycle has a named reference number. Oracle’s $90–95 billion full-year capex guidance is the anchor for server, networking and cooling demand — the mechanism behind Friday’s HPE, Dell, Super Micro and ON Semiconductor moves.
  • Watch breadth, not the index. If the equal-weight S&P keeps underperforming the cap-weighted index by more than a point a week, the rally is narrowing regardless of what the headline number prints.
