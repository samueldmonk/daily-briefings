The Lead

A jobs blowout closes the week lower — and the Fed debate is now about hiking, not cutting

Stocks finished Friday in the red and the market is now shut until Tuesday. The Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 271.86 points, or 0.51%, to close at 53,414.25; the S&P 500 slid 0.38% to 7,718.60; and the Nasdaq Composite dropped 0.29% to 26,506.99. CNBC headlined the session “Dow tumbles more than 260 points after strong jobs report reignites rate hike fears.”

Those levels are published because they check out twice. 53,686.11 minus 271.86 is 53,414.25 exactly; 26,584.06 less 0.29% is 26,506.97 against the 26,506.99 reported. Points change, percent change and level reconcile against each other and against Thursday’s close in every case.

New this edition: a post-close recap published at 4:33 PM ET independently reports the same three directions and the same rate-hike figure, and adds the rest of the week’s labour data, which pointed the other way — Wednesday’s ADP report had private employers adding just 38,000 jobs, and Thursday’s jobless claims rose slightly to 206,000. A “low hire, low fire” read of the economy held right up until Friday morning.

Then nonfarm payrolls grew 162,000 in August. The unemployment rate held at 4.1%. Per the CME FedWatch tool, the probability of a rate hike at the September 15–16 meeting climbed to 58%. That 58% is carried by two independent sources fetched this run; the 49.4% prior-day reading behind it comes from CNBC alone and is labelled as such. Reuters, writing before the print, had traders at “nearly 50%” against 63% before Waller’s Thursday remarks — so the week’s round trip ran roughly 63 to 50 and back to 58. Short-dated Treasuries sold off hardest: the 2-year and 5-year yields both set fresh 52-week highs.

“The August jobs report was much better than expected, focusing the Fed squarely on controlling inflation when they meet next in September. The next Fed decision will be finely balanced: Next week’s releases of the CPI and PPI reports have the power to decide whether the Fed hikes or holds.”
— Bill Adams, chief U.S. economist, Fifth Third Commercial Bank

The consensus that was missed is still four different numbers, and this desk still does not merge them. Desks put the August estimate at 53,000 (CNBC/Dow Jones), 55,000 (TheStreet) and 56,000 (Reuters survey of economists — sourced this run directly from Reuters’ own pre-market wire, not from an aggregator). All agree on the 162,000 actual and the 4.1% unemployment rate. Reuters also notes the July print showed the economy shed 23,000 jobs, with the prior two months revised sharply lower — which is what made August look like a reversal rather than a trend.

The President’s ultimatum — quoted, not paraphrased

“LOWER THE RATE OR I’LL STOP TRADING WITH COUNTRIES WITH WHICH WE HAVE A DEFICIT, which the U.S. Supreme Court, in its ridiculous and very costly Tariff decision, strongly acknowledged ‘the President’ has an absolute right to do. IT’S BETTER THAN TARIFFS! The Fed Board, with its great new leader, must get smart – BE PATRIOTS for a change.”
— President Donald Trump, Truth Social, reacting to the payrolls print. The chairman referred to is Kevin Warsh.
NOTE ON SOURCING. TheStreet’s headline renders the threat as aimed at countries running surpluses with the U.S.; its own body text and the quoted post both say deficit. This page prints the post verbatim and does not adopt the headline’s wording.

On the Radar

  • Markets are closed Monday, September 7 for Labor Day — U.S. stock and bond markets both shut. Friday was the last session of the week; the next open is Tuesday, September 8.
  • CPI and PPI land next week, and Fifth Third’s Bill Adams frames them as the deciding inputs for whether the Fed hikes or holds on September 15–16. Fed Governor Christopher Waller’s Thursday remarks were the last major Fedspeak before the meeting blackout, per BCA Research’s Felix Vezina-Poirier — so there is no further official guidance until the decision.
  • Norway’s $2.3 trillion sovereign wealth fund has proposed deep cuts to its U.S. Treasury holdings. Norges Bank Investment Management recommends reducing government bonds within its benchmark bond index to 50% from 70%; Reuters calculates that would mean cutting nearly $80 billion from about $215 billion of Treasuries held at end-June. Any cuts are unlikely to be implemented until several months into 2027 at the earliest. The fund owns an average of 1.5% of all listed companies globally.
  • NewSeptember seasonality, with the caveat attached. September is historically the weakest month for stocks, but most of the weakness comes in the second half of the month, with returns there averaging worse than −1%, according to Melissa Brown, global head of investment decision research at SimCorp.
  • GameStop (GME) reports second-quarter earnings September 8. The company expects quarterly net income of $290–310 million against $168.6 million a year earlier, and has amended its $1.4 billion convertible-notes exchange to pay roughly $358.4 million in cash and issue about 55.5 million shares, fixing the share count.
  • The Iran conflict remains the energy wildcard. The European Union has formally joined the U.S.-led sanctions campaign — “Operation Economic Outcast” — and South Korea is weighing a military role in reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Vice President JD Vance declined to call the six-month-old fighting a war: “I wouldn’t call it a war … Right now, there is no active shooting,” he said — a remark the Associated Press notes came even as Iran fired at U.S. ally Kuwait on Thursday, continuing its retaliation for U.S. strikes earlier in the week.
