Top Story

ShinyHunters defaced Clop’s leak site and says it took the gang’s onion keys

On Friday 19 September the ShinyHunters extortion group compromised and defaced the Clop ransomware operation’s dark-web data leak site, exploiting an unauthenticated file-upload vulnerability in Grav CMS. The uploaded page read “THIS SITE HAS BEEN PWN3D BY SHINYHUNTERES” and linked to ShinyHunters’ own leak site.

ShinyHunters claims it took the leak site’s source code and Grav plugins, system logs from /var/log, and the private cryptographic keys for Clop’s Tor onion service — then set a 72-hour deadline and said it intends to extort Clop. Asked what it planned to do with the material, the actor replied: “Going to extort them.”

Reporting ties the move to a dispute over Clop’s 2025 Oracle E-Business Suite data-theft campaign, in which a Clop representative is said to have threatened violence against and doxxing of ShinyHunters members. No victim data is implicated; the exposure here is the extortion infrastructure itself.

Patch Priority

CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco Identity Services Engine, CVSS 10.0, federal deadline passed

An authentication bypass caused by insufficient authentication control on an ISE / ISE-PIC API endpoint. Successful exploitation gives an unauthenticated remote attacker command execution as root. Cisco’s PSIRT says it is aware of active exploitation; the flaw surfaced while support engineers were working a customer’s TAC case, meaning at least one enterprise had already been broken into before the bug was known. Fixes: ISE 3.1 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.4 Patch 7, 3.5 Patch 4. Added to CISA KEV 16 September, federal remediation due 19 September 2026 — (1 day overdue). Patch, then hunt: an auth bypass leaves no failed logins to find.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Ransomware affiliateCisco FMC

UAT-11988 — a Qilin affiliate living inside firewall management

Cisco Talos attributes part of the exploitation of CVE-2026-20079 (Secure Firewall Management Center authentication bypass, CVSS 10.0) to a cluster it tracks as UAT-11988, assessed as a Qilin ransomware affiliate. The cluster logged into FMC devices using static credentials exposed by CVE-2026-20316 and used that access for reconnaissance and pre-encryption endpoint enumeration before deploying Qilin. Talos says three separate threat groups are exploiting the FMC flaws, with other activity linked to state-sponsored operations; Cisco’s PSIRT became aware of exploitation in August 2026 and updated its advisory to confirm it on 9 September. Compromising the box that manages the firewalls collapses the network’s segmentation and its logging in one step.

Breaches & Incidents

NewSupply chain

CrowdSec: 170 private repositories copied, undetected for nearly four months

The French security vendor says an attacker copied about 170 private GitHub repositories on 22 May using the account of an employee who had just left and whose GitHub access had been left open. The entry point was May’s TanStack npm supply-chain compromise (CVE-2026-45321), in which 84 malicious releases across 42 @tanstack packages ran an install-time payload harvesting GitHub and npm tokens, cloud credentials, Kubernetes and Vault secrets and SSH keys. The theft went unnoticed until the stolen code appeared on a cybercrime forum on 16 September. CrowdSec says production infrastructure and databases were not accessed and the account performed only Git fetch operations; more than 130 of the repositories were already public. Exposed: email addresses of 83 users (under 0.05% of its 150,000) and details on 51 potential investors.

Claimed, not confirmedHealthcare

McKesson — ShinyHunters’ record claim, with the caveats stated

McKesson has confirmed on its website that attackers broke into several cloud-hosted accounts and exfiltrated data. ShinyHunters claims 284 million patient records and a demand of $55,236,150 — while conceding it has not analysed the data, does not know how many unique individuals it covers, and is counting raw database rows. The record count is therefore the attacker’s arithmetic, not a victim total, and this desk does not publish it as one.
