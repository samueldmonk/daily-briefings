The Lead

As of ~1 PM ET: stocks lower, the 10-year touching 5%, and the AI trade hit by the AI labs themselves

The selling began with a weekend essay. Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published a 3,800-word argument that AI companies should deliberately slow the pace at which they improve frontier models — “we must slow the pace at which we improve the capabilities of A.I. models,” he wrote — and OpenAI CEO Sam Altman agreed on X: “I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier.” Elon Musk backed the call as well. Altman separately told Fortune that safety concerns have pushed OpenAI’s IPO to 2027; Anthropic still plans to list in the fall and has reportedly chosen the Nasdaq.

The readings, in sequence and all attributed — none averaged. Open, 9:33 a.m. (TheStreet): S&P 500 −0.75%, Dow −0.28%, Nasdaq Composite −1.17%, Russell 2000 +0.45%. At the open (Reuters/MarketScreener): S&P 500 −0.59%, Nasdaq Composite −1.19%; a companion read gives S&P −0.7%, Nasdaq 100 −1.7%, Dow −0.3%. 11:26 a.m. (TheStreet): S&P 500 −0.65%, with just 233 holdings advancing. Midday, 12:00 p.m. (24/7 Wall St.): S&P 500 −0.4%, Dow −0.4%, Nasdaq down more than 1% — “roughly triple the S&P 500’s loss.” An intraday session read (ChartMill): S&P 500 −0.62%, Nasdaq 100 −1.49%, Dow −0.13%, Russell 2000 −0.27%. A ~1:03 p.m. reading returned this run from Bloomberg’s live blog: Dow −0.1%, S&P 500 −0.3%, Nasdaq 100 −0.4%.

Those last two disagree sharply on how much the Nasdaq is down, and nothing read this run reconciles them, so both are printed and neither is adopted as the session figure. What every read agrees on: the market is lower, and the damage is concentrated in high-multiple tech rather than spread across the index.

Two macro forces are compounding it. The 10-year Treasury yield reached 5.014%, its highest since October 2023, before easing — CNBC reports it last down more than 2 basis points at 4.947% later in the session. And Brent crude climbed above $108 after Houthi attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and a drone strike that prompted Saudi Arabia to shut its East-West pipeline, the workaround for the Hormuz closure.

On the Radar

  • FOMC decision, Wednesday 16 September, 2:00 p.m. ET — the meeting that carries the Summary of Economic Projections. Hike odds for a 25bp move are quoted at 86% (Yahoo, post-CPI), 85% (Capital.com via TheStreet) and 88% (CME FedWatch via Schwab, on file). All printed, none averaged. Goldman Sachs revised its forecast for this week from no change to a hike after Friday’s August inflation print, and another hike is increasingly expected before year-end. Chair Kevin Warsh has said he will not give forward guidance.
  • The Hormuz story has no diplomatic off-ramp scheduled. Oman postponed planned talks with Iran on the future of the Strait, with no new date set; Foreign Minister Sayyid Badr Albusaidi said Oman remains committed to “fostering dialogue.” The Houthis claim direct hits on Saudi Arabia’s King Khalid Air Base at Khamis Mushait and say they have seized the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands, after taking Perim in the Bab al-Mandab on Sunday. Riyadh had not responded at the time of the report.
  • The bull case is on the record too. BofA Securities says it remains bullish on semiconductors, calling the frontier-lab controversy “noise relative to a secular market where AI-capex could surge 3x to $3tn+ by decade-end,” while flagging near-term volatility from rates, Middle East tensions and the 2 November US midterms. It cites 100% network utilisation and rising rental rates even on older-generation chips.
  • SpaceX (SPCX) fell 2% to $148.16 premarket amid the AI-linked selloff and continuing post-IPO insider lockup releases. David Einhorn’s Greenlight Capital second-quarter letter calls the company’s $1.75 trillion IPO valuation a marker that a “speculative top is near.” Separately, Sunday’s SES launch was SpaceX’s 700th Falcon flight (687 Falcon 9, 13 Falcon Heavy). Shares debuted at $135 on 12 June and hit a record $225.64 on 16 June.
