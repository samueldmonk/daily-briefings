The Lead

Markets are closed for the weekend. Friday's rebound broke what one week-ahead piece calls the S&P 500's deepest four-day decline since June — and the week ahead is a Federal Reserve week.

There is no live U.S. session to report: it is Saturday, and the most recent close is Friday 11 September. On that session the S&P 500 rose 65.28 points, or 0.9%, to 7,656.98, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 509.19 points, or 1%, to 52,573.29, and the Nasdaq composite rose 251.31 points, or 1%, to 26,333.04. The Russell 2000 added 13 points, or 0.4%, to 2,903.94. The S&P 500's gain broke a four-day losing streak.

Re-confirmed this run, for the twentieth consecutive edition, and this time with the streak stated in full. A search return this run restates all three closes to the point — S&P 500 +65.28 to 7,656.98, Dow +509.19 to 52,573.29, Nasdaq +251.31 to 26,333.04 — and the weekly figures with them: S&P 500 −0.8%, Dow −1.6%, Nasdaq −0.7%. It also states plainly what earlier editions of this page said of the S&P alone: U.S. stocks opened higher and stayed there, snapping the four-day losing streak for all three indexes, and still finished the week lower ahead of the FOMC meeting. So the rebound and the losing week are the same fact seen at two different lengths, which is the whole reason the scorecard below carries both.

The Associated Press attributes the rebound to two things: a drop of nearly 3% in Brent oil's price, which took pressure off inflation, and an inflation update that came in close to economists' expectations. In the same session, expectations strengthened in the bond market for an upcoming hike to interest rates by the Federal Reserve — a rebound in equities and a firming hike bet arriving together.

Two things were newly sourced in an earlier edition and are carried here. First, the Friday percentages have a second decimal that reconciles with the AP wire's rounded ones: S&P 500 +0.86%, Dow +0.98%, Nasdaq composite +0.96% — each consistent with the points figures above and with AP's "0.9%" and "1%". Second, the context for that rebound: a week-ahead piece describes the index as having been on track for its deepest four-day decline since June, with WTI crude above $100 and the 10-year yield closing in on 5%, before buyers stepped in on Friday to halt the slide. The same piece attributes the losing week to a warmer-than-expected producer price index, surging energy prices and higher bond yields.

On the Radar

  • The meeting is two days long, and the print that decided it is now quantified on both sides. Newly sourced this run: the FOMC meets 15–16 September and announces at 2:00 p.m. ET on 16 September. Trader consensus has moved to a 25-basis-point increase, driven by an August core CPI of +0.3% month over month against +0.2% expected and annual readings near 3.4% — the first time this page has had the annual figure rather than the monthly one alone. The same source lists the supporting conditions as strong August payrolls, elevated energy prices tied to Middle East supply pressure, and hawkish signals from Chair Kevin Warsh, and notes that economist surveys are more divided than market pricing is, citing moderating price pressure and the coming midterm elections as arguments for a hold. That gap between what traders price and what economists expect is the actual uncertainty in Wednesday's decision.
  • Also on the calendar, newly sourced this run: Lennar (LEN) reports on 16 September, the same day as the decision, and August housing starts and building permits land on 17 September — the first read on housing after a summer of higher rates. Re-sourced and extended this run from Schwab’s own week-ahead table: 14 and 15 September carry no major earnings or data, the 16th is the Fed decision plus Lennar, the 17th is housing starts and permits, and the 18th brings the Bank of Japan rate decision, August industrial production and the Conference Board’s August leading indicators — the last two new to this calendar. Schwab separately notes that Asian indexes closed lower on the week ahead of a possible BoJ hike, and that European indexes fell for the week after the European Central Bank raised rates on 10 September.
  • Who is deciding, and what the expectation is.
