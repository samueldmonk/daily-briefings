Top story

ShinyHunters roots Clop’s leak site through Grav CMS — and says it will extort the extortionists

On Friday 19 September, ShinyHunters compromised and defaced the Clop ransomware gang’s dark-web data leak site, using what it says is an unauthenticated file upload vulnerability in Grav CMS. The first sign was a single text file dropped onto Clop’s site reading “THIS SITE HAS BEEN PWN3D BY SHINYHUNTERES #Skids10p — Maybe don’t try to threaten us next time”, with a link back to ShinyHunters’ own leak site. Hours later the whole site was gone, replaced with ASCII art of Umbreon — the Pokémon ShinyHunters uses as its logo — above the line “rooting your systems since ’19 ;)”.

What is confirmed and what is claimed. BleepingComputer confirmed the defacement itself. Everything about the depth of the intrusion is ShinyHunters’ claim and is not independently verified: that it took the leak site source code and Grav plugins, system logs from /var/log, and the private cryptographic keys for Clop’s Tor onion service. It set a 72-hour deadline and said it intends to extort Clop.

Motive. The attack is presented as retaliation for threats of violence, and threats to identify group members, allegedly made by a Clop representative during a dispute over Clop’s 2025 Oracle E-Business Suite data-theft campaign.

Patch priority

CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco ISE and ISE-PIC — CVSS 10.0 — deadline expired 19 September

This is the one to do first and it is already late. An unauthenticated remote attacker can send a crafted request to the affected privileged API, bypass the web-based management interface without valid administrator credentials, and ultimately execute commands as root — with the ability to wipe logs behind them. Cisco disclosed it on 16 September; CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day, with a federal remediation deadline of 19 September under BOD 26-04. CISA categorises exploitation as active, the flaw as automatable, and the technical impact as total. Cisco PSIRT confirms active exploitation and found the issue in a customer support ticket. There is no workaround. Fixed releases: 3.1 P12, 3.2 P11, 3.3 P12, 3.4 P7, 3.5 P4.

Threat actor spotlight

Three clustersCisco Talos

UAT-11988, Sandworm and a Qilin operator — all in the same firewall manager

Cisco Talos says three distinct threat groups are exploiting two recently patched flaws in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center. CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0) is an authentication bypass letting unauthenticated attackers run scripts and potentially gain root. CVE-2026-20316 is a hard-coded password for a built-in, low-privileged web account — a remote attacker who knows the credential simply logs in; no CVSS has been published for it. Cisco updated its advisory on 9 September to confirm active exploitation, with activity traced back to August 2026.

The clusters: an unattributed actor deploying web shells and harvesting credentials; a cluster overlapping with the Russian state-sponsored group Sandworm, which used both flaws to deploy a variant of the Cyclops Blink implant; and a suspected Qilin ransomware operator (tracked as UAT-11988) that logs in with the static credential, runs network and endpoint reconnaissance, steals credentials, establishes additional access, deploys AV killers and delivers the ransomware. Hotfixes are already out for both.

CISA KEV & federal deadlines

  • CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco ISE / ISE-PIC. Added 16 September, due 19 September.
  • CVE-2025-39682, CVE-2026-53266 and CVE-2025-39964 — Linux kernel. Added 18 September under two separate CISA alerts that day, due 21 September. All three are additionally flagged as requiring forensic triage — agencies must investigate potentially exposed assets for evidence of compromise rather than treating patching as the whole response.
  • CVE-2026-87886 — Acronis Backup. Part of the same 16 September batch as the Cisco ISE flaw. No due date is published here, because none appeared in any source read this run.
  • The window is three days, not three weeks. Every one of these is a three-day window — 16→19 September and 18→21 September — set under BOD 26-04. The older three-week BOD 22-01 timetable does not apply to these entries.

By the numbers — and where they disagree

  • September Patch Tuesday is the largest on record and nobody agrees how large. The count of CVEs requiring customer action is reported as 963 (Computerworld), 964 (Tenable), 973 (ntcompatible and Trinetri), 974 (SecurityWeek and CyberExpress) and 1,169 (Senserva). Critical totals split 104 against 113. No single count is adopted here.
  • What every account does agree on: it is more than double August, it is the largest Patch Tuesday on record, and there are exactly two already-exploited flaws — CVE-2026-81963 and CVE-2026-85880. Windows accounted for 723 of the fixes and Office for 222. Also carried from an earlier edition today: 22 critical Office patches exploitable via the Preview/Reading Pane, at least 17 unauthenticated network-reachable RCEs across DNS, DHCP, MSMQ, NFS and SSTP VPN, and critical RCEs in Netlogon and Kerberos.
  • A tracker that contradicts itself. One exploited-CVE tracker read in recent runs is titled for 15 new exploited CVEs for its week, states 9 in its body and shows 7 in its own counter. None is adopted. Its stable figures — 1,315 exploited non-Microsoft CVEs across 282 vendors, and 1,716 confirmed exploited in the wild out of roughly 12,000 tracked, 1,088 Critical — are carried.
