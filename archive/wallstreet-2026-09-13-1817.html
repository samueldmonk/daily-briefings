The Lead

Markets are shut for the weekend; Friday’s relief rally is the last word before the Fed

US equity markets are closed — it is Sunday. The most recent session is Friday 11 September, when all three major indexes snapped four-day losing streaks: the S&P 500 rose 0.86%, the Dow gained 0.98%, and the Nasdaq Composite added 0.96%. Closing levels and points changes are in the Weekly Scorecard below.

Two things did the work. Oil fell hard — Brent dropped about 3% after Iranian state media said Tehran would meet Gulf states in Oman to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, pausing a rally that had carried crude into triple digits. And August CPI was more mixed than the rally suggests: headline came in at +0.4% month over month, as expected, with the year-over-year rate at 3.4%, but core ran hot at +0.3% against a +0.2% consensus — and it was that core reading that pushed rate-hike expectations up, not down.

So the relief is narrower than it looks, and Wednesday’s FOMC decision is the week. The meeting runs 15–16 September with the statement and rate decision due on the 16th. How heavily a hike is priced is disputed across what has been read: one reading of CME FedWatch as of 11 September implies roughly 90%, another 85%, a third 80–90%, and a fourth about 71% for a 25 basis-point increase. The 71% figure appears to predate the CPI print — the same source says the probability “jumped to around 90% following a hotter-than-expected monthly core CPI reading” — so the gap is a sequence, not a disagreement. All four are printed; none is averaged. Volatility has already repriced: the VIX broke decisively above its 50-day moving average to just below 18, implying a daily S&P 500 move of about 1.13%.

Unreconciled, both printed: this site has carried the week’s moves as S&P −0.8%, Dow −1.6%, Nasdaq −0.7%. A week-ahead summary gives S&P −0.6%, Nasdaq −0.7%, Dow −426 points. Only the Nasdaq agrees; neither set has been retired or averaged. Separately, a week-ahead piece read this run describes the S&P 500 as having been on track for its deepest four-day decline since June before Friday reversed it, with WTI above $100 and the 10-year closing in on 5% — which is the same story from the other end.

Movers & Drivers

Newgainer

Oracle — up about 7%, and the backlog number behind it

The catalyst is now sourced rather than described as “sentiment.” Oracle’s quarterly results put remaining performance obligations at $664 billion, up from $638 billion the prior quarter, after more than $30 billion of new AI cloud contracts closed in the period. Revenue was a record $19.3 billion with cloud infrastructure revenue up 121% year over year. CFO Hilary Maxson said on the earnings call that the newest contracts were signed via prepayment or bring-your-own-hardware arrangements that do not require incremental capital from Oracle. CoreWeave and Nebius each climbed about 4% alongside it. Two notes: the coverage carrying these figures is dated 10–11 September 2026, and this site has carried Friday’s move as +7.5% against the 7% in this run’s headline — both are printed. An EPS and total-revenue-growth pair also circulated but traced to a filing page dated to the previous year, so neither is printed here.

Newgainer

Refiners — a whole group at 52-week highs

Valero, Phillips 66, PBF Energy, Marathon Petroleum, HF Sinclair and Delek US all hit 52-week highs on Friday. Worth separating from the index story: crude fell about 3% on the day, and refining margins are not the same trade as crude. No individual percentage moves were stated in what was read, so none are printed.

Breadth, with a caveat: coverage read across this weekend says both “ten of the 11 S&P sectors were higher” and “nine of eleven finished higher”. At industry level, Printed Circuit Boards (+4.96%) and Wholesale-Computers (+4.56%) topped the leaderboard, consistent with the HPE and Dell moves rather than a separate story.
