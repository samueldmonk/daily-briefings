Top Story

Metabase zero-day: CVSS-10 SQL injection grants admin access without authentication — Framework among the victims

Metabase has warned that a maximum-severity flaw in its business-intelligence software has been exploited in the wild as a zero-day. The vulnerability (CVSS 10.0, tracked as GHSA-vwf4-m7j8-wcjf; no CVE assigned) lets an unauthenticated remote attacker inject arbitrary SQL into the Metabase application database and gain administrator access — from there, attackers can change configuration, steal stored credentials for connected databases, read any reachable data and export it.

Metabase said its Cloud service "was attacked by someone utilizing an unknown ('0-day') security vulnerability in versions 1.58 and above"; Cloud instances have been updated and self-hosted users should patch immediately (fixed builds x.58.24, x.59.21, x.60.17, x.61.11, x.62.9 and x.63.5). Until patched, block the /api/session/reset_password endpoint. The IoC pattern to hunt: a POST /api/session/reset_password returning 400, followed by a GET /api/user/current returning 200 — "it is likely that your instance has been compromised," CEO Sameer Al-Sakran said.

PC maker Framework told customers that names, login IPs, addresses, phone numbers and emails were accessed in a breach tied to the flaw — no order or payment information was taken (Engadget via THN). Help Net Security reports Tally and Kilo Code were also affected.

Patch Priority — federal deadline TODAY

CVE-2026-8037 — Progress Kemp LoadMaster (CVSS 9.6, vendor)

CISA added this pre-auth OS command-injection RCE to the KEV catalog on Aug 7 after 792 reported exploitation attempts; under BOD 26-04 the federal remediation deadline is today, Monday Aug 10. Move exposed appliances to GA 7.2.63.2 / LTSF 7.2.54.18 — the fixes Progress shipped in June. Exploitation attempts have been observed since June 29 (eSentire).

Not in the KEV (no CVE) but just as urgent: patch or fence off Metabase per the top story above.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Storm-1175 — from Medusa affiliate to StormEncryptor operator

Microsoft says the China-linked, financially motivated group Storm-1175 is deploying a previously undocumented ransomware strain, StormEncryptor, in a campaign that began Aug 2 — a shift from its previous use of Medusa ransomware. The C++ encryptor appends .encrypted to files and drops a ransom note named !!!README_FIRST!!!.txt in every scanned directory. Initial access likely comes via CVE-2026-18577 in N-able N-central — the RMM console used by MSPs, where one compromised server can gateway into dozens of downstream businesses. Microsoft has previously clocked the group going from initial access to full encryption in under 24 hours, after exploiting flaws in Mirth Connect, ConnectWise ScreenConnect, JetBrains TeamCity and Fortinet FortiClient EMS (THN, BleepingComputer).

Iran-linked attacks on U.S. water utilities keep spreading

Hackers targeted municipal water systems in seven states in a single week, the FBI says (NBC News), with SecurityWeek reporting ICS attacks now touching at least a dozen states. Attackers hit internet-exposed PLCs; the FBI and EPA have warned utilities nationwide. Water systems are reported operating safely, and the U.S. government has not formally attributed the activity, which intelligence agencies assess as likely Iranian (Time, CNN).

OpenAI pauses internal activities around its Astra model

OpenAI said preliminary evaluations of its upcoming Astra model show cyber and agentic-coding performance strong enough that it cannot rule out a "Critical" capability level — triggering isolated testing environments, restricted network and tool access, enhanced weight protections and sandboxed execution (THN).

Research

Passkey attacks bypass phishing-resistant MFA

New research shows attacks abusing Windows logs, Google Password Manager and Windows Hello can recover synced private keys or bypass phishing-resistant MFA — without breaking the FIDO2 protocol itself. A related THN report details how Google Password Manager attacks could let malware hijack passkey-protected accounts (THN).

NewSupply chain

Head Mare trojanizes TrueConf installers with PhantomCore

Kaspersky researchers say the hacktivist group Head Mare is exploiting flaws in unpatched TrueConf video-conferencing servers — connecting without authentication over TCP port 4307, planting a web shell, and replacing the server-hosted client installer with an unsigned version carrying the PhantomCore backdoor. A second implant, PhantomGraph, hides its C2 traffic inside Microsoft OneDrive. Victims are Russian organizations across instrumentation, electronics, transport, energy, IT and software development (The Hacker News, BleepingComputer).
