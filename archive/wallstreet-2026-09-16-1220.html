The Lead

A rate decision hours away, oil off the boil and a bond market at 2007 highs — as of ~11:46 a.m. ET

Wall Street carried modest gains into midday with the Federal Reserve due to announce its decision at 2 p.m. ET. The freshest reading carrying its own timestamp is Yahoo Finance’s 11:46 a.m. ET update: the S&P 500 up 0.2%, the Nasdaq Composite up 0.4% and the Dow dipping below the flat line. A later Yahoo quote-board read returned this run — with no minute stamp published — had the S&P 500 +0.39%, the Nasdaq +0.82% and the Dow −0.05%. TheStreet’s live blog was headlined “Dow edges higher ahead of Fed decision” at its 11:10 a.m. ET update, with stocks “climbing” but no index percentages attached.

At the 9:32 a.m. ET opening bell TheStreet had the S&P 500 +0.25%, the Dow +0.15%, the Nasdaq +0.44% and the Russell 2000 −0.76% — the same small-cap weakness that left that index at a three-month closing low on Tuesday.

Futures put the odds of a quarter-point hike at 92.5% on the CME FedWatch Tool in TheStreet’s 11:10 a.m. blog and 92.9% in Yahoo Finance’s 11:08 a.m. read — each printed with its own timestamp rather than averaged. A hike would be the Fed’s first since 2023 and would lift the target range from 3.50%–3.75% to 3.75%–4.00%.

Tuesday’s session was the warning shot. TheStreet Pro’s James “Rev Shark” DePorre described “a steady drip of selling all day, with abysmal breadth of just 32% positive. New 12-month lows expanded to over 350 names against 100 new highs.” On today’s vote he added: “A hike paired with reassuring language could actually calm the bond market. A hike with hawkish or vague commentary could push yields higher and take stocks down with them. We will find out at 2 p.m.”

Oil, which drove Tuesday’s slide, eased: West Texas Intermediate fell 1.80% to $103.90 and Brent 0.90% to $107.80 after Reuters reported Saudi Arabia is offering extra crude loadings to Asian refiners by ship-to-ship transfer off Oman’s Sohar port, following drone damage to its Red Sea pipeline.

On the Radar

  • Today, 2 p.m. ET — the FOMC decision, with the Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot, followed by Chair Kevin Warsh’s press conference. A quarter-point move would be the first hike since 2023.
  • Later today — an updated Atlanta Fed GDPNow estimate for third-quarter growth. The most recent reading was 4.4% annualised, against an average analyst estimate still below 3%.
  • Thursday, 17 September — August housing starts and building permits.
  • Friday, 18 September — Bank of Japan rate decision, August industrial production, and the Conference Board’s August leading indicators.
  • Context for the vote — July’s FOMC voted 9–3 to pause. Schwab notes today may be the last chance to hike at a regularly scheduled meeting until December, because October’s meeting falls just before the mid-term elections.
  • Off the tape — the Congressional Budget Office said the Iran conflict had cost the United States more than $38 billion through 1 August and could cost another $2–3 billion a month for as long as it continues. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen proposed opening the door for Canada to become the EU’s first “associate member,” NPR reported. Bloomberg reported OpenAI is in early talks on a funding round valuing it above $1.2 trillion ahead of an IPO.
