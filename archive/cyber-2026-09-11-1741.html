Top Story

GitLab CVE-2026-85706 — CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated file read, probed in the wild a day after disclosure

A path-traversal flaw in the repository commits API of GitLab Community and Enterprise Edition lets an unauthenticated user read arbitrary files off the server. GitLab assigned it CVSS 10.0 — the maximum. The root cause is improper path confinement combined with missing authentication enforcement on the endpoint.

Affected builds are 18.7 through 19.1.7, 19.2 before 19.2.6 and 19.3 before 19.3.2. GitLab shipped fixes on 10 September in 19.1.8, 19.2.6 and 19.3.2.

Exploitation followed within a day. watchTowr reports behavioural probes against its global honeypot network, meaning attackers have already reverse-engineered and reproduced the bug; CyberScoop describes internet-wide scanning. There is no CISA remediation deadline attached to this CVE — it is not in the KEV catalogue as of the reads taken this run — but self-hosted GitLab servers reachable from the internet should be treated as a same-day patch.

⚑ Patch Priority — Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center, CVE-2026-20079 (CVSS 10.0) — federal deadline tomorrow, 12 September

The authentication-bypass flaw in Cisco Secure FMC is the most urgent item with a hard date attached. It sits in the CISA KEV batch whose remediation deadline for federal civilian agencies is Saturday 12 September 2026 — one day from today, alongside Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway (CVE-2026-19490) and Fortinet FortiOS (CVE-2025-25249). Cisco Talos reports that two recently patched Secure FMC flaws have been exploited by three separate threat clusters, tied to both ransomware operations and state-sponsored activity — which is why this batch, not the louder GitLab bug, holds the priority slot. GitLab's CVSS 10.0 carries no federal deadline but is under active probing, so treat it as the same tier of urgency operationally.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Newextortionhardcoded credentials

FulcrumSec — the "Hardcoded Horrorshow"

The cyber-extortion group has built a campaign, which it named itself, around a single technique: finding hardcoded credentials left in public-facing IT infrastructure and using them to walk in. Named victims include Novo Nordisk, England's Manchester Airports Group, London consultancy Arup Group and Singapore's Global Schools Group. FulcrumSec began extorting Novo Nordisk in June and later released more than 1 terabyte of stolen data after the Danish drugmaker refused to pay; reporting this week ties that intrusion to stolen GitHub access tokens. The transferable point is the same one the group keeps proving: a credential committed to a repository or shipped in client-side code is a key left in the door, and its exposure window starts the day the code went out.

extortiondata theft

ShinyHunters — the Metabase campaign

The extortion group added Metabase to its dark-web leak site on 11 August, and the fallout is still landing a month later. Mathspace's stolen data came out of a self-hosted Metabase instance; separately, ShinyHunters has been linked to the intrusion at hardware-wallet maker Trezor, whose affected-customer count grew from roughly 14,000 to 81,000 in a matter of weeks, and to the AdaptHealth attack. The group's model is exfiltration and extortion rather than encryption.

Breaches & Incidents

Neweducationsql injection

Mathspace: 1,079,819 students, staff and parents

The online maths-learning platform disclosed a breach affecting 1,079,819 students, teachers, staff and parents or guardians across Australia and New Zealand. Attackers compromised its self-hosted Metabase internal reporting system using CVE-2026-72898, a CVSS 10.0 SQL-injection flaw patched on 6 August; the intrusion was discovered roughly three weeks later. Taken: names, user IDs, usernames, email addresses, verification status, time zone, country and join/login dates.

phishingsupply chain

Trezor: 347,000 addresses targeted, 2,500 clicked

Phishing against Trezor customers reached 347,000 email addresses, and 2,500 users clicked the embedded malicious link. The campaign has been tied to ShinyHunters, and the count of affected customers has climbed from about 14,000 to 81,000 as the investigation has widened.

healthcarethird party

Veradigm: vendor API credentials, patient SSNs

The health-tech firm formerly known as Allscripts told the SEC that an attacker took credentials from a vendor's environment for a Veradigm customer-services API and used them to copy patient data including Social Security numbers. Clinical and medical data were not affected, and the credentials reached no other part of the environment. The Gentlemen claimed the intrusion on 5 September, alleges 3.5 million patient records and threatened to leak today.

exposuretravel

220M+ passenger and crew records exposed

An Advance Passenger Information System database was left exposed, spanning January 2017 to April 2026 and including passport and travel-document numbers. Found by Kinryū Labs on 3 June and disclosed 8 September; the IP traces to Viettel, Vietnam's state-owned telco. Two misconfigurations were involved, one a cloud path accepting default credentials.

Newransomwaregovernment

Spain's national weather agency hit by Panzer ransomware

Agencia Estatal de Meteorología (AEMET), the Spanish state meteorological agency, was listed as a Panzer ransomware victim in an incident surfaced on 11 September. Two other victims appeared in the same window: a US medical department store, also surfaced 11 September, and CO-OP URBAN BANK LTD in India, claimed by the Global Secret Group on 10 September. No record counts, ransom demands or organisational confirmations appeared in the reads taken this run, and none is supplied here.

healthcareshinyhunters

AdaptHealth: 4.1 million people

The home-medical-equipment provider confirmed that data on 4.1 million people was exposed in a cyberattack discovered in July, attributed to the ShinyHunters threat group.

Newavailabilitypatch tuesday

September Windows Server updates break Remote Desktop Services

Session hosts on Windows Server 2019, 2022 and 2025 begin freezing hours after boot, leaving RDP connections stuck at "Connecting…" and preventing clean logoff. Three cumulative updates are implicated: KB5122876 (2019), KB5122882 (2022) and KB5122871 (2025). Microsoft says it is investigating. Rolling back restores stability but strips the security fixes shipped in the same package — including two actively exploited zero-days.
