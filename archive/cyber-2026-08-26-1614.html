Top story

Boston Scientific’s 8-K: a cyber incident that has reached the ability to ship customer orders, worldwide

The filing itself is now available, and it is more specific than yesterday’s secondhand reports. In a Form 8-K filed with the SEC under Item 8.01 and fetched in full this run, Boston Scientific states that “On August 25, 2026, Boston Scientific Corporation identified a cybersecurity incident affecting certain of its information technology systems that has resulted in a global disruption to the Company’s operations.” The medical device maker is one of the largest in the world, and the disclosure is unusual in that it leads with operational impact rather than data exposure.

The operational language is the part that matters. The company says the incident “has caused, and is expected to continue to cause, disruptions and limitations of access to certain of the Company’s information systems and business applications that support aspects of the Company’s operations, including the ability to process and ship customer orders.” On restoration: “While the Company is working diligently to restore affected functions and systems access, the timeline for a full restoration is not yet known.” On response: it “activated its incident response protocols and began an investigation with the assistance of third-party cybersecurity experts to assess and to contain the threat.” And on materiality, the filing is explicit that the company “has not yet determined whether the incident is reasonably likely to have a material impact.” The 8-K is signed by Susan Thompson, Vice President, Chief Corporate Counsel and Assistant Secretary.

The market read it immediately. Boston Scientific stock fell 3.2% in premarket trading as of Investing.com’s wire published at 7:10 a.m. ET; later reads surfaced this run put the premarket decline at 3.5% to 4% and at 5.8%. ⚠ Those later figures carry no timestamp on the pages that returned them, so all three are published as successive snapshots of a moving premarket rather than reconciled into one number. That is the rare case where a security event and a market event are the same event, and both desks on this site are carrying it this morning.

What is still not known matters as much as what is. Nothing in the 8-K names a threat actor, a ransomware family, a CVE, an intrusion vector, or any patient-safety or device-integrity consequence, and the filing does not characterise the incident as a data breach or state that any data was exfiltrated. None of those things is asserted here. The company says its own investigation is ongoing and that “the full scope, nature and impacts, including operational and financial impacts, of the incident are not yet known.”

The defensive read-through, stated conservatively. Healthcare and medical-device suppliers sit upstream of hospital operations, so a disruption of this kind propagates as a third-party availability problem for customers before it becomes anything else. If Boston Scientific is in your supply chain, the useful work today is confirming your own contingency for delayed shipments and degraded vendor support — not speculating about the intrusion vector.

● Carried from the 11:05 edition — CVE-2026-15981 (CVSS 9.8) and CVE-2026-61979 (CVSS 8.1), Xecurify miniOrange SAML 2.0 Single Sign On for WordPress. The Hacker News, under Ravie Lakshmanan’s byline dated August 25, 2026, reports that bad actors are actively attempting to exploit both flaws. They are unauthenticated authentication bypasses that let an attacker sign in as any WordPress user, including administrators. CVE-2026-15981 exists because mo_saml_validate_signature() performs “a loose boolean check on the raw tri-state integer returned by PHP’s openssl_verify()”, so an error return value of −1 is evaluated as truthy and therefore treated as a successful signature verification — a deliberately malformed signature bypasses verification entirely and wp_set_auth_cookie() is called for the targeted account. CVE-2026-61979 is a privilege escalation stemming from signature algorithm confusion. Disclosed by Patchstack, which credited the DigitalOcean security team after DigitalOcean spotted an anomalous WordPress administrator session attempt from outside its trusted network. Fixed in version 17.0.5 (61979) and 17.0.6 (15981) for the Standard edition. Patchstack recorded scanning from 207.211.214.41, 79.127.224.14, 102.91.71.83, 162.243.116.148, 84.201.6.54 and 64.225.25.188 and judged the spread to suggest “opportunistic scanning rather than a targeted campaign” — whoever is running it is “throwing the exploit at every site with the plugin installed without checking which edition or version is behind it.” Proof-of-concept code is public and chains the two flaws to admin. ⚠ Neither CVE is in KEV, so neither carries a federal deadline — and ⚠ a separate headline read this run described both as “CVSS 9.8”; the CVE.org-sourced figures above give 9.8 only to 15981 and 8.1 to 61979, and those are what this page publishes.

CVE-2026-21962 — Oracle HTTP Server and Oracle WebLogic Server Proxy Plug-in — CVSS 10.0. An unauthenticated attacker with network access over HTTP can compromise the affected components, leading to unauthorized creation, deletion or modification of critical data, or complete access to all data those components can reach. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on August 24 citing evidence of active exploitation, with a remediation date of August 27 — that is tomorrow, on the tightest window the agency assigns. Affected versions are 12.2.1.4.0, 14.1.1.0.0 and 14.1.2.0.0; the fix has been available since Oracle’s January 20, 2026 critical patch update. If you run an internet-facing WebLogic proxy component or an Oracle HTTP Server front end, this is today’s work, not tomorrow’s.

And the flaw with no clock at all. GitLab CVE-2026-19478 (CVSS 9.4) came under active exploitation within days of disclosure, per watchTowr, which reproduced it within minutes of the advisory. It lets an unauthenticated attacker modify or delete publicly accessible GitLab projects and rewrite their data through a GraphQL directive — no credentials, no user interaction. It is not in KEV, so it carries no federal deadline and will not appear on the countdown below; that absence is precisely why it is named here. Affected: 18.2 before 18.11.11, 19.0 before 19.0.8, 19.1 before 19.1.6, 19.2 before 19.2.4. Fixed in 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8 and 18.11.11.

Carried · 8:46 edition — Gitea CVE-2026-60004, due Friday. CISA added the critical Gitea code-injection flaw to KEV on Tuesday, August 25, confirming exploitation in the wild; Help Net Security reports that CISA has ordered US federal civilian agencies to remediate by August 28, 2026. The bug lets an attacker abuse Gitea’s diffpatch endpoint to install and execute a Git hook from repository-controlled content. Cruciferra: a loader that kills 145 security processes with a signed driver, and never downloads a file

F5 Labs’ bulletin dated today describes a chain worth reading in full because almost every step is designed to sidestep a control defenders rely on. From late July 2026, actors began using the ErrTraffic malware-as-a-service platform, operated by an actor tracked as LenAI, to run ClickFix social-engineering campaigns delivering the Cruciferra loader. It starts on compromised WordPress sites, where an obfuscated JavaScript implant queries a Polygon smart contract to resolve live command-and-control domains — C2 that cannot be taken down by seizing a domain — and then serves fake Google reCAPTCHA, Cloudflare Turnstile or Windows Blue Screen of Death pages. The lure gets the user to copy a malicious PowerShell command to the clipboard and run it by hand, which means no download ever happens and download controls never fire.

After execution, Cruciferra uses DLL side-loading through a malicious mscoree.dll and process hollowing to inject the Remus information stealer into ServiceModelReg.exe. Then the part that matters most: a Bring Your Own Vulnerable Driver attack. It exploits a UAC bypass, drops the signed-but-vulnerable MocoMsys driver DCRCVDrv.sys into C:\Windows\Temp\, and registers it as a service. That driver exposes an IOCTL that lets the malware terminate 145 antivirus and EDR processes — F5 names Microsoft Defender, CrowdStrike Falcon and SentinelOne among them. Victim countries listed: India, South Korea and the United States. The practical defences F5 gives are unglamorous and effective: enable the Microsoft vulnerable-driver blocklist (the “Block vulnerable signed drivers” ASR rule or a WDAC policy), alert on any new .sys file written to C:\Windows\Temp\ followed by service creation (Event ID 7045 or 4697), hunt for powershell.exe whose parent is a browser and whose command line contains -enc, -encodedcommand, Get-Clipboard or FromBase64String, and watch for outbound traffic to blockchain RPC infrastructure. ⚠ The blocked hashes and domains are in the F5 bulletin; they are not reproduced here, and defenders should take them from the source.

Carried · 8:18 editionPhishing-as-a-serviceMirage2FA

Mirage2FA has been quietly eating Microsoft 365 sessions for two years

Thousands of companies have been affected by the Mirage2FA campaign from 2024 to 2026, according to ANY.RUN research reported by The Hacker News on August 25. It is a commercial phishing-as-a-service toolkit that targets Microsoft 365 accounts by abusing legitimate login flows and bypassing two-factor authentication — the headline count is 4,500 US and EU companies.

The number that should change your incident model is 48%. ANY.RUN assesses that 48% of targeted email addresses were potentially compromised. Because the toolkit steals passwords and session cookies, an attacker lands inside an already-authenticated Microsoft 365 session — and, with it, SSO-connected services. That is the part 2FA does not save you from: the second factor was satisfied, by the victim, on the attacker’s behalf. What follows is impersonation, fraud and onward compromise from a mailbox that colleagues and counterparties trust.

Geography: the United States accounts for 63.7% of the total, and most affected companies are US-based. This item was carried on this page as search synthesis for two days and has been promoted to a fetched primary this run — the standing note that it lacked one is now retired.

Carried from the 12:58 editionPhishing-as-a-serviceVoice AI

A rented AI voice agent called 200 phone-theft victims pretending to be “Alice from Apple Support”

Researchers at SOCRadar have documented AnonyMousKIT, a phishing-as-a-service platform built for one specific job: harvesting the Apple ID credentials needed to strip Activation Lock from stolen iPhones. It has been active since early 2024 and sits inside a structured resale ecosystem — 506 connected domains feeding 168 storefront brands acting as resellers, alongside access to iCloud backups and Keychain credentials. Delivery is a credit-based mix of phishing email, SMS, WhatsApp, recorded calls and AI-powered voice agents.

The call records are the striking part. Researchers recovered 200 calls placed to victims between August 31, 2025 and May 30, 2026, with 55 distinct interaction transcripts handled by a voice AI agent operating under five personas — all five carrying the same translated identity, “Alice from Apple Support,” across English, Spanish and Portuguese. 179 of the 200 calls went to numbers in Brazil. In the recovered transcript the agent asks the victim to confirm ownership, requests the four- or six-digit passcode and reads the digits back to confirm them, then explains that someone visited an Apple Store to remove the Activation Lock and asks whether a recovery link has arrived by text.

The defensive point is not technical. There is no CVE, no patch and no federal deadline here — the control that fails is a human one, and the attacker’s marginal cost of a fluent, patient, trilingual support call has collapsed. ⚠ No dollar figure, no victim-loss total and no arrest or takedown is stated in the reporting read this run.

Attackers are trying to log in as your WordPress administrator

Bad actors are attempting to exploit two severe unauthenticated authentication bypasses in the Xecurify miniOrange SAML 2.0 Single Sign On plugin that make it possible to sign in as any WordPress user, including administrators, per Patchstack. CVE-2026-15981 (CVSS 9.8) is an authentication bypass stemming from accepting malformed signatures as valid, fixed in 17.0.6 for the Standard edition; CVE-2026-61979 (CVSS 8.1) is an unauthenticated privilege escalation from signature-algorithm confusion, fixed in 17.0.5.

The root cause is a textbook one: mo_saml_validate_signature() performs a loose boolean check on the raw tri-state integer returned by PHP’s openssl_verify(), so the error return value of −1 evaluates as truthy and a failed signature check is treated as a successful one.

CarriedMalwareNovel tradecraft

E4del and PINHOLE read their orders out of FTP welcome banners

Researchers have flagged a campaign that uses FTP banners as dead drop resolvers to deliver two previously unreported remote access trojans, E4del and PINHOLE. An FTP banner is the welcome text a server sends the moment a client connects; the mechanism lets “malware stagers fetch commands directly from the protocol’s initial response,” SOCRadar said. Abusing legitimate services as dead drops is well established — doing it over FTP is, per the report, the first time this technique has been spotted in the wild.

It is also, as the report notes, less stealthy than web-based dead drops, because security controls are likely to flag FTP connections. That is the detection opportunity: ordinary endpoints have essentially no reason to speak FTP to the internet. No CVE, no CVSS and no KEV entry attaches — this is malware tradecraft, not a product flaw.
