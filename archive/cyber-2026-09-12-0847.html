Top Story

Three perfect scores, three federal clocks — one runs out today, two already have

CISA added four vulnerabilities to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on 9 September and set the federal remediation deadline for today, 12 September. The headline entry is CVE-2026-20079, an authentication bypass in Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center carrying a CVSS 10.0. Alongside it: CVE-2026-19490, an authentication bypass in Citrix NetScaler that Citrix scores at 9.3 — the vendor figure, not the 9.8 that circulated in blog coverage — and CVE-2025-25249, a heap-based buffer overflow across multiple Fortinet products at 7.3.

Two other clocks have already expired. CVE-2026-75650 in Adobe Commerce and Magento, also a 10.0, was due 11 September and is now overdue by one day. Worse, the pair of SonicWall SMA1000 zero-days added on 2 September — CVE-2026-83548, a pre-authentication SSRF at CVSS 10.0, chainable with CVE-2026-83549, an OS command injection in the Appliance Management Console, to reach unauthenticated remote code execution — were due 5 September and are overdue by a week.

One point of process worth restating, because it has caused errors before: the directive governing these deadlines is BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk”, and the windows it produces are measured in days, not the three weeks of the older BOD 22-01 regime. Take the due date CISA publishes; do not derive it from a rule of thumb.

Patch Priority — deadline is today

CVE-2026-20079 — Cisco Secure Firewall Management Center, CVSS 10.0, authentication bypass. Added to the KEV catalogue on 9 September with a federal remediation deadline of today, 12 September — 0 days left. Apply Cisco’s fixed release now. If you cannot, the same 12 September deadline covers the Citrix NetScaler and Fortinet entries added in the same batch, so treat all three as one job.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ExtortionSaaS-native

ShinyHunters — and the alliance around it

ShinyHunters is a financially motivated data-extortion crew that has moved almost entirely off malware and onto enterprise SaaS. Its current playbook is voice phishing an employee into approving a malicious connected app or handing over SSO credentials, then pulling records wholesale out of Salesforce, Snowflake and Okta-fronted platforms — no ransomware, no encryption, just exfiltration and a demand. Researchers describe an operating alliance with Scattered Spider, which supplies initial access, and LAPSUS$, coordinated over shared Telegram channels and a common leak site. In one August intrusion at a healthcare organisation the group claimed more than 200 million records in a four-day window and issued a $55 million ransom demand. The AdaptHealth breach below is attributed to the same group.

Breaches & Incidents

NewCVSS 10.0Education

Mathspace — 1,079,819 people, via a self-hosted Metabase

The Australian maths-learning platform disclosed that attackers stole personal information on 1,079,819 students, teachers, staff and parents across Australia and New Zealand from a self-hosted Metabase reporting instance. The vector was CVE-2026-72898, an SQL injection scored 10.0 and patched on 6 August after being exploited as a zero-day. Unauthorised access began 10 August; data was downloaded from the Australian reporting database on 27 August. Mathspace upgraded Metabase on 29 August but did not complete the compromise checks Metabase recommended alongside the update. Taken: names, user IDs, usernames, email addresses and verification status, time zone, country, join date and last-login dates. Not taken: academic records, results, password hashes, authentication tokens, SSO or API credentials.

The home medical-equipment provider confirmed that the data of 4.1 million people was exposed in a cyberattack discovered in July. The intrusion is attributed to the ShinyHunters extortion group profiled above.

RansomwareGovernment

Rhysida — the Berlin leak, now measured

Berlin’s state government refused the gang’s demand of 30 bitcoin, about €2 million, and let the 4 September deadline pass. Rhysida published 5.26 terabytes across 1,439,893 files; it claims to have taken roughly 5.7 TB. The dump includes personnel files, absence lists, payroll data and home addresses of state workers; plaintext passwords and vulnerability assessments for the city’s water utilities; and a folder titled “AG CBRN-Rahmenplanung” holding state emergency plans for terrorist attacks and other disaster scenarios. Counted so far: 16,389 email addresses, 11,963 phone numbers and personal data on 12,076 individuals. Forensics traced the first internally detected outflow to 7 August, continuing to roughly 12 August; affected departments were not physically disconnected from the network until 14 August.

NewActively exploitedNetwork edge

“MikroTrick” — full admin on any RouterOS box with SSH exposed

Researchers confirmed active exploitation of a two-stage chain against MikroTik RouterOS, dubbed MikroTrick, combining CVE-2026-67276 with CVE-2026-86060 to give an unauthenticated attacker full administrative control of any device with SSH reachable from the internet. CVE-2026-86060 is an argument-handling flaw in the SSH login path: a username beginning with a prohibited character alters the trusted RouterOS policy mask. CISA catalogued CVE-2026-86060 and CVE-2026-67277 on 10 September with a 13 September deadline. Fixed builds — RouterOS 6.49.21 (long-term), 7.23.4 (long-term) and 7.24.2 (stable) — shipped on 3 September.
