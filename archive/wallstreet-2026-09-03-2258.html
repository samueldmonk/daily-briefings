The Lead

Stocks post their best day in a month as yields retreat and rate-hike fear drains out of the tape

Every major index finished more than a percent higher on Thursday. The S&P 500 rose 1.06% to 7,747.71. The Dow Jones Industrial Average added 624.16 points, or 1.18%, to 53,686.11 — its best day since August 4. The Nasdaq Composite gained 1.4% to 26,584.06. Yahoo Finance's headline on the session read "Dow, S&P 500 post best day in a month."

The driver was the bond market rather than anything corporate. The 10-year Treasury yield eased, the dollar fell, and the effect was to drain out of the tape the fear that the Fed will hike at its September meeting — a risk that has hung over the last several sessions and that Chair Kevin Warsh's emphasis on stubborn inflation had kept alive. Governor Waller's comments pointed the other way.

The gains were broad but the leadership was software and AI infrastructure: Snowflake closed up more than 17% on a blowout quarter, Dell rose for a second day, and Tesla ran into its Cybercab launch. The exceptions were instructive — Broadcom fell despite beating, on a soft fiscal fourth-quarter forecast, and Tyson Foods was cut down by its own guidance.

These are official closing figures for Thursday, September 3, corroborated on four independent fetches. The arithmetic checks: 53,686.11 − 624.16 = 53,061.95, exactly Wednesday's verified Dow close.

Movers & Drivers

NewDown

Tyson Foods slashes its year on beef

The food company tumbled nearly 7% after cutting full-year guidance, citing pressure in beef. Adjusted operating income is now expected between $1.85 billion and $2.05 billion, down from a prior $2.1 billion to $2.3 billion.

UpSoftware

Snowflake's blowout quarter

Shares closed 17.43% higher on better-than-expected second-quarter earnings and revenue plus strong guidance — described as the session's standout move among large caps. Two other readings of the same move circulated, "more than 16%" and "more than 20%"; all three are printed and none adopted.

UpRobotaxi

Tesla runs into the Cybercab

Tesla climbed ahead of its evening launch event, reaching levels last traded in mid-July. Two readings printed, neither adopted: "more than 7%" and "6% to $378.75". The event itself delivered the $30,000 Cybercab, rolling out in Austin; Tesla has said production started in April with limited initial output, and the next step is turning those cars into a scalable commercial service. Morgan Stanley holds a Hold rating and a $400 target.

DownSemis

Broadcom beats and falls anyway

Shares dropped nearly 3% following quarterly results, undone by a disappointing revenue forecast for the fiscal fourth quarter.

UpHardware

Dell rises for a second day

The maker of laptops and servers climbed 5% in midday trading, a second consecutive advance after quarterly results topped estimates and the company lifted its fiscal 2027 forecast.

UpAI

Meta on a model release

The Instagram parent rose 3% following the rollout of its artificial intelligence model Muse Spark 1.3.

Shares tanked on a glum forecast for the current quarter: earnings of 93 to 98 cents per share on revenue of $2.29 to $2.32 billion, against analyst expectations of $2.40 and $2.53 billion. An earlier roundup on the same session put the drop at "about 15%"; both are printed and neither adopted.

The cloud security company's fourth-quarter results beat, with revenue roughly 2.4% ahead and adjusted EPS about 9% ahead. Earlier in the evening this name carried three irreconcilable readings and no price was published; the +2% figure here comes from a named after-hours roundup and is not merged with those.

After-hours prices come from post-close roundups, not closing prices, and trade on thin liquidity — they frequently do not survive the next open. Tesla is reported to have shown little reaction to the Cybercab event itself, despite the run into it.

On the Radar

  • The Fed's September meeting. Thursday's decline in bond yields was read as easing the risk of a rate hike at the upcoming meeting — the directional question that has been driving the tape all week.
  • Tesla's Cybercab, from launch to service. The $30,000 vehicle is rolling out in Austin. Production started in April with limited output; the open question is scaling it into a commercial service. Some analysts model an all-in build cost near $18,000, against more than $100,000 per vehicle for competing services.
  • Software guidance is doing the damage, not the quarters. Guidewire, Asana and Lululemon all beat or met and fell anyway on forward guidance — the pattern to watch as the rest of the September reporters land.
