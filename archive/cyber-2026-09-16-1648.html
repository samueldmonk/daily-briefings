Top Story

Boston Scientific says it is unlikely to meet its 2026 guidance after an August cyberattack

Boston Scientific, the Massachusetts medical-device maker, says it is unlikely to meet its previously issued third-quarter and full-year 2026 sales and adjusted-profit forecasts because of a cyberattack. In July the company had guided to 2026 adjusted earnings of $3.28 to $3.32 per share and reported sales growth of 5.5% to 6.5%. Those targets are now described as unlikely to be achieved.

The company first identified unauthorised activity on some of its information-technology systems on 25 August. The resulting network outage cut access to operating systems and business applications, disrupting manufacturing and the processing and shipment of customer orders globally. Boston Scientific says the incident is likely to have a material impact on both third-quarter and full-year results.

Recovery is under way: the company says it has made progress restoring affected systems and that major distribution centres are now processing and shipping orders. It expects to recover a portion of the lost revenue as it ramps operations and works down backlogs, but says the full financial impact remains uncertain. Stifel analyst Rick Wise said investors increasingly view 2026 as a “lost year” for the company and are likely to focus on its 2027 prospects. An update is promised with third-quarter results on 28 October.

No threat group appears to have claimed responsibility, and none is named here. The reason this story belongs at the top of a security page rather than a markets page is the mechanism: the loss is not a ransom payment or a regulatory fine but weeks of interrupted manufacturing and shipping — the operational-technology tail that most breach accounting still misses.

Patch Priority

Cisco Secure Email Gateway — CVE-2026-76461 — federal deadline 17 September (1 day left)

An unauthenticated SQL injection (CWE-89) in the email-parsing path of Cisco AsyncOS for Secure Email Gateway, CVSS 9.8, reachable by sending a crafted email containing malicious SQL through an affected gateway and leading to operating-system command execution with root privileges. Cisco disclosed it on 14 September after its PSIRT became aware of exploitation, and CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog the same day — a zero-day exploited before disclosure. Federal civilian agencies must remediate by 17 September 2026. There is no workaround; fixed builds are 15.5.5-014, 16.0.4-302 and 16.5.0-780. Patch first, then assume any exposed appliance may already have been reached and triage accordingly.

Threat Actor Spotlight

GENESIS — extortion without encryption

GENESIS emerged in October 2025 with a coordinated batch of nine simultaneous attack claims posted to its Tor v3 data-leak site. Tracking data puts it at more than 60 claims between October 2025 and late April 2026, with the tempo accelerating through Q1 2026 — on at least two posting dates it claimed more victims than any other tracked actor — and a running total somewhere in the 90-to-115 range as of early September 2026, concentrated in professional services, manufacturing and healthcare in the United States. Claimed breach volumes run from 200 GB to 2 TB per target. What separates it from a conventional ransomware crew is what it does not do: the group operates a data-exfiltration-only model with a structured extortion timeline and a centralised posting cadence, and no encryption capability has been substantiated. Among its listings is Interim HealthCare, posted on 10 August 2026 with a claim of roughly 1 TB of exfiltrated data including medical and clinical records; a second ransomware group has separately claimed the same victim.

Provenance: these are leak-site tracking figures compiled by threat-intelligence trackers, not victim confirmations, and the victim total is published as a range because the sources give one. Claims posted to a leak site are assertions by the actor until a victim confirms them.

IDScan.net — FBI confirms it is looking into the ID-image leak

The identity-verification firm posted a security notification saying that around 1 September an unauthorised third party may have accessed or copied customer information, potentially including full names and driver’s licence or other government-issued ID numbers. ITRC president James Lee: “We’ve been told that there’s in excess of 150 million images of driver’s license, of state IDs, of medical dispensary cards.” The number of people affected, and which states are involved, are both still unknown.

Since May · Microsoft Security Blog

Passkey-themed social engineering into Microsoft cloud accounts

Attackers pose as IT helpdesk staff and call or text a user’s personal phone, manufacturing urgency around a passkey, MFA or SSO configuration that must be updated “immediately”, then steer the target to a fraudulent sign-in page or a device-code authorisation flow. Microsoft has seen unusual sign-ins followed by attacker-added authentication methods, high-volume Graph activity, SharePoint and OneDrive downloads and mailbox collection via REST APIs, and attributes the initial access to a range of actors including Storm-3121 and Storm-3032.
