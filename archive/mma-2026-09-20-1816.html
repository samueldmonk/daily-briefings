Top story

Van keeps the flyweight belt — and on the same broadcast, Gane is handed the heavyweight title without throwing a punch

Joshua Van retained the UFC flyweight championship at UFC 331 on Saturday 19 September at Crypto.com Arena in Los Angeles, handling Alexandre Pantoja’s grappling and picking him apart standing for a unanimous decision. It was the promotion’s sixth visit to Los Angeles and its first since UFC 227 in August 2018.

The other title news happened on the broadcast, not in the cage. Ciryl Gane was announced as elevated from interim to undisputed heavyweight champion; Tom Aspinall vacated the belt while recovering from the eye poke he suffered against Gane at UFC 321. Gane did not compete that night.

What it sets up. Gane’s first defence is against Josh Hokit at UFC 334, Saturday 14 November at Madison Square Garden, with Kayla Harrison vs Amanda Nunes for the women’s bantamweight title as co-main — Harrison’s first defence, in a bout the promotion is billing as its “most anticipated women’s fight ever”. Hokit is 10-0 as a professional and 4-0 in the UFC and the No. 4 contender, with stoppages of Derrick Lewis and Denzel Freeman and a decision over Curtis Blaydes at UFC 327.

One inconsistency, named rather than smoothed. A reference page for UFC 334 still carries the interim notation against Gane’s name, while a current-champions listing read this run gives him a title-win date of 19 September. Neither is quite right: he was announced as champion that night and did not fight. The champions board below records it that way.

Bonuses & payouts

  • Fight of the Night: Joshua Van and Alexandre Pantoja, $100,000 each — re-verified this run.
  • Performance of the Night: Arman Tsarukyan, $100,000, for the elbow knockout of Ruffy as the first round was drawing to a close; and Casey O’Neill, $100,000, for the first-round armbar on Eduarda Moura.
  • A “Proper Chaos” bonus went to Sean Sharaf for the 12-second knockout of Gable Steveson — the third fastest in UFC heavyweight history. Itemised: base $60,000 ($30,000 to show, $30,000 to win), a $50,000 promotional bonus that Dana White said he would personally match with another $50,000, plus a $25,000 finish bonus — $185,000 total.
  • Gable Steveson earned $160,000 for the 12-second loss — a larger base than the winner’s.
  • Top payouts: Alexandre Pantoja $650,000, the largest disclosed, and Joshua Van $500,000, on a card payroll of roughly $4.6 million. Over $500,000 in bonus money was awarded in total.
  • A figure that disagrees with itself. Sharaf’s reported debt is given as around $600,000 in one source and $688,000 in another. Both are printed; neither is adopted.

Around the sport

  • The heavyweight title changed hands without a fight. Aspinall vacating and Gane being elevated means the division’s belt moved on a broadcast announcement — and goes straight into a defence against an undefeated 10-0 challenger in November.
  • The women’s flyweight belt is the only vacant title in the promotion, and it is filled on 3 October at UFC 332.
  • Hokit drew criticism from Dana White for disparaging Michelle Obama in a post-fight interview on the South Lawn.
  • Frank Silva of Fighting Nerds withdrew from Contender Series week 5 with visa issues; Christian Natividad stepped in as a late replacement.
  • Charles Oliveira’s withdrawal from a planned UFC 331 bout with Tsarukyan came days after the death of his longtime teammate, UFC flyweight Allan Nascimento; no official reason was given.

Rankings & business

  • Rankings movement. Josh Hokit is the No. 4 heavyweight contender heading into a title shot. No other ranking change was stated in any source read this run.
  • Broadcast. Paramount is in the first year of a seven-year, $7.7 billion media-rights deal with the UFC, which is owned by TKO Group. UFC 332 on 3 October is the first numbered-event main card to air on CBS.
  • The viewership benchmark. UFC Freedom 250 drew 17 million viewers on Paramount+ across the US and Latin America and 34 million globally once Australia, China, India, South Korea, New Zealand and the UK were counted. The US average of 7.0 million made it the most-watched UFC event ever domestically.
  • Social. Across that fight week (8–14 June), UFC accounts recorded 126 billion total views, 5 billion engagements and 536,000 new followers.
