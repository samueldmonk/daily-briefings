The Lead

As of ~11:34 AM ET: S&P 500 and Dow off 0.39%, Nasdaq down 0.62% as surging oil and Treasury yields near 24-year highs weigh; Chipotle jumps on Starbucks takeover report

New Late morning (Yahoo Finance, 11:34 AM ET). As of 11:34 AM ET, Yahoo Finance had the Dow Jones Industrial Average at 50,981.86, down 198.01 points or 0.39% — consistent to the cent with Wednesday’s 51,179.87 close — with the S&P 500 also down 0.39% and the Nasdaq Composite off 0.62% (Yahoo Finance live blog, as rendered in search results). Yahoo’s headline: “S&P 500, Nasdaq fall as oil rises, reviving inflation worries,” with the market “caught in a tug-of-war between earnings optimism and revived inflation concerns.” TheStreet’s live blog (updated 11:33 AM ET) still leads with stocks “falling, as a surge in oil prices, spiking Treasury yields and hawkish signals from the Federal Reserve weighed on risk appetite” and added no newer index read.

Late morning (Yahoo Finance, 11:05 AM ET; TheStreet, 11:01 AM ET). The Dow Jones Industrial Average stood at 51,020.61 at 11:05 AM, down 159.26 points or 0.31% (Yahoo Finance live blog) — consistent to the cent with Wednesday’s 51,179.87 close. Yahoo’s live blog headline now reads “S&P 500, Nasdaq fall as oil rises, reviving inflation worries,” and TheStreet retitled its 11:01 AM live blog “Nasdaq, Russell 2000 suffer on higher oil, Treasurys,” with Fed minutes seen as reinforcing the central bank’s hawkish bias (TheStreet). No timed S&P 500 or Nasdaq percentage after the open was verified this edition.

Late morning (TheStreet, 10:55 AM ET). “Stocks were falling, as a surge in oil prices, spiking Treasury yields and hawkish signals from the Federal Reserve weighed on risk appetite.” Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn said markets “have turned more defensive this morning as renewed Middle East tensions, rising oil prices and persistently elevated bond yields put pressure on risk appetite,” adding that the 10-year yield “continues to trade around 5.3%.” At 10:06 AM the 10-year was up 5.32 basis points at 5.35%, closing in on its highest level since 2002, and the 30-year was at 5.69%. Chipotle (CMG) jumped 6.21% after the Financial Times reported Starbucks (SBUX, −2.91%) has explored a takeover of the burrito chain; Chipotle’s market value is nearly $39 billion vs. about $107 billion for Starbucks, per LSEG (TheStreet). TheStreet’s live blog had no index read after the open; the live quotes above show the real-time picture (TheStreet).

At the open (Reuters and TheStreet, 9:34 AM ET). “US stocks opened lower on Thursday as oil prices surged and Treasury yields hovered near multi-year highs, stoking inflation worries ahead of an earnings season that will test whether corporate profits can sustain the momentum,” Reuters reported: the Dow fell 0.35%, the S&P 500 0.37% and the Nasdaq Composite 0.49% at the opening bell (Reuters via Investing.com). TheStreet’s 9:34 AM read had the S&P 500 down 0.34%, the Dow 0.25%, the Nasdaq 0.47% and the Russell 2000 1.31%. Weekly initial jobless claims slipped 2,000 to a seasonally adjusted 197,000 for the week ended Oct. 3, below the 200,000 Reuters poll, holding near 57-year lows for a fourth straight week in what economists call a “low-hire, low-fire” labor market (TheStreet, citing Reuters, 9:41 AM).

Pre-market (Schwab, 9:13 AM ET; Yahoo Finance, 7:30 AM ET). Schwab said signs of escalation in the Middle East sent oil about 5% higher, while yields got a boost from a Fed governor’s statement that multiple rate increases might be needed to tame inflation within “an acceptable period of time”; Yahoo notes Fed Governor Chris Waller is speaking in Turkey today (Schwab; Yahoo Finance). Yahoo had Dow futures down 0.9%, S&P 500 futures 0.5% and Nasdaq-100 futures 0.7% as “a global bond rout rolled on,” with the 10-year at 5.28% and the 30-year at 5.66%. Earnings season gets its appetizer from PepsiCo; FactSet estimates S&P 500 third-quarter earnings grew 29.5%, which would be a third straight quarter above 25% (Yahoo Finance). Oil’s jump came on fresh tanker attacks in the Persian Gulf, a drop in transit through the Strait of Hormuz, reports of potential U.S. strikes on Iran and Gulf Coast production shut-ins ahead of an approaching storm: at 7:18 AM WTI was up 4.80% at $92.52 and Brent 4.89% at $105.10. President Trump said late Wednesday he no longer wants a deal with Iran, as NBC News and Axios reported the U.S. is weighing renewed large-scale strikes (TheStreet, citing CNBC).

Wednesday, Oct. 7 — the session in review

Official close (4 PM ET). The Dow Jones Industrial Average lost 341.41 points, or 0.66%, the S&P 500 shed 0.22% a day after its record close and the Nasdaq Composite slipped 0.22% (levels in the Weekly Scorecard). CNBC said pressure kept building in the bond market, pushing the 10-year to 5.365%, its highest since April 2002, and the 30-year to 5.732%, its highest since May 2002, and that bank stocks dropped on fears higher rates would hinder lending (CNBC; Dow close corroborated by Brownfield Ag News). Seven of 11 S&P 500 sectors lost ground; yields retreated after the $39 billion 10-year auction found strong demand; health care was one of the few gainers, up 1%, with Moderna, Eli Lilly, Amgen and Intuitive Surgical all up more than 2% (Schwab).

Fed minutes (Investing.com, 2:00 PM ET). “Most U.S. policymakers see another interest rate hike by the end of this year,” the minutes of the Sept. 15–16 FOMC meeting showed; at that meeting the Fed unanimously raised the federal funds rate 25 basis points to 3.75%–4.00%, its first hike in over three years (Investing.com). Schwab adds that “some participants” took a more hawkish view, arguing higher rates were needed to tamp down emerging demand-driven inflation, and that futures priced a 17% chance of a hike at the Oct. 27–28 meeting on Wednesday afternoon, down from 19% earlier in the day, per CME FedWatch (Schwab).

Movers & Drivers

Haemonetics (HAE)

Chipotle Mexican Grill (CMG)

Jumped in morning trading after the Financial Times reported Starbucks has worked with advisers in recent months on a takeover proposal; a deal would deepen Starbucks CEO Brian Niccol’s ties to Chipotle, where he built his reputation. Starbucks (SBUX) fell 2.91% (TheStreet, 10:55 AM).

+4.73%

Skydance (SKYD)

Rose to $9.31 in its third day of trading (later +5%) after co-CEO David Ellison told CNBC the combined Paramount–Warner Bros. company is “positioned to win in every single vertical that we operate in,” citing more than 200 million global streaming subscribers across HBO Max and Paramount+ (TheStreet, 10:17 & 10:41 AM).

−4%

Eli Lilly (LLY)

Dropped on short-term profit-taking after a sharp rally, valuation sensitivity and net-price erosion risk from expanding GLP-1 coverage. Skyworks (SWKS) lost 4.22% in a broad chip selloff and Applied Optoelectronics (AAOI) 5.22% on dilution worries tied to a $600 million at-the-market offering (TheStreet, 10:41 AM).

+1.92%

PepsiCo (PEP)

Rose to $126.10 early after a third-quarter beat: revenue grew 5.6% to $25.27 billion vs. roughly $25 billion expected and adjusted EPS of $2.34 topped $2.29 (Bloomberg consensus); tariff refunds totaled $178 million. It now sees fiscal 2026 net revenue growth of about 6%, the high end of its range, but cut its core EPS growth outlook below the low end of its prior 5%–7% range (Yahoo Finance; TheStreet, 8:04 AM).

+15.35% pre

Wolfspeed (WOLF)

Extended Wednesday’s after-hours surge before the bell after announcing a conditional financing commitment of up to $1.5 billion from the U.S. Defense Department for domestic silicon-carbide production; Equifax (EFX) rose 4.46% premarket, while Lumentum (LITE) fell 3.88% and Allegro MicroSystems (ALGM) 2.51% (TheStreet, 8:53 AM).

Palantir (PLTR)

Rose more than 2% premarket after Goldman Sachs upgraded it to buy from neutral. Broadcom (AVGO) fell more than 1% after The Wall Street Journal reported it is trying to borrow more than $50 billion tied to custom AI chips it is developing with OpenAI, and TSMC (TSM) slipped more than 1% despite reporting a sharp jump in third-quarter revenue (Schwab, 9:13 AM).

−0.50% pre

Goldman Sachs raised its target to $230 from $220 ahead of the Nov. 5 report. Separately, Jeff Bezos told Fox News that Blue Origin “will have an IPO” several years from now (TheStreet, 7:09 AM; Yahoo Finance).

−20%
