The lead

As of ~1:00 PM ET the Nasdaq’s loss has narrowed a second time, to about 1.0%–1.3% — but the chips are still where the damage is, with Intel down about 5%

The regular session is three and a half hours old as this edition is written, and the technology-led selling has now eased for a second consecutive check. As of roughly 1:00 PM ET the Nasdaq Composite was down 1.04%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average down 0.16% and the Russell 2000 down 0.91%, with the S&P 500 off about 0.6%; Yahoo Finance’s live Tuesday coverage renders the same session as Nasdaq −1.3%, Dow −0.2%, S&P 500 −0.6%, so a range is given for the Nasdaq rather than one figure picked. The Nasdaq’s loss widened at every check through the morning — roughly 1.2% at 10:30, about 1.34% at 10:55, about 1.6% at 11:36 — then turned at midday and has narrowed again, to roughly 1.0%–1.3%; the Dow has all but closed its gap to unchanged. Earlier in the session the moves read Nasdaq −1.2%, S&P 500 −0.6%, Dow −0.3% on Yahoo Finance’s and TheStreet’s live Tuesday coverage; every read is given, each stamped with its time. That extends Monday’s losses and puts the S&P 500 on course for a third straight down day.

What is doing the damage is now precisely locatable: it is memory and storage. Western Digital fell about 7%, to $500.05 — the move the reporting keeps returning to — with SanDisk lower alongside it (rendered at 6% in one headline and 8% in the body of the same report, so both are given), Marvell Technology and Seagate Technology each down about 8% and Micron down about 5%. The framing from the reporting is macro, not company-specific: rising Treasury yields are pressuring what had become the most extended trade in the semiconductor sector. The reversal is sharp — on Monday Western Digital gained 6.5% as the memory-storage complex rallied on SanDisk’s investor day and its ninth-generation QLC flash. Wider out, the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell roughly 3.7% in early trade, with Nvidia, Meta and other large technology names weakening — a sector-level move nearly three times the Nasdaq’s own. An earlier intraday snapshot this run had the Nasdaq 100 down 1.58% and the Russell 2000 down 0.68% while the Dow had pared to −0.07% and the S&P 500 sat at −0.53% — the index carrying the least technology is the index taking the least damage. The Dow's leaders were Johnson & Johnson (+2.29%), IBM (+1.43%) and Chevron (+1.41%); its laggards were Caterpillar (−2.86%), Nvidia (−1.90%) and Home Depot (−1.24%). Defensives, an industrial-conglomerate bid and energy are absorbing what the semiconductors are giving up.

The cause has not changed since the pre-open. President Trump rejected extending the 60-day temporary ceasefire with Iran, which expired Monday, absent a broader peace agreement; a senior Iranian official responded by threatening that Tehran would shift to a "fully offensive" military posture if diplomacy fails. Transit data underlines the risk: only five commodity vessels crossed the Strait of Hormuz on Saturday and none on Sunday, against 31 the previous weekend, after attacks on tankers.

The long end is where it was being paid for — and this hour it stepped back. Yahoo Finance’s live coverage has the 10-year Treasury yield easing in morning trading but still elevated at 4.70%, and the 30-year edging down to 5.30% after hitting a 19-year high. That is the first easing of the session, and it has not yet stopped the chip selling. Yields on 30-year government bonds climbed again worldwide before that, and the US 30-year reached a 19-year high in the process. CNBC has it topping 5.31% — 5.311%, up more than four basis points, the highest since June 2007 — and Bloomberg’s live coverage puts it around 5.32%, up about two basis points on Tuesday. CNBC followed up this morning with a piece on three things that could drive it higher still. Charles Schwab's market-open note — the edition served at this URL when it was fetched this run is dated August 17 and carries the 10-year at 4.71%, up two basis points — describes an early rally attempt that flattened as yields and oil pushed higher. Technology carried the damage — rising long yields hit long-duration equities hardest, and the memory names were marked down as a group. The fear gauge moved with it: one live-market read this run had the VIX up 3.83% on the session.

This morning's data was ugly, and it landed before the bell. July housing starts, released at 8:30 AM ET, fell to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 1.239 million against the 1.350 million expected — down 12.4% from June's revised 1.415 million and 13.5% below July 2025's 1.432 million. Single-family starts dropped 9.9% to 808,000. Permits went the other way: 1.443 million, up 5.0% on the month and 3.1% on the year, single-family permits up 2.5% to 894,000, five-units-or-more at 490,000. Planned construction is holding up; actual building is not.

Earnings are the offset. Home Depot reported before the open and beat on both lines while reaffirming full-year guidance, and there is a heavy retail week still to come — Schwab's week-ahead calendar puts Analog Devices, TJX, Lowe's, Target and the FOMC minutes on Wednesday and Walmart, Alibaba, Deere, NetEase and Ross on Thursday. Ed Yardeni's Fed Stock Valuation Model still reads the index as marginally cheap: a forward P/E of 19.9 against the 21.4 reciprocal of the bond yield, with fair value at 8,300 on last week's 4.68% ten-year.

The index recovered a little; inside the chips it got worse

The two things are happening at once, and that is the point. Yahoo Finance’s live Tuesday coverage has the Nasdaq Composite down about 1.3%, the Dow about 0.2% and the S&P 500 about 0.6% — an index-level picture that has improved twice since 11:36 AM — while noting that within technology chip stocks took a leg lower, with Nvidia falling 2% and Intel dropping 5%. Intel is the newly-named casualty here; it had not appeared in any earlier read of the session, and its move is roughly five times the Nasdaq’s own. Read alongside the memory names already down all day, the pattern is not a broad de-rating but a narrowing one: the money leaving semiconductors is not dragging the rest of the tape with it, and the recovery in the headline indices is being paid for by an increasingly concentrated markdown of long-duration chip risk.

Reddit (RDDT) — into the S&P 500 today

S&P Dow Jones Indices added Reddit to the S&P 500 effective before the opening of trading today, Tuesday, August 18, replacing AvalonBay Communities (AVB), which is being acquired by fellow index member Equity Residential. CNBC's movers list has the stock up 12% on the news; Reddit becomes only the second pure-play social media company in the index, after Meta. JPMorgan estimated passive funds would need to buy roughly 16.7 million shares, close to three times average daily volume.

The long end backed off — and the chips kept falling anyway

The session’s driver reversed without the session following it. Yahoo Finance’s live Tuesday coverage has the 10-year Treasury yield easing in morning trading, though still elevated at 4.70%, and the 30-year edging down to 5.30% after hitting a 19-year high — the first relief at the long end since the selling began. Yet as of roughly 11:36 AM ET the Nasdaq’s loss had widened to about 1.6%. If rising yields were the whole explanation for the technology markdown, this hour should have stabilised it. It did not, which argues the AI-valuation nervousness cited alongside the yield move is doing independent work.

WDC −7%

The memory trade cracked — and Western Digital is the day’s worst print

Western Digital is cratering about 7%, to $500.05 — the name the session is being reported through, though not the largest fall on the board. It is far from alone: Marvell Technology and Seagate Technology are each down around 8%, SanDisk is sharply lower (one report renders the move at 6% in its headline and 8% in its body, so both are given) and Micron is off about 5%. The stated cause is macro rather than company-specific — rising Treasury yields are pressuring what had become the most extended trade in the semiconductor sector, a trade built on AI-server demand and supply-chain policy expectations. What makes it striking is the turn: on Monday Western Digital rose 6.5% as the memory-storage group rallied on SanDisk’s investor day and its ninth-generation QLC flash announcement. One day of higher long yields has taken back more than the good news gave.

SOX −3.7%

The chip index, not the Nasdaq, is the real move

The Philadelphia semiconductor index fell roughly 3.7% in early trade, against the Nasdaq Composite’s own decline of about 1%–1.34% — the sector is falling roughly three times as fast as the index it dominates. Nvidia, Meta and other large technology names weakened together. This is the cleanest read on the session: it is not a broad de-rating of equities but a concentrated markdown of long-duration semiconductor risk as the long end of the curve prints a 19-year high, with the rest of the market largely absorbing it.

A live-market summary fetched this run has Micron Technology down 5.4% and Nvidia down about 2% in the session, against pre-market reads from CNBC of Micron, SK Hynix and Sandisk each off more than 4% — that is a single-source intraday figure for Micron and is labelled as such. Reversed to −1.24%

Home Depot (HD) — beat the number, lost the session

The pre-market pop did not survive the open. Home Depot gained about 1.5% before the bell on the results below, but by mid-morning it had turned over and sat among the Dow's three biggest laggards at −1.24% — a clean illustration of a market that is not paying for an in-line retail beat while the long end of the curve is at a 2007 high. The quarter itself was good: fiscal Q2 results topped expectations on both lines, adjusted EPS of $4.92 against $4.73 expected, revenue of $47.86 billion against $47.27 billion forecast. Comparable sales rose 1.7% and US comparable sales 1.3%. Full-year guidance was reaffirmed — comparable sales flat to up 2%, adjusted diluted EPS flat to up 4.0% from fiscal 2025's $14.69, or $14.69 to $15.28.

Fabrinet (FN) — the steepest fall before the bell

Fabrinet dropped more than 9% pre-market — CNBC's own copy renders the move at 8.6% in one place and “more than 9%” in another, so both are given here — the largest decline on its morning list.
