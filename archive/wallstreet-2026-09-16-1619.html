The Lead

The Fed hikes, Warsh talks, and the tape turns — Dow closes down 630 points

The Federal Reserve raised the overnight funds rate by a quarter percentage point at 2:00 p.m. ET, lifting the target range to 3.75%–4.00% from 3.50%–3.75%. CNBC calls it the first hike since July 2023, and it is the first policy change of Chair Kevin Warsh’s tenure. The decision was unanimous.

The projections did the damage. TheStreet reports the central bank’s own picture “seems to augur further tightening ahead as officials forecast one additional rate hike in 2026 before holding steady in 2027.” All three major averages were higher at a point during the session; they turned during the press conference, where Warsh said: “This summer’s inflation readings do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved.”

At the bell, CNBC has the Dow down 630 points, or 1.2%, with financial-related shares leading the way lower; the S&P 500 down 0.5%; and the Nasdaq Composite near flat. CNBC’s own headline — “Dow falls 700 points” — reflects the deeper late-afternoon trough rather than the closing print. Going in, CME FedWatch had a quarter-point move at 92.5%, per TheStreet.

No source read this run stated the closing index levels themselves, so none are published here; the Weekly Scorecard carries levels only through Tuesday’s verified closes.
