The Lead

Official close: S&P 500 −0.02%, Dow −0.31%, Nasdaq +0.01% after a whipsaw day of yield and oil swings

After a couple of sudden reversals, stocks ended Thursday roughly where they began. The S&P 500 fell 1.90 points (−0.02%), the Dow Jones Industrial Average dropped 161.61 points (−0.31%) — its third straight decline — and the Nasdaq Composite rose 3.34 points (+0.01%) (AP). That is a slightly weaker finish than the preliminary quote data carried in the 4:06 PM edition, which had the S&P 500 and Nasdaq about 0.1% higher; this edition replaces those figures with the official close. The day opened lower The day opened sharply lower —mdash; S&P 500 −0.51%, Dow −0.32%, Nasdaq −0.78% (TheStreet) — before stocks came off their lows around midday on a Reuters report that U.S. and Iranian negotiators in New York are discussing a phased deal under which Iran would reopen the Strait of Hormuz and the U.S. would lift its economic blockade (CNBC).

Bonds set the tone again: the 10-year Treasury yield jumped to 5.20% from 5.11% late Wednesday, back to where it was in 2007 (AP), and TheStreet’s read heading into the close had the 30-year at 5.475%, its highest since 2004. Yields tracked scattershot oil prices: AP says the most actively traded Brent contract swung from $102 to roughly $99 within minutes at midday before settling at $100.22, up 2.1%, while CNBC reported Brent up 2.5% at $105.69 after a $108.23 session high hit when Iran-allied Houthi militants fired missiles at Saudi Arabia. A drop in weekly jobless claims added to the case that the economy can withstand more Fed hikes; CME data show better than a coin flip’s chance of two more increases by year-end (AP). Nvidia slipped 0.4% and was the heaviest weight on the S&P 500 (AP).

After-Hours Movers

New · ~+1%

Costco (COST)

Fiscal fourth-quarter net income rose to $2.998 billion, or $6.75 a share, from $5.87 a year earlier, on net sales up 11.2% to $93.9 billion; total-company comparable sales rose 9.4% (6.7% excluding gasoline and currency) and digitally enabled comps 19.5% (Costco release). The quarter included a non-recurring benefit of $0.15 a share from IEEPA tariff refunds (Costco). Analysts had expected about $6.53 a share (24/7 Wall St.), and the shares were reported up about 1% at $904.89 in after-hours trading, reversing the day’s loss.

On the Radar

  • Friday: Costco’s first full session after its Q4 beat ($6.75 EPS vs. ~$6.53 expected); its earnings call was scheduled for 2:00 PM PT Thursday (Costco release).
  • Rate path: CME data show better than a coin flip’s chance of two more Fed hikes by year-end, after last week’s first increase in three years (AP).
  • Trump–Xi follow-through: the leaders met in Washington Thursday; the trade truce was extended by two months past Nov. 10, and the two sides discussed a notification system for AI incidents that could affect national security (Motley Fool). Several top U.S. tech CEOs were set to join them for dinner (Yahoo Finance).
  • Iran: a senior adviser to Iran’s supreme leader warned Iranian forces and Houthi allies could open “a new front” targeting Red Sea energy supplies if the U.S. attacks again (CBS News via TheStreet) — the backdrop to the reported Hormuz talks.
  • Starbucks: plans to close about 250 underperforming North American cafés, roughly 1% of its footprint, in its second round of closures under CEO Brian Niccol (CNBC via TheStreet).
