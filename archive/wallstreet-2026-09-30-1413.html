The data (8:30 AM ET). The Fed’s preferred inflation gauge cooled more than expected in August. The PCE price index rose 0.3% on the month and 3.4% from a year earlier; core PCE, which excludes food and energy, rose 0.2% on the month and 3.0% from a year earlier (Commerce Department via Fox Business and CNBC/TheStreet). Economists polled by LSEG had expected 0.4% and 3.7% headline and 0.3% and 3.3% core (Fox Business); Dow Jones’ survey had 0.3% and 3.7% headline (CNBC via TheStreet). Inflation remains well above the Fed’s 2% target (Fox Business). Separately, ADP said private employers added 90,000 jobs in September, up from a downwardly revised 36,000 in August and above the Dow Jones consensus of 68,000; base pay rose 3.2% from a year ago (CNBC via TheStreet, 8:50 AM). After the data, CNBC’s live blog was headlined “Dow futures gain 100 points after data shows inflation slowed”; a search summary of its coverage said the report sent Treasury yields lower. Earlier, TheStreet’s 8:19 AM update had Formula One Group (FWONK) up 8.51%, Alliant Energy (LNT) 7.58% and Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) 4.34% premarket, with Concentrix down 9.24%, Moderna 6.94% after Citi downgraded it to Sell, and Northrop Grumman 3.66% after losing a $20 billion Navy fighter contract to Boeing. On Iran, the UK Maritime Trade Operations Centre said unknown projectiles struck three ships in the Strait of Hormuz on Tuesday, and Iranian media said Tehran received a U.S. response Wednesday to its proposed seven-day ceasefire plan (TheStreet, citing UKMTO and CBS News). Sources: Fox Business (PCE, 8:35 AM) · TheStreet live blog (8:43 / 8:50 AM) · CNBC live blog

Before the open, Wednesday Sept. 30. Stock futures were mixed as Wall Street awaited a key inflation indicator after Treasury yields advanced to fresh multidecade highs (TheStreet, 7:40 AM). Tuesday recap. Official close, Tuesday Sept. 29: the S&P 500 fell 12.85 points, or 0.16%, to 7,670.84; the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 131.59 points, or 0.26%, to 51,349.92; and the Nasdaq Composite fell 22.84 points, or 0.09%, to 26,797.54 (AP’s “How major US stock indexes fared”; the same levels appear in a search summary of CNBC’s Sept. 29 coverage). Stocks drifted lower as long-term Treasury yields ticked higher: the 10-year yield rose to 5.25% and touched its highest level in 24 years, while Brent crude fell 1.7% to settle at $96.16 a barrel — still well above the roughly $72 it cost before the U.S. and Israel attacked Iran in late February (AP closing report). Energy stocks had some of the broadest losses, with Exxon Mobil down 1% (AP).

The session opened mixed and faded. At 9:33 AM TheStreet had the S&P 500 up 0.09%, the Dow down 0.08% and the Nasdaq up 0.27% as oil and yields eased and chip stocks bounced; by 11:10 AM the S&P had slipped to what was then its session low, down 0.19%, as losses spread across sectors (TheStreet). Yahoo Finance reported the 30-year yield touched 5.61%, its highest since 2002, with the 10-year at 5.29% late in the morning, and said Treasurys are on track for their worst September since 2023; traders see a 70% chance of a Fed hike in October per CME FedWatch (Yahoo Finance, 11:49 AM). At 3:05 PM AP had the Dow down 119 points, with Nvidia giving up an early gain and Broadcom up 2% (AP).

Two data releases weighed. The Conference Board’s consumer confidence index fell 6.7 points to 81.9 in September from 88.6, its lowest since 2014 and well below the roughly 89 economists expected; the expectations index fell for a third straight month to 63.6 (Conference Board coverage via CNBC and Yahoo Finance). August JOLTS job openings slid to 7.079 million from 7.335 million, below the ~7.23 million expected, with hires about 5.2 million (FXStreet; AP: layoffs fell and fewer people quit). “Americans feel jobs are more scarce and are pulling back on plans for homes, cars, and big-ticket purchases,” said LPL Financial’s Jeffrey Roach (AP). The Russell 2000 was down 0.71% at 1:58 PM with only 507 holdings advancing (TheStreet).

Elsewhere: mediators kept working with the U.S. and Iran on a deal to end the fighting and reopen the Strait of Hormuz, after Trump over the weekend rejected Tehran’s offer (AP). Oura postponed its U.S. IPO, citing market uncertainty (AP; Reuters via TheStreet). OpenAI delayed its GPT-6.1 Astra model over safety concerns, and a Reuters report on Anthropic’s leaked IPO prospectus — targeting a valuation as high as $2 trillion — drew attention ahead of a White House meeting between tech executives and President Trump (TheStreet; Yahoo Finance). Sources: AP via WTOP (closes) · AP closing report (4:28 PM) · TheStreet live blog (Sept 29) · Yahoo Finance (yields, 11:49 AM) · CNBC (consumer confidence) · FXStreet (JOLTS) · Yahoo Finance live (premarket)
