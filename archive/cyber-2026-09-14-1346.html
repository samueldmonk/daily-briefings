Top Story

GitLab’s CVSS 10.0 file-read flaw is being probed in the wild, and the federal clock runs out today

CVE-2026-85706 is a path-traversal bug in GitLab’s repository commits API, caused by improper path confinement combined with missing authentication enforcement. An unauthenticated attacker can read arbitrary files from vulnerable self-managed Community and Enterprise Edition servers.

GitLab shipped fixed versions on 10 September. watchTowr’s Attacker Eye honeypot network observed behavioural probes against the vulnerability from roughly 06:00 UTC on 11 September — evidence that outside actors had already reverse-engineered the weakness and begun testing internet-facing GitLab systems. CISA added the flaw to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog later the same day and instructed Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies to remediate by 14 September 2026, which is today. It also marked the vulnerability as requiring forensic triage under Binding Operational Directive 26-04.

What an attacker can pull matters as much as the patch: reporting this run lists .gitlab_shell_secret, SSH host keys, deploy tokens, CI/CD pipeline variables and database passwords among the files readable by the gitlab user. Patching closes the read; it does not un-disclose anything already taken, so the advice being given is to patch and then rotate what the flaw could have exposed. Affected self-managed versions: 18.7 before 19.1.8, 19.2 before 19.2.6, and 19.3 before 19.3.2.

Patch priority — today
Two deadlines land today, both on flaws confirmed under attack. GitLab CVE-2026-85706 (CVSS 10.0) — upgrade self-managed instances to 19.1.8 / 19.2.6 / 19.3.2, then rotate secrets. ConnectWise ScreenConnect CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9) — on-premises deployments must move to 26.6.5 or higher; cloud deployments were updated automatically. Both carry a federal remediation date of 14 September 2026, matching the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NewExtortionClaim — unconfirmed

TheGentlemen — tracked as Storm-2697

On 9 September 2026 the extortion crew TheGentlemen claimed responsibility for an attack on Air Canada, alleging it exfiltrated more than 51,000 files. That figure is the group’s own claim and has not been confirmed by the airline in anything read this run; it is printed as a claim, not a finding. The group was first observed in September 2025 and is tracked as Storm-2697 by Microsoft and by Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42. It grew into one of the more active extortion brands by offering affiliates a 90% cut of ransoms, and runs negotiation portals with a deliberately faux-polite “gentlemen’s club” persona as a pressure tactic.

Breaches & Incidents

NewAviation

Air Canada listed by TheGentlemen

The ransomware group named Canada’s largest airline on 9 September and claims over 51,000 exfiltrated files. Nothing read this run carries an airline confirmation or an operational-impact statement, so neither is asserted here.

FintechImpersonation

Revolut handed data to an impersonator

Fraudulent requests sent from a legitimate government agency domain carried valid technical domain authentication and were fulfilled by staff as routine legal compliance. Identity documents, facial verification images, statements with IBANs and full transaction history were disclosed for what Revolut calls a very limited group of customers. Extortionists demand 10,000 BTC, reported as more than $782 million.

GovernmentDisputed

Florida DMV: two incompatible accounts

FLHSMV says it learned of the breach on 4 September, that it was quickly mitigated, and that the intruder used compromised credentials belonging to a single Plant City Police Department user. ShinyHunters instead claims a password-reset flaw across multiple DAVID accounts and more than 200,000 driver records — a number the agency has not confirmed. Both accounts are printed; neither is adopted.

Supply chain

Trezor phishing arrived through Trezor’s own mail vendor

Attackers exploited a login weakness at Brevo on 9 September and mailed roughly 347,000 newsletter subscribers. Because the mail went out under Trezor’s own account it cleared email authentication. Trezor killed the domain at DNS about 20 minutes after detection; roughly 2,500 people had already clicked.

Education

Mathspace breached via self-hosted Metabase

Exploitation of CVE-2026-72898, a SQL injection flaw in the self-hosted Metabase tool, exposed names, emails, usernames and locations. Coverage puts the service at over one million people. No CVSS for the CVE appears in anything read this run, and none is printed.
