The Lead

A closed tape, a settled week — and a $50 billion buyout that fell apart on the way out of it

It is Saturday evening and U.S. equity markets are closed. Nothing on this page is an intraday read: the most recent session is Friday, August 28, and its official closes stand unchanged, re-verified a fourteenth time at 6:20 PM, and an eleventh time at 12:35 PM — and this check was the broad one the tenth was not. ⚠ Stated exactly, because the previous edition had to state its own narrowness: the search run this hour returned all three index levels and all three percentage moves together — S&P 500 7,711.76, −0.25%; Nasdaq Composite 26,402.42, −0.52%; Dow Jones Industrial Average 53,559.99, −9.45 points, −0.02% — alongside the weekly figures (S&P +0.5%, Nasdaq +0.9%, Dow +0.5%). Nothing in the numbers changed between the two checks — what changed is which fields the check itself returned, which is the whole of what a verification claim is entitled to assert.

The week still finished green, and for the Dow it was the first one in three. Friday's decline was shallow enough that the S&P 500 notched a winning week anyway, with the Nasdaq's fall driven by semiconductors — Nvidia and Intel were both named as drags. The session's frame was Fed Chair Kevin Warsh's Jackson Hole address, in which he said, in his own words, that “while this summer's PCE and CPI readings were better than expected, they do not tell me that underlying trends have meaningfully improved.”

What that speech was, in reporting fetched at 10:50 AM. Coverage of the address is consistent on the substance and careful about its limits. Warsh said inflation remains higher than the central bank's longstanding 2% goal and suggested the Fed may need to raise interest rates in the coming months; one analysis frames the speech as his answer to critics of a muddled July news conference, delivering the clearer warning that had been missing from it. Another account, equally sourced, stresses what he withheld: he gave no indication of where he thinks rates should be and declined to set out the conditions under which he would advocate a change in policy. Both characterisations are printed, and they are not in conflict — warning about inflation and refusing to pre-commit to a rate path are different acts, and he performed both.

The corporate story of the weekend is unchanged since the 8:19 edition and is repeated here in full because it is still the largest single-name item on the board — it is not new to this edition. The rate pricing turned over in the 9:40 AM edition and gained a third, independent read at 10:50 AM; both are in On the Radar below. Stripe and Advent International have abandoned their pursuit of PayPal, an offer worth more than $50 billion. The bid stood at $60.50 per share, valuing PayPal at more than $53 billion; PayPal's board considered it too low and the company had not sent any formal reply. Block had been part of the original April approach but left the group before Stripe and Advent put the bid forward. A transaction on that scale would have ranked among the largest leveraged buyouts on record. PayPal fell as much as 16% in premarket trading on Friday — that is a premarket figure, explicitly not a closing move, and no Friday closing percentage for PYPL was stated by any source seen this run, so none is printed. Two reads of that move are on the record and neither is adopted: one report describes the fall as “as much as 16% in premarket trading”, another headlines it as a 15% plunge. Reporting sourced in the 9:15 AM edition adds that the board rejected the offer over concerns it undervalued the company, that the two sides had been negotiating a potential higher price, and that a future approach by Advent and Stripe remains possible should conditions shift.

Weekend standing: the next scheduled session is Monday, August 31. The live widgets on this page will show weekend/last-traded values until the tape reopens.

Reporting fetched this run supplies both. Around mid-August, a rate-probability tool was showing roughly 30% odds of a 25bp September hike with the chance of a pause rising to almost 70% — the same pair of numbers this page has been carrying as “one in three” and “nearly 70% holding”, now attached to a period rather than floating. The move down to that level has a cause attached to it as well: an earlier report describes September-hike odds tumbling after a big July jobs miss, and Goldman Sachs chief economist Jan Hatzius told clients that recent declines in retail sales and disappointing employment figures made a September increase “extremely unlikely”, with the firm expecting the target range held at 3.50%–3.75% through the remainder of 2026. ⚠ All of that is pre-Jackson-Hole and is labelled so. The post-speech figures do differ from each other: above 50/50 against the prediction market's 48%. They are close, they come from different sources, and this page adopts neither and averages nothing. The direction is the finding, and it has now survived three independent looks.
