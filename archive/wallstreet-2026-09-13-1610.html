The Lead

A one-day reprieve, and then the Fed

US markets are closed for the weekend. The most recent session is Friday 11 September, and it broke a run: stocks opened higher and stayed there, snapping a four-session losing streak for the S&P 500, the Dow and the Nasdaq alike. The S&P 500 rose 65.28 points, or 0.86%, to 7,656.98; the Dow gained 509.19 points, or 0.98%, to 52,573.29; the Nasdaq composite added 251.31 points, or 0.96%, to 26,333.04.

Two things did the work. August CPI came in broadly as expected — though monthly core inflation rose 0.3% against 0.2% expected, which is not a dovish detail — and oil pulled back sharply after Iranian state media said Tehran would meet Gulf states in Oman to discuss the Strait of Hormuz, with Brent settling around $104.

The reprieve was one day inside a losing week: the indexes finished the week lower, and the bond market did not join the rally — the 10-year yield climbed almost 20 basis points on the week to trade just below 5%. Everything now funnels into Wednesday 16 September, when the FOMC delivers a decision, a fresh Summary of Economic Projections and a dot plot, with futures pricing a hike rather than a cut.

NewDown

The bond market sat the rally out

Equities up and yields up on the same day is a rally the fixed-income market declined to endorse.
