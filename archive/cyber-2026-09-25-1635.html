Top Story

Bitget says suspected North Korean hackers stole $351.6 million from its exchange wallets

Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget disclosed on Friday that attackers stole about $351.6 million from its hot and warm wallets after its security systems flagged multiple unauthorized transfers on Thursday evening. The company has suspended all withdrawals while it investigates with law enforcement, on-chain security institutions and experts from Mandiant and SlowMist. CEO Gracy Chen said the attacker compromised a critical backend system in the wallet infrastructure, used it to spoof transaction data and triggered Bitget’s own authorization process to move the funds out; how the intruders got in remains under investigation.

Chen said the theft spanned the Ethereum, XRP Ledger, Arbitrum, Avalanche, Optimism, BSC and Base chains — with XRP the largest single-chain loss — and that IP behavior and on-chain analysis are “highly consistent with known patterns of North Korean hacker organizations.” Some chains have frozen the attacker’s wallet addresses. Bitget says its cold wallets and its self-custodial Bitget Wallet were not affected, customer balances remain accurate, deposits and trading continue normally, and its User Protection Fund — holding more than $464 million — will cover all losses.

Patch Priority

Four exploited appliance flaws are due today; WSO2 is due Sunday and a newly listed SharePoint flaw Monday

The federal deadline to remediate four actively exploited flaws added to CISA’s KEV catalog on September 22 is today, September 25, 2026 (0 days left): Arista VeloCloud Orchestrator CVE-2026-93952 (CVSS 10.0), F5 BIG-IP APM CVE-2026-94127 (CVSS 9.8) and Check Point CVE-2026-85102 (CVSS 9.8) and CVE-2026-93616. Next is CVE-2026-5430, a flaw in WSO2 API Manager, API Control Plane, Traffic Manager and Universal Gateway — described in WSO2’s advisory as a JWT authentication bypass and in CISA’s KEV listing, per The Hacker News, as a path traversal that can allow unrestricted file upload and remote code execution — that WSO2 rates CVSS 10.0 (9.8 in single-tenant deployments). CISA added it to KEV on September 24 with a deadline of September 27, 2026 (2 days left); watchTowr says it captured forged JWT tokens targeting the flaw on September 13. WSO2’s May 3 advisory lists the fixed update levels (for example API Manager 4.2.0 update level 197 and 4.1.0 update level 257). On September 25 CISA also added CVE-2026-65660, a code-injection flaw in on-premises SharePoint Server 2016, 2019 and Subscription Edition that lets an authorized attacker execute code over the network (CVSS 3.1 8.8 per NVD; Microsoft first published it in August as a 6.5 spoofing bug), with a federal deadline of September 28, 2026 (3 days left) and forensic triage required under BOD 26-04 (HOL, citing the KEV catalog). Fixed builds are the August 2026 updates: 16.0.5565.1001 (2016), 16.0.10417.20198 (2019) and 16.0.19725.20522 (Subscription Edition). MikroTik RouterOS CVE-2026-67279, added the same day, shares the September 28 deadline (BleepingComputer via WindowsForum); fixed in RouterOS 6.49.21, 7.23.4 and 7.24.2. These dates match the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ShinyHunters’ claimed FBI breach remains unconfirmed

The extortion group ShinyHunters claims it breached FBI systems by exploiting a previously unknown Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day, pivoted to AWS GovCloud servers and stole 2 to 3 terabytes of data. It casts the attack as retaliation for a May 2026 FBI FLASH report on the group and has given the FBI one week to correct or remove that report, while claiming the demand is not extortion. The FBI has published a statement acknowledging the claims without confirming data theft. No CVE or Oracle confirmation exists for the alleged new flaw; in June Oracle patched CVE-2026-35273, a critical unauthenticated PeopleSoft PeopleTools RCE that Google and Mandiant later linked to ShinyHunters activity against education-sector targets.

Sources: BleepingComputer · Security Affairs

Breaches & Incidents

Botnet

Carbonato botnet puts an AI agent in charge of hijacked Docker hosts

ThreatDown researchers at Malwarebytes found Carbonato spreading to Docker hosts that expose the API on port 2375 without authentication, launching a privileged container, opening a reverse SSH tunnel and installing the Hermes Agent AI framework, which takes tasks over Telegram such as collecting AI API keys, SSH credentials and access tokens. The evidence spans October 2024 to August 2026.

BleepingComputer

Cloud

Cloudflare fixes cross-tenant data leak in Containers

A flaw reported on September 4 by Oren Yomtov of Accomplish let a paying customer read leftover disk data from other accounts’ deleted containers on the same server; Cloudflare Sandboxes was affected too. Cloudflare finished its cleanup on September 19 and says it found no use of the method beyond authorized testing.
