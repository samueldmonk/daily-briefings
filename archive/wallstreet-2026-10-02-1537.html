G7 reserve release. The Group of Seven agreed Friday to coordinate the release of 100 million barrels of oil and fuel products from emergency reserves, beginning immediately and running over four months, with a substantial amount of diesel released within the first 20 days; the International Energy Agency will coordinate, and the G7 did not say how the total splits between diesel, crude and other products. U.S. diesel hit a record $6.52 a gallon on Sept. 22 and stood at $6.37 on Friday, per AAA (TheStreet, 12:39 PM, citing CNBC).

Tesla beats on deliveries. Tesla delivered 486,532 vehicles in the third quarter, down 2.1% from last year’s record 497,099 but about 25,000 above the company-compiled consensus of 461,974; it produced 464,391 vehicles and deployed 13.7 GWh of energy storage, short of the 15.9 GWh analysts expected (Electrek).

Opening bell. “The U.S. stock market is now open. Stocks were rising in early trading, with the Dow surging 300 points, as Wall Street digested a key jobs report,” TheStreet wrote at 9:36 AM ET: the S&P 500 gained 0.89%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average rose 0.64%, the Nasdaq jumped 1.35% and the Russell 2000 climbed 0.35%. “There is zero chance for a rate hike in October now — in retrospect, September should have been a hold,” said Jamie Cox, managing partner at Harris Financial Group. “Outside of energy, the inflation impulse is lower, and the Fed was zigging when it should have been zagging” (TheStreet).

Schwab’s first look (9:13 AM). “Major indexes jumped early as yields sagged on the news,” Schwab wrote in its market-open update published 9:13 AM ET, with the 10-year Treasury yield at 5.18% (−0.05), the VIX down 4.88% at 15.59 and WTI crude down 3.66% at $89.41 in its table. The government cut its estimates for the prior two reports by a combined 60,000 — August to 133,000 from 162,000 and July to a loss of 10,000 from a gain of 21,000 — and the three-month average is now around 50,000. Soon after the report, CME FedWatch odds of a rate hike later this month fell to just 14%, down from 70% early this week. “It wasn’t all bad, as the household survey showed a gain of 406,000 in September and the participation rate rose,” said Collin Martin of the Schwab Center for Financial Research; “the markets expect the Fed to remain patient here.” Health care was the only area with a significant job increase. Breadth remains weak: only 21% of S&P 500 stocks are above their 50-day moving average (Schwab). The live quotes above stream the current move.

The first verdict, before the bell. Major U.S. stock futures rose after the report: futures tracking the S&P 500 were up 0.9% and those tracking the Nasdaq Composite up 1%, Reuters reported in a story published 8:45 AM ET. Treasury yields fell sharply — the 2-year down 7 basis points to 4.716%, the 10-year down 6 basis points to 5.176% and the 30-year down 4 basis points to 5.569% — and expectations for a rate hike at this month’s Fed meeting fell as low as 12%. The dollar index slipped 0.2% to 101.79 and gold rose 1% to $4,219 (Reuters via Investing.com). “Treasury yields tumbled Friday after the weaker-than-expected jobs report, as investors reassessed the outlook for a Federal Reserve rate hike this month,” TheStreet wrote in its 8:55 AM update (TheStreet).

The jobs report is out — and it missed badly. Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000 in September versus 90,000 expected; unemployment edged up to 4.2% from 4.1%; the participation rate rose to 61.8% from 61.6%; average hourly earnings rose 0.1% on the month (0.3% expected) and 3.0% on the year (3.2% expected); private payrolls rose 46,000 vs. 85,000 expected (investingLive; FXStreet). FXStreet reports August was revised down to 133,000 from 162,000. Just before the release, fed funds pricing showed a 28% chance of a rate hike, with the 2-year yield at 4.78% and the 10-year at 5.23% (investingLive). The dollar index turned lower on the print, last down 0.23% at 101.80 (FXStreet).

Before the open (markets not yet trading). Yahoo Finance’s futures strip, read about 7:20 AM ET, had S&P 500 futures up 0.49%, Dow futures up 0.58%, Nasdaq futures up 0.71% and Russell 2000 futures up 0.68%, with the 10-year yield at 5.24 (−0.06), the VIX at 15.97 and November crude down 3.92% (Yahoo Finance). “Stock futures were rising, with oil prices easing, as Wall Street awaited a key jobs report,” TheStreet wrote at 7:39 AM (TheStreet). Benzinga’s 5:56 AM table had the Dow up 0.49%, the S&P 500 0.46%, the Nasdaq 100 0.78% and the Russell 2000 0.64% (Benzinga).

What was expected. The Dow Jones consensus had called for 84,000 jobs and an unemployment rate steady at 4.1%, after August’s 162,000 (TheStreet). FactSet’s projection is 90,000 (Benzinga); Yahoo Finance’s calendar listed +100,000 expected for nonfarm payrolls, 4.1% unemployment and average hourly earnings +0.3% m/m and +3.2% y/y. Yahoo Finance said it would likely take a big surprise to sway bets on a Fed hike this year: traders have pared October hike bets, but most still see at least one 25-basis-point hike in December. CME FedWatch showed a 23.8% chance of a hike after the October meeting (Benzinga). Capital.com’s Kyle Rodda said a hike “is considered a matter of when and not if,” and that Friday’s tone will be set by war headlines or the payrolls data (TheStreet).

Oil and the Middle East. WTI fell 3.75% to $89.39 and Brent 2.34% to $99.92 on reports that governments are discussing more diesel and crude stockpile releases — EU governments discussed a French proposal to release 50 million barrels of diesel, with IEA members releasing 50 million barrels of crude (TheStreet, citing Reuters). Separately, TheStreet, citing The Independent, reported President Trump has sent 9,000 U.S. troops to the Middle East after warning new strikes on Iran could be coming; the Trump administration also imposed sanctions on Iran’s land transportation sector, including automakers IKCO and SAIPA (Benzinga, citing Reuters).

Most recent close (Thursday, Oct. 1). The S&P 500 rose 0.2% to break a three-day losing streak, while the Dow and Nasdaq Composite each rose less than 0.1% (AP via KRMG); official levels are in the Weekly Scorecard. Benzinga said most S&P 500 sectors closed lower Thursday, with health care, communication services and real estate the biggest losers and energy and industrials higher.
