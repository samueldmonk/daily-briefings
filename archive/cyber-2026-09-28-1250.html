Top Story

CISA orders agencies to patch the exploited Citrix NetScaler zero-days by Wednesday

CISA has given federal civilian agencies until Wednesday, September 30, 2026 to address CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772, two actively exploited flaws in customer-managed Citrix NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway appliances that it added to the KEV catalog on September 27. The deadline binds federal agencies under BOD 26-04; for everyone else Citrix’s advice is to update as soon as possible (WindowsForum, citing BleepingComputer’s report “CISA orders feds to patch exploited Citrix flaws by Wednesday”).

Both flaws can lead to remote code execution and Citrix rates each 9.5 under CVSS v4, saying exploitation of both has been observed on unmitigated deployments. CVE-2026-88771 is an improper-input-validation flaw that can let an unauthenticated remote attacker execute commands, and Citrix says all affected ADC and Gateway deployments meet its precondition, including default installs. CVE-2026-88772 is a memory overflow leading to RCE or denial of service when DTLS is enabled — which it is by default on VPN virtual servers unless set to -dtls OFF. Citrix’s September 27 bulletin CTX697096 covers eight NetScaler flaws in all, but confirms exploitation only of these two.

Fixed builds are 14.1-73.37 and 13.1-64.23 for ADC and Gateway, 14.1-73.37 FIPS, and 13.1-37.279 for 13.1 FIPS and NDcPP; Secure Private Access hybrid deployments using NetScaler instances must also upgrade. CISA advises checking for signs of compromise before patching when possible and preserving forensic evidence first if compromise is suspected, since an update may reduce forensic visibility, and Citrix warns its generic indicators in NetScaler Console may miss real compromises (WindowsForum, citing BleepingComputer and Citrix).

Patch Priority

Exploited SharePoint, MikroTik and WordPress flaws are due today; the Citrix NetScaler pair is due Wednesday

Three exploited flaws hit their federal deadline today, September 28, 2026 (0 days left): CVE-2026-65660, a code-injection flaw in on-premises SharePoint Server 2016, 2019 and Subscription Edition (CVSS 3.1 8.8 per NVD; fixed in the August 2026 builds 16.0.5565.1001, 16.0.10417.20198 and 16.0.19725.20522), with forensic triage required under BOD 26-04 (HOL, citing the KEV catalog); MikroTik RouterOS CVE-2026-67279, fixed in RouterOS 6.49.21, 7.23.4 and 7.24.2 (BleepingComputer via WindowsForum); and WordPress core CVE-2026-87902 (CVSS 9.2), fixed in 7.1.2 and backported point releases (HOL). Next is the most dangerous open item: exploited Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway zero-days CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS v4 9.5), added to KEV September 27 and due Wednesday, September 30, 2026 (2 days left) — upgrade to 14.1-73.37 or 13.1-64.23 (13.1-64.24 if show ns variable lists variables) and hunt for compromise first (WindowsForum, citing CISA). Already overdue: WSO2 CVE-2026-5430 (CVSS 10.0) and Adobe Commerce / Magento CVE-2026-71362, due September 27, and the Arista, F5 and Check Point flaws due September 25. These dates match the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ShinyHunters’ claimed FBI breach remains unconfirmed

The extortion group ShinyHunters claims it breached FBI systems by exploiting a previously unknown Oracle PeopleSoft zero-day, pivoted to AWS GovCloud servers and stole 2 to 3 terabytes of data. It casts the attack as retaliation for a May 2026 FBI FLASH report on the group and has given the FBI one week to correct or remove that report, while claiming the demand is not extortion. The FBI has published a statement acknowledging the claims without confirming data theft. No CVE or Oracle confirmation exists for the alleged new flaw; in June Oracle patched CVE-2026-35273, a critical unauthenticated PeopleSoft PeopleTools RCE that Google and Mandiant later linked to ShinyHunters activity against education-sector targets.

Sources: BleepingComputer · Security Affairs

Breaches & Incidents

NewCrypto theft

Bitget restarts Bitcoin withdrawals and blames a third-party security product

Bitget reopened Bitcoin withdrawals at 08:00 UTC on September 28, the first stage of a phased restart four days after it froze withdrawals; ETH is due September 29, USDT September 30 and remaining tokens, fiat and P2P on October 2. The exchange now says the attacker exploited a vulnerability in a third-party security product it uses to obtain high-level internal credentials, then sent fraudulent withdrawal commands that bypassed risk controls; private keys and cold wallets were not compromised. Losses stand at about $387.5 million; THORChain node operators rejected CEO Gracy Chen’s request to block wallets tied to the stolen funds, and Bitget expects to complete an internal security report this week. Mandiant and SlowMist continue to support the investigation (The Crypto Times). Earlier, AMLBot traced about 4 BTC of the loot into a Wasabi CoinJoin round (The Cryptonomist).

Zero-day

Citrix patches the two exploited NetScaler zero-days — upgrade now

Citrix on Sunday published security bulletin CTX697096, fixing eight NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway flaws and stating that exploitation of CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 “on unmitigated NetScaler deployments has been observed.” CVE-2026-88771 (CVSS v4 9.5) is an improper input validation flaw that lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker execute arbitrary commands on every customer-managed ADC and Gateway deployment, including default configurations. CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS v4 9.5) is a memory overflow leading to RCE or denial of service when DTLS is enabled — which it is by default on VPN virtual servers unless set to -dtls OFF. Fixed builds: 14.1-73.37 and 13.1-64.23 (ADC/Gateway), 14.1-73.37 FIPS, and 13.1-37.279 for 13.1-FIPS/NDcPP. Appliances patched in August for CVE-2026-19490 (builds 14.1-73.32 / 13.1-63.21) remain vulnerable. Admins on 13.1 should run show ns variable first: if it lists variables, Citrix advises going to 13.1-64.24 to avoid a reboot-loop issue. The new builds also stop accepting unsigned SAML assertions. Citrix did not say who is behind the attacks or when exploitation began; it is offering generic indicators of compromise through NetScaler Console, warns they may miss real compromises, and patching does not evict an attacker already inside. Later on Sunday CISA added both flaws to its KEV catalog, with a federal deadline of Wednesday, Sept. 30 (WindowsForum, citing CISA and BleepingComputer). The fixes follow a weekend in which organizations took NetScalers offline on shutdown advice attributed to the Dutch NCSC, after watchTowr said the flaws were being exploited.

Precaution

Kiteworks urged customers to take systems offline for a nine-hour window over a possible attack

Secure file-sharing vendor Kiteworks advised all customers to facilitate a nine-hour precautionary shutdown window last weekend, in their local time zone, after what it called credible threat intelligence from federal intelligence authorities that a threat actor may attempt to target some Kiteworks systems. Self-managed customers (on-premises, AWS or Azure) were told to shut down their own systems; Kiteworks said it would shut down the systems it hosts on their behalf. The company says the advisory is preventative, that it has no indication Kiteworks or customer systems were compromised, and that all known vulnerabilities are addressed in its current release, 9.5.1. Subsidiaries including Zivver, DRACOON, totemo and ownCloud are not affected. Correction: earlier editions, following BleepingComputer, described a six-hour window; Kiteworks' own press release says nine hours.

Botnet

Carbonato botnet puts an AI agent in charge of hijacked Docker hosts

ThreatDown researchers at Malwarebytes found Carbonato spreading to Docker hosts that expose the API on port 2375 without authentication, launching a privileged container, opening a reverse SSH tunnel and installing the Hermes Agent AI framework, which takes tasks over Telegram such as collecting AI API keys, SSH credentials and access tokens. The evidence spans October 2024 to August 2026.

BleepingComputer

Cloud

Cloudflare fixes cross-tenant data leak in Containers

A flaw reported on September 4 by Oren Yomtov of Accomplish let a paying customer read leftover disk data from other accounts’ deleted containers on the same server; Cloudflare Sandboxes was affected too. Cloudflare finished its cleanup on September 19 and says it found no use of the method beyond authorized testing.
