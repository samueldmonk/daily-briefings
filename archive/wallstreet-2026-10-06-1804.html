The Lead

Record close: S&P 500 +0.58% to its first finish above 7,800 as tech, easing yields and the Constellation–Google nuclear deal lift stocks; small caps slip

Closing recap (Anadolu Agency). Anadolu’s wrap matches TheStreet’s closes point for point and says chipmakers helped drive the rally amid continued optimism over AI demand: during the session Marvell Technology climbed around 5.8%, Broadcom gained 3.7% and AMD rose 2.8%. It put the 10-year yield down about three basis points at 5.281%, and said oil settled little changed — Brent +0.26% to $100.58 and WTI +0.01% to $89.44 — with investors now awaiting Wednesday’s minutes of the Fed’s September meeting, at which policymakers raised rates (Anadolu Agency via aNews). Breadth was thin: Benzinga notes only 15 of the S&P 500’s 504 stocks hit their own record highs Tuesday (Benzinga).

Market wrap (TheStreet, 4:03 PM ET). “The S&P 500 set a fresh record, following the Nasdaq Composite higher after it started the week with an all-time close. Treasury yields eased and mega-cap tech remained strong amid optimism around a nuclear deal between Constellation Energy and Google parent Alphabet.” Official closes: S&P 500 +44.98 points (+0.58%), Dow +253.38 (+0.49%), Nasdaq Composite +122.48 (+0.45%) and Russell 2000 −16.17 (−0.57%) — levels are in the Weekly Scorecard (TheStreet). CNBC’s headline: “S&P 500 posts first close above 7,800, boosted by tech gains and cooler yields” (CNBC). The Nasdaq’s finish topped Monday’s record close.

Treasury watch into the close (TheStreet, 3:33 PM ET). Yields moved lower across the curve Tuesday afternoon, with the biggest declines in shorter maturities: the 2-year at 4.791% (−4.2 bp), the 3-year at 4.918% (−4.3 bp), the 5-year at 5.030% (−3.6 bp), the 10-year at 5.275% (−3.6 bp), the 20-year at 5.691% (−1.9 bp) and the 30-year at 5.647% (−1.7 bp), leaving the 2s/10s spread at about 48 bp (TheStreet).

Index check-in (TheStreet, 2:28 PM ET). After the Nasdaq 100 and Nasdaq Composite set records Monday, the S&P 500 (+0.71%) was following suit with its own advance, which TheStreet said could see its first close over 7,800 ever; TheStreet’s live blog says tech led the rally once again as easing oil prices allowed investors to focus on earnings growth (TheStreet).

Small caps lag (TheStreet, 1:47 PM ET). The Russell 2000 was down 0.45% — the only major index declining — as healthcare got “hammered”: only 48 of the index’s 428 healthcare holdings were advancing, against about half of holdings across the wider index (TheStreet).

Early afternoon (Benzinga, 1:11 PM ET). The S&P 500 was up 66 points, or 0.9%, “marking a fresh all-time high”; the Nasdaq 100 gained 0.8%, extending its advance to a sixth straight session; and the Dow rose 365 points, or 0.7%. The small-cap iShares Russell 2000 ETF slid 0.3%. Industrials led the S&P 500 sectors (XLI +2.1%), followed by technology (XLK +1.7%) and consumer discretionary (XLY +1.2%), while biotech (IBB −3.4%) and oil services (OIH −3.0%) lagged. The 10-year yield fell about 4 basis points to 5.27%, retreating from a 24-year high of 5.33%; the 2-year was at 4.80% and the 30-year at 5.63%. WTI slipped about 0.3% to roughly $89.12 and Brent about 0.7% to around $99.64 (Benzinga). TheStreet’s 1:13 PM midday-winners list of $2 billion-plus stocks was led by OPCH, CEG, VST, CIEN and NBIS and “skews towards independent power producers,” with Constellation up 14.84% (TheStreet).

Midday read (Yahoo Finance strip ~12:05 PM ET; TheStreet 12:03 PM ET). The S&P 500 was up 0.85%, the Nasdaq Composite 0.78% and the Dow 0.71% (quoted +0.71% as of 12:05 PM), while the Russell 2000 added just 0.07%; the 10-year yield was down 5 basis points at 5.26%, the VIX fell 1.87% to 15.23 and November WTI was down 0.73% at $88.78 on the strip (Yahoo Finance). TheStreet’s 12:03 PM update had the S&P 500 (+0.87%) leading the major indexes, with the Nasdaq Composite up 0.79%; 386 S&P 500 holdings were advancing, utilities and real estate led the sectors and only communication services was declining (TheStreet).

Benzinga midday update (12:04 PM ET) and TheStreet (12:40 PM ET). Benzinga’s midday read matched the Yahoo strip: the Dow up 0.69% (around 350 points), the Nasdaq 0.79% and the S&P 500 0.86%. Utilities jumped 1.9% while energy fell 0.4%, and oil was down 0.6% at $88.87 (Benzinga). TheStreet’s 12:40 PM midday-losers list of $2 billion-plus stocks (led by TWST, TXG, TEM, STX and WDC) was “very healthcare heavy, with all but three holdings coming from the sector” (TheStreet). The live quotes above show the real-time picture.

Trade data. The U.S. trade deficit widened to $105.6 billion in August from a revised $92.8 billion in July, wider than the $102.0 billion estimate Benzinga cited and the largest since March 2025; exports rose $4.5 billion to $315.2 billion while imports climbed $17.2 billion to a record $420.8 billion (Benzinga).

Late morning (Benzinga, 11:42 AM ET). The S&P 500 rose 0.86%, the Nasdaq 0.82% and the Dow 0.75%; industrials advanced 1.18% as investors moved back into economically sensitive stocks, and breadth stayed positive with 10 of 11 sectors trading higher. Crude fell for a third straight session, approaching $87 a barrel, its lowest since early September, as Middle Eastern exports showed signs of recovering; the 10-year yield eased to about 5.27% after reaching 5.33% Monday. Airlines benefited from cheaper fuel: American Airlines (AAL) was up 2.38% at $13.13 (Benzinga).

Yahoo Finance live blog. Yahoo’s 11:02 AM update said the S&P 500’s gain put it “on track for a record high at the close,” after it touched a record minutes after the open; the Nasdaq also touched an all-time high (per Yahoo’s headline) after Monday’s record close, a rally Yahoo tied in part to record highs in Nvidia. The 10-year yield sat around 5.29% after Monday’s 5.31% close — its highest closing level in 24 years — and the 30-year was at 5.66%. AMD CEO Lisa Su said demand for compute continues to outrun supply, and Yahoo noted Nvidia, Apple and Microsoft now make up more than 21% of the S&P 500, the most concentrated top three on record, per Creative Planning.

Opening bell (TheStreet, 9:34 AM ET). “Stocks are climbing in early trading as Treasury yields retreated and oil prices eased following the Nasdaq’s record-high close”: S&P 500 +0.50%, Dow +0.45%, Nasdaq +0.56%, Russell 2000 +0.50%. Capital.com’s Kyle Rodda pointed to “extraordinarily strong (albeit concentrated) earnings growth expectations,” noting FactSet estimates for the coming earnings season have been revised up to nearly 30% after 50% growth in Q2 (TheStreet).

Deals and debuts. The $110 billion Paramount Skydance–Warner Bros. Discovery merger closed Tuesday after Justice Elena Kagan denied a last-minute effort to halt it (Yahoo Finance); the combined company, Skydance (SKYD), fell 2.70% to $9.56 in its first day of trading (TheStreet, 9:55 AM). Nvidia rose 1.16% early, giving it a market value of about $5.84 trillion as it approaches $6 trillion, per Bloomberg (TheStreet, 10:19 AM).

Oil and policy. Early Tuesday WTI fell 1.86% to $87.77 and Brent 1.64% to $98.67 after the G7 said it will deploy 100 million barrels of reserves over four months, front-loading diesel; Gulf exporters topped pre-war levels for roughly half of September, per Reuters. President Trump signed an executive order Monday evening temporarily allowing tax-exempt red-dyed diesel on roads, after U.S. diesel topped $6 a gallon in September; CNBC reports nearly 20 commercial ships have come under attack around the Strait of Hormuz over the past month (TheStreet).
