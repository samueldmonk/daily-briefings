The Lead

Stocks close lower a fourth straight session as crude tops $105 and rate-hike odds near 70%

All three major averages finished Thursday in the red, extending the losing run to four consecutive sessions. Two post-close reads taken minutes apart put the declines at roughly −0.5% on the S&P 500, −0.6% on the Dow and −0.5% on the Nasdaq Composite. Those two reads disagree on the exact closing levels — one gives the S&P at 7,596.97 (−39.39) and the Dow at 52,082.88 (−297.78); the other gives 7,591.75 (−44.61) and 52,064.22 (−316.44). Both sets reconcile arithmetically against Wednesday's verified closes, so neither can be dismissed as an error and no closing level is published here until the settle is corroborated. TheStreet noted at 11:38 AM that roughly 60% of U.S. issues were in the red, with the Russell 2000 angling for the worst showing among the major indexes for a third straight day.

The pressure is coming from energy and the bond market rather than from earnings. Oil surged back above $100 on a sharp escalation between the U.S. and Iran: at 9:47 AM West Texas Intermediate was up 4.2% to $100.10 and Brent up 3.6% to $105.37; by late morning Brent had pushed past $106. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.92%, described as a 10-year high.

August's producer price index rose 0.4% month over month, matching the Dow Jones consensus, but followed an upwardly revised 0.1% July reading and pushed the annual rate to 5.4% — above forecasts and up from 4.8% in July. The odds of a rate increase at next week's Fed meeting rose to nearly 70% on the CME FedWatch gauge. Friday's CPI, with consensus at +0.4% monthly and +3.4% annually, is widely framed as the deciding input.

On the Radar

  • Friday: August CPI. Dow Jones consensus is +0.4% monthly and +3.4% over twelve months, with core expected at +0.2%. TheStreet Pro's James DePorre notes Fed Governor Waller said last week he would vote to hold at 0.2% and support a hike above it — a single tenth could decide the meeting.
  • Next week: the FOMC decision on September 16, Chair Kevin Warsh's meeting to define. Rate-hike odds sit near 70% on CME FedWatch.
  • Tonight's earnings: Oracle (ORCL) and Adobe (ADBE) report after the close — see After-Hours Movers above.
  • Housing cooled in August. Existing-home sales fell 2% from July to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.98 million, the slowest pace since June 2025 and down 1.2% year over year. Inventory rose to 1.62 million homes — a 4.9-month supply, the highest in more than a decade — while the median price rose 1.6% to $429,100.
  • Geopolitics remains the swing factor. Multiple U.S. military aircraft were damaged in Iranian strikes on Muwaffaq Salti Air Base in Jordan overnight Tuesday into Wednesday, with no U.S. deaths reported; President Trump warned Iran "not to get cute" over activity at the suspected Pickaxe Mountain nuclear site, and said oil prices are unlikely to fall until after the midterms.
