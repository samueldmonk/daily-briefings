The lead

Hot on the headline, steady at the core — and as of this ~9:40 a.m. ET edition the tape is slipping into Nvidia night

U.S. markets are open. The bell rang at 9:30, minutes before this edition went out, and the first of Wednesday’s two events is behind us with the number confirmed. The Commerce Department reported on Wednesday that the personal consumption expenditures price index rose 0.2% from a month ago and was up 3.7% on an annual basis in July. Both figures were hotter than the expectations of economists polled by LSEG, who projected readings of 0.1% and 3.6% respectively. Core PCE, which excludes food and energy, was up 0.2% on a monthly basis and is 3.3% higher than last year — both in line with the LSEG poll. That is Fox Business, published 8:35 a.m. EDT today under Eric Revell’s byline and fetched in full; CNBC independently headlines its own release story “Fed’s preferred inflation gauge shows core prices rose 3.3% annually in July.”

A correction to the 8:46 edition of this page. That edition declined to publish any PCE figure, on the grounds that every number then in circulation traced back to a preview written before the release. That was the right call at 8:44. It is superseded now: the figures above come from a source timestamped after the 8:30 release that states them as reported by the Commerce Department, which is the standard this page requires.

What the premarket board does say, at 7:10 a.m. ET. Investing.com’s wire had Dow futures mostly unchanged, S&P 500 futures down 7 points, or 0.1%, and Nasdaq 100 futures down 66 points, or 0.2% — the same shape Yahoo Finance described overnight, with the tech leg carrying the softness into the print.

On Yahoo Finance’s own quote board, read at roughly 4:25 a.m. ET, S&P futures stood at 7,687.00, down 5.00 points or 0.07%; Dow futures at 53,701.00, up 56.00 or 0.10%; Nasdaq futures at 29,215.50, down 61.25 or 0.21%; and Russell 2000 futures at 3,014.70, up 0.30 or 0.01%. The VIX was 15.67, up 0.22 or 1.42%. A separate premarket board surfaced later in the morning had the ETF proxies telling the same story — SPY −0.04%, QQQ −0.21%, DIA +0.06%, IWM −0.03%. Neither reading is a session move; both are a market declining to take a position before 8:30.

What the split actually says. The core rate did what the market was positioned for — it held. At 3.3% it sits below May’s 3.4% year-on-year peak but still comfortably above the Fed’s 2% target, and on one preview’s count a hold at that level marks the 65th consecutive month above target. The surprise is on the headline, where energy does its work: 3.7% against a 3.6% forecast, and a monthly 0.2% against 0.1%. It is a tenth in each direction — not a regime change, but not the clean disinflation print the tape wanted either.

And the direction of the risk is still up. Ahead of the release, the CME FedWatch Tool put a 38% probability on a quarter-point rate hike in September — down from 55% one month ago, per FXStreet, which attributes the decline to doubts about Chairman Kevin Warsh’s commitment to fighting inflation in the absence of forward guidance. Read that number carefully: this is a market pricing the odds of a hike, not a cut. Bloomberg reported Tuesday that falling oil prices were curbing expectations for more than one Federal Reserve interest-rate hike in the coming year — the debate is over how many, not whether. Strategists at DBS Bank frame Friday’s Jackson Hole appearance as a test of whether Warsh can spell out “how a Fed without forward guidance intends to anchor expectations, how much tightening the Fed is prepared to tolerate through long-term yields, and the policy boundary between the Fed and the Treasury.” The dollar has not been waiting for an answer: the US Dollar Index is 0.75% lower on the month and more than 2.5% below its late-July top.

The immediate market read, such as it is. Yahoo Finance’s live blog has now re-titled a second time, and the word “futures” has dropped out of it. At 8:46 this desk recorded the headline as “Dow, S&P 500 futures hold steady as PCE inflation stays sticky, Nvidia earnings loom”; the same live blog now reads “Dow, S&P 500, Nasdaq slip as PCE inflation stays sticky, Nvidia earnings loom.” A summary attached to it this run describes the Dow slipping about 0.1%, the S&P 500 dipping below the flat line and the Nasdaq Composite off about 0.1%, with stocks “mostly flat” after the print. Earlier, before the bell, a post-print futures read had S&P 500 futures down 0.1% and Nasdaq-100 futures down 0.4%. ⚠ None of these reads carries a timestamp on the page it came from, so every one of them is published as direction rather than as a level at a stated moment. No opening level for any index is published in this edition, because no source fetched this run states one against a stated clock time; the live quote widgets above carry the actual tape. A post-release summary also returned personal income up 0.4% on the month against a 0.3% expectation, and personal spending up 0.2%; those two figures come from a search summary rather than a page fetched in full, and are labelled accordingly.

The second lands after the closing bell, when Nvidia opens its books in what Yahoo Finance calls “a quarterly yardstick for the health of the artificial intelligence trade.” Expectations are high, with investors increasingly focused on whether the enormous AI capital budgets of the last two years are producing returns. The company guided to $91.0 billion plus or minus two percent for the quarter; Visible Alpha consensus is $92.2 billion and a 40-analyst poll has $91.85 billion. Against the year-ago quarter’s $46.74 billion, consensus implies roughly a doubling. CrowdStrike, Williams-Sonoma, Okta and Abercrombie & Fitch also report Wednesday.

Per-share estimates came back mutually inconsistent in the three earlier editions of this page — one preview gave $2.08, another centred on $0.65–$0.68 adjusted — and no EPS figure was published. That refusal is now lifted, because two independent reads this run agree: a CNBC preview surfaced this morning puts Wall Street at $2.09 a share on $92.28 billion in revenue, per FactSet, and a Motley Fool preview returns $2.09 on $92.2 billion, up 97.4% year over year. ⚠ Both are search summaries rather than pages fetched in full and are labelled as such; the stray $0.65–$0.68 figure remains unexplained and is not endorsed here. The revenue figures above each carry their stated source. Overnight futures are not an opening print; the levels quoted are stamped to the time they were read.

The filing itself is now on file and fetched. Carried · 8:46 edition+5.6%Solar

An FCC list, not an earnings report, moved SolarEdge

SEDG rose 5.6% premarket after UBS upgraded the solar equipment maker to Buy from Neutral and raised its price target to $42 from $36. The rationale is regulatory: the FCC’s decision to add foreign-produced power inverters to its national-security Covered List could tighten supply and strengthen domestically aligned suppliers, with the restrictions potentially affecting more than half of the U.S. inverter market. A separate wire put the move at nearly 7%; both prints are recorded here rather than averaged.

Carried · 8:46 edition−11.7%Biotech

A Phase 2 sub-study reading takes Spyre Therapeutics down double digits

Spyre Therapeutics stock fell 11.7% pre-open after the company released topline results from the rheumatoid arthritis sub-study of its Phase 2 SKYWAY basket trial evaluating SPY072, which fell short of Spyre’s internal threshold for advancing the drug as a standalone treatment. The trial name, the programme and the threshold language are the company’s own, as reported; no read-across to the rest of the basket is stated in the source and none is inferred here.
