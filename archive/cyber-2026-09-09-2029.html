Top Story

A CVSS 10.0 pre-auth RCE in N-able N-central is KEV-listed and due Friday — while the vendor contradicts itself on whether it has been exploited

CVE-2026-86218 carries a CVSS 4.0 score of 10.0, assigned by N-able itself as the CVE Numbering Authority, and is described by CISA as a static code injection flaw (CWE-96) that “could allow for pre-authentication remote code execution.” CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on Tuesday 8 September, requiring Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies to apply the fix by 11 September 2026. It is patched in N-central 2026.3 Hotfix 4 (build 2026.3.1.14), which shipped in the early hours of 6 September UTC — late on 5 September Eastern, which is why several reports date it to the 5th. It affects every N-central build below 2026.3.1.14, including servers updated to Hotfix 3 (2026.3.1.13) a little over eight hours earlier. New this run, from three further reads: the flaw affects every on-premises N-central build below 2026.3.1.14 across the 2025.4, 2026.1, 2026.2 and 2026.3 release lines — so an installation several versions behind is in scope, not only current-line servers. One conflict is printed rather than smoothed: two of those reads date the KEV addition to 9 September while CISA’s own alert page and The Hacker News put it on 8 September. The 8 September date is kept, because it is the one carried by the primary alert; the 11 September remediation deadline is stated identically by both.

N-central is a remote monitoring and management platform — the console managed service providers use to reach every endpoint they administer. No authentication and no user interaction are required, so anyone with network access to an exposed N-central instance can run arbitrary commands, and the foothold is not limited to the platform itself. In its incident notice on its uptime status page, N-able said the flaw “has been observed being exploited in the wild,” that it is “actively investigating this matter and [has] taken additional steps to help protect customer environments,” and urged immediate application of the hotfix.

N-able’s own channels contradict each other on that point, and this page now says so. The Hotfix 4 release notes and status post state that the flaw was responsibly disclosed by a third party and that N-able has “no confirmations that this vulnerability has been exploited in production environments.” The same release notes also call it a “critical zero-day vulnerability,” a term the company does not define. The incident notice says the opposite — observed exploitation in the wild — without saying who observed it, where, or when, and with no attribution to any actor. Huntress’s cybersecurity advisor Ben Bernstein has confirmed that Huntress’s own “actively exploited” description is based entirely on N-able’s statements; Huntress has not reproduced CVE-2026-86218 and has not observed exploitation definitively attributable to it in its telemetry. Earlier editions of this page reported the vendor confirmation flatly; that was too clean a reading of a split record.

The KEV listing followed Huntress opening an investigation into the compromise of a customer’s fully patched N-central production environment on 4 September. Huntress has not been able to say which flaw was used: “Due to limited historical logging available directly on the appliance, we cannot definitively confirm which specific exploit the threat actor used to achieve their compromise, nor can we rule out the use of alternative vulnerabilities.”

Two other flaws are in the frame for that intrusion. CVE-2026-86206 (CVSS 6.9, Medium — unauthorised access to internal APIs through the access-control filter) and CVE-2026-86207 (CVSS 7.7, High — authentication bypass in internal-only APIs), both found and reported by Rapid7’s Stephen Fewer, can be chained to let a remote unauthenticated attacker bypass authentication and create an attacker-controlled System Administrator account. They were fixed in Hotfix 3 on 5 September. Huntress says it reproduced a proof-of-concept chain against build 2026.3.1.10 that may use one or both of these two. This is N-able’s fourth N-central hotfix since 2 August and covers the third distinct set of vulnerabilities. Because the flaw is a pre-authentication RCE, Bernstein says, “an internet-exposed console is the primary attack vector” — hence Huntress’s advice to restrict inbound access by IP allowlist or VPN, and to consider taking an internet-reachable server offline until the hotfix is on.

Patch Priority

Do this first

Apply N-central 2026.3 Hotfix 4 to every N-able N-central server, today. CVE-2026-86218 is a CVSS 10.0 unauthenticated RCE, KEV-listed on 8 September — which is CISA’s own determination that it is being exploited — with a federal remediation deadline of 11 September 2026 ( ). The blast radius is the reason it outranks everything else: a compromised N-central reaches every downstream endpoint the MSP manages. Because Huntress saw a fully patched environment fall on 4 September, patching alone is not sufficient — hunt as well.

Immediately after it: Adobe Hotfix VULN-39341 for Adobe Commerce, Commerce B2B and Magento Open Source (StyleSmuggler, CVE-2026-75650, also CVSS 10.0, exploited from 4 September — three days before any patch existed). And for this one, patching is explicitly not the whole job. Adobe’s own remediation guidance requires operators to rotate the Commerce encryption key and then every credential class that key may have protected — Admin passwords, REST/SOAP/GraphQL integration tokens, OAuth client secrets, payment-gateway API credentials, database credentials, SSH and deploy keys, and third-party extension keys — because Adobe states that rotating the encryption key alone does not invalidate credentials already exposed. Patch first, then rotate, so new secrets are minted by a fixed application.

Then the two flaws with next Wednesday’s clock on them. A CISA-stated deadline of 16 September 2026 ( ) applies to CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette, request smuggling) and CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM, MCP authentication bypass). The other five in that 2 September batch — SonicWall ×2, Switchvox, Artifactory, Kestra — were due 5 September and are therefore already overdue for federal agencies.

On the deadline. The 11 September date for CVE-2026-86218 is stated by The Hacker News reporting on, and linking to, CISA’s 8 September KEV alert; CISA’s own pages again returned an empty body when fetched directly this run. No due date is asserted for the other three CVEs added the same day, including CVE-2026-75650. That was tested again this run and the test failed. A search result this run summarised a StyleSmuggler article as stating a KEV due date of 11 September for CVE-2026-75650; the article itself was then fetched in full and contains no due date at all — it predates the KEV addition and never mentions one. The summary was refused and the blank stands. A date that only exists in a summary of a source, and not in the source, is not a date. The 5 September and 16 September dates for the 2 September batch are different and better: they come from The Hacker News reporting that names the directive by number and splits the batch into two tiers, which is exactly the shape a risk-based assignment takes. The old flat three-week BOD 22-01 arithmetic remains unsafe to apply, because BOD 26-04 sets remediation deadlines tiered by risk instead — and this batch, five days versus fourteen for the same add date, is the demonstration.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Active
ShinyHunters

An extortion brand that has moved from SaaS tenants to state records

ShinyHunters is an extortion crew that targets web applications and cloud SaaS environments for data theft; the name has been associated with numerous threat actors since 2018 and survives repeated arrests. Its recent method is voice phishing against Okta, Microsoft and Google single sign-on accounts — impersonating IT support to harvest credentials and MFA codes — plus device-code vishing for Microsoft tokens, then using hijacked SSO to reach Salesforce, Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, SAP, Slack, Adobe, Atlassian, Zendesk and Dropbox. The Florida DMV claim below is a departure: a password-reset flaw rather than social engineering, against a government law-enforcement database. The group told BleepingComputer to expect more DMV breaches in the coming weeks.

Breaches & Incidents

CarriedGovernment

ShinyHunters claims 200,000+ records from Florida’s “DAVID” DMV database

The gang says it breached DAVID — the Driver and Vehicle Information Database operated by the Florida Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles agency and used by law enforcement to look up drivers — and took over 200,000 records since the intrusion began on 3 September. The route in was a password-reset flaw that let them compromise multiple accounts, allegedly belonging to DMV employees and an FBI agent; they then iterated through record IDs and downloaded the HTML and images. As proof they released a screenshot of Jeffrey Epstein’s DAVID record, containing address, Social Security number, birth date, licence ID and registered vehicles. FLHSMV was added to the leak site on the night of 7 September. The attackers say they have since lost access and the flaw is being patched. FLHSMV has not confirmed a breach; BleepingComputer says it contacted the agency and the FBI and had no response at publication.

CarriedMSP

Huntress: a fully patched N-central environment fell on 4 September

Huntress opened an investigation after a customer’s fully patched N-central production environment was compromised on 4 September — the trigger for the vendor and CISA activity in the top story. Limited on-appliance logging means the specific exploit used cannot be confirmed, and alternative vulnerabilities cannot be ruled out. New this run: Huntress says it reproduced a proof-of-concept chain against build 2026.3.1.10 that may use one or both of the Hotfix 3 flaws, and that it has not reproduced CVE-2026-86218 nor seen exploitation definitively attributable to it. For anyone running N-central, that makes this a hunting exercise as well as a patching one — and an argument for restricting inbound access to the console.

CarriedIdentity data

FBI probes “Nexus” selling 153 million driver’s licence scans

A dark-web service that surfaced on a Russian cybercrime forum on 31 August offered scans of more than 153 million driver’s licences, 10 million ID cards, 3 million travel documents and 579,000 medical cards covering the U.S. and Canada. Its operators claimed the material came from an ongoing breach at an identity-verification provider; Krebs on Security names IDScan.net. The FBI’s New Orleans field office has opened a formal investigation. Nexus went offline shortly after Krebs published.

CarriedRansomware

Panzer: a new RaaS with 16 victims in 11 countries

A newly identified ransomware-as-a-service operation has claimed 16 victims across 11 countries since its leak site went live on 5 August 2026, spanning technology, manufacturing, government, agriculture, energy, education and retail — technology (4) and manufacturing (3) worst hit. Both data theft and encryption are involved.

CarriedE-commerce

StyleSmuggler now has at least two actors and several implants behind it

Sansec’s running analysis of the Adobe Commerce zero-day, read in full this run, describes multiple implant variants and a separate actor using the same flaw to drop a PHP web shell under Magento’s media directory. Its timeline: first confirmed exploitation 4 September, chain reproduced and published 5 September, an fc-cache-named implant variant on the 6th, and on the 7th both the second actor’s web shell and another variant masquerading as chronyd — the same day Adobe’s patch arrived. Persistence hides behind plausible process names ([kworker/u:8:0], fc-cache, chronyd) under ~/.local/share/.gvfsd/, ~/.cache/fontconfig/ and /tmp. Two cheap checks for anyone running Magento: find pub/media -name '*.php', and an unexplained burst of failed-payment reminder emails — the exploit deliberately triggers that rendering path.

CarriedRefused

A ShinyHunters claim that is three months old, not six days old

A 2026 breach tracker read this run shelves the American Tower Corporation intrusion — ShinyHunters claiming more than 5.2 million records including customer and landowner PII, tower GPS coordinates and plaintext gate codes — under 3 September 2026. The underlying reporting dates the claim to 12 June 2026, with a leak-site countdown that expired on 15 June. Same actor as the Florida DMV story above, three months earlier. It is not carried as current, and the 5.2 million figure is in any case the attacker’s claim: curated breach collections that have since circulated the data describe a verified subset in the low hundreds of thousands.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • 8 September — four added. CVE-2026-75650 (Adobe Commerce/Magento), CVE-2026-81963 (Windows link following), CVE-2026-85880 (Windows heap overflow), CVE-2026-86218 (N-able N-central static code injection). CVE-2026-86218 is due 11 September 2026 ( ) — the one due date corroborated this run, via The Hacker News reporting on and linking to the CISA alert. No due date is asserted for the other three, and none is printed.
  • 4 September — one added. CVE-2026-85046, Google Chromium V8 type confusion. No due date verified this run.
  • 2 September — seven added, and this run finally has their deadlines. Under BOD 26-04, FCEB agencies were told to patch five of the seven by 5 September — SonicWall SMA 1000 CVE-2026-83548 (CVSS 10.0, unauthenticated SSRF) and CVE-2026-83549 (7.8, post-auth OS command injection), Sangoma Switchvox CVE-2026-9586 (9.3), JFrog Artifactory CVE-2026-82329 (9.8) and Kestra OSS CVE-2026-49869 (10.0). Those five are now overdue. The remaining two, Kludex Starlette CVE-2026-48710 (6.5) and BerriAI LiteLLM CVE-2026-59822 (8.8), are due 16 September 2026 ( ). Earlier editions printed “no due date verified” for this whole batch; that gap is now closed.
  • What the split tells you about the new directive. One add date, one alert, two different clocks — three days for the maximum-severity network appliances and repository servers, fourteen for the two library-level flaws. That is BOD 26-04 working as designed, and it is the clearest available proof that the old flat three-week window can no longer be assumed for anything.
  • Why only two countdowns. Previous editions ran a three-week clock from the add date under BOD 22-01. BOD 26-04 has since replaced that flat window with deadlines tiered by risk, so that arithmetic no longer holds — and CISA’s own pages returned empty bodies on direct fetch again this run. The two countdowns on this page are printed because both dates were read in reporting this run that names the directive and links the CISA alert; every other date is left blank rather than guessed. Operationally, treat both CVSS 10.0 flaws in the 8 September batch as due now.
  • Volume check. Twelve CVEs entered the catalogue in the last seven days — a heavy week by any measure. Of those twelve, five are past their federal due date, two are due next Wednesday, one is due Friday, and four carry no due date this page has been able to verify.

Around the Industry

  • September Patch Tuesday has no single count — and this run found out why. Tenable’s advisory says 964 CVEs, BleepingComputer 966, six other reads 973, and SecurityWeek 974 (calling it a record). Reading BleepingComputer’s own article in full this run supplies the missing methodology note: it counts only vulnerabilities released by Microsoft on Patch Tuesday itself, and its total therefore excludes 204 flaws fixed earlier in the month in Azure AI Language, Azure Cosmos DB, Copilot Studio, Entra ID, Mariner, Azure AD B2C, Microsoft Discovery Studio, Edge, Fabric and Power Automate. So the trackers are measuring different sets, not disputing a fact — though the stated exclusion does not by itself reconcile 966 to 973, so the spread is still printed as a spread. Its own breakdown: 438 elevation of privilege, 258 remote code execution, 173 information disclosure, 56 denial of service, 19 security feature bypass, 16 spoofing, with 105 rated Critical (81 of them RCE).
  • Why the number is exploding. BleepingComputer calls this Microsoft’s largest security update ever, against 570 in July and 400 in August — and attributes the increase to Microsoft having begun using an AI-powered vulnerability discovery system across its products. The operationally interesting consequence is on this page already: a record 966-flaw release whose only two exploited zero-days are local privilege escalations, while every pre-authentication, internet-facing flaw on this page — Adobe Commerce, SonicWall, Artifactory, Kestra, Switchvox, and the contested N-able case — belongs to some other vendor. Volume of fixes and exposure to initial access are not the same measurement.
  • Vendor advisories landing alongside the same release. BleepingComputer’s round-up lists, among others: SAP shipping September updates including a maximum-severity “OVERPASS” flaw in the SAP Kernel; SonicWall patching two SMA1000 zero-days that are being chained in RCE attacks — the same appliance line as the two KEV entries now past their federal due date; HPE fixing a critical RCE in ArubaOS-CX; MikroTik fixing two actively exploited RouterOS flaws used to hijack devices over SSH; ConnectWise issuing mitigations for a ScreenConnect flaw it has not yet patched; CrowdStrike telling customers to disable the Office suspicious-macro-removal policy after an anonymous researcher dropped a zero-day (“FalconFlank”) granting SYSTEM privileges; and Plex urging immediate patching without disclosing details. Each is carried only as far as that round-up states it.
  • Wormable flaws in the same release. Twenty of the fixed vulnerabilities are considered wormable, among them CVE-2026-55007 (Exchange RCE), CVE-2026-69465 (SharePoint RCE), CVE-2026-65669 (SQL Server EoP), CVE-2026-69525 (RDS RCE) and CVE-2026-80097 (Authenticator EoP).
  • The 2 September KEV batch is mostly an AI-infrastructure story. Three of the seven — Kestra, LiteLLM and Starlette — sit in the AI plumbing layer, and Microsoft and Wiz both report the same pattern behind them: credential collection, durable access, and monetisation through cryptomining. Attackers are hitting LiteLLM gateways via CVE-2026-42271 and CVE-2026-48710 to drop an XMRig miner, fingerprinting the host and killing competing miners first, then reaching the LiteLLM-backed PostgreSQL tier to harvest model configuration, upstream provider key material and proxy-issued virtual keys, with persistence via ~/.ssh/authorized_keys. Threat actors associated with Qilin (also known as Agenda) ransomware are linked to exploitation of that chain. Microsoft’s conclusion is the useful one for defenders: “monitor AI workloads according to their control-plane role, not only as isolated applications.”
  • Headline-level items, carried only as far as their headlines go. Three stories surfaced on BleepingComputer’s front page this run that were not read in full, so only what each headline states is repeated here: the Trezor data-breach impact now reaches 81,000 customers; a Microsoft 365 phishing service called BigBear bypassed MFA at 258 organisations; and over 5,400 hacked sites are serving ClickFix payloads stored on the blockchain. No further detail is asserted for any of the three.
