Top Story

CISA adds Google’s Pixel modem zero-day to the KEV catalogue, a day after Google’s own advisory

CISA added one new vulnerability to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue today, 16 September 2026: CVE-2026-58704, a Google Pixel improper-authorization flaw, listed on evidence of active exploitation.

The bug carries a CVSS score of 8.0 and is a privilege-escalation flaw in the Pixel Cellular Modem. Per the NVD description, “there is a possible permission bypass due to a logic error in the code”, which “could lead to remote (proximal/adjacent) escalation of privilege with no additional execution privileges needed” — and “user interaction is not needed for exploitation”.

In an advisory issued Tuesday, Google said it had found indications that CVE-2026-58704 “may be under limited, targeted exploitation”, without detailing the attacks or naming the actor. Reporting notes that Google has historically used the same “limited, targeted” phrasing for zero-days leveraged by commercial spyware vendors and state-aligned surveillance operators.

Besides CVE-2026-58704, Google addressed 109 other flaws in the September Pixel updates — 88 privilege escalation, 10 information disclosure, 9 remote code execution and 2 denial-of-service — including two high-severity kernel privilege-escalation bugs (CVE-2026-56914 and CVE-2026-58773) and 46 critical-severity flaws across Pixel components such as BigOcean, Bootloader, IP Multimedia Subsystem and the Trusted Execution Environment. Security patch levels of 2026-09-05 or later resolve all of them.

Patch Priority

Due today: BerriAI LiteLLM CVE-2026-59822 and Kludex Starlette CVE-2026-48710 — 0 days left

The single most urgent federal deadline on the board lands today, 16 September 2026. CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM, improper authentication, CVSS 8.8) and CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette, HTTP request/response smuggling, CVSS 6.5) were both added to the KEV catalogue in the seven-vulnerability batch of 2 September and carry a 16 September remediation date — zero days left. Next up is Cisco Secure Email Gateway CVE-2026-76461 (CVSS 9.8), due tomorrow. Both dates match the KEV section below exactly; nothing on this page uses a different deadline.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Extortion · voice phishing

ShinyHunters

The English-speaking gang has spent 2026 running simple but highly effective voice-phishing campaigns — tricking companies into handing over access by posing as IT support, or conversely as an employee who has forgotten a password. TechCrunch credits the group with some of the year’s largest breaches by record count: over 30 million students and staff via education-tech giant Instructure’s Canvas platform, some 40 million records from internet provider Charter and at least 6 million customer records from cruise line Carnival, plus victims across higher education, finance and government. When Instructure declined to pay, the hackers broke in again and defaced Canvas login screens during school finals.

Breaches & Incidents

Healthcare · 2026 to date

DentaQuest, CareCloud and Aesto Health: tens of millions of medical records

The largest known breach of 2026 hit dental-insurance company DentaQuest, with the health data of 15 million people stolen. A separate breach at CareCloud, which hosts electronic patient records, let attackers take the medical information of at least 3.7 million people. And a breach at healthcare data and billing giant Aesto Health at the end of last year was later confirmed to affect at least 9.5 million patients across dozens of providers and practices using its software.

Supply chain · extortion

Klue’s 2022 credential cost close to 200 customers their cloud keys

Market-research provider Klue sat at the centre of a breach affecting close to 200 companies, several of them security firms including Jamf, HackerOne and LastPass. Klue said an extortion gang dubbed Icarus broke in using a credential the company had issued in 2022 for a limited pilot — roughly four years to decommission it. The breach exposed keys to customers’ cloud services. Klue told customers it had reached an agreement with the hackers not to publish the data; as part of that deal the hackers conceded that another group also held a portion of the data.

Education tech · repeat intrusion

Instructure paid after a second break-in defaced Canvas during finals

ShinyHunters breached Canvas, Instructure’s flagship learning-management system, taking private data and personal information of over 30 million students and staff. When the company did not pay, the hackers broke in a second time and defaced Canvas login screens during school finals, disrupting exams across the United States. Instructure eventually paid the ransom, despite FBI efforts to dissuade it.

Identity · ongoing

IDScan: a dark-web search engine covering 150 million driving licences

Identity-document checking company IDScan confirmed a breach after hackers touted a dark-web search engine capable of listing photos of 150 million drivers in the U.S. and Canada. The attackers appear to be holding the cache — stolen over the course of a year — hostage for a ransom, and details are still emerging.

  • Added today, 16 Sep — CVE-2026-58704 (Google Pixel). One vulnerability, added on evidence of active exploitation. No countdown is shown: the KEV catalogue page returned an empty body on direct fetch this run, so no remediation date for this entry could be verified, and none is invented.

  • Due tomorrow, 17 Sep — CVE-2026-76461 (Cisco Secure Email Gateway, 9.8): 1 day left.
  • Due 14 Sep — CVE-2026-85706 (GitLab, 10.0): Overdue by 2 days.
  • Due 13 Sep — the MikroTik RouterOS pair added 10 September (CVE-2026-67277 and CVE-2026-86060): Overdue by 3 days.
  • Due 12 Sep — the four vulnerabilities added 9 September: Overdue by 4 days.
  • Due 5 Sep — the remainder of the 2 September batch of seven: Overdue by 11 days. That batch covered Sangoma Switchvox, Kestra OSS, JFrog Artifactory and two SonicWall SMA1000 flaws alongside the two due today.
  • The four vulnerabilities added 8 September still carry no stated due date in anything read, so no countdown is given for them.
  • How these dates are set. The flat three-week clock is gone: BOD 26-04, “Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk”, issued 10 June 2026, sets remediation windows of 3, 14 or 60 calendar days depending on whether the asset is publicly exposed, whether the flaw is in KEV, whether exploitation can be automated and how much control an attacker gains. The highest-risk combinations must be remediated or mitigated within three calendar days, some with forensic triage in the same window. Agency policies had to support ongoing remediation by 7 August 2026; agencies must begin evaluating against BOD 26-04 timelines by 7 December 2026. KEV listing alone no longer sets the deadline.
Countdowns are computed from today, 16 September 2026, against the remediation dates recorded for each entry. Where no due date has been verified, no countdown is shown rather than a guess.
