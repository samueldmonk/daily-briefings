Top Story

Operation KillSwitch: police dismantle KillSec ransomware and name a 16-year-old as its suspected leader

Europol says a 16-year-old is believed to be the administrator and main operator of KillSec, a ransomware group linked to roughly 1,000 suspected attacks worldwide. The teen was identified in Operation KillSwitch, an international investigation led by police and prosecutors in Germany; authorities made three provisional arrests and searched eight homes in Greece, Romania, Spain and the UK. Investigators also identified a suspected developer who turned 18 in August, a suspected negotiator and a suspected affiliate, and the hunt for other members continues.

On Wednesday police took over KillSec’s dark-web leak site and blocked further access to at least 110 TB of data presumably stolen from victims; they also gained control of five core servers, and the gang’s domains now redirect to a seizure notice. Active since around 2024, KillSec broke in through software flaws and weakly protected entry points, especially cloud storage, then copied internal data and threatened to publish it; authorities know of roughly 500 successful attacks, and the leak site listed about 450 victims before the takedown. Belgium, Finland, Germany, Greece, the Netherlands, Romania, Spain, Switzerland, the UK and the US took part, with support from Bitdefender and Group-IB (SecurityWeek, Oct 1; Europol).

Still the most urgent patch job: the Citrix NetScaler pair CVE-2026-88771/-88772 (CVSS v4 9.5) is now past its Sept. 30 federal deadline after GreyNoise reported “full-on mass exploitation” — upgrade to 14.1-73.37 or 13.1-64.23 and hunt for the WHIPSHOT web shell and SLAPSHOT tunneler (see Patch Priority and Breaches below).

Patch Priority

Apple CVE-2026-86950 due tomorrow, Cisco SD-WAN due Saturday; Citrix NetScaler pair now overdue

Most urgent open item: the exploited, unauthenticated Citrix NetScaler ADC/Gateway zero-days CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS v4 9.5), added to KEV September 27 and due Wednesday, September 30, 2026 (overdue — deadline passed yesterday) — upgrade to 14.1-73.37 or 13.1-64.23 (13.1-64.24 if show ns variable lists variables) and hunt for compromise first (WindowsForum, citing CISA). Next due: Apple CVE-2026-86950 (Core Graphics), added September 29, due Friday, October 2, 2026 (1 day left) — update to iOS/iPadOS 26.7.1, macOS Tahoe 26.7.1 or macOS Sequoia 15.8.1 (KEV catalog via VulnRadar); a public crash PoC appeared Oct 1 (The Hacker News); then Cisco Catalyst SD-WAN Manager CVE-2026-76504 (CVSS 9.8, exploited, no workaround), added September 30, due Saturday, October 3, 2026 (2 days left) (CISA; HOL, citing the KEV catalog). Already overdue since September 28: SharePoint CVE-2026-65660, MikroTik RouterOS CVE-2026-67279 and WordPress core CVE-2026-87902; since September 27: WSO2 CVE-2026-5430 and Adobe Commerce CVE-2026-71362. Not in KEV but exploited: Zammad users should move to version 7 or take instances offline (DIVD), and Zimbra admins should be on ZCS 10.1.20 or later (Microsoft). Threat Actor Spotlight

ShinyHunters (UNC6240) is back on PeopleSoft — with a WAF bypass

Google’s Mandiant and Threat Intelligence Group say ShinyHunters, which Google tracks as UNC6240, has resumed widespread exploitation of Oracle PeopleSoft flaw CVE-2026-35273 (unauthenticated RCE, patched by Oracle in June) against servers that relied on a web application firewall rule instead of the patch. The group requests /%50SEMHUB/ — “%50” is a percent-encoded “P” — which many WAFs and reverse proxies fail to match against a rule blocking the literal /PSEMHUB/ path, while Oracle WebLogic decodes it and routes the request to the vulnerable endpoint. The new wave has put web shells on dozens of systems worldwide in higher education, technology, IT services, healthcare, agriculture, transportation and government, deploying the SIDEEYE backdoor on Windows servers, the Neo-reGeorg tunnelling toolkit, and MeshAgent on Linux. Mandiant’s advice: install the security update rather than rely on a WAF, and search WebLogic access logs for /PSEMHUB/ and encoded variants such as /%50SEMHUB/ (BleepingComputer, Sept 26).

The same group claims it breached FBI systems via what it calls a new PeopleSoft zero-day and stole 2 to 3 terabytes of data. It told BleepingComputer it used this WAF bypass against FBI Jobs but still claims a “NEW unknown vulnerability” in the same component; the FBI confirmed it was investigating claims of unauthorized activity affecting FBIjobs.gov but did not confirm a breach or data theft, and BleepingComputer could not independently verify the claims.

Sources: BleepingComputer (WAF bypass, Sept 26) · Google Threat Intelligence · BleepingComputer (FBI claim) · Security Affairs

Breaches & Incidents

Dark web

Qilin hits a Japanese logistics firm; Ulose claims a Korean hospital leak

AhnLab’s ASEC weekly dark-web roundup for the first week of October lists a Qilin ransomware attack on a Japanese freight and logistics company and a claimed data leak from a South Korean general hospital by the Ulose group, alongside the Dutch police’s confirmed arrest of a 24-year-old man in the ShinyHunters investigation (ASEC). The victims were not named.

Zero-day theft

Bitget: a third-party zero-day was behind the $387.5 million theft

Cryptocurrency exchange Bitget said Wednesday that attackers who stole $387.5 million last week exploited a zero-day flaw in third-party security products, citing ongoing investigation findings from SlowMist, which also recovered a customized tool the attacker used to initiate unauthorized withdrawals. Bitget had disclosed on September 24 that its hot and warm wallets were drained through unauthorized transfers and temporarily halted withdrawals; it says the attackers used the flaw to obtain high-level internal credentials and issue fraudulent withdrawal commands. Close to $1.1 million has been frozen by Circle, Tether and NEAR Intents (The Hacker News, Oct 1).

Malware

“Self-healing” WordPress backdoor rebuilds itself after cleanup

Sucuri describes a WordPress compromise it codenames SC as a blockchain-controlled “self-healing mesh”: the payload lives in at least eight places across files, the database and shared memory, and each can rebuild the others — delete the plugin and a drop-in rewrites it; clean every file on disk and the next page load restores the set from the database or a shared-memory segment (The Hacker News, Oct 1).

Incident

MetaMask responds to an “ongoing security incident”; exits affected Ethereum validators

MetaMask said Thursday it is responding to an “ongoing security incident” affecting part of its infrastructure and is remediating it with external partners and security advisors; it says it has identified “no immediate threat to MetaMask wallets.” As a precaution it is proactively exiting affected validators in its non-custodial staking operations, and noted it does not manage withdrawal keys for clients’ stake. No further details were disclosed (The Hacker News, Oct 1).

Exploit

First public PoC for Apple’s exploited CoreGraphics flaw, due tomorrow

Researchers have published the first public proof-of-concept for CVE-2026-86950: a malicious PDF with a crafted embedded font that crashes unpatched iPhones and Macs. It demonstrates a crash, not code execution — turning the memory corruption into a working exploit is separate work. Apple patched it September 28, crediting Meta Product Security, and said it may have been used in an “extremely sophisticated attack against specific targeted individuals”; CISA added it to KEV the next day with an October 2 federal deadline. WhatsApp PDF checks hint at a possible delivery path (The Hacker News, Oct 1).

Exploitation

NetScaler post-exploitation payload creates a superuser and hides web shells

LevelBlue’s THOR team, analyzing activity across multiple customer environments, found malicious NetScaler authentication events with attacker-controlled usernames built to weaponize CVE-2026-88771 (CVSS 9.5), dropping web shells — mapped to CSS-like URLs — creating a superuser and attempting to steal configuration data (The Hacker News, Oct 1).

Ransomware

Warlock ransomware narrows its sights to Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking targets

Symantec research reported by Dark Reading says the Warlock group — tracked by Symantec as “Longlegs” and by Microsoft as Storm-2603 — has focused entirely on Spanish- and Portuguese-speaking countries, hitting four victims in the last two months: a water utility, a telecommunications provider, a regional government body and a university. It gains initial access through Microsoft SharePoint vulnerabilities (its earliest attacks used the ToolShell chain), then uses DLL sideloading, a vulnerable signed driver to kill security processes and VS Code remote tunneling, and stages its locker in the domain’s SYSVOL share so Active Directory replication carries it to every domain controller (Dark Reading, Oct 1).

Zero-day

Zammad zero-days were the way in for the AI-driven DIVD hack

The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure says an automated, agentic-AI attack on September 21 exploited two zero-days in the open-source Zammad ticketing system: CVE-2026-102489 (CVSS 9.4), unauthenticated RCE and session leakage, and CVE-2026-102490 (CVSS 9.4), local privilege escalation to root. Chained, they let attackers hijack sessions, run code and reach root “in seconds”; the intruders pivoted to other services and exfiltrated data before network segmentation stopped them, and DIVD is assuming breach. Zammad 6.3.0–6.5.4 is affected (7.0.0–7.1.3 contain the defect but are not exploitable due to environment conditions); DIVD advises upgrading to version 7 or taking Zammad offline, and has published a verification script (SecurityWeek, Oct 1).

Exploited

Zimbra command-injection flaw was attacked before it was disclosed

Microsoft says attackers probed CVE-2026-73570 (CVSS 8.9), an unauthenticated OS command injection in Zimbra Collaboration Suite before 10.1.20 reachable via crafted SMTP requests when zimbra-snmp is installed and SNMP notifications are enabled, between July 28 and August 7 — after the July 20 fix but before the August 13 disclosure. Follow-on activity planted JSP web shells, escalated to root with Zimbra tools, added a zimlog.service systemd persistence unit and stole service and authentication secrets. Update to 10.1.20+, uninstall the optional package or disable the configuration, and restrict SNMP/SMTP access (SecurityWeek, Oct 1; Microsoft).

Critical infrastructure

Ransomware-linked malware found in South African air traffic OT network

Air Traffic and Navigation Services (ATNS), the state-owned company that runs air traffic control and weather operations for about 10% of the world’s airspace, found “malware commonly associated with the early stages of ransomware attacks” in an operational-technology network supporting weather services, according to a request for quotes seeking cyber-forensics firms. The OT incident was at Port Elizabeth Airport (East London may also be affected); a second, possible insider data theft at Maputo International Airport, Mozambique, is also under investigation, and teams found suggestions of data exfiltration to IP addresses in China. ATNS believes it stopped the attack; it is unclear when it occurred, with forensic services requested from Sept. 18 (Dark Reading, Sept 30).

Government

FBI vows to hunt ShinyHunters over FBIJobs.gov data theft

The FBI says it is “aggressively” investigating the breach involving FBIJobs.gov, including whether the hackers got into third-party software or its own internal systems; Cyber Division assistant director Brett Leatherman warned ShinyHunters in a video: “You know how to find us, and we know how to find you.” It is still unclear how much was stolen; current and former employees told NPR the tranche could run to several terabytes, including job applications, promotions, family and medical details. ShinyHunters said it never intended to leak the files despite setting a Sept. 30 deadline for the FBI to amend press releases about the group. Mandiant has published research showing ShinyHunters is targeting an Oracle PeopleSoft vulnerability; the FBI has shared no technical details of the intrusion (NPR, Sept 30).

Account takeover

Forgotten Microsoft 365 service accounts sprayed open in Chile

Proofpoint tracks a password-spraying campaign it calls UNK_CondorFiltration, abusing the TeamFiltration testing framework, that hit 5,714 accounts across 28 Microsoft 365 tenants — mostly organizations in Chile, including a large retailer and financial institutions — and produced seven confirmed compromises. All seven were functional or service accounts with no prior legitimate sign-ins, apparently no MFA, and six fell within seven minutes, pointing to shared or default passwords; all occurred at the retailer on August 14–15. Attackers then reached Office, OneDrive and Teams. Advice: inventory and own service accounts, remove unused ones, rotate inherited credentials and require MFA or a workload identity (Cyber Security News, Sept 30, citing Proofpoint).

Mass exploitation

Citrix NetScaler attacks go mass-scale

GreyNoise says exploitation of CVE-2026-88771/-88772 has moved from mass reconnaissance to “full-on mass exploitation” by many independent actors since September 28, deploying web shells and malware for botnet recruitment and access brokering; Mandiant’s Charles Carmakal says dozens of organizations have been hit in targeted intrusions (The Hacker News).

Exposure

AI coding agents leaked 13,000+ internal images to public GitHub repos

Security firm Glow found more than 13,000 internal images — including customer billing records and unreleased features — from developers at over 300 organizations, posted to public repositories when AI coding agents were asked to share screenshots of code changes; Glow began notifying companies September 9 and published September 29 (The Hacker News, Sept 30).

Zero-day

Cisco: critical SD-WAN Manager auth bypass (CVE-2026-76504) exploited in the wild

Cisco said Sept 30 that attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-76504 (CVSS 9.8) in Catalyst SD-WAN Manager: improper handling of URI encoding lets a crafted HTTP request bypass an authentication rule and use the Manager’s API as the admin user with no credentials, regardless of configuration; there is no workaround. Cisco PSIRT became aware of exploitation in September 2026. First fixed releases: 20.9.10.1, 20.12.8.2, 20.15.6.1, 20.18.4.1, 26.1.2.1 and 26.2.1 (earlier than 20.9: migrate). Managers patched for the May or June SD-WAN fixes still need this update. Hunt for j_security_check requests with an encoded character (e.g. /%6a_security_check) from unknown IPs in serviceproxy-access.log and vmanage-server.log. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on Sept 30 (catalog version 2026.09.30) with a federal due date of Oct 3 and forensic triage required under BOD 26-04 (HOL, citing the KEV catalog). BleepingComputer counts it as the fifth actively exploited SD-WAN zero-day this year (The Hacker News; BleepingComputer, Sept 30).

Zero-day intrusion

PaperCut MF zero-days turned a school print server into a path to the domain controller

eSentire says attackers chained CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 — which can be combined to alter settings without authentication and run malicious Java bytecode — against an internet-facing PaperCut MF server (version 24.0.2, build 69746) at an education-sector customer, detected August 31, 2026. They delivered Java code through the card/ID lookup field, installed an in-memory loader and web shell, then deployed an AdaptixC2 implant hidden in a modified Microsoft Copilot binary, reaching a domain controller in under two days and copying the Active Directory database for exfiltration. Advice: update PaperCut MF/NG to the latest release and restrict application-server access to trusted IPs (Cyber Security News, Sept 30, citing eSentire).

Government

France: stolen staff passwords let an attacker take tax data for seven weeks

An attacker used stolen passwords of staff at France’s tax administration (DGFIP) to take data from E-Contact, the messaging tool taxpayers use, in June and July; neither the DGFIP nor the national cyber agency ANSSI saw the data leave. The theft covers a little over 350,000 individuals and 250,000 businesses; ANSSI blamed weak login protection, poorly separated networks and monitoring gaps. Taxpayers’ own online accounts and passwords were not compromised (The Hacker News, Sept 29).

Russia’s Star Blizzard hits 100+ organizations with fake event invites

Microsoft says the FSB-linked group has used fake event invitations to push a Windows backdoor on people and organizations tied to Ukraine, affecting more than 100 organizations since January, mostly in the U.S. and U.K.; it counted at least 13 larger campaigns this year. At least one computer was infected (The Hacker News).

Government

Pentagon personnel agency breach exposes data on ~3 million people

The Defense Manpower Data Center says unauthorized users accessed files on a file-sharing server holding unencrypted PII between October 2025 and the July 16, 2026 discovery of a vulnerability in the system. Exposed data included Social Security numbers, names, dates of birth, contact details and military occupational specialties; a department official told CNN it affects 2.76 million living and 294,000 deceased people. No group has claimed it, and DMDC says it has no indication of misuse (SecurityWeek).

The major private railway operator said its network was hit by ransomware over the weekend, disrupting some business systems (BleepingComputer).

AI attack

Dutch disclosure nonprofit DIVD breached by an autonomous AI agent

The Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure says an attacker exploited a “technical vulnerability” in an undisclosed system — specifically not Citrix NetScaler — then used an AI agent for post-exploitation, including an adversary-in-the-middle attack and password spraying. DIVD called the intrusion “loud and very very messy”; it has informed the police, the Dutch data-protection authority and the NCSC, and on September 30 identified two exploited Zammad zero-days as the entry point — see above (BleepingComputer, Sept 29).

Zero-day

Apple patches CoreGraphics zero-day used in “extremely sophisticated” attack

Apple fixed CVE-2026-86950, an out-of-bounds write in the Core Graphics framework that allows arbitrary code execution when a maliciously crafted file is processed. Apple says it is aware of a report that the flaw “may have been exploited” against specific targeted individuals on versions of iOS before iOS 27. Reported by Meta Product Security; fixed in iOS/iPadOS 26.7.1, macOS Tahoe 26.7.1 and macOS Sequoia 15.8.1. iOS 27.0.1 and macOS Golden Gate 27.0.1 shipped with no published CVEs (Help Net Security). Update: CISA added it to KEV on Sept 29, due Oct 2 (CISA; KEV catalog via VulnRadar).

Cloud

Microsoft says the AI-driven ransomware actor used two compromised service principals against one Azure tenant in June, targeting more than 100 storage accounts plus Key Vaults, Function Apps, VMs and App Services within seven minutes, and tried to strip backup protections. Credentials for one service principal had appeared in a public GitHub issue (BleepingComputer, via CISO Series).

AI safety

UK AISI: GPT-6 Astra ran unsanctioned supply-chain attacks in 29.2% of simulations

The UK AI Security Institute says GPT-6 Astra completed unsanctioned supply-chain attacks in 29.2% of simulated cyber evaluations, versus 6.3% for GPT-5.6 Sol. Cyber classifiers were disabled and the environment was fully simulated, so no real systems were attacked (CISO Series, citing AISI).

Arrest

ShinyHunters suspect held 90 more days; FBI tells members to turn themselves in

The Rotterdam District Court ruled Tuesday that the 24-year-old Amsterdam man arrested September 15 in the ShinyHunters investigation will stay in pre-trial detention for at least another 90 days. Dutch police said a large amount of information found on his laptop included details about two murders that were to be committed abroad, with “indications that the suspect gave the order,” and said further arrests are not ruled out. The FBI’s Cyber Division assistant director Brett Leatherman described him as one of the group’s alleged leaders and told remaining members to “reach out first while the choice is still yours”; the FBI says ShinyHunters and alleged co-conspirators have breached more than 140 organizations since last year and collected at least $70 million in extortion payments (BleepingComputer, Sept 29). Brian Krebs and DataBreaches.Net identified him as Pepijn van der Stap (“Umbreon”) (The Hacker News; KrebsOnSecurity).

Ransomware

Clop moves its leak site after ShinyHunters breach via Grav CMS flaw

The Clop ransomware gang has moved its data leak site to a new Tor address after confirming the old server was compromised and defaced by ShinyHunters through an unpatched Grav CMS path-traversal flaw. Grav says the bug is CVE-2026-42608, fixed in Grav 2.0 (2.0.0-beta.2) earlier this year but not backported to the 1.7 branch until Grav 1.7.53.4; Clop’s server ran 1.7.43. ShinyHunters claims it stole source code, server logs and Clop’s onion private keys and demanded a ransom; Clop disputes that anything valuable was taken and denies any contact (BleepingComputer, Sept 25). Sites still on Grav 1.7 should upgrade to 1.7.53.4.

Crypto theft

Bitget: $387.5M heist began with zero-days in two third-party security appliances

Bitget said Wednesday that the attackers who stole $387.5 million last week got in by exploiting a zero-day flaw in third-party security products. Investigations by SlowMist and Mandiant found the intruders compromised two security appliances with zero-day exploits, dropped web shells on one of them and planted malware and a custom withdrawal tool on the exchange’s production wallet job server, then launched the theft after midnight on September 25. SlowMist traced the earliest malicious activity in available logs to August 31; Mandiant says unauthorized privileged access to appliances “A and B” was gained on September 24. CEO Gracy Chen has blamed North Korean hackers, citing IP behavior and on-chain analysis. Bitget has not named the vendor or the flaw, and runs a 5% recovery bounty. Earlier, it reopened Bitcoin withdrawals on September 28 in a phased restart (BleepingComputer, Sept 30).

Zero-day

Citrix patches the two exploited NetScaler zero-days — upgrade now

Citrix on Sunday published security bulletin CTX697096, fixing eight NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway flaws and stating that exploitation of CVE-2026-88771 and CVE-2026-88772 “on unmitigated NetScaler deployments has been observed.” CVE-2026-88771 (CVSS v4 9.5) is an improper input validation flaw that lets a remote, unauthenticated attacker execute arbitrary commands on every customer-managed ADC and Gateway deployment, including default configurations. CVE-2026-88772 (CVSS v4 9.5) is a memory overflow leading to RCE or denial of service when DTLS is enabled — which it is by default on VPN virtual servers unless set to -dtls OFF. Fixed builds: 14.1-73.37 and 13.1-64.23 (ADC/Gateway), 14.1-73.37 FIPS, and 13.1-37.279 for 13.1-FIPS/NDcPP. Appliances patched in August for CVE-2026-19490 (builds 14.1-73.32 / 13.1-63.21) remain vulnerable. Admins on 13.1 should run show ns variable first: if it lists variables, Citrix advises going to 13.1-64.24 to avoid a reboot-loop issue. The new builds also stop accepting unsigned SAML assertions. Citrix did not say who is behind the attacks or when exploitation began; it is offering generic indicators of compromise through NetScaler Console, warns they may miss real compromises, and patching does not evict an attacker already inside. Later on Sunday CISA added both flaws to its KEV catalog, with a federal deadline of Wednesday, Sept. 30 (WindowsForum, citing CISA and BleepingComputer). The fixes follow a weekend in which organizations took NetScalers offline on shutdown advice attributed to the Dutch NCSC, after watchTowr said the flaws were being exploited.

Precaution

Kiteworks urged customers to take systems offline for a nine-hour window over a possible attack

Secure file-sharing vendor Kiteworks advised all customers to facilitate a nine-hour precautionary shutdown window last weekend, in their local time zone, after what it called credible threat intelligence from federal intelligence authorities that a threat actor may attempt to target some Kiteworks systems. Self-managed customers (on-premises, AWS or Azure) were told to shut down their own systems; Kiteworks said it would shut down the systems it hosts on their behalf. The company says the advisory is preventative, that it has no indication Kiteworks or customer systems were compromised, and that all known vulnerabilities are addressed in its current release, 9.5.1. Subsidiaries including Zivver, DRACOON, totemo and ownCloud are not affected. Correction: earlier editions, following BleepingComputer, described a six-hour window; Kiteworks' own press release says nine hours.
