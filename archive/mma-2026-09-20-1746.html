Top story

Gane is the undisputed heavyweight champion — and Madison Square Garden gets two title fights on 14 November

Ciryl Gane has been elevated from interim to undisputed UFC heavyweight champion, announced on the UFC 331 main-card broadcast on Saturday 19 September. He did not fight that night; Tom Aspinall vacated the belt while recovering from the eye poke he suffered against Gane at UFC 321.

The first defence is set: Gane vs Josh Hokit at UFC 334, Saturday 14 November, Madison Square Garden. The co-main is Kayla Harrison vs Amanda Nunes for the women’s bantamweight title — Harrison’s first defence, against a Hall of Famer, in a fight the promotion is billing as its “most anticipated women’s fight ever”. It is the UFC’s 13th visit to New York City and its first since UFC 322 in November 2025.

Hokit is 10-0 as a professional and 4-0 in the UFC, the No. 4 contender, with stoppages of Derrick Lewis and Denzel Freeman and a decision over Curtis Blaydes at UFC 327 — three wins in six months after coming through the 2025 Contender Series.

One inconsistency, named rather than smoothed. A reference page for UFC 334 read this run still carries the interim-champion notation against Gane’s name, and a separate listing gives him a title-win date of 19 September. Neither is adopted: he was announced as champion that night and did not compete. The champions board below records it that way.

Bonuses & payouts

  • Over $500,000 in bonus money was awarded, with Dana White adding $50,000 of his own.
  • Sean Sharaf’s night, itemised. Base pay $60,000 ($30,000 to show, $30,000 to win); a $50,000 MobLand “Chaos” promotional bonus, which White said he would personally match with another $50,000; plus a $25,000 finish bonus — $185,000 total. Gable Steveson earned $160,000 for the 12-second loss, a larger base than Sharaf’s.
  • Fight and performance bonuses carried from an earlier edition today: Fight of the Night to Van and Pantoja, $100,000 each; Performance of the Night to Arman Tsarukyan and Casey O’Neill, $100,000 each.
  • Top payouts: Alexandre Pantoja $650,000 — the largest disclosed — and Joshua Van $500,000, with a card payroll of roughly $4.6 million. Two fighters topped $500,000.
  • A figure that disagrees with itself. Sharaf’s reported debt is given as around $600,000 in a source read this run and as $688,000 in one carried from earlier today. Both are printed; neither is adopted.

Rankings & business

  • Rankings movement. Josh Hokit is the No. 4 heavyweight contender heading into a title shot. No other ranking changes were stated in any source read this run.
  • Broadcast. Paramount is in the first year of a seven-year, $7.7 billion media rights deal with the UFC, which is owned by TKO Group. UFC 332 on 3 October will be the first numbered-event main card to air on CBS.
  • The viewership benchmark. UFC Freedom 250 drew 17 million total viewers on Paramount+ across the US and Latin America, and 34 million total global viewers once Australia, China, India, South Korea, New Zealand and the UK were counted. The US average of 7.0 million made it the most-watched UFC event ever domestically.
  • Social. Across that fight week (8–14 June), UFC social accounts recorded 126 billion total views, 5 billion engagements and 536,000 new followers.
