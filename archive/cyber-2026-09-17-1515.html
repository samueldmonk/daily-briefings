Top Story

NewExploitedNetwork edge

Cisco hardens its firewall line — and two of the flaws are already being used

Cisco published a software hardening release for Secure Firewall ASA, Threat Defense and Management Center on 16 September 2026 at 16:00 GMT, rated Critical. Rather than one CVE per bug, Cisco grouped the findings by vulnerability class and assigned a single CVE to each CWE grouping — eight in all, from CVSS 9.9 down to 7.5. The two highest are CVE-2026-20329 (CWE-703, improper handling of exceptional conditions) and CVE-2026-20330 (CWE-707, structured data not verified as well-formed).

The advisory states that two of the vulnerabilities are known to be actively exploited, and attaches that footnote to the CWE-284 improper-access-control class — CVE-2026-20332, CVSS 9.0 — pointing to the separate FMC Static Credential and FMC Authentication Bypass advisories. A search-level summary read this run instead asserted that CVE-2026-20329 and CVE-2026-20330 were the exploited pair; that reading is refused and the vendor advisory is followed.

There are no workarounds. Fixed ASA releases are 9.16.4.103, 9.18.4.94, 9.20.4.49, 9.22.3.26, 9.23.1.47 and 9.24.1.26; fixed FTD/FMC releases are 7.0.10, 7.2.12, 7.4.8, 7.6.6, 7.7.13, 10.0.2 and 10.1.0. Snort rules 67121–67122 are published.

One detail worth noting from the advisory’s own Source section: the flaws were found during internal security testing “using existing testing processes as well as frontier AI models.”

Patch Priority

CVE-2026-76461 — Cisco Secure Email Gateway (AsyncOS), CVSS 9.8. A crafted email carrying SQL statements is enough: pre-authentication SQL injection in the email-parsing logic leads to arbitrary SQL execution and then command execution as root on the underlying OS. Cisco PSIRT learned of exploitation while resolving a TAC support case and disclosed on 14 September; CISA added it to KEV the same day with a remediation date of 17 September 2026 — that is today, 0 days left, and a requirement for forensic investigation. There is no workaround. Upgrade to AsyncOS 15.5.5-014, 16.0.4-302 or 16.5.0-780; Cisco recommends 16.5.0-780.

Threat Actor Spotlight

EspionagePRC-linkedModerate confidence

Red Heron

A suspected PRC-linked, Chinese-speaking actor that turned a public proof-of-concept into an automated intrusion framework within days of disclosure. The target was CVE-2026-60004, a CVSS 9.8 flaw in Gitea affecting versions 1.17 through 1.27.0 and patched in 1.27.1 on 27 July 2026. The tooling registered accounts, exploited vulnerable servers, stole repositories and removed selected traces.

Acronis’ Threat Research Unit counts 1,386 Gitea instances scanned across seven countries, plus a separate dataset of 477 Taiwan-based systems, and 13 confirmed compromises across six countries: Taiwan (4), the United States (4), Canada (2), Argentina (1), Qatar (1) and Sri Lanka (1). Targets were labelled in Simplified Chinese and span defence, election, energy, aerospace, telecommunications, government, public safety and research.

The intrusions ran on from source-code theft to persistent access, credential collection and lateral movement, including root on a three-node Proxmox cluster, and are linked to a newly documented Linux implant called JITTERLY carrying an embedded LD_PRELOAD rootkit named SIXZUT. Attribution is stated at moderate confidence.

Breaches & Incidents

NewFintechExtortion

Revolut: $3M demanded after five months of forged legal requests

A threat actor using the moniker IAmNotAVillain publicly demanded $3 million from Revolut — 6,000 XMR, worth about $2.9 million as of early Thursday — after siphoning data for roughly five months. The access came from an infostealer infection on a government employee’s account, which was then used to send fraudulent legal requests to Revolut Bank UAB, the Lithuania-based subsidiary; obliged to answer law-enforcement requests, Revolut complied. At least 680 accounts are affected, with passports, email addresses, phone numbers and financial information exposed. A public ransom demand is unusual and normally signals a target that has refused to engage.

RansomwareGovernment

Berlin: Rhysida publishes roughly 6TB after a refused demand

Berlin’s state government is dealing with the fallout after the Rhysida extortion group published roughly 6 terabytes of stolen data, reported at that volume by 11 September. The leak followed a refused demand of 30 bitcoin, close to €2 million at the time.

HealthcareShinyHunters

McKesson: attackers claim 284 million patient records

The incident was discovered 25 August; attackers claim 284 million patient records including names, addresses, dates of birth and Social Security numbers. The figure is the attackers’ claim as reported, not a company-confirmed count.

HealthcareSupply chain

Veradigm: breach traced to a third-party vendor

The healthcare technology company disclosed a breach after a cybersecurity incident at one of its third-party vendors exposed patients’ personal data. No record count is stated by the sources read this run.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • CVE-2026-76461 — Cisco Secure Email Gateway (AsyncOS). Due 17 September 2026 (due today — 0 days left). Added 14 Sep, due 17 Sep — a three-day window under the risk-based directive. Pre-authentication SQL injection in email parsing leading to root-level command execution.
  • CVE-2026-76460 — Cisco Identity Services Engine. Due 19 September 2026 (2 days left). Added 16 Sep, due 19 Sep (a Saturday). CVSS 10.0 API authentication bypass; Cisco found it while working a TAC support case.
  • CVE-2026-87886 — Acronis Backup for cPanel & WHM / Plesk (Linux). Due 19 September 2026 (2 days left). Added 16 Sep, due 19 Sep. Insecure permissions allowing a low-privileged authenticated user to escalate without user interaction. No CVSS stated by the sources read this run.
  • CVE-2026-84869 — ConnectWise ScreenConnect. Due 14 September 2026 (overdue by 3 days). Due 14 Sep. Carried from this site’s standing corrections ledger, not re-fetched this run.
  • CVE-2026-59310 — VMware vCenter Server (Syslog service). Due 21 August 2026 (overdue by 27 days). Reported remediation date 21 Aug. Carried from this site’s standing corrections ledger, not re-fetched this run; the date is attributed, not asserted.

Deadlines are assigned per CVE under the risk-based directive that replaced the old flat three-week window, so a three-day window is normal and is not “corrected” here. cisa.gov returned empty on direct fetch again this run, so the catalog itself was not read; the 16 September dates come from Security Affairs’ report of the alert. One discrepancy is worth stating plainly: that report’s headline names a Google Pixel flaw alongside the Cisco ISE and Acronis entries, while CISA’s own alert for 16 September is titled as adding two vulnerabilities. The Pixel flaw is therefore not attached to that alert here.
