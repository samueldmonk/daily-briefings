The Lead

Records on both boards: Palantir's "otherworldly" quarter and a 5% oil slide power the tape (as of ~1 PM ET)

The rally that opened August is compounding. On a Trading Economics board reading at roughly 12:35 PM ET Tuesday, the Dow stood at 53,932.95, up 754.54 points or 1.42%; the S&P 500 at 7,685.04, up 84.54 or 1.11%; and the Nasdaq-100 at 29,375.60, up 598.80 or 2.08%. The Russell 2000 was up 0.99% at 3,011.38, with the VIX flat at 15.88. The same page's latest index tick put the S&P 500 at 7,678, up 1.02%, just off the intraday all-time high of 7,682.05 set earlier in the session. By roughly 1 PM ET the advance had steepened further: Yahoo Finance's live coverage had the Dow up about 1.7%, the S&P 500 up about 1.5% and pacing for a record closing high, and the Nasdaq Composite up more than 2% — extending Monday's record Dow close. Every index level above reconciles exactly to Monday's verified closes plus the quoted point change.

Two engines are driving it. First, earnings: Palantir delivered what CEO Alex Karp called an "otherworldly" quarter and the stock rocketed as much as 27% (CNBC). With roughly 85% of S&P 500 companies beating estimates and aggregate profit growth tracking above 47% this season, "investors have a genuine, bottom-up reason to stay invested," Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn told TheStreet. Second, oil: crude climbed overnight on fresh reports of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz, then reversed hard — WTI fell 4.6% to about $76.63 and Brent 3.9% to about $80.50 by midday — after Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said the U.S. and Iran could reach an agreement to reopen the Strait, and Qatar signaled progress toward a short-term deal (Trading Economics) — natural gas and gasoline fell roughly 4% alongside crude. The diplomacy remains fragile: Iran disputed that talks were even happening, and President Trump called the Islamic Republic "unbelievably duplicitous," adding "this is the last chance for them to sign a good document."

On the data front, the morning's JOLTS report showed job openings fell by 178,000 to 7.359 million — the first labor-market reading in the run-up to Friday's July jobs report. After the bell: SpaceX's first earnings report as a public company, plus AMD.

Movers & Drivers

▲ Earnings

Palantir (PLTR)

Rocketed as much as 27% (CNBC) after Q2 revenue of $1.935 billion (vs. $1.81B expected) grew 93% year over year, adjusted EPS of $0.41 beat the $0.35 consensus, and U.S. commercial revenue more than doubled. The full-year revenue outlook was raised from a $7.656 billion midpoint to $8.154 billion. CEO Alex Karp: "otherworldly." By early afternoon the stock had settled to a roughly 20% gain near $151 (Benzinga movers list).

▲ Dow leader

Caterpillar (CAT)

Led the Dow with an 11% gain at the open (Trading Economics) after quarterly sales and revenues topped $20 billion for the first time. The gain pared through the morning — midday coverage had CAT, the Dow's second-largest component by weight, up about 6% (Yahoo Finance). Cisco (+3.9%) and Goldman Sachs (+3.0%) rounded out the early Dow leaders.

▲ Earnings

Zebra Technologies (ZBRA) & Gartner (IT)

Two of the session's biggest earnings pops outside megacap land: barcode-scanner maker Zebra surged 18.6% after beating second-quarter estimates, and research firm Gartner jumped 16.3% on better-than-expected results. Online retailer Wayfair also surged nearly 19% on its Q2 beat.

▼ Decliner

Aptiv (APTV)

Fell 15.5% — the day's sharpest large-cap decline — after the automotive-technology company issued weak forward guidance and reported a large sequential revenue decline following its recent business spin-offs.

▼ Energy

Crude's round trip

Oil climbed overnight on fresh reports of attacks in the Strait of Hormuz (TheStreet), then reversed: by midday WTI was down 4.6% near $76.63 and Brent down 3.9% near $80.50 after Treasury Secretary Bessent said a deal to reopen the Strait could come soon. WTI's session range spanned $76.60–$82.32 (Investing.com).

▲ Small-cap pops

Ibotta (IBTA) & Backblaze (BLZE)

The session's biggest percentage movers were small caps: cash-back platform Ibotta jumped 50.1% to $36.89 after better-than-expected Q2 results and above-consensus Q3 sales guidance, and cloud-storage firm Backblaze gained 50.8% to $23.51 after beating Q2 estimates and raising full-year sales guidance (Benzinga movers list).

▲ Follow-through

Amazon (AMZN)

Monday's engine is still running: Amazon's market value closed above $3 trillion for the first time in Monday's session and the stock remains at records as hyperscaler earnings continue to validate AI capital spending. Palantir's guidance hike Tuesday reinforced the same AI-demand story.
