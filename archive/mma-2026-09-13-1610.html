Top Story

New

Silva finished Delgado, called his shot — and then said he was knocked out in camp

Jean Silva defeated Jose Miguel Delgado by submission — a rear-naked choke, applied one-armed — at 2:57 of round three in the Noche UFC main event at Desert Diamond Arena in Glendale, Arizona, on Saturday 12 September. Delgado bowled Silva over with a punching flurry in the first, but the Brazilian hung on, took the second and third, and took Delgado’s back after a stunned low single-leg attempt. Silva is now 7-1 inside the Octagon with six finishes.

He spent none of the post-fight interview being modest. Silva declared he will be the next UFC champion and called for the winner of Alexander Volkanovski vs. Movsar Evloev, picking Evloev: “I think Evloev wins. It’ll be me and him. But if Evloev pulls out, then it’s me and Volkanovski.”

New today: Silva has revealed that he was knocked out several times during the Noche UFC training camp, disclosed while defending the gameplan he fought to. It reframes a win that already looked like a survival job in round one, and it is the kind of detail that usually surfaces only after the hand is raised.

Sean King III — 6-0, and a bonus on debut

A direct signing who arrived 6-0 and skipped the Contender Series season he had been slated for. He made his UFC debut at featherweight on the Noche UFC card on 12 September in Glendale, against Rosas — a surname only, as stated in the source, and not completed here — and walked away with Performance of the Night.

Prospect

Tommy McMillen — a three-round striking record in his second fight

McMillen landed 252 significant strikes on 517 attempts against Marwan Rahiki, breaking Nate Diaz’s three-round UFC record and placing third all-time behind Max Holloway’s 290 (vs. Kattar) and Rob Font’s 271 (vs. Vera) — both of those over five rounds. It was his second Octagon appearance, and he was dropped early in it. Carried from this site’s standing corrections file, where it was sourced in an earlier run.
