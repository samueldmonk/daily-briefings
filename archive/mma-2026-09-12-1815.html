Top Story

Martinez takes the main-card opener and runs it to eleven straight, and the night's biggest stakes are the next fight in

David Martinez defeated Dan Ige by unanimous decision in the bantamweight bout that opened the Noche UFC main card, on scores reported as 30-27, 30-27, 29-28. It is his eleventh consecutive win and his fourth in the UFC. The shape of it, from the recaps: Martinez chopped at Ige's lead leg and kept him moving backwards for most of the fifteen minutes, and in the third round caught him with a punch that put him down on one knee. The result is carried by three independent post-fight pages — a LowKickMMA recap headlined on the win streak, a BJPenn results post, and an MMA Insight report filing it under UFC Fight Night 288 — which is the standard this page has been applying all night: a page written after the fight it describes, never a live blog with an empty field.

What is still to come is the part with something on the line. The women's flyweight bout between Manon Fiorot and Alexa Grasso is a title eliminator by the fighters' own account — Fiorot has said publicly that the winner gets the next shot at the belt, and multiple outlets carried that framing into the week. A Yahoo preview put their walk to the cage at roughly 6:15 PM ET. That belt is currently vacant, and is being contested by Natalia Silva and Wang Cong at UFC 332 on 3 October — so tonight's winner is fighting for the shot at whoever comes out of that, not for a shot at a sitting champion. Both women arrive off wins: Fiorot knocked out Jasmine Jasudavicius in May after losing her title bid to Valentina Shevchenko, and Grasso knocked out Maycee Barber in Seattle in March for a $100,000 bonus, having lost twice to Shevchenko before that.

Nothing above the opener is being reported yet, and this page will not infer it. The heavyweight bout between Waldo Cortes-Acosta and Curtis Blaydes should have finished by now on the card's running order, but no post-fight page naming a winner has appeared in anything searched — UFC.com's own main-card results article and its official scorecards page were both fetched in full this run and both still carry nothing but the pre-fight previews, with every scorecard heading empty. A prediction page exists for that bout. It is not a result, and it is not used. The rest of the table below stays marked undecided until a recap says otherwise.

Prospect Watch

Prospect

Regina Tarin — 9-0, and through a ranked opponent at 21

The Mexico City flyweight arrived 8-0 off a February debut win and leaves the Noche UFC opener 9-0 with a unanimous-decision win over the division's No. 15. Beating a ranked veteran with a 22-fight record in a second UFC appearance is the kind of result that reorders a prospect's timeline.

Prospect

Sean King III — 7-0 after 36 seconds

The New Orleans featherweight arrived 6-0 per UFC.com and leaves 7-0, having slammed Jessie Rosas (8-1) unconscious at 0:36 of the first round in a meeting of newcomers. He was a −200 favourite. Sourced in an earlier edition, not this one: he is 22 years old, and he was originally booked for Dana White's Contender Series before being moved onto this card on nine days' notice. A knockout that fast, on a card built for highlights, is the kind of debut-tier win that gets a fighter booked again quickly — though no next booking is announced in anything read.

Prospect

Thomas "Tommy" Gantt — still unbeaten, and through the card's best veteran

The former ACC wrestling champion at NC State arrived 12-0 with 1 no contest and a −450 price, and submitted the lightweight veteran Drakkar Klose (16-3-1) — who fights out of Glendale itself — with a reverse triangle at 2:46 of round three. Two rounds of fence control, then the back, then elbows into the finish. Daniel Cormier cornered him, having been on broadcast duty that night, after Gantt's own coach fell ill before the fight. The naming varies by source and both are real: UFC.com's copy says Tommy Gantt; its own athlete link, Sherdog and Cageside Press say Thomas Gantt.

Prospect

Yousri Belgaroui — a sixth straight win, on the card's widest prelim line

The middleweight, fighting out of Danbury, Connecticut by way of Amsterdam, took a five-fight win streak into a matchup with Djorden Santos (11-3) as a −900 favourite — the shortest price on the preliminary card — and stopped him by TKO in the first round, per a headline carried by both MMA Mania and Yahoo. The finishing sequence described in search results is not published here; the headline is what is sourced.

Prospect

Zhu Rong — 28-6, and past the card's hottest Mexican lightweight

The Sichuan lightweight, listed by UFC.com as Rongzhu and by Cageside Press and Sherdog as Zhu Rong, came in a +145 underdog and beat Rafa García (19-4), who had been looking for his second win of 2026 in front of a Mexican-Independence-weekend crowd. The winner comes from a published headline; the scoreline does not, and is not asserted.

Refused This Run

Two retired, none standing on a winner, and a new trap avoided

Retired, and the accounting is uncomfortable. Both remaining refusals fell this run to fetched official-result lines. Bahamondes def. Salikhov, unanimous decision, 30-27, 30-27, 29-28 — refused across four editions as a phantom — is correct in every particular, including all three cards. Elliott def. Cháirez, unanimous decision is also correct, and the only page that had been naming a winner was an AI-prediction page that gave the opposite answer one edition earlier. So of the five prelim refusals this page has now retired, every single one was a true statement. That is the ledger being honest against itself: the standard withheld nothing false on this card, and the correct conclusion is not that the standard was too strict but that the source list was too narrow, which is the change already made.

New this run, and refused — the trap this site is on record about. A search for tonight's results returned a CBS Sports article headlined “Noche UFC results, highlights: Diego Lopes knocks out Jean Silva with spinning elbow that causes massive gash” — a detailed, well-reported piece about a Noche UFC main event featuring Jean Silva, with a stated finish, round and time. It was fetched, and its byline date is 13 September 2025: it is last year's Noche UFC, in San Antonio, a different opponent and a different result. Jean Silva headlines this event for the second straight year, which is exactly why the old article outranks the new one in search. Nothing from it appears on this page. This is the failure mode this site's standing corrections file warns about in plain terms — heavily covered old events outrank newer ones — and the only thing that caught it was checking a byline date on an article that read entirely plausibly.

Logged rather than published: “33 seconds.” A search return this run again gives Sean King III's finish as a 33-second win; this page publishes 0:36, from a filled result field. Both cannot be right and neither materially changes the fight. The result field wins on source class, and the caption's figure is recorded here.

Still refused, unchanged. The Athlon Sports prediction pages stay unpublished for the reason they always have, notwithstanding that one of them eventually landed on the right man. Any champions list seating Valentina Shevchenko at 125 pounds is stale and refused on sight. That regression appeared in the champions cross-checks of the four previous editions; no champions cross-check was run this edition, so the board below is carried unchanged from the last verified one rather than re-tested. Alex Pereira is not the light-heavyweight champion however often a cached page says so, though the cross-check read in the previous edition did not make that error. And a Sports Illustrated headline returned this run about Grasso and Shevchenko battling to a draw is a 2023 Noche UFC result, refused for the same reason as the CBS piece above.

The wider rule, after five retirements. What held: “def.” carries no evidential weight; a set of winners in correct bout order is what a picks list looks like by construction; favourites winning is not confirmation. What is now settled: precision is not evidence and not disqualifying either — “30-27, 30-27, 29-28” was refused for being too specific to trust and turned out to be transcribed from a real scorecard. What is added: a byline date is part of the evidence, because an annual event with a returning headliner produces old articles that match every keyword of the new one.
