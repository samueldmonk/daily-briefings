The Lead

As of ~1:45 PM ET: Gains fade — Dow +0.11%, S&P 500 −0.02%, Nasdaq −0.13% — as the 10-year yield climbs to 5.11% and Nvidia stays down about 5% after Huang resigns

Latest read. Yahoo Finance’s market strip with about 2 hours 15 minutes left in the session (≈1:45 PM ET) had the S&P 500 at 7,617.75, down 1.63 points (−0.02%); the Dow at 52,542.17, up 55.68 (+0.11%; Dow quote stamped 1:45:58 PM ET); and the Nasdaq Composite at 25,432.34, down 33.85 (−0.13%). The Russell 2000 was −0.92%, the 10-year Treasury yield 5.11% (+0.11), the VIX 17.70 (+1.20%), and December WTI crude $88.10 (+1.81%) (Yahoo Finance). The live blog’s quote chips (post updated 1:06 PM ET) showed Nvidia −4.98% and Intel −0.94%; Yahoo noted some chipmakers including Intel and Micron fell while Nvidia rival AMD rose (Yahoo Finance).

Earlier. At ≈12:51 PM ET, Yahoo’s quotes had the Dow +0.37% (52,681.73 at 12:51:40 PM), the S&P 500 +0.26% and the Nasdaq +0.21%; “Stocks rebounded from session lows in midday trading as tech pared losses and energy stocks gained,” Yahoo’s Grace O’Donnell wrote around noon, when the 10-year yield was up 8 basis points to 5.09% (prior edition — Yahoo Finance). Since then the S&P 500 and Nasdaq have slipped back below Wednesday’s close.

Nvidia trims losses. Nvidia’s quote at 12:51:39 PM ET was $165.44, −$8.29 (−4.77%), and −4.98% on Yahoo’s quote chip at the ≈1:45 PM read; the stock “sank as much as 13% in early trading” before paring the drop to about 5% as traders weighed the company’s future without Huang. Other notable movers Yahoo flagged included Alibaba, Alphabet, Nebius and Intuitive Machines (Yahoo Finance). Former Goldman Sachs CEO Lloyd Blankfein posted on X: “Sad about Jensen. Shakespearean. But as [Charles] de Gaulle said, ‘The graveyards are filled with indispensable men’” (David Hollerith — Yahoo Finance). Bernstein’s Stacy Rasgon, who downgraded the stock overnight and cut his price target to $200 from $400, said fundamentals “are clearly still intact” but questioned what happens to Nvidia’s “myriad of investments in the space, various backstops and residual guarantees” (per Bloomberg, via TheStreet Pro).

Midday movers. As of 11:30 a.m. ET the Dow led the major indexes, up 0.29%, with the S&P 500 +0.19% and the Nasdaq Composite +0.15%; industrials and technology led sector gains while healthcare lagged, and gold fell 3.05% to $3,878.30. In its wrap published at 12:45 PM ET, the Motley Fool said AMD “surged 6.30% following an analyst upgrade,” HubSpot jumped 7.54% after a leadership overhaul aimed at its AI strategy, Super Micro Computer tumbled 10.25%, and Alibaba fell 6.49% “amid reports that U.S. regulators are considering additional restrictions on remote access to advanced AI chips.” Nvidia’s day range as of 12:35 PM ET was $148.80–$167.14, with $148.80 also marking the bottom of its 52-week range (Motley Fool). Separately, Citigroup lowered its Nvidia price target to $225 from $285 (Motley Fool headline — Motley Fool).

Off the session lows. FXEmpire’s James Hyerczyk noted the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite had turned higher after early declines of 0.8% and 1.5%, respectively; at 12:27 PM ET (17:27 GMT) he had the S&P 500 +0.31%, the Nasdaq +0.32% and the Dow +0.44%, with AMD and Broadcom up 4% and 5% as investors appeared to move into Nvidia competitors, and Nvidia down about 4% after an earlier double-digit decline. He also cited Treasury yields and oil rising after President Trump said “We don’t need a deal with Iran” (FXEmpire). That 12:27 PM read predates the ≈12:51 PM Yahoo quotes above, which remain the latest timed read found this run.

Strategy and bitcoin slide. Strategy (MSTR) sank as much as 6% Thursday after MSCI confirmed Wednesday evening it will remove the company from two US stock indexes; bitcoin fell below $72,000 and is down about 8% so far in November, pressured by expectations of a more hawkish Fed (David Hollerith — Yahoo Finance). As of 10 a.m. ET the VIX was around 18.26, just above its 2026 average of 18.2 — a relatively muted reaction to the Nvidia upheaval (Yahoo Finance).

Winners and losers. AMD rose about 3% and Broadcom jumped as much as 6% Thursday morning as investors bet on Nvidia’s rivals; CoreWeave dropped 12%, Nebius sank 19%, SanDisk fell 7% in early trading, TSMC more than 2%, Intel 3% (Nvidia is an investor) and Micron and SK Hynix roughly 3% (Yahoo’s Ines Ferré, 9:37 AM ET — Yahoo Finance).

Breaking. “NVIDIA (NASDAQ: NVDA) Board of Directors announced today that Jensen Huang has resigned as President, Chief Executive Officer, and a member of the Board, effective immediately,” the board said. CFO Colette Kress will serve as interim CEO while continuing as CFO, and the board is searching for a permanent CEO among internal and external candidates (Tom’s Hardware; CBS News, 8:57 AM ET). Nvidia announced the resignation at 8:48 AM ET; newly appointed Chair Harvey Jones said the board has full confidence in Kress and expects operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap to continue without interruption, and Q3 results remain scheduled for Nov. 17 (Business Upturn).

The stock. NVDA was quoted at $154.68 pre-market at 8:55 AM ET, down $19.05 or 10.97% from Wednesday’s $173.73 close and about 5.8% below the previous 52-week low of $164.27 (Business Upturn).

Pre-open futures (superseded by the open). CNBC’s live blog reported Nasdaq-100 futures down 1.2%, S&P 500 futures down 0.6% and Dow futures down 47 points, or 0.1%, with Broadcom and AMD up about 3% each pre-market after the resignation (CNBC; time of that read not stated). The last timestamped read, Yahoo Finance at ≈8:08 AM ET, had Nasdaq futures −1.05% and S&P futures −0.46% (prior edition). Yahoo’s ≈8:39 AM ET strip had Nasdaq futures −1.37% and S&P futures −0.75% (prior edition) — the cash open was milder.

Data. Initial jobless claims rose 9,000 to 200,000 in the week ended Nov. 7, above the 195,000 Reuters poll; the prior week was revised to 191,000, the four-week average held at 195,250 (lowest since October 1969) and continuing claims fell 20,000 to 1.697 million (Reuters via Investing.com). Existing home sales are due at 10 AM ET (CNBC).
