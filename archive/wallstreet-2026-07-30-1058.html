The Lead

The morning after: stocks rebound as Microsoft’s Azure blowout splits the AI trade — while GDP slows, core inflation sticks and the U.S. strikes back at Iran (as of ~10:30 AM ET)

The tape. A day after the Dow’s 1,153-point plunge on the Fed’s “hawkish hold,” U.S. stocks are staging a comeback led by Microsoft and the chip complex: by mid-morning CNBC’s live coverage had the Nasdaq Composite up about 2% and the S&P 500 up about 1%, with 24/7 Wall St’s live blog reading the S&P up about 0.63% (49 points), the Dow up 0.4% (208 points) and the Nasdaq up about 1.57% just before 10 AM — and its index board showing the S&P at +0.96%, the Dow +0.56% and the Nasdaq 100 +2.12% at our ~10:05 AM check. The engine is Microsoft, up as much as 15% in morning trading and on track for its best day since March 2020 (CNBC), after fiscal Q4 EPS of $4.74 on $90.01 billion in revenue, Azure growth of 43% at constant currency and Azure’s first $100 billion fiscal year (CNBC/Yahoo Finance). The semiconductor complex rallied with it — the iShares Semiconductor ETF was up more than 7% (CNBC) after Wednesday’s rout. The drag: Meta, down 8–9% in early trading and extending what CNBC calls a record losing streak, after its ~$1 EPS miss, a soft revenue outlook, no spending guidance for 2027 and a 91% collapse in second-quarter free cash flow (CNBC/Quartz/Yahoo Finance). This morning’s premarket casualties included Teladoc, down 18.5% on a revenue miss, and Cigna, off nearly 4% after earnings (CNBC premarket movers).

The data. The economy is cooling faster than the inflation is: the BEA’s advance estimate put second-quarter GDP growth at a 1.5% annual rate — below the 1.8% economists expected (24/7 Wall St) and down from Q1’s 2.1% — on consumer spending, investment and exports partly offset by lower government outlays (BEA); FXStreet’s preview had consensus higher still, at 2.1%. The Fed’s preferred gauge offered only partial relief: the June PCE price index slipped 0.1% on the month, taking the annual rate down to 3.7% from May’s 4.1%, while core PCE rose 0.1% monthly and held at 3.3% a year — matching expectations but still far above the 2% target (24/7 Wall St/CNBC/FXStreet). It all lands on a market still chewing on Wednesday’s 9-3 hold: Chair Kevin Warsh declined to spell out what would trigger a hike, suggested the Fed could even reconsider using PCE as its main target, and left economists calling his guidance hard to follow (24/7 Wall St) — with the 30-year Treasury yield sitting at a multidecade high near 5.20% after Wednesday’s bond-market revolt (Yahoo Finance).

The war. Overnight the U.S. answered Iran’s intercepted missile attack: a wave of retaliatory strikes — the first to directly target Iran in days — hit dozens of Revolutionary Guard targets across the country, with Iranian state media reporting explosions in Bandar Abbas, Kish and Qeshm Island and across Khuzestan province (The Washington Post/CNN/Bloomberg). Tehran vowed to “punish the aggressor today” after what it called a “heavy wave” of strikes (CNBC), and CNN’s live coverage describes a widening war pulling in more countries. Crude, remarkably, took it in stride: after Wednesday’s ~8% Brent rally, oil steadied — Brent traded near $90 and WTI above $84, edging lower after the strikes (Bloomberg), with 24/7 Wall St’s morning read showing WTI down 60 cents at $83.86.

Tonight, the week’s last megacap test. Apple (consensus EPS $1.89, revenue expectations around $109–110 billion — with the focus on margins amid memory-chip price hikes), Amazon (consensus EPS $1.82, with AWS growth and capex under the microscope) and Reddit (consensus EPS $0.97) all report after the close (Yahoo Finance/TipRanks/Finance Calendar/indmoney). Polymarket traders give Apple a 90% chance of an EPS beat (TipRanks). The analyst desk is already repositioning: Bank of America reiterated its buy on Microsoft (“increasing validation of Microsoft’s AI strategy”), Wells Fargo raised its Target price target to $165 from $140, and Bernstein reiterated outperform on Nvidia and AMD on the neocloud build-out (24/7 Wall St, quoting CNBC).

Movers & Drivers

Microsoft (MSFT) +~15% New

Up as much as 15% in morning trading and on track for its best day since March 2020 (CNBC) — from +8.3% before the bell (Quartz) to +9% at the open (24/7 Wall St) to CNBC’s mid-morning 14–15% readings — after fiscal Q4 EPS of $4.74 vs $4.24 expected on $90.01B revenue, Azure +43% at constant currency and Azure’s first $100B fiscal year, with more than 30 million paid Microsoft 365 Copilot seats (CNBC/Yahoo Finance/Bloomberg). Bank of America reiterated its buy: “increasing validation of Microsoft’s AI strategy” (24/7 Wall St, quoting CNBC).

Meta Platforms (META) −8–9% New

Fell 8–9% in early trading — extending what CNBC calls a record losing streak — after the $6.18 EPS miss (vs ~$7.13), a light-low-end Q3 revenue guide, capex guidance raised at the low end to $130–145B, no spending guidance for 2027 and a 91% collapse in Q2 free cash flow to $784M (CNBC/Quartz/Yahoo Finance/Benzinga print). Yahoo Finance’s tech live blog frames the divide as Microsoft’s AI clarity vs Meta’s “lack of clarity.”

GDP slows, core PCE sticks New

Q2 GDP grew at a 1.5% annual rate (advance estimate) — below the 1.8% economists expected (24/7 Wall St) and Q1’s 2.1% — while June headline PCE fell 0.1% on the month (3.7% annually, down from 4.1%) and core PCE held at 3.3%, matching expectations but far above target (BEA/24/7 Wall St/CNBC). A slowing economy with sticky core inflation is exactly the bind three Fed dissenters flagged Wednesday — and Warsh’s suggestion the Fed might reconsider PCE as its target adds a fresh wrinkle (24/7 Wall St).

U.S. strikes Iran; crude steadies New

Overnight U.S. strikes hit dozens of Revolutionary Guard targets across Iran — explosions reported in Bandar Abbas, Kish, Qeshm and Khuzestan — in retaliation for Iran’s intercepted missile attack on U.S. forces; Tehran vows to “punish the aggressor today” (The Washington Post/CNN/CNBC). Oil, after Wednesday’s ~8% Brent surge, steadied rather than spiked: Brent near $90, WTI above $84 and edging lower (Bloomberg); 24/7 Wall St’s morning read had WTI down 60 cents at $83.86.

Semis rebound SOXX +7% New

The chip complex snapped back from Wednesday’s rout: the iShares Semiconductor ETF jumped more than 7% as Microsoft’s $100B Azure year re-lit the AI-infrastructure trade (CNBC), and Bloomberg’s premarket wrap had semiconductor giants rallying with Nasdaq-100 futures up 1.5% before the open. Bernstein reiterated outperform on Nvidia and AMD, arguing neoclouds “don’t have the balance sheets to build their own facilities at scale” — so the pick-and-shovel names win (24/7 Wall St, quoting CNBC).

Teladoc (TDOC) −18.5% New

Plunged 18.5% after second-quarter revenue fell short of expectations — the morning’s ugliest print on CNBC’s premarket movers list, which also flagged Cigna down nearly 4% after its Q2 report and Norwegian Cruise Line among the day’s notable movers (CNBC).

The analyst desk New

Wells Fargo raised its Target price target to $165 from $140 and stayed overweight (“momentum is on the side of the bulls”); Bank of America reiterated buy on Microsoft; Bernstein reiterated outperform on Nvidia and AMD on the neocloud build-out (24/7 Wall St, quoting CNBC).
