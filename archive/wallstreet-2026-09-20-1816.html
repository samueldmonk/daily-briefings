The lead

No session today — Friday’s print still stands, and the week ahead belongs to Trump and Xi

Markets are closed for the weekend. There is no intraday direction to report and no after-hours block; nothing on the tape carries a 19 or 20 September stamp. Everything below is Friday 18 September unless it says otherwise.

Friday closed a volatile week mixed and very nearly unchanged: the S&P 500 rose 0.17% to 7,650.50, the Nasdaq Composite advanced 0.39% to 26,522.55, and the Dow shed 95.40 points, or 0.18%, to 51,682.64. The session was shaped by rising Treasury yields, elevated oil and the Federal Reserve’s first rate increase in three years. Nine of the eleven broad sectors of the benchmark index finished in the green.

The week ahead has a set-piece. A Trump–Xi meeting is named as the potential wildcard for the 21–25 September week. The data calendar is light at the front: no noteworthy earnings land on Monday; ADP weekly employment change arrives Tuesday alongside AutoZone, Thor Industries and KB Home; S&P Global flash manufacturing PMI follows Wednesday. Oil, Treasury yields and rate expectations are the named swing factors into Monday’s open.

A conflict worth naming. A market-news summary read this run describes the same Friday as the Nasdaq gaining 0.23% and the S&P 500 falling 0.28% — both sign and magnitude at odds with the closes above, which are corroborated by CNBC, TheStreet and Yahoo Finance independently. The closing figures are what this page publishes; the alternative is printed here rather than buried.

Movers & drivers

About +11–12%

Coinbase (COIN)

Friday’s biggest named advance. One source has it rising 11.7% to $194.25. Bitcoin recovered sharply to an intraday high above $81,000 after a CFTC proposal to regulate crypto asset transactions and markets, and Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Needham and Compass Point raised or initiated targets clustering in the $200–$250 range. Four readings sit between 11% and 12.07% — +11.7%, +11.77%, +12.07% and “jumps 11.5%” — so the range is published rather than a single rounded figure.

NewHigher — no figure

Applied Materials (AMAT)

Up on a commitment to invest $5 billion in India over the next decade as the country builds out its semiconductor industry, with the money going to research, supply-chain expansion and workforce development. No percentage is published: sources read this run give +6.38%, +6.51%, an opening move of +3.03% and “rose almost 2%” for the same session, a spread far too wide to resolve.

Xenon Pharmaceuticals (XENE)

Named among the biggest decliners in Friday’s midday movers list after the company said it was pausing enrollment of new patients in its clinical studies for major depressive and bipolar depression treatments. The figure is a midday reading; no closing percentage has appeared in any source, so it is not called the day’s biggest decline.

About −5%

Netflix (NFLX)

Lower on a Wells Fargo downgrade, and named among the session’s notable decliners. The source says “almost 5%” and gives no exact figure, so none is printed.

Higher
