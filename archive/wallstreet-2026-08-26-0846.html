The lead

The inflation print has landed, the premarket board has not — and Nvidia is still eight hours away

U.S. markets are still not open — but the first of Wednesday’s two events is now behind us. The Bureau of Economic Analysis released the July Personal Income and Outlays report, containing the Fed’s preferred inflation gauge, at 8:30 a.m. ET. ⚠ As of 8:44 a.m. ET, no source fetched this run states the actual print. Every figure returned so far — headline PCE at 3.6% and core at 3.3% — comes from pre-release previews stating what economists expected, and this page will not pass a forecast off as a result. The number goes up here when a source states it as released.

What the premarket board does say, at 7:10 a.m. ET. Investing.com’s wire had Dow futures mostly unchanged, S&P 500 futures down 7 points, or 0.1%, and Nasdaq 100 futures down 66 points, or 0.2% — the same shape Yahoo Finance described overnight, with the tech leg carrying the softness into the print.

On Yahoo Finance’s own quote board, read at roughly 4:25 a.m. ET, S&P futures stood at 7,687.00, down 5.00 points or 0.07%; Dow futures at 53,701.00, up 56.00 or 0.10%; Nasdaq futures at 29,215.50, down 61.25 or 0.21%; and Russell 2000 futures at 3,014.70, up 0.30 or 0.01%. The VIX was 15.67, up 0.22 or 1.42%. A separate premarket board surfaced later in the morning had the ETF proxies telling the same story — SPY −0.04%, QQQ −0.21%, DIA +0.06%, IWM −0.03%. Neither reading is a session move; both are a market declining to take a position before 8:30.

Why the print carries so much weight. Investors find out whether the gauge continued its downward trajectory, stalled, or reaccelerated — the last of which, in Yahoo’s framing, would put more pressure on the central bank to raise rates. Economists expected a core PCE of 3.3%, unchanged from the month before, and a headline rate easing to 3.6% from June’s 3.7%. A hold at 3.3% would mark, on one preview’s count, the 65th consecutive month above the Fed’s 2% target, with futures pricing roughly a 38–40% chance of a quarter-point hike in September. Note the direction of that pressure: this is a market pricing the risk of a hike, not a cut. Bloomberg reported Tuesday that falling oil prices were curbing expectations for more than one Federal Reserve interest-rate hike in the coming year — the debate is over how many, not whether.

The second lands after the closing bell, when Nvidia opens its books in what Yahoo Finance calls “a quarterly yardstick for the health of the artificial intelligence trade.” Expectations are high, with investors increasingly focused on whether the enormous AI capital budgets of the last two years are producing returns. The company guided to $91.0 billion plus or minus two percent for the quarter; Visible Alpha consensus is $92.2 billion and a 40-analyst poll has $91.85 billion. Against the year-ago quarter’s $46.74 billion, consensus implies roughly a doubling. CrowdStrike, Williams-Sonoma, Okta and Abercrombie & Fitch also report Wednesday.

Per-share estimates for Nvidia’s quarter came back mutually inconsistent across the previews surfaced this run — one gives $2.08, another centres on $0.65–$0.68 adjusted — a spread that cannot be reconciled from the snippets available. No EPS estimate is published here. The revenue figures above each carry their stated source. Overnight futures are not an opening print; the levels quoted are stamped to the time they were read.

Carried · 8:18 editionEarningsAfter the close

Nvidia reports tonight, and the AI trade is the thing being measured

Nvidia opens its books after Wednesday’s close. The company’s own guide was $91.0 billion ±2%; Visible Alpha consensus sits at $92.2 billion and a 40-analyst poll at $91.85 billion, against $46.74 billion in the year-ago quarter. The Nasdaq-100 was already the weak leg of the futures complex overnight, down 0.2% while the Dow held flat — the index most exposed to the print is the one giving ground into it.

New−11.8%Premarket

Intuit’s guidance problem got worse overnight, not better

INTU tumbled 11.8% in premarket trading to $315.30 after fiscal 2027 guidance that fell well short of analyst expectations, overshadowing a strong fourth-quarter earnings beat, per Investing.com’s 7:10 a.m. ET wire. That is a materially deeper move than the after-hours prints this desk carried on Tuesday night — which ranged from “more than 7%” to 7.3% to 9% across three sources read at three different moments. All four readings stand as what they are: successive snapshots of a still-moving tape, not competing claims about one instant.

New−3.2%Cyber

Boston Scientific discloses a cybersecurity incident — and it is disrupting operations globally

BSX fell 3.2% premarket after the medical device maker disclosed a cybersecurity incident that disrupted its information technology systems on Tuesday. A company filing reported Wednesday says the incident affected certain IT systems and has led to global disruption in operations. No threat actor, ransomware family or CVE has been named, and the filing as reported gives limited further detail — so nothing further is asserted. This is also the lead item on The Cyber Wire today.

New+4.7%Semis

Semtech surges on a record quarter and a guide well clear of the street

SMTC jumped 4.7% pre-open after reporting record fiscal second-quarter results and a stronger-than-expected outlook. The company forecast revenue of $405 million to $415 million, putting the midpoint about 14% above Wall Street expectations, with adjusted EPS guidance of $1.02 to $1.08 against consensus of roughly $0.73. In a session whose tech complex is soft into the Nvidia print, this is the one chip name going the other way.

New+5.6%Solar

An FCC list, not an earnings report, moved SolarEdge

SEDG rose 5.6% premarket after UBS upgraded the solar equipment maker to Buy from Neutral and raised its price target to $42 from $36. The rationale is regulatory: the FCC’s decision to add foreign-produced power inverters to its national-security Covered List could tighten supply and strengthen domestically aligned suppliers, with the restrictions potentially affecting more than half of the U.S. inverter market. A separate wire put the move at nearly 7%; both prints are recorded here rather than averaged.

New−11.7%Biotech

A Phase 2 sub-study reading takes Spyre Therapeutics down double digits

Spyre Therapeutics stock fell 11.7% pre-open after the company released topline results from the rheumatoid arthritis sub-study of its Phase 2 SKYWAY basket trial evaluating SPY072, which fell short of Spyre’s internal threshold for advancing the drug as a standalone treatment. The trial name, the programme and the threshold language are the company’s own, as reported; no read-across to the rest of the basket is stated in the source and none is inferred here.

New−4.9% / −3.5%Software

nCino misses, GoDaddy gets downgraded, Sadot files

Three smaller moves from the same 7:10 a.m. wire. nCino declined 4.9% before the open after fiscal second-quarter earnings that significantly missed expectations. GoDaddy fell 3.5% after Wells Fargo downgraded it to Underweight from Equal Weight with a $76 price target — a break from a consensus that had held eight Buys and nine Holds; the stock has been under pressure since Q2, when full-year revenue guidance was narrowed to $5.215–$5.255 billion, midpoint below the street. Sadot Group climbed 10.3% after filing to register a secondary offering of up to 4.25 million shares by selling stockholders.

Tuesday closeRetail

DICK’S Sporting Goods still sits at the bottom of the board

Yahoo Finance’s trending-tickers board, read this run, has DKS at $124.31, down $55.02 or 30.68% — Tuesday’s close, and the single largest move on the tape. The company cut full-year GAAP EPS guidance to $10.94–$11.94 from $13.27–$14.27, roughly $2.33 off the midpoint, alongside adjusted EPS of $3.53 against $3.78 expected and revenue of $5.59B versus $5.65B — a revenue figure that was nonetheless up 53.2% year on year on the Foot Locker consolidation, where pro-forma comparable sales fell 3.6% and the operating loss was $31.9 million.

Tuesday closeSoftware

Intuit and Zoom carried Tuesday’s guidance damage into Wednesday

Both names closed lower Tuesday before their post-close reports made it worse: INTU $357.46, −$12.46 or 3.37% and ZM $100.92, −$3.91 or 3.73%, per Yahoo’s board this run. After the bell, Intuit’s FY27 guidance of $22.88–$23.12 EPS on revenue of $23.3–$23.5B came in under consensus of roughly $27.30 EPS on $23.74B, with slower-than-expected TurboTax growth cited. A market summary this run notes the two names as the reason Nasdaq futures turned lower overnight.
