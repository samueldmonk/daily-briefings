The Lead

Waller opens the door to a September hold, and stocks close at their best level in a month

Federal Reserve Governor Christopher Waller said Thursday he is leaning toward keeping interest rates steady at this month's meeting, provided upcoming inflation data holds up. In remarks prepared for a Reuters interview he said: “If this continues in the data due over the next two weeks, I would be inclined to support holding the target for the federal funds rate at its current setting.” He conceded inflation is “meaningfully above” the 2% target but said recent trends “suggest we are finally seeing some signs of disinflation” — a reading that sits against Chair Kevin Warsh's emphasis on stubborn inflation.

Market-implied odds of a hike at the September 15–16 FOMC meeting fell sharply after the remarks, to a 48.4% probability, down about 15 percentage points from Wednesday, per CME Group's FedWatch tool. The benchmark 10-year Treasury note yield last traded around 4.75% after the remarks.

The official Thursday close. The S&P 500 rose 1.06% to 7,747.71. The Dow Jones Industrial Average climbed 624.16 points, or 1.18%, to 53,686.11 — its best day since August 4. The Nasdaq Composite added 1.4% to 26,584.06. All three levels reconcile exactly against Wednesday's verified closes.

The shape of the day, for reference: at the 11:46 AM ET midday mark the Nasdaq Composite was up 1.35% to 26,572, the Dow up 1.20% to 53,699 and the S&P 500 up 1.01% to 7,744 — so the gains were set early and held into the bell rather than building through the afternoon.
