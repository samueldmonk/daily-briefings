The Lead

CPI cools to 3.4% and stocks close higher — the S&P 500 ends just shy of its record

The week's main event landed tame, and the tape held the relief through the bell. July's consumer price index rose just 0.1% on the month, easing the annual rate to 3.4% from 3.5%, while core CPI rose 0.2% to run at 2.5% year over year — all in line with Dow Jones consensus forecasts (CNBC, TheStreet, Yahoo Finance). At the close, Yahoo Finance's live coverage had the S&P 500 up about 0.3%, the Nasdaq Composite up roughly 0.5% and the Dow ending near the flat line (Yahoo's closing read put it just under it), while Trading Economics' post-close stats line settled at 7,748.21 on the S&P 500, +0.26% from Tuesday — its Actual/Previous table now reconciles that level exactly against Tuesday's corrected 7,728.20 close, after an earlier post-bell snapshot briefly read ~7,742/+0.18% — corroborated by 24/7 Wall St's late-session board at 7,747.90, and just shy of Friday's record close of 7,757.64. Eight of the 11 S&P 500 sectors were green in afternoon trading, with information technology leading the charge (Yahoo Finance); the VIX sat at 14.77 on TE's board. After the print, Yahoo reports traders tilted their bets toward the Fed holding rates steady in September, from a roughly 50-50 split the day before.

Under the CPI hood: energy fell another 1.5% in July after June's 5.7% slide (though it is still up 14.7% over the year after the spring's Hormuz-driven spikes), food and shelter each rose just 0.1% — shelter still drove about two-thirds of the headline gain — and airline fares jumped 2.2% (CNBC). LPL's Jeffrey Roach says falling energy prices "helped soften the inflation pressures of the month" and sees inflation decelerating to 2.7% by year-end, but expects a "lively" September FOMC debate — his baseline is a hold, "but an increasing number of voting members are hawkish and could convince the majority to implement a hike" (TheStreet). Fifth Third's Bill Adams says the report "narrowly meets the bar to nudge the Fed toward holding rates steady," and Kitco's morning report puts market-implied odds of a September quarter-point hike near 48% — this cycle's live debate is hike-or-hold, not cuts.

The other engine is AI earnings. CoreWeave surged about 20% after revenue more than doubled and guidance blew past estimates, Nebius jumped 16.5% as its AI-cloud revenue rose nearly sixfold, and Super Micro rallied hard on an above-consensus full-year outlook (TheStreet, CNBC, Trading Economics). Strong Tencent results added a global read-through for hyperscalers and chipmakers, with Nvidia and Oracle higher (Trading Economics) — and the AI-infrastructure test rolled on after the bell: Cisco delivered a clean fiscal-Q4 double beat — revenue of $17.25 billion against the $16.83 billion consensus (+18% year over year) and adjusted EPS of $1.22 vs. $1.17 expected (+23%), per the first post-report takes indexed at publish — after leading the Dow at +2% during the session. Seeking Alpha's post-report read: results and guidance both topped estimates, with shares up 2.5% in extended trading — and Coherent followed with a beat of its own (see After the Bell) (24/7 Wall St, Yahoo Finance, Seeking Alpha, Trading Economics). Uncertainty over the return of Middle East energy supply is still the tape's standing inflation worry: Houthi rebels killed six people on a Red Sea vessel — the first fatalities from shipping attacks in more than a year — and U.S. forces fired on a container ship allegedly trying to breach the blockade of Iranian ports (CBS News via TheStreet).
