Top Story

Trezor’s phishing emails came from Trezor’s own email platform — so they passed every authentication check

On 9 September 2026 attackers exploited a login weakness at Brevo, the third-party email-marketing platform Trezor uses for its newsletter, and used it to send phishing mail to roughly 347,000 Trezor newsletter subscribers. Because the messages went out through Brevo’s own infrastructure under Trezor’s account, they cleared the email-authentication checks that normally flag a spoofed sender.

The mail carried the subject line “Critical Security Alert: STM32 Entropy Vulnerability”, claimed a hardware flaw in the STM32 microcontrollers used in Trezor’s cold-storage wallets could expose seed phrases to brute force, and pushed recipients to download an application that then asked for their wallet backup. Trezor detected the phishing domain and disabled it at the DNS level about 20 minutes later; by then roughly 2,500 people had already clicked.

Reporting on the Brevo side describes the attacker creating a Brevo account, enabling single sign-on, and inviting genuine Brevo account holders into their workspace — after which an authorization-boundary defect handed the attacker access to the organizations tied to those invited accounts. No figure for funds lost was stated in anything read this run, so none is published.

Patch Priority — today

GitLab CE/EE — CVE-2026-85706 (CVSS 10.0), CISA remediation due 14 September 2026 (today). A path traversal in the repository commits API: improper path confinement plus missing authentication enforcement lets an unauthenticated remote attacker read arbitrary files off the GitLab server, which Rapid7 notes can expose SSH keys, database credentials, deploy tokens and CI/CD variables. Self-managed CE and EE remain vulnerable on 18.7 before 19.1.8, 19.2 before 19.2.6, and 19.3 before 19.3.2. CISA added it on 11 September and, per the advisory read this run, marked it subject to forensic-triage requirements under BOD 26-04.

Second, same deadline: ConnectWise ScreenConnect CVE-2026-84869 (CVSS 9.9), also due 14 September 2026 — versions prior to 26.6.5. Both dates are taken from the advisories as stated, not derived from a rule of thumb, and the same 14 September date is used in the CVE table and the KEV countdowns below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

ShinyHunters

The extortion group behind the Florida DMV claim below. No source read this run links it to the dark-web identity service reported alongside the IDScan breach, so no such link is drawn here. Its Florida account is instructive precisely because the victim’s account differs: ShinyHunters told BleepingComputer it exploited a password-reset flaw to take over multiple DAVID accounts, including ones belonging to DMV employees and an FBI agent, then iterated through record IDs pulling HTML pages and images from 3 September.

FLHSMV’s own investigation instead attributes access to compromised credentials belonging to a single Plant City Police Department user, improperly stored on that employee’s personal device. ShinyHunters later said it had lost access and believed the flaw was being patched. Both accounts are printed; neither is adopted — no source read this run reconciles them.

Breaches & Incidents

NewGovernment

Florida confirms its DAVID driver database was breached

The Florida Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles says it learned of the breach on 4 September 2026 and that it was “quickly mitigated.” ShinyHunters claims more than 200,000 driver records; FLHSMV has not confirmed that number and has not disclosed how many records were taken. The group published a screenshot of Jeffrey Epstein’s DAVID record as proof. The agency has notified the Florida Attorney General and is working with the Florida Digital Service and FDLE.

NewSupply chain

Brevo → Trezor: 347,000 subscribers phished

See the top story. The defining detail is that the phishing mail was sent through the vendor Trezor actually uses, so SPF/DKIM/DMARC had nothing to object to. Roughly 2,500 clicks landed in the ~20 minutes before Trezor killed the domain at DNS.

NewRecord count

Mathspace breach now carries a scale figure

BleepingComputer’s headline read this run puts the Mathspace disclosure at over 1 million people — a number this site did not have when it first carried the item. The cause on file is unchanged: exploitation of CVE-2026-72898, a SQL injection in self-hosted Metabase, exposing names, emails, usernames and locations. No CVSS for CVE-2026-72898 appeared in anything read this run, so none is printed.

The Louisiana identity-verification provider detected unauthorised access on or around 1 September 2026, with the FBI New Orleans field office investigating.

Availability

Microsoft’s September updates are still breaking things

Microsoft has confirmed the September 2026 security updates cause Remote Desktop Services failures on Windows Server. Returned alongside it this run: Excel update KB5002914 breaking copy and paste for some users. Both are defects with no CVE and no attacker — availability problems, not intrusions, and listed here so they are not mistaken for either.
