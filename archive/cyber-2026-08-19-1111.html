Top story

Four exploited flaws, one Friday deadline: CISA gives federal agencies three days to fix vCenter, SharePoint, Windows IKE and macOS

On August 18, 2026 CISA added four vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog, each on evidence of active exploitation, and ordered federal civilian agencies to remediate all four by August 21, 2026. Three of the four carry a CVSS of 9.8; the fourth is 9.1. The list: CVE-2026-33824 (Microsoft Internet Key Exchange Service Extensions, double free, 9.8), CVE-2026-55040 (Microsoft SharePoint, weak authentication, 9.1), CVE-2026-59310 (Broadcom VMware vCenter, path traversal, 9.8) and CVE-2026-65400 (Apple macOS, improper authentication).

The vCenter entry is the one with a body count. German incident-response firm QUIRSO traced a live campaign against internet-facing vCenter servers: path-traversal activity consistent with the flaw, followed by a malicious cron job establishing persistence via reverse_ssh, an open-source tool for opening outbound SSH connections to attacker infrastructure — which neatly bypasses controls built to stop suspicious inbound traffic. Compromised systems first contacted attacker domains on August 3, five days after Broadcom disclosed the flaw. QUIRSO counted 361 unique victim IP addresses across 47 countries, concentrated in Germany, the US, Turkey, Iran and France. The actor is unattributed but assessed to be a suspected APT; VMware appliances have long been a target of Chinese groups such as UNC5174, and the reverse_ssh tradecraft echoes the China-nexus PurpleHaze cluster SentinelOne documented in April 2025. QUIRSO’s COO, Denis Szadkowski, told The Hacker News there is not yet enough evidence to link this intrusion set to the separate scanning wave against CVE-2026-59309, but that the forensic evidence “strongly points toward CVE-2026-59310 as the initial access vector.”

The SharePoint entry is the one that is easiest to exploit. Rapid7 describes a chain of four distinct weaknesses in SharePoint Server Subscription Edition’s JWT validation pipeline that together let an unauthenticated remote attacker forge a valid token and impersonate any site user, administrators included: send a JWT with alg: none in the outer header so no signature is required, use SharePoint’s own STS certificate thumbprint to resolve a signing key without verification, then attach a non-empty but never-verified signature. Defused researchers have observed attackers running Rapid7’s proof-of-concept against honeypots.

The macOS flaw sits in Screen Sharing, the remote-desktop tool built into every Mac: an attacker on the network could authenticate without valid credentials. The Dutch National Cyber Security Centre confirmed active exploitation on August 15, less than two weeks after Apple shipped the fix in macOS Tahoe 26.6.1, Sequoia 15.7.9 and Sonoma 14.8.9. For the Windows IKE flaw, blocking UDP ports 500 and 4500 reduces external exposure, but internal attackers can still use it for lateral movement — so the mitigation buys time, not safety.

Patch priority

⚠ Do this first

Ray — CVE-2025-62593 (CVSS 9.4), fixed in Ray 2.52.0. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Monday, August 17 citing evidence of active exploitation, and the federal remediation deadline is Thursday, August 20, 2026 — tomorrow, a three-day window from Monday’s listing. It remains the nearest deadline still ahead of us, which is why it holds this slot. It is exploited by the RondoDox DDoS botnet, which folded it in two days before public disclosure, and by the ShadowRay 2.0 campaign that turns unpatched NVIDIA-GPU clusters into a self-replicating mining botnet.

Then, by Friday: the four flaws CISA added on August 18 — CVE-2026-59310 (VMware vCenter, 9.8), CVE-2026-33824 (Windows IKE, 9.8), CVE-2026-65400 (macOS Screen Sharing) and CVE-2026-55040 (SharePoint, 9.1), all due August 21, 2026. If you can only reach one of them, make it vCenter: Broadcom has confirmed there is no workaround and patching to a fixed build is the only remediation, and it is the only one of the four with confirmed compromises already counted in the hundreds.

Threat actor spotlight

A suspected China-nexus APT, working the VMware vCenter hole

Beyond the campaign metrics in today’s top story, the attribution work is worth its own entry. German incident response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that the campaign exploiting CVE-2026-59310 (CVSS 9.8, a directory-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter patched by Broadcom on July 29, 2026) is run by a Chinese-speaking threat actor operating in the UTC+08:00 time zone. The assessment rests on converging evidence: Chinese-language artefacts inside attacker-created scripts, apparent reuse of research from a Chinese security publication, and repeated operational use of Chinese-language tooling and management software. In at least one compromised environment the intrusion led to a backdoor and a reverse shell, and researchers have tied the activity to the deployment of Babuk-derived ransomware — a reminder that an espionage-shaped toolkit and an extortion payload increasingly turn up on the same box.

Breaches & incidents

NewCVSS 9.5 / 9.3AI platformOT / SCADA

Two open-source platforms — one for AI, one for factory floors — are being scanned and exploited right now

New this run. Independent reports from watchTowr and VulnCheck put two critical flaws under active scanning and exploitation attempts. CVE-2026-64849 (CVSS 9.3) is an unauthenticated server-side request forgery in MLflow, the open-source AI/ML lifecycle platform: an attacker who can reach the Tracking Server (mlflow server) can make it issue HTTP requests to arbitrary internal cloud metadata endpoints and pull out credentials and secrets. It affects versions before 3.15.0. CVE-2026-25895 (CVSS 9.5) is a missing-authentication-plus-path-traversal flaw in FUXA, the web-based SCADA/HMI package used in operational technology and industrial automation, allowing an unauthenticated remote attacker to write arbitrary files to the server filesystem and achieve remote code execution. Neither is in the KEV catalog, so neither carries a federal deadline — but the pairing is the story: the same commodity scanning that finds an ML experiment tracker also finds the software running a plant.

1.2M peopleThird-party cloudSSNs

Heights Finance is notifying more than 1.2 million people — and the data is the worst kind

Consumer lender Heights Finance Holdings Co. is notifying more than 1.2 million people that their personal and financial information was stolen. The company discovered on May 7, 2026 that attackers had accessed a third-party cloud-based platform it used to store customer data. The exposed fields are close to a complete identity-theft kit: names, addresses, phone numbers, email addresses, account details, bank name, account number and routing number, Social Security numbers, tax IDs, driver’s licence and state ID numbers, and dates of birth. State breakdowns filed so far: 734,828 in Texas, 486,463 in South Carolina, 26 in New Hampshire and 21 in Vermont. Heights says the platform has been secured, operations were unaffected, outside specialists were engaged and federal law enforcement notified; affected individuals are being offered 24 months of credit monitoring and identity protection. Note the shape of it — a lender’s own systems were not breached; its vendor’s were. This is the third-party-risk failure mode, not a patching one.

50,000+ keysSecrets sprawl

50,000 live Stripe merchant keys are sitting in public code — and Stripe was never breached

A dataset published to a data-trading forum on August 18, 2026 contains live Stripe API keys for 659 merchant accounts alongside roughly 35 GB of customer and payment data. Researchers looking wider found more than 50,000 unique Stripe merchant API keys exposed across public code repositories, GitHub Actions logs and misconfigured web servers. The largest share came from GitHub repositories — keys hardcoded into config and .env files committed without a .gitignore entry. GitHub Actions build logs were the second-largest source, where secrets that were never properly masked end up readable by anyone with repository access. More than 3,000 servers were found leaking Stripe-related strings, about 12% of them containing real keys. To demonstrate impact, researchers took an active key and within 17 hours accessed the merchant’s customer list, created a fraudulent payment link and pushed through a $1 test charge. Stripe itself was not compromised — the keys belong to merchants, which is precisely why nobody is going to patch this for them.

4.9M recordsVishingSalesforce

Brinks Home refused to pay — and ShinyHunters has started publishing

The ransom deadline passed, the money did not move, and 41GB went up. ShinyHunters claims more than 4.9 million Salesforce records taken from Brinks Home, the residential-security arm of one of the best-known names in physical security, and after the company declined to pay has leaked more than 41GB of files said to have come from the intrusion. The company has confirmed a cyberattack. The route in was not a software flaw: the actors say they breached Brinks Home on July 13 through a Microsoft Entra voice-phishing (vishing) call, and the company discovered the intrusion on July 20 — roughly a week of dwell time before containment. The victim was listed on the leak site as “BH Security, LLC (brinkshome.com)” with a payment deadline of July 30, 2026. The claimed haul breaks down as more than 1.1 million rows from the Salesforce “Contacts” object, over 4,000 rows of employee PII (names, emails, job titles, phone numbers) and roughly 3.8 million customer support chat logs from the Brinks Care Cresta platform. One caveat worth keeping in front of the headline number: 4.9 million is a count of database records, not of people — a single customer can appear across many rows, and the support-log tranche is the bulk of it. The lesson for defenders is the same one the Salesforce extortion wave has been teaching for a year: the tenant was not exploited, a help desk was, and no patch closes that.

3.6M recordsCredential theft

‘TheHatman’ is selling the employee directories of nine household-name companies

A single actor using the moniker TheHatman is offering roughly 3.6 million records said to have been pulled straight out of corporate Azure and Entra tenants. The named victims are not obscure: McDonald’s Corporation tops the list with more than 1.7 million records, followed by Tata Consultancy Services at roughly 800,000, Vodafone at about 425,000 and HCL Technologies at around 250,000, with InterContinental Hotels Group, Kyndryl, Gap Inc., Hexaware Technologies and Wyndham Hotels also listed. The contents are what make this different from a marketing-list dump: full names, corporate email addresses including active domains and tenant-specific .onmicrosoft.com structures, phone numbers, physical addresses, employee IDs, job titles, departments, manager details and direct reports, plus user group memberships, service accounts and Global Administrator listings. That is an org chart and a privileged-account map handed to whoever buys it — the ideal starting material for targeted vishing and business email compromise. Hudson Rock assesses with high confidence that the data is authentic but could not establish the initial access vector; the actor claims compromised credentials, and researchers float infostealer-harvested credentials or session cookies, phishing, weak or absent MFA and over-permissive third-party applications as the candidates. No software vulnerability is implicated. Treat the exposure as identity-layer, and the mitigation accordingly: conditional access, phishing-resistant MFA on privileged accounts, and a review of what third-party apps can read from the directory.
