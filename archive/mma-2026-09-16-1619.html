Van and Pantoja face off at UFC 331 media day: “I lost for myself”

Fight week reached the podium on Wednesday. Flyweight champion Joshua Van and challenger Alexandre Pantoja spoke to reporters and faced off at UFC 331 media day in Los Angeles, alongside lightweight co-headliners Arman Tsarukyan and Maurício Ruffy.

Pantoja framed the rematch on his own terms. He said he wants it more than ever, that Van is the champion and that he himself has nothing to lose — and told CBS Sports that the first meeting’s outcome came down to his own injury rather than to Van’s performance: “I lost for myself.”

That first meeting was UFC 323 on 6 December 2025, where Van took the belt by first-round TKO. Van has defended it once since — a fifth-round TKO of Tatsuro Taira at UFC 328 on 9 May 2026. The betting boards have the champion a narrow favourite: Van −130, Pantoja +110 at DraftKings.

Around the Sport

  • Ki MMA has a name and a plan. Scott Coker’s new promotion — announced at Cipriani 25 Broadway in Lower Manhattan — is called Ki MMA, pronounced “Key MMA”; ki is the Japanese word for spirit, mind and energy. It plans to launch early next year with a 32-fighter Grand Prix spanning four continents, opening at 145 lb. The fights will take place in a ring, a nod to Japan’s martial-arts history. Veteran matchmaker Rich Chou, who worked with Coker at Strikeforce and Bellator, joins the venture.
  • Heavyweight is vacant. Tom Aspinall relinquished the undisputed heavyweight title on 14 September 2026. Ongoing complications with his eyes — traced to the eye poke by Ciryl Gane at UFC 321, compounded by multiple surgeries, accidents and infections, and further damage to his right eye after he had agreed a date and returned to sparring — left him unable to obtain medical clearance. He is not retiring and intends to return once cleared.
  • Women’s flyweight is vacant too. Valentina Shevchenko vacated the belt while injured and sidelined for roughly a year. Silva vs. Wang settles it at UFC 332 on 3 October.
