How far the card has actually got, this page cannot say — and that is the story. What this page can see of a live event is not the event; it is a cache of it.

Search, meanwhile, has raced ahead — into detail that exists nowhere this page could read. One query returned five further prelim winners, three of them carrying a method and two of those a fight time to the second: a TKO by punches at 4:25 of round one, a submission by reverse triangle choke at 2:46 of round three, a unanimous decision, 29-28 across the board. None of those methods or times appears in any of the six pages fetched. They are refused in full, and the refusal block below names all five.

This is the third consecutive edition in which this page has traced a set of phantom results, and the escalation is the thing to notice. Two runs ago the source was a prediction table on an outside site. Last run it was the "Prelim Picks" block inside the live blog itself, every line of it written as "Thomas Gantt (−450) def. Drakkar Klose (+350)" — forecasts in the syntax of results. That block is still there, still posted at 5:35 PM UTC, still naming the same winners. What is new this run is that the winners now arrive dressed in methods and round times that the picks list does not contain and that no fetched page contains either. A picks list can explain who; it cannot explain reverse triangle choke at 2:46.

The two results that are published here are published because a live blog carries each under a filled heading with a round-by-round account attached. Regina Tarin def. JJ Aldrich by unanimous decision, 29-28 on all three cards, in the opener; Sean King III def. Jessie Rosas by KO (slam), round 1, 0:36, in the second bout. One small discrepancy is worth recording rather than smoothing: the Forbes live blog describes King's finish in prose as a "33-second win", while the MMA Mania blog's filled "Official decision" field reads 0:36. This page prints the result field and notes the prose.

Prospect Watch

NewProspect

Regina Tarin — 9-0, and through a ranked opponent at 21

The Mexico City flyweight arrived 8-0 off a February debut win and leaves the Noche UFC opener 9-0 with a unanimous-decision win over the division's No. 15. Beating a ranked veteran with a 22-fight record in a second UFC appearance is the kind of result that reorders a prospect's timeline.

NewProspect

Sean King III — 7-0 after 36 seconds

The New Orleans featherweight arrived 6-0 per UFC.com and leaves 7-0, having slammed Jessie Rosas (8-1) unconscious at 0:36 of the first round in a meeting of newcomers. He was a −200 favourite. Newly sourced this run: he is 22 years old, and he was originally booked for Dana White's Contender Series before being moved onto this card on nine days' notice. A knockout that fast, on a card built for highlights, is the kind of debut-tier win that gets a fighter booked again quickly — though no next booking is announced in anything read.

NewProspect

What is new, and what it rules out. Three of the five names match, in order, lines from the "Prelim Picks" block that sits inside the Yahoo/Uncrowned live blog, posted at 5:35 PM UTC before the card began: "Ignacio Bahamondes (−600) def. Muslim Salikhov (+425)", "Yousri Belgaroui (−900) def. Djorden Santos (+600)", "Thomas Gantt (−450) def. Drakkar Klose (+350)", "Rong Zhu (−180) def. Rafa García (+145)", "Édgar Cháirez (−200) def. Tim Elliott (+165)" — forecasts written in the syntax of results, which is the mechanism this page identified last edition. But that list contains no methods and no round times, so it cannot be the whole explanation for "reverse triangle choke at 2:46 of R3". This page does not know where that detail came from and will not guess; what it knows is that the detail is in no page it could read, which is the only test that matters here.

The "29-28 All" attached to the Rongzhu line is character-for-character the scoreline of Tarin's real decision — the only 29-28 sweep confirmed anywhere on this card. That is stated as an observation about the two strings, not as a claim about where either came from.

The wider rule, three editions in. "Def." carries no evidential weight. A set of winners in correct bout order is what a picks list looks like by construction. Favourites winning is not confirmation — every refused line above is the favourite. And a method stated to the second is not more credible for being more specific; specificity is a property of the sentence, not of the evidence behind it. The remaining Athlon Sports predictions stay unpublished for the same reason they always have.

Any champions list seating Valentina Shevchenko at 125 pounds is stale and refused on sight, and Alex Pereira is not the light-heavyweight champion however often a cached page says so.
