Top story

Fight night in Sacramento: Anthony Hernandez rebuilds, Gregory Rodrigues climbs, and the 185-pound queue re-forms

UFC Fight Night: Hernandez vs. Rodrigues takes place tonight, Saturday, August 22, 2026, at the Golden 1 Center in Sacramento, California, streaming on Paramount+. Prelims began at 5:00 p.m. ET / 2:00 p.m. PT across a seven-fight preliminary card; the main card follows at 8:00 p.m. ET / 5:00 p.m. PT. No results were available from any source at the time this edition was assembled.

Hernandez arrives with something to prove. “Fluffy” had won eight consecutive fights after a May 2020 defeat to Kevin Holland, working through Rodolfo Vieira, Marc-André Barriault, Roman Kopylov, Michel Pereira, Brendan Allen and Roman Dolidze. That run ended in February, when Sean Strickland stopped him in the third round — his first loss in nearly six years.

Rodrigues arrives hot. After a fourth-round TKO loss to Jared Cannonier in February 2025, he knocked out Jack Hermansson in the opening round, beat Roman Kopylov by unanimous decision and stopped Brunno Ferreira in the first round at UFC 326 in March — three straight, and six wins in his last seven. Tonight is his second UFC main event; the first, against Cannonier, he lost.

The books make Hernandez a clear but not overwhelming favourite. Hard Rock Bet lists him at −185 with Rodrigues at +150; DraftKings has it −198 / +164; at least one other book has gone as wide as −225 / +185. The co-feature is a light-heavyweight bout between Reinier de Ridder (−400) and Roman Dolidze (+300).

Sacramento matters here as more than a venue. This is the UFC’s fifth trip to the city and second at Golden 1 Center. The promotion first came for UFC 65 in November 2006, returned for UFC 73 the following summer, and staged Benson Henderson’s lightweight title defence against local favourite Nate Diaz in December 2012. Its most recent visit was July 2019, when Germaine de Randamie stopped Aspen Ladd and Sacramento’s own Urijah Faber came out of retirement to knock out Ricky Simon in the first round. Tonight another Northern California fighter headlines.

NewContract

Curtis Blaydes signs an eight-fight deal

Amid a run of surprise roster removals, the No. 7 heavyweight has secured his future with a new eight-fight contract. The first bout under it is Noche UFC on September 12 in Glendale against No. 5 Waldo Cortes-Acosta.

Rising

Jalin Turner — back-to-back first-round finishes

“The Tarantula” took 39 seconds to stop Kauê Fernandes at UFC 330, his second consecutive first-round stoppage. He has been ranked before and could be again, and he maintained a 100 percent finishing rate on the night.

Rising

Esteban Ribovics — the biggest win yet

The Argentine crowded Edson Barboza from the opening bell, hurt him repeatedly and forced a second-round stoppage without ever putting him on the canvas — the biggest name-brand win of his career.

Around the sport

  • Edson Barboza retired in the cage. After the Ribovics stoppage at UFC 330, Barboza took off his gloves and laid them in the centre of the Octagon, ending a 16-year UFC career defined by highlight-reel finishes and a brutal strength of schedule.
  • Makhachev owns the streak record. The welterweight champion’s decision over Machado Garry was his 17th straight UFC win and set a new record for the most consecutive victories inside the Octagon. He also became only the fifth two-division champion to record successful title defences in both divisions.
  • Dern banked her first defence. Mackenzie Dern swept the cards against Gillian Robertson for her fourth straight win — though she spent the final five minutes on bottom, which, as UFC.com noted, is not the optics a champion wants.
  • Machado Garry proved the level. The Irishman falls to 17-2 but out-landed Makhachev in the third and again in the fourth, showing he can hang with the pound-for-pound best.

Rankings & business

  • Rankings movement. Gregory Rodrigues’ three-fight run has moved him up the middleweight ladder — though the sourcing disagrees on exactly where. Hard Rock Bet’s fight-week card lists him at No. 10 with Hernandez at No. 7; other fight-week coverage renders the pair No. 11 and No. 6. The disagreement is printed rather than resolved.
  • Heavyweight order. Noche UFC pairs No. 5 Waldo Cortes-Acosta (17-3, out of Phoenix) with No. 7 Curtis Blaydes (19-6, 1 NC, out of Chicago) — a genuine gatekeeper fight for the top of the division.
