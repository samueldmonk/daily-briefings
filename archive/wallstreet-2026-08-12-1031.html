The Lead

CPI cools to 3.4% and the S&P 500 tests its record as AI earnings deliver (as of ~10 AM ET)

The week's main event landed tame. July's consumer price index rose just 0.1% on the month, easing the annual rate to 3.4% from 3.5%, while core CPI rose 0.2% to run at 2.5% year over year — both annual rates down a tenth from June (CNBC, Yahoo Finance). Stocks opened higher on the print: Yahoo Finance's live coverage had the S&P 500 up about 0.3%, the Nasdaq up about 0.6% and the Dow up about 0.1% shortly after the bell, and Trading Economics' board read the S&P 500 +0.35% and "testing its record" at roughly 10 AM ET, with the Nasdaq 100 up 0.7%, the Russell 2000 up 0.8% and the VIX easing to about 14.9. Quartz's headline went further, calling it a push to a record high — Friday's official record close of 7,757.64 is the bar.

Under the CPI hood: energy fell another 1.5% in July after June's 5.7% slide (though it is still up 14.7% over the year after the spring's Hormuz-driven spikes), food and shelter each rose just 0.1% — shelter still drove about two-thirds of the headline gain — and airline fares jumped 2.2% (CNBC). The read-through for the Fed: CNBC says the report "possibly [takes] the urgency out of an imminent interest rate hike," and Fifth Third's Bill Adams says it "narrowly meets the bar to nudge the Fed toward holding rates steady" in September. Kitco's morning report puts market-implied odds of a September quarter-point hike near 48% — this cycle's live debate is hike-or-hold, not cuts.

The other engine is AI earnings. CoreWeave surged about 20% after revenue more than doubled and guidance blew past estimates, and Super Micro rallied hard on an above-consensus full-year outlook (CNBC, Trading Economics). Strong Tencent results added a global read-through for hyperscalers and chipmakers, with Nvidia, Oracle and Amazon higher (Trading Economics) — and the AI-infrastructure test isn't over: Cisco and Coherent report after tonight's close (AskTraders, Yahoo Finance). Uncertainty over the return of Middle East energy supply is still the tape's standing inflation worry (Trading Economics).
