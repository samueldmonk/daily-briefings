Top story

A Mac listening on port 5900 is being rooted and put to work mining Monero

The Netherlands' National Cyber Security Centre has warned that CVE-2026-65400 — a critical authentication issue in screensharingd, the system daemon behind macOS's built-in remote desktop feature — is under active exploitation in the wild. Rated CVSS 9.8, the flaw lets an attacker already on the network authenticate to the Screen Sharing service without valid credentials.

The NCSC advisory (NCSC-2026-0280) describes confirmed exploitation on multiple systems with port 5900 reachable from the internet. In every confirmed case the attacker obtained root access and planted a Monero miner — cryptojacking rather than data theft, which is precisely why it can sit unnoticed on a machine for a long time.

Apple addressed the flaw in an emergency update on August 6, 2026, shipping fixes in macOS Tahoe 26.6.1, macOS Sequoia 15.7.9 and macOS Sonoma 14.8.9; the patch improves state management to enforce correct credential validation. Apple credited researcher Alfredo Pesoli of Bynario with the discovery. Where patching has to wait, the advice from responders is blunt: turn Screen Sharing off when it isn't in use, get port 5900 off the public internet, and reach machines over a VPN or SSH tunnel instead.

Patch priority

⚠ Do this first

Ray — CVE-2025-62593 (CVSS 9.4), fixed in Ray 2.52.0. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Monday, August 17 citing evidence of active exploitation, and the federal remediation deadline is Thursday, August 20, 2026 — two days away. This is the single most urgent item on the board: it is exploited, it is in KEV, and its deadline is the nearest one still ahead of us. Everything else in the KEV list below is already past due.

Threat actor spotlight

A suspected China-nexus APT, working the VMware vCenter hole

German incident response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that the campaign exploiting CVE-2026-59310 (CVSS 9.8, a directory-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter patched by Broadcom on July 29, 2026) is run by a Chinese-speaking threat actor operating in the UTC+08:00 time zone. The assessment rests on converging evidence: Chinese-language artefacts inside attacker-created scripts, apparent reuse of research from a Chinese security publication, and repeated operational use of Chinese-language tooling and management software. In at least one compromised environment the intrusion led to a backdoor and a reverse shell, and researchers have tied the activity to the deployment of Babuk-derived ransomware — a reminder that an espionage-shaped toolkit and an extortion payload increasingly turn up on the same box.

Breaches & incidents

NewSupply chainRansomware

Clop claims 43 organisations via a PTC Windchill flaw

Check Point Research reports the Clop ransomware gang targeted 43 organisations — among them Shell, General Electric and Philips — by exploiting CVE-2026-12569, a critical improper input validation flaw in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM. The chain deployed JSP webshells to steal blueprints, engineering drawings and facility photographs. Clop claims 89GB taken from Shell; Philips has confirmed a breach of one enterprise server holding internal data.

NewHealthcareClaimed

MyDr, Poland's main healthcare platform

Per Check Point Research's August 17 threat intelligence report, MyDr — Poland's primary platform for appointments, medical records and prescriptions — suffered a data breach potentially affecting close to 19 million citizens. The attackers claim to hold 2.5TB of information. Treat the volume as an attacker assertion until the operator confirms scope.

CryptoThird party

SafePal — 39,798 customers

The hardware wallet maker disclosed on August 16 that an authorisation flaw in an order-tracking plug-in exposed names, email addresses, shipping addresses, phone numbers and purchase details. SafePal says the incident did not involve seed phrases, private keys, wallet passwords, bank details, card numbers or government ID, and that affected customers were emailed individually from security@safepal.com. It is the second hardware-wallet customer list to surface in a fortnight.

FinanceSSNs

Heights Finance — over 1.2 million people

The consumer lender discovered unauthorised access on May 7, 2026 to a third-party cloud platform holding customer data, and began notifications on August 11. Exposed: names, addresses, Social Security numbers and bank account details, with phone, email, tax IDs, driver's licence or state ID and dates of birth possibly involved. Loan management systems were not affected. Twenty-four months of credit monitoring is on offer.

Also on the wire

  • Snowflake's public .NET connector repo carried a GitHub Actions workflow injection flaw, Wiz disclosed — a crafted GitHub issue could execute commands in a workflow that held internal Jira credentials (JIRA_BASE_URL, JIRA_USER_EMAIL, JIRA_API_TOKEN). Confined to CI/CD automation; no affected connector release identified.
  • Cavern (aka Cav3rn), the C2 framework used by Iranian nation-state hackers against Israeli targets, has gained a module that uses DNS A-record responses to switch between direct HTTPS and a Google Apps Script relay per transaction, Kaspersky reports.
  • Evooo1Bot, a previously undocumented Linux botnet built on leaked Mirai source, has been active since July 2026 — adding encrypted C2, an SSH brute-force scanner, a SOCKS relay and a credential sniffer, and turning internet-facing edge devices into SOCKS5 proxies (Fortinet FortiGuard Labs).
  • Unisoc modem firmware can be chained from a VoLTE video call to full Android kernel access, per SSD Secure Disclosure's August 17 advisory — with no fix from the chipset maker, which has not responded to contact attempts.
  • Gunra, the destructive extortion syndicate hitting government and healthcare targets, now operates as a large ransomware-as-a-service network, per a joint federal advisory noted in Check Point's August 17 report.
