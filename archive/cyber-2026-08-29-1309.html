Top Story

Carried · updated 11:05 AM Healthcare Claims unverified

McKesson tells the SEC it was breached. ShinyHunters says it took 284 million records and asked for $55 million.

What the company itself says. McKesson — a major U.S. healthcare company and pharmaceutical distributor supplying medicines, medical supplies, technology and services to providers and pharmacies — disclosed a cybersecurity incident in a Form 8-K filing with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission, after CyberInsider first reported it. McKesson says it discovered the incident on August 25, 2026 and that the investigation remains in the early stages. In a separate notice to customers it confirmed the incident involved third-party applications and the unauthorised access and exfiltration of data. As of the filing date the company had not determined that the incident is material, or reasonably likely to have a material impact on its financial condition or results. It warns customers may see intermittent service degradation believed to be related to the attack, while saying it is not proactively disconnecting systems in its environment.

What the company has not said — and this page does not fill in. McKesson has not publicly disclosed which third-party applications were compromised, how the attackers gained access, or what information was stolen. Everything in the two paragraphs below is the attacker's account, relayed by the outlet it spoke to, and the outlet states plainly that it has not independently verified it.

What the attacker claims. ShinyHunters says it was behind the attack and that it got in by voice phishing — vishing — multiple McKesson employees — and at 11:05 AM that count stopped being vague: a report fetched this run puts it at two employees, which is the first specific number any edition has been able to attach to the intrusion — compromising their Okta single sign-on accounts and using those to reach the company's Salesforce and Snowflake environments. It claims it fully compromised the Salesforce environment including support cases, took a much larger set of patient-related data out of Snowflake, and exfiltrated about 1TB over four days, between August 21 and August 25. It says it contacted McKesson after finishing on August 25 and demanded $55,236,150 within 72 hours, and that the company neither responded nor negotiated.

The 284 million figure is a count of records, not of people — and the correction came from the attacker. Earlier reporting stated that information belonging to 284 million patients had been exposed. ShinyHunters clarified that the number is a raw count of roughly 284 million data records, or lines, and that it has not fully analysed the data and does not know how many unique people appear in it. Both forms are printed here, because a count of rows and a count of people are different quantities, and the one the attacker actually stands behind is the count of rows. This page prints neither as a victim count.

The lure domain has a documented pattern behind it, and that part is actionable. Separately from the attacker, the reporting outlet says it learned the campaign used the domain mckesson[.]claims. That matches a campaign documented by ReliaQuest's threat research team, which said ShinyHunters has been registering company[.]claims domains carrying a target organisation's name or abbreviation in order to impersonate its help desk and IT teams. ReliaQuest's post has since been deleted. If you do nothing else with this item, look for newly registered .claims domains bearing your own company's name.

It is not an isolated case. Health-ISAC has warned healthcare organisations about rising ShinyHunters data-theft attacks that use social engineering to compromise corporate accounts and reach cloud and SaaS platforms. Other healthcare and health-technology companies named as recent targets in the same reporting: Medtronic, DentaQuest, iRhythm, OneMedical and AdaptHealth.

Claimed data categories, printed as claims. ShinyHunters says the stolen material includes names, addresses, dates of birth, Social Security numbers, patient IDs, phone numbers, email addresses, Medicaid numbers, medical record numbers, medication and allergy information, illnesses, disabilities, appointment information and physician information, and separately that it covers deceased and terminally ill patients, prescriptions and medication shipments, invoices, employee information, Salesforce records, internal communications, and the providers and clinics using McKesson's services. McKesson has confirmed none of this list, and no regulator has. It is here as an allegation with a name attached to it, not as a finding.

Carried · from the 10:20 edition

PaperCut's emergency patch was bypassed. The second one shipped a day later.

PaperCut published an urgent advisory on August 27 saying it was investigating active exploitation of a flaw in PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF. On August 28 it assigned two identifiers to what turned out to be an exploit chain: CVE-2026-81578, a high-severity authentication bypass rated 8.8 in the web management interface, where “unauthenticated remote requests targeting administrative functions can trigger backend actions prior to the completion of access validation checks”; and CVE-2026-82078, a critical unsafe dynamic class-loading flaw rated 9.4 in PaperCut's database connection utilities, which loads driver classes from a configurable name without validating it against an allowlist.

Chained, they are pre-authentication remote code execution. In watchTowr head of threat intelligence Jake Knott's description: CVE-2026-81578 gets you past authentication, and from there you edit a configuration file to exploit CVE-2026-82078 and gain RCE.

Emergency patches now exist for NG/MF versions 24, 25 and 26 across Windows, Linux and macOS — and the current builds are Emergency Patch Release 2, published August 28 after PaperCut worked with watchTowr and Huntress researchers to close bypasses of the earlier patches. Administrators who applied the first patch are not finished.

The worst of it, first published in the 9:15 edition and re-verified against two independent reports in the 10:20 AM edition. watchTowr reports it found multiple patch bypasses plus an additional authentication bypass; one of those bypasses was closed in Emergency Patch Release 2, but the researchers state that new patch bypasses affecting the latest, fully patched version have been identified. Read that precisely: applying the current patch is necessary and, on this reporting, not provably sufficient — which is why PaperCut's own advice to take the application server off the internet still matters more than the version number.

Exploitation is also no longer abstract. Huntress reports detecting exploitation of the chain in two customer environments as of August 27; in one, the attacker ran base64-encoded whoami and ver commands to identify the account context and the operating system version. That is manual reconnaissance immediately after access — a useful thing to hunt for in your own command-line logging.

Still not stated by any source seen in any edition to date, and therefore still not printed: the PaperCut threat actor, the number of victims, whether ransomware is involved, or how many servers are exposed. This block is carried unchanged from the 10:20 AM edition; no source fetched at 10:50 AM added to it.

Patch Priority

CVE-2026-8452 — Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway — federal deadline expires TODAY

CISA added this to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 26 with a federal remediation date of Saturday, August 29. That is today: the countdown below reads 0 days left, and it does not get another edition. It is a pre-authentication heap memory overflow in the code that parses SAML single-sign-on messages in the appliance's AAA service, carrying a CVSS v4.0 base score of 8.8. Defenders have observed web shells and discovery activity on compromised appliances. If you run NetScaler with AAA or Gateway exposed, this is the item on this page most likely to be already overdue when you read it.

The exploitation is quantified — carried from the 8:46 edition, where it was first sourced. Telemetry cited by CISA-tracking researchers records 36 exploitation attempts over the past 12 days from 12 unique attacker IP addresses across Switzerland, Germany, Hong Kong, Japan, the Netherlands, Russia, Singapore, Türkiye, the United States and Vietnam. The observed post-exploitation behaviour is specific enough to hunt for: attackers dropped web shells named x.php and z.php and ran discovery commands including id and echo. If you cannot patch today, look for those filenames first.

A second deadline expires alongside it. CVE-2019-1068, a remote code execution flaw in Microsoft SQL Server that lets an attacker execute code in the context of the SQL Server Database Engine service account, was added to KEV in the same August 26 batch and carries the same August 29 due date. It ranks second here only because there is currently no public information on how it is being exploited in the wild — which is a reason to patch it, not a reason to deprioritise it.

A build-number discrepancy, recorded rather than resolved. The source seen in the 8:19 AM edition, which first raised the conflict, gives the fixed builds as 14.1-73.32 and later, or 13.1-63.21 and later (with corresponding FIPS and NDcPP builds). The previous edition carried 14.1-72.61 / 13.1-63.18 / 13.1-37.272 from a different source. Both are printed; neither is discarded. Take the higher build in each train — if the newer numbers are right, the older ones leave you exposed; if the older ones were sufficient, installing the newer build costs nothing. Verify against Citrix advisory CTX696604 before you act on either.
Vendor framing vs researcher finding, both kept. Citrix disclosed and patched the issue on June 30, 2026, describing it only as a memory overflow that could cause a denial-of-service condition. In mid-August, watchTowr Labs demonstrated the same flaw driven to unauthenticated remote code execution. This page prints both characterisations and does not pick one.

CarriedAgentic AIChina-nexus

UAT-10147 — and the SPECTRE implant

First published in the 8:46 edition and carried unchanged. Cisco Talos has documented a Chinese-speaking cybercrime group tracked as UAT-10147, active since early 2026, that targets internet-facing Windows and Linux web servers for data theft and SEO fraud. What sets it apart is that it has wired AI-driven tooling into exploitation, reconnaissance, payload generation, validation and persistence. Talos recovered AI-generated operational playbooks from actor infrastructure — including a nine-section ASP.NET ViewState exploitation guide documenting a real intrusion in granular detail, down to hostnames, IP addresses and the MachineKey values needed to forge a valid ViewState payload — alongside a target list of roughly 170,000 URLs. Tooling observed: Metasploit, ysoserial, PentestGPT, DeepAudit and multiple privilege-escalation exploits. Its most capable payload is SPECTRE, a custom C backdoor for Windows and Linux; the Windows build supports 45 commands covering screenshots, credential theft, keylogging, token impersonation, process injection, in-memory .NET execution and privilege escalation, with Linux rootkit and BYOVD capabilities reported. This is the group behind four of the six CVEs CISA added on August 26 — the two Red Hat flaws, the Linux kernel write and the Ajax.NET deserialization bug.

New · 11:05 AMCVSS 9.8No exploitation stated

An AI agent chained six flaws in a WordPress theme on a million sites, and wrote the exploit itself

What it is. A critical vulnerability chain in the Avada theme for WordPress lets an unauthenticated attacker execute arbitrary PHP code on the server. The six issues are tracked collectively as CVE-2026-18431 and carry a 9.8 critical severity score. Chained in a specific order they form a zero-click attack; the components are authorization, input-validation, trust-boundary and file-handling weaknesses. A successful attacker can plant malware, reach databases, redirect visitors or add rogue administrator accounts.

Why the prerequisites do not narrow it. Exploitation requires vulnerable versions of both the theme (Avada up to 7.16) and the Fusion Builder plugin (up to 3.16) to be active. That sounds like a filter and is not one: Wordfence researchers clarified that Fusion Builder is a required plugin for the Avada theme, so all sites running the theme are also running the plugin. Avada has more than 1 million sales. In the researchers’ own words, “any site that has the Avada theme installed is going to be exploitable.”

The part that is arguably the story. The chain was found by Argus, an internal agentic framework at Wordfence, which also developed proof-of-concept exploit code — the whole thing in about two hours. Argus reproduced the flaw on July 30; the vendor got full details on August 5, acknowledged on August 10, and ThemeFusion shipped Avada 7.16.1 and Fusion Builder 3.16.1. Wordfence is withholding complete technical details to give administrators time to update, and published only a six-step outline of the chain.

⚠ What is not established. No source seen this run states in-the-wild exploitation, a victim, or a public proof of concept outside Wordfence’s own; the flaw is not KEV-listed and carries no federal deadline. It is on this page for its severity and its reach, not for evidence of attacks. One aggregator seen this run renders the affected range as “≤ 7.1” rather than 7.16; the vendor-sourced reporting says 7.16, and that is the figure printed here.

The Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives confirmed a cybersecurity “major incident” in a press release on August 26. CareCloud — 3,756,469 individuals, filed this month

The health-IT vendor has confirmed to the U.S. Department of Health and Human Services in an August 2026 filing that its March 2026 intrusion affected 3,756,469 people. An unauthorised third party had access to one of CareCloud’s Amazon Web Services environments between March 10 and March 16; that environment hosted one of the company’s six electronic health record systems, and the intruder claimed to have exfiltrated databases stored in it. Reported categories: names, addresses, Social Security numbers, driver’s licence numbers, dates of birth, health insurance information and medical records, with full payment card information for a very limited subset. Identity-theft protection and credit monitoring are offered through IDX. No ransomware group or extortion gang has publicly claimed the attack, and none is named here. ⚠ The victim count grew rather than changed: the same outlet headlined “over 350,000” earlier and “3.7 million” later — that is a filing being amended upward over time, not two sources contradicting each other, and the page prints the current filed figure with its history rather than the larger number alone.

CarriedAviation8.7M

Manchester Airports Group — 8.7 million customers

MAG, which operates Manchester, London Stansted and East Midlands airports, says an unauthorised third party accessed customer data tied to car park, lounge and Fast Track bookings and in-airport Wi-Fi sign-ups. Accessed: email addresses, phone numbers, vehicle registrations and postcodes. Explicitly not held by MAG or the affected system: bank or payment details. Flights, airport operations and parking continue normally; the online Manage My Booking service is temporarily suspended and existing reservations remain valid. Newly sourced at 12:05 PM: MAG says passenger safety and aviation security were not affected; it restricted access to the affected systems, brought in specialist cybersecurity advisers and notified the relevant authorities; and it has emailed affected customers warning them to be alert for phishing. ⚠ That last item is the one with a defender action attached: because the stolen fields are contact details, vehicle registrations and postcodes rather than payment data, there is nothing here a customer can cancel or reissue — the residual risk is targeted phishing built from real booking details, and it does not expire.

CarriedMedical devices

Boston Scientific — a supply chain stopped

The company identified a cybersecurity incident affecting certain IT systems on August 25, causing a network outage and an ongoing global disruption to operations. Among the systems hit are those used to process and ship customer orders — the practical effect reported is that pacemaker and cardiac stent shipments to hospitals have halted. Incident response is active with third-party experts; the company does not know when full system access will be restored. Piper Sandler analysts suggest a return to shipping all products in under three weeks. Added in the 10:20 AM edition: thousands of employees in Ireland, where the company runs three manufacturing and research sites, were told to work from home on August 26 after network communications were severed; analysts are modelling a hit of up to 700 basis points to third-quarter revenue, and the shares fell as much as 6% after disclosure. A spokesperson declined to say whether ransomware was involved and said a full-restoration timeline is still unknown. Added at 11:35 AM — the patient-safety question, answered narrowly. Reporting fetched this run, alongside the company’s own incident update, states that the investigation has not found any impact on implantable cardiac rhythm management device function. Read the scope of that precisely: it is a finding about device function, not about data, and it is stated as what the investigation has found so far — the same reporting repeats that the company remains in a network outage, that manufacturing and the processing and shipping of customer orders are still disrupted, and that the timeline for full restoration is not yet known. Four days on from the August 25 identification, that combination — devices working, logistics stopped — is the whole of what is established. No actor, ransom demand or data-theft claim is stated; none printed.

Analysing CVE records from 2024 and 2025, the agency found injection weaknesses the most dominant category — 7,701 CVEs in 2024 and 21,019 in 2025 — and reported that memory safety and improper input validation weaknesses appear disproportionately in KEV entries compared with the full CVE population. CISA's framing: attackers are exploiting simple, known flaws that persist in exposed assets, and AI is being used to automate that exploitation. The four aged CVEs above — two from 2015 — are the argument made in list form. New at 12:35 PM — the same review, reported this week, puts an age on the problem rather than a volume. Across the 2024–2025 records the most common weakness classes were injection flaws — cross-site scripting, OS command injection and SQL injection — with improper input validation named the single most common weakness type across both the KEV catalogue and registered CVEs, and seven of the top ten weakness types in 2025 were ones already classed as “unforgivable” in 2007.
