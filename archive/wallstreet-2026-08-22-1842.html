The Lead

Stocks close the week lower despite a Friday bounce, as the bond market refuses to settle

US markets are shut for the weekend, so the figures below are Friday's official closes rather than a live read. Stocks rose on Friday to cap a volatile week: the Dow Jones Industrial Average gained about 1%, while the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite each added 0.4%. Yahoo Finance attributed the advance in part to a report showing US business activity growing at its fastest pace in more than four years. Even so, all the major indexes finished the week lower, after a bond sell-off put pressure on riskier assets — and on tech and AI names in particular.

The bond story drove the week. On Thursday, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent laid out plans to expand the Treasury's long-term debt buybacks beyond $4 billion per issue. "Part of it is signaling here to show that we believe yields don't reflect the underlying fundamentals," Bessent told CNBC. The relief was brief: markets read the measure as a limited fix, and the 10-year and 30-year yields snapped back to the higher levels they had held earlier in the week.

The other headline was crypto. Bitcoin kept climbing, reaching $77,000 and recording its best week in two years — a 22% weekly advance, per CNBC — which pulled crypto-linked equities sharply higher and helped the financials sector support the broader tape.

On the Radar
  • Monday, August 24 — Bessent press conference on Iran. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is due to detail the US plan to economically isolate Iran and its trading partners. He has described it as "the greatest co-ordinated economic isolation in the history of the world," combining the naval blockade with what he called the toughest sanctions in history, and framed the choice for other countries as "whether you are either with us or against us." Al Jazeera reports he said the measures would "collapse" Iran's economy; CNBC reports he also said the US likely would not restart large-scale combat. Earlier in the week President Trump threatened "TREMENDOUS Economic Consequences" for any country trading with Iran, putting the focus on China, which sources oil from the Gulf.
  • August 27–29 — Jackson Hole. The Kansas City Fed's Economic Policy Symposium is Kevin Warsh's first as Fed Chair. Coverage points to Friday, August 28 as the session to watch, when Warsh delivers his keynote.
  • Next week — Nvidia's second-quarter earnings. The results land in a week when the AI trade has already been under pressure from the bond sell-off.
  • August 27 — Marvell earnings. Marvell fell about 6% on Friday on what TheStreet characterised as profit-taking into the print.
  • The long end remains the swing factor. Bessent's expanded buyback programme bought only brief relief; the 10-year and 30-year yields returned to their prior highs, and the bond market — not equities — has set the tone all week.
