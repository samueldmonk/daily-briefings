Top story

The tracker still lists ten identifiers and counts eight — and a second consecutive day has not reconciled them

Eight to ten vulnerabilities crossed from theoretical to confirmed-exploited in the last seven days, across six vendors. The page’s title and opening line both say “8 new exploited CVEs”; its own “last few weeks” prose says CISA “confirmed 10 newly exploited vulnerabilities in the last 7 days”; and its at-a-glance strip lists ten. This is the second consecutive day the same page has disagreed with itself, and it has not self-corrected. That persistence is the story rather than a transient glitch, and no count is adopted here.

The ten identifiers read this run: Google Pixel (CVE-2026-58704, improper authorization, 8.8); Cisco Identity Services Engine (CVE-2026-76460, 10.0); Acronis Backup (CVE-2026-87886, incorrect default permissions, 7.8); Cisco Secure Email Gateway (CVE-2026-76461, SQL injection, 9.8); JFrog Artifactory (CVE-2026-42016, incorrect authorization, 8.8 and CVE-2026-42018, improper authentication, 7.5); ConnectWise ScreenConnect (CVE-2026-84869, improper privilege management and missing authorization, 9.9); GitLab CE/EE (CVE-2026-85706, path traversal, 10.0); and two MikroTik RouterOS flaws (CVE-2026-67277, missing authentication for a critical function; CVE-2026-86060, improper neutralization of argument delimiters in a command).

The wider catalog now stands at 1,325 actively exploited non-Microsoft CVEs across 282 vendors, 243 of them ransomware-linked. Against that, 47 entries in 30 days with one ransomware link is a low ransomware share of a month’s intake. The tracker states both figures; no explanation for the gap was given in anything read this run, and none is offered here.

BOD 26-04 (“Prioritizing Security Updates Based on Risk”) is the governing directive for the federal remediation dates below. It replaced the older three-week directive, which is why a three-day deadline on the Cisco entry is coherent rather than a transcription error.

Patch priority

Cisco Identity Services Engine — CVE-2026-76460, due 19 September 2026 (1 day left)

Rated CVSS 10.0. Authentication bypass through incorrect use of privileged APIs in ISE and ISE-PIC; exploited in the wild; emergency fixes are out. It was added to the KEV catalog on 16 September, which is confirmed this run by CISA’s own alert index listing the 16 September addition of CVE-2026-76460 alongside CVE-2026-87886.

Provenance, split and stated. The 10.0 score, the affected products and the active exploitation were re-confirmed this run on the live tracker, which lists CVE-2026-76460 at 10.0 and exploited-in-the-wild, and the 16 September add date is newly confirmed this run from CISA’s alert listing — it had been carried rather than verified in the previous edition. The due date itself was not re-fetched: a direct fetch of the CISA alert page returned an empty body for a fourth consecutive run, so 19 September is carried from this site’s sourced corrections entry. It is consistent with the newly confirmed add date under BOD 26-04’s risk-based windows, but consistency is not verification and this page does not claim otherwise. The same date appears in the banner above and the deadline section below; no other date is used anywhere on this page.

Threat actor spotlight

Crimson Collective — extortion without the encryption

A new and aggressive cyber-extortion group, Crimson Collective has claimed the theft of data belonging to more than a million customers of the telecommunications provider Brightspeed. The group is characteristic of where the economics have gone: the leverage is the threat to publish, not the loss of access, which is why a victim with good backups is not thereby a victim with good options.

Re-confirmed this run in a 2026 breach round-up. Not tagged new — “Crimson Collective” has 22 prior hits and “Brightspeed” 22 across this site’s 771 archived snapshots.

Phishing

Invisible Unicode slips past filters

Among the day’s reported items: zero-width and otherwise invisible Unicode characters being used to defeat phishing filters — a detection problem rather than an exploitation one, since the payload is ordinary and only the string matching fails.
