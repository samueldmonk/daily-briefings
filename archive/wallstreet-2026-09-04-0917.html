The Lead

Payrolls Land at 162,000 — and the Futures Tape Splits in Two (as of ~8:47 AM ET, pre-open)

The Bureau of Labor Statistics released the August employment report at 8:30 AM ET and it came in far above forecast: nonfarm payrolls rose 162,000, against a consensus TheStreet gives as +55,000, CNBC's Dow Jones poll as +53,000, and a Reuters poll as +56,000. All three consensus figures are printed as reported and none is merged into an average. The unemployment rate held at 4.1%. July's reading was a decline of 23,000.

What is genuinely unresolved this morning is how the tape took it, because two reputable sources published different pictures within ten minutes of each other. At roughly 8:38 AM ET, CNBC had Dow futures down 151 points, or 0.3%, S&P 500 futures down 0.2% and Nasdaq-100 futures up 0.1%. At 8:47 AM ET, TheStreet's live blog was headlined “S&P 500 futures edge higher after key jobs report” and described futures as mixed. Both readings are printed with their timestamps rather than reconciled; the defensible summary is that the Dow contract is the weak leg, the Nasdaq contract the firm one, and the S&P contract has been oscillating around unchanged.

The policy read is what matters. This was the last employment report before the FOMC meets September 15–16 with the funds rate at 3.50–3.75%, and the live directional question this year has been whether the Fed hikes, not whether it cuts. Governor Christopher Waller said he would support holding steady if inflationary pressure abates; Kyle Rodda of Capital.com, quoted by TheStreet, says Waller's remarks plus a falling dollar had already pushed market pricing for a September hike back to roughly 50% before this morning's print. A hiring number roughly three times consensus pulls the other way. August CPI is now the swing factor.

One scheduling note that changes the shape of the week: U.S. stock and bond markets are closed Monday, September 7, for Labor Day. Today is the last session before a three-day weekend, which tends to thin liquidity into the close.

NewUpEurope

Volkswagen — up 6.1% on 50,000 more job cuts

Europe's biggest automaker said it will cut a further 50,000 positions, including management roles, under its Future Plan 2030 — twelve initiatives its supervisory board approved Thursday and which the company calls the most strategically profound transformation programme in its 89-year history. The new cuts come on top of 50,000 already approved, for a total of 100,000. Cited drivers: tariff pressure and competition from China.

NewUpCrypto

Up 3.23% to $81,224.60 in early trading. The stated drivers — easing rate-hike concern and falling Treasury yields — come from an 8:22 AM ET item, which is before the 8:30 payrolls release; that release pushed short-end yields the other way, so treat the rationale as pre-print. Bitcoin has historically been weak in September, falling in nine of the past fifteen years.

On the Radar

  • Labor Day, Monday September 7 — markets closed. U.S. stock and bond markets do not open. Today is the last session before a three-day weekend.
  • FOMC, September 15–16. Today's payrolls report was the last employment reading before the decision. The funds rate sits at 3.50–3.75%.
  • August CPI. Waller has explicitly tied his policy bias to it, saying he would back holding rates if inflationary pressures are abating. With hiring this strong, the inflation print becomes the swing factor.
  • Rate-path positioning. Market pricing for a September hike was back to roughly 50% before this morning's release, after Waller's remarks and a falling dollar; the direction of travel in the debate this year has been toward a hike, not a cut.
  • Iran and the oil complex. The EU has formally joined the U.S.-led sanctions campaign on Tehran — “Operation Economic Outcast” — and South Korea is weighing a military role in reopening the Strait of Hormuz. Vice President JD Vance declined to call the six-month-old conflict a war, saying “right now, there is no active shooting”. Crude is the transmission line from that story into equities.
