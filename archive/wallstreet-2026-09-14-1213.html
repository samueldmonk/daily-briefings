The Lead

As of ~11:26 a.m. ET: an AI-safety warning and a $108 oil print knock stocks lower into a Fed week

Markets opened the week defensively and have stayed there. At the opening bell, per TheStreet's live blog, the S&P 500 fell 0.75%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average slipped 0.28%, the Nasdaq Composite lost 1.17% and the Russell 2000 gained 0.45% — a small-cap gain against a semiconductor rout, which looks more like rotation than a broad flight, though nothing read this run characterises it that way. By 11:26 a.m. ET the same live blog put the S&P 500 down 0.65%, with just 233 of its holdings advancing, and described technology and industrials as particularly hard hit. A separate read this run gives the Nasdaq 100 down 1.5%, the S&P 500 down 0.6% and the Dow down 0.2%; the readings are minutes apart and all three are printed rather than averaged.

Two shocks landed over the weekend and they are pulling in the same direction. The first is internal to the AI trade: Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published an essay urging the industry to slow development of frontier models over safety concerns. OpenAI CEO Sam Altman backed him — writing that the pace could go “very badly,” that humans could lose control of AI systems, and that OpenAI is “unapologetically on Team Humanity” — and, per TheStreet, said OpenAI has delayed its 2026 IPO plans over unresolved safety and alignment concerns. Elon Musk also backed the call. Former Anthropic researcher Jacob Coxon resigned last week alleging both companies are “gambling with our lives.” President Trump rejected the whole framing on Truth Social, writing that the only guardrail AI needs is a “STRONG AND SMART (High IQ!) PRESIDENT” and calling the slowdown push a “SICK conspiracy.” Semiconductors took the hit regardless.

The second is external and older: oil. Brent has jumped more than 3% toward $106 per Capital.com's Daniela Hathorn — after gaining almost 9% last week — following fresh attacks on shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and a drone strike that prompted Saudi Arabia to temporarily shut its East-West pipeline, the route being used to bypass the Hormuz closure. By 9:05 a.m. ET, TheStreet had Brent above $108. Yemen's Houthis said they hit Saudi Arabia's King Khalid Air Base at Khamis Mushait with ballistic missiles and drones and claimed direct hits, with no immediate Saudi response; they also say they captured the island of Perim in the Bab al-Mandab Strait and the Greater and Lesser Hanish islands. Oman postponed its planned Iran–Gulf talks on the future of Hormuz without setting a new date.

The rates channel is where the two shocks meet. “The oil shock is feeding directly into the rates story,” Hathorn said — Friday's hotter CPI pushed markets to price roughly an 85% probability of a 25-basis-point Fed hike on Wednesday, with another increasingly expected before year-end. The 10-year Treasury yield rose more than 2 basis points to around 5%, its highest since October 2023, per CNBC via TheStreet; the 2-year advanced more than 2 basis points to 4.666% after touching its highest since July 2024 last week. Bank of America Securities went the other way, saying it remains bullish on semiconductors, calling the frontier-lab controversy “noise” against a secular market in which it expects AI capex to roughly triple, to more than $3 trillion, by the end of the decade — while conceding near-term volatility from rates, Mideast tensions and the Nov. 2 US midterms. Both sides of that argument are on this page because both were published today.

Movers & Drivers

Newgainer

CrowdStrike +10.45% — the CEO who answered Amodei, and got paid for it

CrowdStrike Holdings (CRWD) surged 10.45% as of TheStreet's 10:29 a.m. ET movers post after Chief Executive George Kurtz responded to Amodei, saying frontier AI laboratories would continue developing their technology regardless of any individual company's decision, and that the cybersecurity industry's role is to make that development safer. That is the single largest percentage move named in anything read this run, which is why it takes Chart of the Day below. It is also the neatest illustration of the day's logic: if AI is dangerous, the companies that secure it are not the ones you sell.

Newgainer

Palo Alto Networks +8.97% — earnings, not narrative

Palo Alto Networks (PANW) gained 8.97% following what TheStreet describes as a strong fiscal fourth-quarter earnings beat and continued enthusiasm for AI-driven cybersecurity demand. It was up 5.10% in premarket trade, so the move built through the morning rather than gapping and fading. No specific revenue or EPS figures were stated in anything read this run, so none are printed.

Newgainer

FactSet +7.39% — the rotation destination

FactSet Research Systems (FDS) gained 7.39% as investors rotated out of chip stocks and into established enterprise software and data providers. The company also announced that its Investor Day on Nov. 10 will feature live demonstrations of its integrated next-generation GenAI tools. ServiceNow (NOW) was the same trade earlier, up 4.68% premarket as money moved toward names seen as less exposed to a frontier-AI slowdown.

Newdecliner

Hewlett Packard Enterprise −9.3% — a downgrade about the price, not the business

HPE sank 9.3% after Evercore ISI cut its rating to In Line from Outperform while maintaining its $65 price target — the firm said the stock had already run up, rather than citing a problem with the underlying business. Worth noting against this site's own record: HPE was up nearly 11% on Friday, and the unresolved HPE-versus-Dell ordering question carried here all weekend has been overtaken by events.

Newdecliner

Teradyne −9.3% — the chip-equipment complex takes it worst

Teradyne (TER), which makes automated test equipment for microchips, lost 9.3% amid broader semiconductor weakness, valuation concerns after recent gains, and recent analyst downgrades. It sits alongside Lam Research (LRCX), down 8% in premarket trade in what TheStreet called a broader semiconductor-equipment selloff. TheStreet Pro's Stephen Guilfoyle had called exactly this order of operations before the open: memory, hyperscalers, high-end chip designers and semiconductor-equipment providers leading the market lower. “This is going to at least start out ugly,” he wrote.

Newdecliner

Coherent −8.68% — AI photonics keeps bleeding

Coherent (COHR) tumbled 8.68%, the laser and engineered-materials developer pulled down by what TheStreet describes as persistent selling pressure in the AI photonics sector. Also down: Marvell Technology (MRVL) lost 7.59% premarket as Amodei's call sent semiconductor stocks tumbling globally, and Corning (GLW) sank 9.16% premarket — that one for its own reason, a new equity offering that could raise up to $2 billion and the dilution concern attached.

decliner

SpaceX −2% premarket — and a warning from Einhorn

SpaceX (SPCX) fell 2% to $148.16 in premarket trading amid the AI-linked selloff and the continuing effects of post-IPO insider lockup releases. Separately, David Einhorn warned in Greenlight Capital's second-quarter letter that the IPO could mark a speculative peak, calling the $1.75 trillion IPO valuation a marker that a “speculative top is near.” SpaceX also launched three satellites for SES on a Falcon 9 on Sunday — its 700th Falcon launch, 687 Falcon 9 and 13 Falcon Heavy. Shares debuted at $135 on June 12 and peaked at $225.64 on June 16.

On the Radar

  • Wednesday 16 September — the week in one afternoon. The FOMC's two-day meeting runs 15–16 September; the rate decision lands at 2:00 p.m. ET Wednesday alongside the Summary of Economic Projections and dot plot, followed by the press conference. Markets price roughly a 25 basis-point hike: 85% per Capital.com today, 85.5% on a CME FedWatch read dated 12 September, and 83% on a third read. With a hike this heavily priced, the information is in the projections, not the decision.
  • Today and Tuesday carry no major US data — the last week-ahead read on file says so explicitly, which leaves oil and the AI tape to set the tone until Wednesday.
  • The oil story has no diplomatic off-ramp this week. Oman postponed the Iran–Gulf talks on the Strait of Hormuz without setting a new date, and Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline — the workaround for the Hormuz closure — is itself shut after drone strikes. HSBC has raised its oil forecast, per a TheStreet headline read this run.
  • Fed chair. Kevin Warsh is named as Chair in coverage read this run and on file, and is quoted saying underlying inflation has not “meaningfully improved.” No primary Federal Reserve document confirming the transition has been read on this site, and that caveat stays until one is.
  • Nasdaq 100 rebalance, effective 21 September — carried from Friday's edition, not re-verified this run: SpaceX's index weight set to rise from about 1.28% to roughly 2.82% (a separate read: 2.25%, about $15.5 billion of forced passive buying) as post-IPO lockups expire.
  • The question this tape is actually asking. If two frontier-lab CEOs and the largest private AI investor all publicly argue for slowing down, and the President calls that a conspiracy, the AI capex assumption embedded in semiconductor multiples is now a political variable as well as a demand variable. BofA's answer is that the economics are too large for meaningful deceleration. That is a thesis, not a datum, and it gets tested every session this week.
