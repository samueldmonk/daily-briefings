Top story

A GitLab flaw went from disclosure to in-the-wild exploitation in days

CVE-2026-19478 (CVSS 9.4) is a code-injection flaw that lets an unauthenticated attacker modify or delete publicly accessible GitLab projects and rewrite their data under certain conditions — no credentials, no user interaction, no obscure configuration. GitLab said the issue can be exploited via a GraphQL directive.

Exposure-management firm watchTowr told The Hacker News it reproduced the vulnerability within minutes of looking at it, and that the flaw is now under active exploitation. Affected builds are GitLab Community and Enterprise Edition 18.2 before 18.11.11, 19.0 before 19.0.8, 19.1 before 19.1.6 and 19.2 before 19.2.4; fixes shipped in 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8 and 18.11.11.

The Hacker News, August 21, 2026. No CISA KEV entry for this CVE appeared in the KEV catalog mirror checked this run.

Patch priority
Do this first — the clock runs out today

CVE-2026-72529 — TrueConf Server — CVSS 9.8. A missing-authentication-for-critical-function flaw that lets a remote, unauthorized attacker with network access to port 4307/TCP execute an arbitrary script. CISA added it to the KEV catalog on August 20 with an action due date of August 23 — today. If you run TrueConf Server, patch per the vendor advisory or restrict 4307/TCP now.

Two items outrank it on raw severity but are already overdue rather than due: CVE-2026-72898 (Metabase, CVSS 10.0, exploited as a zero-day) and CVE-2026-59310 (VMware vCenter, CVSS 9.8, path traversal to arbitrary code execution) are nine and two days past due respectively. TrueConf leads here because its deadline is the one that expires today; the overdue entries should already be closed out.

Due dates are the ones stated in the KEV catalog and are assigned per-CVE under BOD 26-04 (risk-based), not on a fixed three-week schedule. The dates used here match the KEV section below exactly.
Threat actor spotlight
EspionageUNC6293 · UNC7005 · UNC5976

Three suspected Russian clusters are phishing with legitimate login flows

Google Threat Intelligence Group researchers Gabby Roncone and Wesley Shields describe three distinct suspected Russian espionage clusters abusing legitimate authentication flows — Google OAuth and WhatsApp device linking — to hijack personal accounts. Targets include academia, aerospace and defense, government and think tanks across Europe, plus academia and think tanks in the US. UNC6293 is assessed as a sub-cluster of Ice Relic (formerly APT29), also tracked as Cozy Bear and Midnight Blizzard; it was previously tied to a campaign abusing Google application-specific passwords. Because the abuse rides on real consent flows, there is no malicious binary for endpoint tooling to catch.

Source: The Hacker News, August 20, 2026, reporting on a GTIG report published that day.
Breaches & incidents
Regulation

TikTok to pay $400 million over US child-privacy claims

The Department of Justice announced Friday that ByteDance-owned TikTok will pay $400 million to settle a 2024 lawsuit accusing it of violating child privacy laws. $300 million is payable immediately, with a further $100 million due “upon entry of an order vacating a prior consent decree entered against TikTok’s predecessor, Musical.ly.” The August 2024 complaint, filed with the FTC, alleged the company knowingly let under-13s create accounts and collected data from them in “Kids Mode.”

Endpoint

Microsoft Defender’s own driver can be turned against security software

Check Point Research disclosed a technique that uses Defender’s legitimately signed boot-time remediation driver, BTR.sys, to perform arbitrary kernel-level file and registry operations on Windows 7 through Windows 11 25H2 — exploiting no software flaw and importing no outside driver. Because BTR.sys is a required Windows component, it cannot be added to Microsoft’s Vulnerable Driver Blocklist or blocked with WDAC without breaking Defender itself. Jiří Vinopal presented the research at Black Hat USA 2026 and DEF CON 34 and published a proof-of-concept tool, BTR_CLI, on August 20. Check Point said it found no evidence of real-world use.

Supply chainNo CVE

Malicious Rust crates ran code at build time

The Rust Project deleted malicious versions of three widely used crates after a compromised maintainer account published releases that added a typosquatted dependency whose build script downloaded and executed a remote payload during compilation. The affected releases — arrayref 0.3.10, internment 0.8.7 and append-only-vec 0.1.9 — were all published August 20 from the same account and removed within 86 to 107 minutes. Because the code sat in a build script, merely resolving the dependency was enough to run it. There is no patched version and no CVE; developers are advised to search ~/.cargo/registry/cache and pin arrayref at 0.3.9 or earlier.

Supply chainnpm

14 trojanized npm packages drop a Linux backdoor

TrendAI, Trend Micro’s enterprise business, identified 14 npm packages posing as calendar and streak utilities that deliver an AI-powered Linux implant dubbed RedC2 4.0. “When the module loads, it locates the bundled binary, marks it executable, and launches it as a detached background process,” the researchers wrote — no install hook required, and a single import anywhere in the dependency graph, even a transitive one, is enough to execute the payload.

Critical infrastructureJoint advisory

AI-generated exploit scripts are probing US industrial controllers

NSA, CISA, FBI, the Department of Energy and the EPA published a joint advisory warning of an “active threat” against US critical infrastructure using AI-generated exploit scripts disguised as legitimate monitoring tools. The activity targets Siemens S7 Series programmable logic controllers for reconnaissance and capability development, though the agencies assess the broader PLC targeting is not limited to Siemens. Operators are using internet scanning services such as Censys and ZoomEye to find exposed, outdated or poorly protected controllers across critical manufacturing, energy and water.

MalwareAutomotive

First documented malware on an Android car head unit

Kaspersky, which discovered the threat in June 2026, flagged a malware family built to infect Android-based vehicle head-unit firmware developed by DoFun, spreading through the units’ own built-in updaters. Researcher Dmitry Kalinin called it the first documented case of malware on a car head unit with an infection chain specific to that device type. The end goal is a multi-stage downloader enabling ad fraud and a proxy botnet; the activity is attributed with high confidence to the MoYu Group, previously named as part of the BADBOX scheme.
