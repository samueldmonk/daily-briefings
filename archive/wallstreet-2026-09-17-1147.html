The Lead

Stocks rebound from the Fed-hike slide — all three indices higher in Thursday morning trade

Markets are open and this edition was built late on Thursday morning. Yahoo Finance’s live blog for 17 September reports that stocks rallied at the open, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq climbing over 1% and the Dow rising 0.7%; later in its session coverage it has the Dow advanced 268 points, or 0.5%, the S&P 500 up 1% and the Nasdaq Composite added 1.7%. TheStreet’s 17 September page is headlined “Nasdaq climbs after Fed rate hike decision”. The move reverses Wednesday, when the Dow shed 631.21 points.

A second read disagrees on magnitude and is printed rather than averaged away: Trading Economics states the S&P 500 “rose to 7,596 points on September 17, 2026, gaining 0.59%”. That is a smaller advance than Yahoo’s 1%. Both are intraday snapshots taken at different moments of a moving session; neither is a close, and this page carries both with their source rather than choosing. Levels are reserved for the Weekly Scorecard below, which holds only settled closes.

The reason offered for the turn is consistent across sources. Bloomberg reports that Treasuries pared losses and equity futures climbed as Fed Chair Kevin Warsh’s resolve on inflation reassured markets after the central bank’s first rate increase since 2023 — a quarter point to a target range of 3.75%–4.00%, approved by all 12 FOMC members, with projections pointing to one more increase this year. Analysts quoted by Yahoo framed the hawkish tone as having restored the Fed’s credibility on inflation rather than damaged sentiment.

Two supports underneath the bounce. Oil edged lower — Trading Economics has WTI crude at $102.13, down 0.29% on 17 September — and the 10-year Treasury yield fell 5 basis points to 4.94% per Yahoo Finance, backing away from the 5% mark it touched this week. And this morning’s labour data cooperated: initial jobless claims fell 10,000 to 196,000 in the week ended 12 September, the lowest since July, with continuing claims at a more-than-two-year low (Bloomberg, citing Labor Department data). Bloomberg notes the period included Labor Day and that the data can fluctuate around holidays.

Movers & Drivers

Thu 17 Sep · session · Schaeffer’s / SEC 8-K / Canaccord

Generac is eyeing its best day ever on an $8 billion Amazon generator deal

Schaeffer’s Investment Research has GNRC last seen up 27.3% at $223.23 and says the stock is eyeing its best day ever. Per Generac’s own 8-K, the company and Amazon entered a Transaction Agreement on 16 September under which Generac issued Amazon a warrant for up to 1,693,745 shares at $200.9266; 307,954 vested immediately and the rest vest against aggregate payments for backup generators for Amazon data centres, up to $8 billion. Initial deliveries are expected to total about $2.4 billion across 2027 and 2028. Canaccord lifted its target to $375 from $275, keeping a Buy and calling the agreement “landmark”.
