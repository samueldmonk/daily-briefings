The Lead

Stocks end a three-day skid, and Dell’s close finally reconciles

All three major U.S. indexes finished Wednesday, September 2 higher, ending a three-session losing streak. The S&P 500 rose 0.46%, the Dow Jones Industrial Average added 295.07 points, or 0.56%, and the Nasdaq Composite gained 0.45%. Those closes have now been independently fetched three times today and have come back identical each time.

The session’s engine was Dell Technologies, the best-performing stock in the S&P 500. Two prior editions of this page could not settle the size of the move, printing +13% against “nearly 11%” and adopting neither. This run resolves it. Dell closed at $492.00, up 15.76% — and that pair reconciles against a separately sourced figure of $425. One source this run reports Dell “closed down 6.8% to $425”; 425 × 1.1576 = 492.03, so this desk reads $425 as the prior close rather than Wednesday’s, on the same reasoning that a figure reconciling against a later quote is a prior close whatever column it appeared in. A second source independently headlines the move as a 16% surge. The catalyst is a record $60.9 billion of AI-server orders, $16.4 billion of AI-optimised server revenue in the quarter, and a record $95 billion backlog; management raised the fiscal 2027 revenue outlook to $192 billion and now expects AI-server revenue to triple to $74 billion.

The mirror image was Palo Alto Networks, the worst performer in the S&P 500, sold hard despite an earnings beat. TheStreet had it down 7.8% at 10:36 AM ET, attributing the slide to slowing growth metrics, margin pressure and elevated expectations rather than the headline print; separate reads put the full-day decline near 10% to 11%. The 10-year Treasury yield touched an intraday high and then eased, which is what allowed the indexes to turn; how far back that high reaches is disputed three ways and is set out in the rates section rather than settled here.

Underneath, the day was broad rather than narrow. TheStreet counted only 33% of U.S. issues declining at 12:41 PM ET and closer to two-thirds advancing by 1:15 PM. The macro print of the morning was soft: ADP reported private payrolls rose just 38,000 in August against a 47,000 estimate and an upwardly revised 46,000 in July — the slowest month since January.

How this edition was built. Research for this run was fetched between 5:36 and 5:50 PM ET, roughly 100 minutes after the close. Index closes are official and reconcile arithmetically against Tuesday’s verified closes. Every intraday figure below carries the time its source published it. Where the live widgets above and this editorial disagree, the widgets are right.
