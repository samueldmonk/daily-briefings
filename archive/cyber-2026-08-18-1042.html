Top story

A past-due KEV bug in Metabase became a list of 13,689 people who own a hardware wallet — and where they live

On Monday, August 10, logistics provider ShipMonk told hardware-wallet maker Trezor that an unauthorised actor had accessed systems holding customer order data. Trezor confirmed the breach publicly on August 13. ShipMonk has told customers the attackers exploited a vulnerability in Metabase, the third-party analytics platform.

That detail is the story. CVE-2026-72898, a Metabase SQL injection flaw, was added to CISA's Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalogue on August 11 with a remediation deadline of August 14 — a date that has already passed. The KEV list is not an abstraction: this is the same class of flaw, exploited against a supplier, landing on a customer base that never touched Metabase.

The exposure totals 13,689 customers — 11,742 with full details (name, email, phone number and shipping address) and 1,947 with partial details (name, city, email). It is limited to customers who received an order between May 10 and August 8, 2026 across seven countries: the United States, United Kingdom, Sweden, Colombia, Brazil, Italy and Portugal.

No private keys were touched and no device was compromised. That is not the risk. What leaked is a verified list of people who own a hardware wallet, complete with the address it was delivered to — close to an ideal targeting list for phishing, and for the kind of threat that does not arrive by email. Trezor is warning customers accordingly: the company will never ask for a seed phrase, and any message that does is fraudulent regardless of how much it knows about your order.

Patch priority

⚠ Do this first

Ray — CVE-2025-62593 (CVSS 9.4), fixed in Ray 2.52.0. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Monday, August 17 citing evidence of active exploitation, and the federal remediation deadline is Thursday, August 20, 2026 — two days away. This is the single most urgent item on the board: it is exploited, it is in KEV, and its deadline is the nearest one still ahead of us. Everything else in the KEV list below is already past due — including the Metabase flaw behind today’s top story. Ray is exploited by the RondoDox DDoS botnet, which folded it in two days before public disclosure, and by the ShadowRay 2.0 campaign that turns unpatched NVIDIA-GPU clusters into a self-replicating mining botnet.

Threat actor spotlight

A suspected China-nexus APT, working the VMware vCenter hole

German incident response firm QUIRSO assesses with moderate confidence that the campaign exploiting CVE-2026-59310 (CVSS 9.8, a directory-traversal flaw in VMware vCenter patched by Broadcom on July 29, 2026) is run by a Chinese-speaking threat actor operating in the UTC+08:00 time zone. The assessment rests on converging evidence: Chinese-language artefacts inside attacker-created scripts, apparent reuse of research from a Chinese security publication, and repeated operational use of Chinese-language tooling and management software. In at least one compromised environment the intrusion led to a backdoor and a reverse shell, and researchers have tied the activity to the deployment of Babuk-derived ransomware — a reminder that an espionage-shaped toolkit and an extortion payload increasingly turn up on the same box.

Breaches & incidents

Vishing1.6M accounts

RingCentral — 1.6 million accounts, and the data is now public

ShinyHunters reached RingCentral's systems through social engineering in July 2026, claimed responsibility and demanded a ransom on July 27; the company detected the activity, brought in a third-party forensic firm and disclosed publicly on July 28. Roughly 623GB was exfiltrated, and after RingCentral refused to pay, a 280GB archive was leaked. The exposed fields are names, email addresses, phone numbers and physical addresses across about 1.6 million accounts. On August 13 Have I Been Pwned added the breach to its database after confirming the leak — which is what moves this from a claim to a usable list. No software version or product release was uniquely at fault; the vector was a person on a phone call.

NewPharmaRansomware

Inotiv — 9,542 people notified after an August ransomware attack

The pharmaceutical research firm is notifying individuals that it was the victim of a ransomware attack in which personal information was stolen. The breach took place in August and affects 9,542 individuals. It is a small count next to the month's headline numbers, and that is rather the point: the disclosed-victim curve is being driven by a steady stream of mid-sized organisations, not by occasional mega-breaches. Black Kite counts 7,551 publicly disclosed ransomware victims between April 2025 and March 2026, up 24.9% on the prior period, with volumes settling into what researchers now describe as an elevated “new normal” baseline rather than a spike.

Supply chainCrypto owners

Trezor via ShipMonk — 13,689 customers

ShipMonk notified Trezor on August 10; Trezor confirmed on August 13. 11,742 customers had name, email, phone and shipping address exposed and 1,947 had name, city and email. Orders received between May 10 and August 8, 2026 in seven countries. ShipMonk attributes the intrusion to a Metabase vulnerability. No keys or devices compromised — the danger is a verified, addressed list of wallet owners.

HardwareAll customers

Framework — every customer notified

The modular-laptop maker told all of its customers that attackers accessed their names, email addresses, phone numbers and physical addresses. As with Trezor, the exposed set is contact and delivery data rather than credentials or payment details — which makes it a phishing and social-engineering problem, not a financial one.

SaaSExtortion

Brinks Home — ShinyHunters claims 4.9 million Salesforce records

The smart-home security provider was breached on July 13 through a Microsoft Entra voice-phishing (vishing) attack, ShinyHunters says; Brinks first detected it on July 20, 2026. The haul is put at more than 4.9 million Salesforce records containing PII — over 1.1 million rows from a customer-contacts database, more than 4,000 employee records (names, emails, job titles, phone numbers) and roughly 3.8 million customer-support chat logs from the Brinks Care Cresta platform. Note the arithmetic: 4.9 million is a count of records, not of people — one customer can appear many times. The group set a July 30 deadline and threatened to leak.

GovernmentTax data

France's finance ministry — 678,000 people and businesses

The ministry confirmed on August 14 that attackers used stolen staff logins to view and copy tax and property data on 678,000 people and businesses. Credential theft, not a software flaw — which is the recurring shape of this month's largest incidents.

Supply chainRansomware

Clop claims 43 organisations via a PTC Windchill flaw

Check Point Research reports the Clop ransomware gang targeted 43 organisations — among them Shell, General Electric and Philips — by exploiting CVE-2026-12569, a critical improper input validation flaw in PTC Windchill and FlexPLM. The chain deployed JSP webshells to steal blueprints, engineering drawings and facility photographs. Clop claims 89GB taken from Shell, and Shell has now confirmed it is investigating a potential security incident in response; Philips has confirmed a breach of one enterprise server holding internal data.

HealthcareClaimed

MyDr, Poland's main healthcare platform

Per Check Point Research's August 17 threat intelligence report, MyDr — Poland's primary platform for appointments, medical records and prescriptions — suffered a data breach potentially affecting close to 19 million citizens. The attackers claim to hold 2.5TB of information. Treat the volume as an attacker assertion until the operator confirms scope.

CryptoThird party

SafePal — 39,798 customers

The hardware wallet maker disclosed that an authorisation flaw in an order-tracking plug-in exposed the names, email addresses, shipping addresses, phone numbers and purchase details of approximately 39,798 customers, who were each emailed individually on August 16 under the subject line “[Important] Your SafePal Order Information Has Been Affected”. Under certain conditions the flaw allowed one customer to reach another customer’s order information. SafePal says the incident did not involve seed phrases, private keys, wallet passwords, bank details, card numbers or government ID, and that affected customers were emailed individually from security@safepal.com. It is the second hardware-wallet customer list to surface in a fortnight.

FinanceSSNs

Heights Finance — over 1.2 million people

The consumer lender discovered unauthorised access on May 7, 2026 to a third-party cloud platform holding customer data, and began notifications on August 11. Exposed: names, addresses, Social Security numbers and bank account details, with phone, email, tax IDs, driver's licence or state ID and dates of birth possibly involved. Loan management systems were not affected. Twenty-four months of credit monitoring is on offer.

Also on the wire

  • A UK police data lapse exposed highly sensitive records, the information watchdog said on August 17 — the kind of process failure that never appears in a CVE table but produces exactly the same downstream harm as an exploited flaw.
  • Qilin hit German engineering firm Motorenmaier GmbH on August 16 and is threatening to leak the stolen data unless its demands are met; a separate operator tracked as xpl0itrs compromised school-management software on August 15, disrupting operations and threatening exposure. Neither victim is large; both fit the steady mid-market pattern the annual counts describe.
  • DeadLock has moved its victim-negotiation and leak infrastructure onto decentralised hosting to improve operational resilience — the same takedown-resistance logic behind earlier bulletproof-hosting migrations, and one that complicates the usual disruption levers.
  • A new typosquatting campaign is targeting RubyGems users with a Windows-based information stealer, flagged by researchers on August 15, 2026 — the same package-registry playbook that keeps working on npm and PyPI.
  • A threat actor is claiming the exfiltration of millions of records from McDonald's, TCS and Vodafone, among other large organisations. It is an attacker claim at this stage, not a confirmed disclosure from any of the named companies — treat the volume accordingly until an operator confirms scope.
  • Snowflake's public .NET connector repo carried a GitHub Actions workflow injection flaw, Wiz disclosed — a crafted GitHub issue could execute commands in a workflow that held internal Jira credentials (JIRA_BASE_URL, JIRA_USER_EMAIL, JIRA_API_TOKEN). Confined to CI/CD automation; no affected connector release identified.
  • Cavern (aka Cav3rn), the C2 framework used by Iranian nation-state hackers against Israeli targets, has gained a module that uses DNS A-record responses to switch between direct HTTPS and a Google Apps Script relay per transaction, Kaspersky reports.
  • Evooo1Bot, a previously undocumented Linux botnet built on leaked Mirai source, has been active since July 2026 — adding encrypted C2, an SSH brute-force scanner, a SOCKS relay and a credential sniffer, and turning internet-facing edge devices into SOCKS5 proxies (Fortinet FortiGuard Labs).
  • Unisoc modem firmware can be chained from a VoLTE video call to full Android kernel access, per SSD Secure Disclosure's August 17 advisory — with no fix from the chipset maker, which has not responded to contact attempts.
  • Gunra, the destructive extortion syndicate hitting government and healthcare targets, now operates as a large ransomware-as-a-service network, per a joint federal advisory noted in Check Point's August 17 report. Colombia's Ministry of Justice was hit by ransomware that disrupted illicit-drug monitoring and legal processes; files were encrypted but no data theft was detected.
