Top Story

PaperCut exploitation escalates from scanning to hands-on intrusions — and the first patch did not hold

Attacks against two PaperCut NG/MF vulnerabilities disclosed on August 27 have escalated, with threat actors shifting from reconnaissance to hands-on-keyboard activity (SecurityWeek). PaperCut’s updated indicators of compromise point specifically to the deployment of remote access tools on targeted systems (Help Net Security), and Cybersecurity Dive reports the flaws are being exploited by multiple threat actors. A material new detail landed this run: the two emergency patches are still not the finished article. SC Media reports PaperCut is working on an official release that addresses both vulnerabilities — meaning an estate that applied Emergency Patch Release 2 is running a hotfix, not a shipped version, with a further update still to come. ⚠ Defender point recorded: “patched” and “on a supported release” have come apart here, and change-management that tracks version numbers rather than patch levels will not see the difference.

The chain has an order, and the order is the whole story. CVE-2026-81578 comes first — missing authentication for a critical function lets an unauthenticated remote attacker modify certain system configuration settings. CVE-2026-82078 then turns those manipulated parameters into execution of arbitrary Java bytecode already on the application classpath, in the security context of the PaperCut server process. Read separately, neither flaw sounds like a takeover; read in sequence, the first supplies the input the second trusts. Because the bytecode is already present, nothing is uploaded — which is why file-drop detection sees nothing until the remote-access tooling arrives.

The timeline now starts before the disclosure. Huntress identified exploitation as early as August 26 — a day before PaperCut disclosed — after review of PaperCut log files turned up base64-encoded reconnaissance commands run by the attacker. Those log entries are the earliest reliable detection artefact available, and they predate every advisory an estate would have been watching for. Emergency Patch Release 2 followed on August 28, published after PaperCut worked with watchTowr and Huntress researchers to close bypasses of the earlier fix.

Two operational points. First, the vendor now considers all versions of PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF potentially impacted — a list of patched versions is not a list of affected ones, and an estate that used the version list as a triage filter filtered on the wrong thing. Second, an estate that patched once, promptly, and moved on is not covered: the September 14 clock is measured against the current build, not against the act of patching. Shadowserver counts more than 1,000 PaperCut NG/MF instances exposed to the internet — the first population figure this page has been able to attach to the campaign.

Patch Priority — Citrix NetScaler ADC / Gateway, CVE-2026-8452, deadline was August 29 (0 days left — three days overdue)

This is the most urgent item on the page and it is urgent because the clock has already run out. CISA added CVE-2026-8452 to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 26, 2026 and, invoking BOD 26-04, ordered federal civilian agencies to remediate by August 29, 2026. That date passed three days ago. The flaw is a pre-authentication memory-buffer overflow in NetScaler ADC and NetScaler Gateway that yields unauthenticated remote code execution — an internet-facing appliance that is, by design, the front door to everything behind it. Exploitation is not theoretical: the KEV addition was accompanied by telemetry showing 36 distinct exploitation attempts over a 12-day window from 12 unique attacker IP addresses spread across Switzerland, Germany, Hong Kong, Japan, the Netherlands, Russia, Singapore, Türkiye, the United States and Vietnam. Observed post-exploitation activity included web shells named x.php and z.php alongside discovery commands — so patching alone does not close the incident. Patch, then hunt those artefacts. ⚠ Two cautions on this CVE, both recorded because the reporting around it is muddled. First, on scoring: Citrix scores it 8.8 under CVSS v4.0 while NVD lists 9.8 under CVSS v3.1. Those are different scales, not a contradiction, and this page leads with the vendor figure per its standing rule. Second, on identity: at least one write-up titles this a “NetScaler SAML RCE.” That is a conflation with CVE-2026-3055, the separate NetScaler flaw whose exploitability is confined to SAML IdP configurations and whose official Citrix score is 9.3. They are two different vulnerabilities and are not merged here.

Second, and still running — PaperCut NG/MF, deadline September 14 (13 days left)

Patch or isolate every PaperCut NG and PaperCut MF server today, then verify you are on Emergency Patch Release 2, published August 28, and not the first fix. Emergency patches are available for NG/MF versions 24, 25 and 26 on Windows, Linux and macOS. CISA added CVE-2026-81578 (CVSS 8.8, missing authentication for a critical function) and CVE-2026-82078 (CVSS 9.4, unsafe reflection) to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on August 31, 2026 with a remediation deadline of September 14, 2026 for federal civilian executive branch agencies. Hunt for unexplained remote-access tooling on print servers — that is the escalation marker, and it appears after the exploitation that precedes it leaves no dropped file.

The Gentlemen — the most prolific government-sector crew of 2026, now running a countdown clock on Glassdoor

In the first half of 2026 The Gentlemen was the most active ransomware group targeting government organisations, claiming 22 attacks, ahead of Qilin with 21 and LockBit with 14. The group runs double extortion against corporate targets and has reported ties to Qilin affiliates — which is worth holding alongside the ATF incident below, where Qilin is the claimant. The Glassdoor claim now has dates and a denominator: the crew listed Glassdoor on its leak site on August 30, 2026, with leak-site monitoring putting the estimated attack date at August 28, and started a 172-hour countdown — just over a week — before it says it will publish. No data samples are public, so the scale and the type of exposed information remain unknown: it is not established whether the claimed haul is Glassdoor corporate data, employee records, job-seeker records, or a mix. What is established is the size of the platform behind the name — up to 67 million unique monthly visitors, more than 2 million companies with reviews, and millions of live job listings. ⚠ An unverified claim against a large platform is still an unverified claim; the countdown is real, the haul is not yet.

The 22-attack figure understates the operation. The Gentlemen is a ransomware-as-a-service business that surfaced publicly in September 2025 and has claimed more than 320 victims by April 2026, roughly 240 of them in the first months of 2026 alone — a scaling curve, not a steady state. Per victim it exfiltrates anywhere from hundreds of gigabytes to multiple terabytes before encrypting, then publishes on a Tor leak site and applies public pressure through a branded X account. Microsoft’s researchers describe the payload as a self-propagating Go encryptor that spreads across a network on its own, which is what turns a single unpatched edge device into an estate-wide event. Its claims are not all bluster: the Portuguese public transport operator Metro Mondego confirmed it was the victim of a ransomware attack after The Gentlemen claimed responsibility, and researchers counted nine of the group’s claims as confirmed in that month alone. That confirmation is dated July 2026 and is cited here as corroboration of the crew’s record, not as today’s news.

Set against a second, differently-shaped operator: CISA reports Medusa affiliates have breached more than 500 organisations across critical infrastructure, working opportunistically by monitoring vulnerability announcements and hitting organisations that have not yet patched. One crew picks a name and starts a clock; the other lets a disclosure pick the names for it. A partially-patched PaperCut estate is squarely in the second group’s target set — which is the argument for treating the KEV date as a floor rather than a target.

Berlin state government — Rhysida auctions 5.79 TB, three weeks before an election

The ransomware group Rhysida claimed Berlin on its leak site on August 28, posting an entry titled “Berlin, Germany” and claiming 5.79 terabytes across roughly 1.44 million files — including 46,500 contracts plus emails, phone numbers, passwords and classified information, with personal information on 12,076 individuals said to be among it. The group offered the data at auction from a starting price of 30 bitcoin ($77,622), behind a countdown of just under seven days.

Berlin says it will not submit to extortion and investigators are pursuing the attackers. The city’s state election falls on September 20; officials say the election infrastructure was not affected and that no election-related data was compromised.

⚠ The timeline is the part to read twice. The exfiltration ran August 7 to August 12, and the affected department flagged an initial outflow internally on August 7 — a full week before the network was cut off. Detection worked; containment did not keep pace with it. That is the opposite failure to the PaperCut story above, where the earliest artefact predated every advisory an estate would have been watching for. Here the signal was raised on day one, from inside, and 5.79 TB left anyway.

HardBreacher — a PoC against Kaspersky Endpoint Security, published with no CVE and no confirmation

A proof-of-concept named HardBreacher was published on GitHub by a user called MSNightmare, claiming a local privilege-escalation flaw in Kaspersky Endpoint Security on a fully patched Windows 11 system running version 14.0.0.504. The claimed effect is the creation of a DLL at C:\Windows\System32\MY_SNAKE_IS_SOLID.dll with full permissions for the current user — System32 being normally protected, that would indicate a low-privileged local user crossing a Windows security boundary.

What is NOT established, stated plainly: the issue is unverified, has not been publicly confirmed and has no CVE assigned by Kaspersky. Kaspersky says the underlying issue has been fixed through a database update, and there is no public evidence attackers used the technique before disclosure. It is not a remote initial-access exploit — an attacker would already need local code execution or an account. It is therefore reported here as an incident and deliberately kept OUT of the CVE table below, which admits only assigned identifiers.

⚠ The researcher’s name is itself disputed and was not resolved. SecurityWeek attributes the release to “Nightmare Eclipse”; Security Affairs attributes it to “Chaotic Eclipse”; the GitHub account is MSNightmare. Only the GitHub handle is a primary artefact — the other two are outlet renderings of a nickname — so all three are printed and none is adopted. This is the Salahdine Parnasse problem in a different vertical: two reputable outlets, one entity, two spellings.

McKesson — the extortion clock runs out today, and the intrusion now has a route

The healthcare distribution giant confirmed a breach on August 31. Its SEC filing places detection on August 25, 2026. ShinyHunters told BleepingComputer how it got in, and the route is entirely human: vishing calls to McKesson employees, then stolen credentials used to take over Okta single sign-on accounts, then movement into the company’s Salesforce and Snowflake environments, from which it claims to have pulled roughly a terabyte of data over four days. The 284 million records figure is the attacker’s claim and is printed as one; nothing fetched establishes how many correspond to distinct people. The group is demanding approximately $55 million not to publish, and says the company has until September 1 — today — to open negotiations. The defender point is that not one step of the described chain required a vulnerability: a phone call, an identity provider and two SaaS platforms the security team may not treat as part of the estate.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • CVE-2026-81578 and CVE-2026-82078 (PaperCut NG/MF) — added August 31, 2026, remediation due September 14, 2026 (13 days left). This is the same date carried in the Patch Priority box above; there is one deadline for this pair and it is that one.
  • CVE-2026-8452 (Citrix NetScaler ADC and Gateway) — added August 26, 2026, remediation due August 29, 2026 (0 days left — three days overdue) under BOD 26-04. This is the same date carried in the Patch Priority box at the top of the page. ⚠ An elapsed deadline is not a closed one — the entry stays in the catalog, and an appliance that was unpatched on August 29 is still unpatched today unless somebody acted.
  • Six vulnerabilities added August 26, including CVE-2026-8452 (Citrix NetScaler), CVE-2019-1068 (Microsoft SQL Server remote code execution) and CVE-2022-0995 (Linux kernel out-of-bounds write). Their assigned due dates span August 29 to September 9 — some already elapsed, the latest (8 days left).
  • CVE-2026-55040 (Microsoft SharePoint Server) — a weak-authentication flaw added to the KEV catalog, with CISA updating its SharePoint alert on August 18, 2026 to reflect it. It affects all supported on-premises versions — Subscription Edition, 2019 and 2016, and the post-exploitation pattern is the one to hunt for rather than the initial entry: theft of IIS machine keys and deserialization techniques used to establish persistence and deploy malware. Rotating machine keys after patching is the step most often skipped.
  • Deadlines are per-CVE and risk-based. Under BOD 26-04, CISA assigns a remediation date to each entry; the old flat “three weeks from the add date” heuristic from BOD 22-01 no longer applies and is not used to compute anything on this page. Every countdown here is measured against a date CISA published. CISA’s own August 31 alert restates the framing: BOD 26-04 requires federal civilian agencies to prioritise rapid remediation of high-risk KEV entries on publicly exposed assets, and CISA urges all organisations — not only FCEB agencies — to adopt the same risk-based prioritisation.
