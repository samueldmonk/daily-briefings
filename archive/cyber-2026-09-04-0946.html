Top Story

Thomson Reuters breach: four months inside a court-records platform, and the list of affected courts is still growing

Thomson Reuters has disclosed a data breach affecting C-Track, the court case management platform operated by its subsidiaries. The company published the disclosure on Wednesday, September 2, alongside separate notification sites for affected individuals in the United States and Canada.

The timeline is the uncomfortable part. Thomson Reuters says it discovered unauthorised activity on June 30, 2026, and that the files were actually taken in March 2026 — roughly four months of undetected access, disclosed roughly two months after discovery. Four questions the company has not publicly answered: who was responsible, how much and which data was taken, how the attacker got in, and why they went unnoticed for months.

The scope reaches courts in at least twelve U.S. states, the U.S. Virgin Islands and Canada. Named among them: the Court of Appeal for Ontario, the Ontario Superior Court of Justice and the Ontario Court of Justice; the Montana Supreme Court, the North Dakota Supreme Court, the New Hampshire Supreme Court, the Supreme Court of South Carolina and its Court of Appeals, Alabama, Kentucky and Nevada appellate courts, eleven Ohio district courts of appeals, two Pennsylvania common pleas courts and the Fifth Judicial District of Pennsylvania, and the entire Wyoming Judicial Branch. The Oregon Judicial Department has separately confirmed its appellate courts are affected. Because individual courts are still issuing their own disclosures, the full list is explicitly not complete.

What may have been exposed: names together with one or more of Social Security numbers, driver's licence numbers, medical information, dates of birth and health insurance information — and, at some courts, confidential, redacted or sealed court information. On the Canadian side the chief justices of the three affected courts said it remains unclear what was compromised or how many people are involved. Thomson Reuters says the incident occurred inside its own cloud environment rather than on court networks, that C-Track remains fully operational, that it has no evidence to date of misuse, and that it will offer affected individuals 12 months of credit monitoring and identity theft protection.

No victim count, no attacker attribution and no intrusion vector has been stated by the company or in any source fetched this edition, so none is published here. The jurisdiction list above is quoted from the disclosure rather than summarised into a number.

⚡ Patch Priority — SonicWall SMA1000, due tomorrow

CVE-2026-83548 — pre-authentication server-side request forgery in the SonicWall SMA1000 Appliance Work Place interface, rated Critical / CVSS 10.0 by SonicWall, actively exploited and in CISA's catalogue with a federal remediation date of September 5, 2026 — one day left. Affected models are the 6210, 7210 and 8200v; affected builds are 12.4.3-03453 and older (fixed in 12.4.3-03526) and 12.5.0-02835 and older (fixed in 12.5.0-02952).

The reason this outranks everything else on the page: the two SonicWall flaws chain. CVE-2026-83548 grants unauthenticated access to functionality that is then used to exploit CVE-2026-83549 and execute operating system commands — pre-auth SSRF to pre-auth command execution, in two steps. SonicWall disclosed both on September 1 and confirmed exploitation in the wild. If you run SMA1000 anywhere, this is today's work.

Threat Actor Spotlight

NewEspionageChina-linked

Fire Ant — out of the hypervisor and into the network itself

Sygnia has documented Fire Ant, a cyber-espionage actor it links to China, expanding beyond VMware hypervisors to compromise Cisco IOS XR routers, TACACS servers and Linux management hosts inside high-value networks. The investigation began with a detail an administrator would have to be looking for: a GRE tunnel on an IOS XR router with no configuration or history to justify it.

The tradecraft is built around hiding from the console rather than from the endpoint agent. Implants tailor-made for the IOS XR control plane replaced a system library so that only log messages containing the string “Health” were forwarded, and altered the command execution path by appending an “| exclude” filter to “show” commands — so an operator typing the obvious diagnostic command could not see the tunnel. On the TACACS server they deployed TacTap, which injects a malicious library into the tac_plus process and dumps credentials to /var/log/.tacplus.acct under a one-byte XOR — a technique Sygnia says had not been publicly described before.

Around that: the Medusa and REPTILE rootkits, custom SSH backdoors, and binaries that impersonated SentinelOne and Cybereason agents, alongside suppressed SNMP traps, disabled SELinux and a rewritten access log. The through-line is an actor moving into the trusted infrastructure that security tooling depends on to report the truth.

IoT

Operation CameraSwarm — 14,530 Dahua cameras

A single operator compromised at least 14,530 Dahua IP cameras between 17 June and 22 July 2026. Three concurrent paths: brute force on TCP 37777 (12,324 unique IPs), authentication bypass via CVE-2021-33044 / CVE-2021-33045 (1,923 cameras), and a cloud-relay attack on 283 cameras behind NAT using serial numbers and SDK credentials embedded in Dahua's own applications. Headline counts vary between 14,000, 14,500+ and 14,530; all three are reported, none averaged.

AI

An AI-agent ransomware intrusion, start to finish in under ten hours

Unit 42 has detailed an incident in which a human attacker used edge AI models and agent-specific frameworks to run a near-autonomous intrusion as part of a ransomware operation. The headline figures come from the attacker’s own statement during ransom negotiations — they are a claim made by the intruder, not an independent measurement, and are reported here on that basis: the agents executed more than 50 MITRE ATT&CK techniques and completed in under ten hours an intrusion that would normally take around two weeks of coordinated manual work.

The chain Unit 42 describes: initial access through a public API endpoint, then mapping the internal architecture, extracting tokens and passwords from code repositories, escalating privileges until the agents held master credentials for the secret manager, hijacking CI/CD pipelines to exfiltrate cloud access keys, and attempting to plant backdoors. The attacker then turned the victim’s own cloud AI infrastructure — already compromised with the stolen keys — into post-compromise infrastructure for later phases. The recommendation that follows is about speed rather than controls: containment playbooks that can revoke credentials and isolate cloud accounts at the pace an automated attack moves. No victim, ransom figure or named actor appears in the sources fetched this edition.

macOSDPRK

Fourteen fake macOS installers deliver a credential-stealing RAT

Mac users are being targeted with 14 fake application installers that present as familiar software and instead deploy a credential-stealing remote-access trojan, linked in reporting to a DPRK campaign. No victim count was stated in the sources fetched this edition, so none is published.

NewMalwareChina-linked

PackClient — an off-the-shelf RAT in the hands of TA4922

Proofpoint has identified PackClient, a full remote-access framework sold through Telegram channels and used by the Chinese actor TA4922 against organisations in China and India. It offers a modular architecture, dual C2 channels, a keylogger, screenshot capture, SOCKS tunnels, remote system access and specific capability for monitoring applications such as Telegram Desktop. Observed campaigns use tax-related lures and ZIP and IMG files that install the malware via DLL sideloading. The concern Proofpoint raises is the commercial availability itself: it lowers the bar for other Chinese-speaking actors to adopt it and expand into global campaigns.

Extortion

Manchester Airports Group — 8.8 million people

Data on 8.8 million people was leaked after the group reportedly refused to pay a ransom demand. Other reporting puts the figure at 8.7 million; both are printed and neither is adopted. Payment information was not compromised and operations were unaffected.

CISA KEV & Federal Deadlines

  • Due September 5, 2026 — 1 day left — five CVEs: CVE-2026-83548 and CVE-2026-83549 (SonicWall SMA1000), CVE-2026-9586 (Sangoma Switchvox), CVE-2026-82329 (JFrog Artifactory), CVE-2026-49869 (Kestra OSS).

  • Five plus two is the full set of seven. The September 5 date was corroborated again this edition by four independent trackers — Rapid7, Qualys, Sophos and SoCRadar — each citing the September 2 KEV addition, and it is the same date used in the Patch Priority callout above and in the Vulnerability Watch table.
  • Next Patch Tuesday is Tuesday, September 8 — unusually early in the month. Ivanti expects the “ShieldBreak” Defender fix in that release, plus the weekly Chrome update; Mozilla shipped its major updates on September 1 and Adobe on August 25, so both are expected to be quiet.
  • End-of-support dates worth diarising now: October Patch Tuesday brings the final updates for Windows 11 Version 24H2 Home and Professional, and ends ESU support for Windows Server 2012 and 2012 R2 and for Exchange Server 2016/2019.
