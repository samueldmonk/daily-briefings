Top Story

Cisco’s perfect 10: an ISE authentication bypass is being exploited for root, and the emergency fix is out

CVE-2026-76460 carries the maximum CVSS score of 10.0. Cisco disclosed it on Wednesday as an authentication bypass affecting Identity Services Engine (ISE) and the ISE Passive Identity Connector (ISE-PIC), regardless of device configuration. The cause is insufficient authentication control on an API endpoint; a successful exploit lets an unauthenticated, remote attacker bypass the web-based management interface and reach command execution with root privileges.

Cisco’s Product Security Incident Response Team says it is aware of active exploitation and urges customers to install the fixes immediately. The Register reports that Cisco found the flaw while resolving a Technical Assistance Center support case — meaning it surfaced inside a customer environment before the bug was known — and that Cisco has not said who is exploiting it, how long the attacks have been running, or what intruders did after gaining access.

It did not arrive alone. Of 77 new CVEs Cisco issued Wednesday, 41 affect ISE and 28 the Secure Firewall portfolio. Two further Cisco advisories also carry maximum 10.0 scores, and a separate trio of remote-code-execution flaws scores as high as 9.9. Permanent fixes for the exploited bug are in ISE and ISE-PIC 3.1 Patch 12, 3.2 Patch 11, 3.3 Patch 12, 3.4 Patch 7 and 3.5 Patch 4.

Patch Priority

Patch Cisco ISE and ISE-PIC today — CVE-2026-76460, CVSS 10.0, exploited, federal deadline 19 September

This is the one thing to do first. It is the maximum severity, it is confirmed under active exploitation by the vendor, it is exploitable regardless of device configuration, and it hands an unauthenticated remote attacker root. CISA added it to the Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on 16 September; Federal Civilian Executive Branch agencies must apply the patches by 19 September 2026 — 2 days left. Fixed releases: 3.1 P12, 3.2 P11, 3.3 P12, 3.4 P7, 3.5 P4. If you cannot patch within the window, treat any internet-reachable ISE management interface as already in scope for investigation, not just for remediation.

Unit 42 — 2026 Global Incident Response Report

The 15-minute window

Unit 42 finds attackers begin scanning for newly disclosed vulnerabilities within 15 minutes of a CVE being announced, with exploitation increasingly automated. It also records a shift away from encryption toward data theft and extortion-only operations — less operational complexity, the same pressure on victims. Read against today’s Cisco advisory, that is the argument for treating the 19 September deadline as a ceiling rather than a target.
