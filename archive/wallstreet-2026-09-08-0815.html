The Lead

Pre-open, as of ~8:05 AM ET: oil is doing the leading and the Dow is doing the losing as the holiday break ends

The session has not started yet. Regular U.S. trading resumes at 9:30 AM ET today, Tuesday 8 September, after the New York Stock Exchange, Nasdaq and the U.S. bond market were shut all day on Monday 7 September for Labor Day. Everything below on equities is either a futures or pre-market quote, or Friday 4 September’s completed session — and each is labelled as such.

Futures are mixed, and the split is the story. Contracts on the Dow Jones Industrial Average fell 308 points, or 0.6%; S&P 500 futures declined 0.2%; Nasdaq-100 futures edged 0.1% higher. A second read this morning gives the Dow figure as 0.7% lower against the same S&P 500 and Nasdaq-100 descriptions, so the Dow decline is stated here as a 0.6–0.7% range rather than resolved to one number. The Nasdaq-100’s resilience is attributed to a rebound in select technology and semiconductor names; the Invesco QQQ Trust was showing a gain of 0.08%.

What is moving them. Investors are weighing rising oil prices, heightened hostilities in the Middle East and an escalating trade dispute with Canada. Energy shares rose in pre-market trading, tracking crude higher after Iran warned that Gulf oil and gas infrastructure could be targeted in retaliation for attacks on its own assets. Crude held above $92 a barrel, hovering at its highest level in three months, after Iran said an agreement with Oman to manage shipping through the Strait of Hormuz was nearing completion — fuelling concern about Tehran’s growing control of the waterway. Separately, Canada’s retaliatory tariffs on roughly $20 billion of U.S. goods are set to take effect today.

The macro backdrop has not softened. Fears of an ongoing Middle East war have revived inflation angst and the possibility of a Federal Reserve rate hike this month, particularly after Friday’s blowout August jobs report: payrolls grew 162,000, the unemployment rate was 4.1%, and average hourly earnings rose 3.1% year-over-year. The consensus figure this desk carries for that print is 53,000, verified repeatedly across earlier editions; a return read this run described forecasts as 56,000 and another earlier gave 55,000, which is separately the June–July upward revision total. The three numbers are printed side by side rather than silently reconciled — the beat is not in dispute, the forecast baseline is.

Friday was the last completed session, and it was red. The S&P 500 fell 0.38%, the Nasdaq Composite fell 0.29% and the Dow fell 0.51%, with Treasury yields rising, as the jobs beat pushed traders toward pricing a hike rather than a cut. Levels are in the Weekly Scorecard. Two events this week will do more to settle the question than anything in the pre-market: Oracle reports after today’s close, and August CPI lands Friday 11 September.

Energy is the whole of the green side of the pre-market board

Shares of major U.S. energy companies gained before the open, tracking crude. Valero Energy +1.9%, Exxon Mobil +1.8%, ConocoPhillips +1.6%, Chevron +1.5%, and Diamondback Energy and Marathon Petroleum each +1.1%. Pre-market quotes, not opening prints; they can and often do fade at 9:30.

NewCrude

Brent runs to $99.19 as Iran threatens Gulf infrastructure

Brent crude futures for November delivery rose 2.3% to $99.19 a barrel; U.S. West Texas Intermediate climbed 3.3% to $94.49. Crude held above $92, its highest level in three months. The trigger was Iran’s warning that Gulf oil and gas infrastructure could be targeted in reprisal, alongside its statement that an agreement with Oman on managing shipping through the Strait of Hormuz is nearing completion.

NewTrade

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs take effect today

Canada’s retaliatory tariffs on roughly $20 billion of U.S. goods are set to take effect Tuesday, adding a second policy shock to a session already pricing a Middle East supply risk. No affected-sector breakdown was reached at this desk this run, so none is printed.

NewEarnings

Oracle reports after the close — the week’s cleanest read on enterprise cloud spend

Oracle Corp (ORCL) reports Q1 2027 results after today’s close, with an estimated EPS of $1.67. Bank of America analyst Tal Liani projects 25% sequential and 116% year-over-year growth in infrastructure-as-a-service revenue and roughly 12.8% growth in Cloud SaaS revenue for the period. As a bellwether for enterprise cloud spending, the print is a major catalyst for the software sector.
