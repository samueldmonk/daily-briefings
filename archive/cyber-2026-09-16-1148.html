Top Story

Two Chinese clusters ran the same Chrome-to-kernel chain through a Chromium patch gap

Volexity has attributed a spear-phishing campaign against multiple non-governmental organisations on 1 September 2026 to a Chinese threat cluster it tracks as UTA0560, which chained three recently patched flaws in Google Chrome and Microsoft Windows to deliver a JavaScript backdoor called GRIMWEDGE. The lure emails pointed at a legitimate U.S. university website carrying a reflected cross-site scripting flaw, which redirected recipients into attacker-controlled infrastructure hosting the exploit chain.

The chain — also called BlueMoon — abuses CVE-2026-85046 for arbitrary read/write inside the V8 sandbox, escapes the browser sandbox via CVE-2026-87491, and uses CVE-2026-85880 in the Windows Advanced Local Procedure Call subsystem to inject code into the Chrome process. It filters out anything not running Chrome on Windows, then delivers three Base64-encoded shellcode payloads — host fingerprinting, kernel privilege escalation and browser process injection — before a DLL sideloading chain fetches an MSI whose custom actions execute GRIMWEDGE in memory via eval(), polling the C2 at ocr.opusaccel[.]top.

Volexity says a second China-nexus actor, JungleBamboo (APT31), ran the same chain at around the same time to install a loader named SUPERSTOMP and then LONGTALE, a credential-stealing Chrome extension masquerading as a Google Gemini add-on that exfiltrates keystrokes, cookies, storage data and navigation history roughly every 30 seconds. The near-simultaneous use by multiple actors raises the possibility the chain was sold on by its developer.

The sting is the patch gap: fixes for the two Chrome flaws had landed in the open-source Chromium codebase but not in a stable Chrome release, making them N-days in Chromium and zero-days against Chrome. Google moved Chrome from a four-week to a two-week milestone release cycle last week. “Patch-gap vulnerabilities present an even greater risk, as they create an additional time window for threat actors to conduct exploitation campaigns,” Volexity said, “[…] as large language models become more popular and effective for rapid vulnerability research and exploit development.”

Patch Priority

Today is the deadline: CVE-2026-59822 (BerriAI LiteLLM) and CVE-2026-48710 (Kludex Starlette)

Federal civilian agencies have until 16 September 2026 — today, 0 days left — to remediate the LiteLLM improper-authentication flaw (CVSS 8.8: an MCP Streamable HTTP endpoint that will establish an authenticated session from an arbitrary Bearer token) and the Starlette HTTP request/response smuggling flaw (CVSS 6.5, which can bypass authentication that depends on a reconstructed URL path). The Hacker News states the extended deadline explicitly: agencies had until 5 September for the rest of the 2 September batch, and “until September 16, 2026, to address the Starlette and LiteLLM flaws.” Wiz has observed exploitation attempts against CVE-2026-59822 on its honeypots and links Qilin-associated actors to the LiteLLM chain; Microsoft has seen CVE-2026-48710 chained with CVE-2026-42271 to deliver an XMRig miner and harvest provider key material from LiteLLM’s PostgreSQL tier. Next up: CVE-2026-76461, the actively exploited Cisco Secure Email Gateway (AsyncOS) flaw at CVSS 9.8, due 17 September — 1 day left.

Threat Actor Spotlight

China-nexus espionage

UTA0560

Volexity’s designation for the cluster that ran the BlueMoon Chrome-Windows chain against NGOs on 1 September 2026. Its payload, GRIMWEDGE, is an obfuscated JavaScript backdoor with a deliberately narrow command set — Info, Dir, Mkdir, Del, Tasklist, Taskkill, Type (reads files up to 5 MB), Run and chunked Upload. Volexity notes it has “no built-in persistence, lateral movement, or exfiltration mechanism beyond the file-read and upload commands,” making it a survey-and-stage foothold rather than a full implant. Researchers credited: Ankur Saini, Conor Quigley, Sean Koessel, Steven Adair and Tom Lancaster.

Breaches & Incidents

Lumen Black Lotus Labs

BambooToken uses MQTT for command and control

A previously undocumented multi-platform malware family, found on VirusTotal in early 2026 and assessed active since at least February 2023, with activity detected as recently as July 2026. Variants built between 2024 and 2025 adopted the MQTT messaging protocol as their C2 channel across Windows and Linux. Lumen’s telemetry identified roughly a dozen compromised enterprises — mostly in Asia and South America — including hotels, biomedical firms, law firms, a financial organisation and a cryptocurrency website in Lithuania. “The actor used Tendyron’s ‘OnKey’ software to sideload agents into targeted machines,” Black Lotus Labs said.
