Top Story

Hackers breach the U.S. Air Force’s Cloud One platform

Hackers have breached the U.S. Air Force’s Cloud One platform, putting sensitive data at risk; the intrusion was discovered during system upgrades in the past month and has caused disruption across the platform. It remains unclear who the hackers are, how extensive the breach is, or whether any data was stolen. The Air Force did not comment on the breach but said it constantly works to protect its systems and information (Modern Diplomacy, with information from Reuters, Nov 27).

What Cloud One runs. Established in 2017, Cloud One integrates resources from Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Alphabet to support Air Force applications for personnel, logistics and maintenance, including the Cloud-Based Command and Control system for air defense and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System. SAIC was the prime contractor until 2025, when Leidos took over; neither company commented (Modern Diplomacy, with information from Reuters, Nov 27).

Impact. Migrations of back-office applications have been paused and updates to existing apps blocked — leaving personnel unable to address new software vulnerabilities — and some users have lost administrator rights. Reports suggest the attackers exploited a software vulnerability to reach the control plane, potentially allowing them to monitor military operations, though it is not known whether that happened; the flaw has not been identified (Modern Diplomacy, with information from Reuters, Nov 27).

Cyber Command called in. U.S. Cyber Command has reportedly been called in to assess the impact; one Reuters source said the hackers exploited an unidentified software vulnerability, potentially exposing sensitive military operational and logistics information. The breach follows other incidents disclosed over the past week at U.S. federal agencies — a reported intrusion into the FBI’s National Crime Information Center (revealed Nov. 21), a reported breach of a CIA open-source intelligence analysis system that also affected ODNI and NGA, and unauthorized access under investigation at Treasury’s FinCEN. It is unclear whether any are connected (BleepingComputer, Nov 27 10:09 AM).

Reuters’ own account. Three people familiar with the matter told Reuters the breach could jeopardize a vast swathe of sensitive tactical, personnel and logistics data; all five sources spoke on condition of anonymity. Among the applications on Cloud One are the Cloud-Based Command and Control system, which collates air defense radar and other data feeds from across North America, and the Theater Integrated Combat Munitions System, which tracks bombs and missiles across more than 200 U.S. Air Force locations worldwide. A source likened the control plane to a “universal remote.” The Air Force told Reuters it “does not comment on the security posture, vulnerabilities or operational status of specific networks and systems”; Cyber Command did not respond. Reuters notes the government is already dealing with at least six other recently disclosed cyber incidents (Reuters via Defense News, Mike Stone & Raphael Satter, Nov 27).

Takeaway: control-plane access in a multi-cloud tenant is the worst case — review who can administer cloud management planes, alert on admin-rights changes, and keep a break-glass path for patching if a platform freezes updates.

Also Leading

CISA adds 10 exploited flaws to KEV — five in Mitel MiCollab; eight are due Nov 28

CISA added 10 vulnerabilities to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities catalog on Nov. 25, citing evidence of active exploitation. Five are in Mitel MiCollab (CVE-2026-133275, -133303, -133311, -133323, -133324: file upload, XML injection, weak authentication, OS command injection and failure to fail securely), three in Danfoss AK-SM 800A refrigeration controllers (CVE-2023-25914, CVE-2023-25915, CVE-2025-41450), one in HPE Juniper Session Smart Conductor and Router (CVE-2026-103406, OS command injection) and one in Vates Xen Orchestra (CVE-2026-133469) (Shattered).

The Hacker News lists CVE-2026-133323 as a command injection in MiCollab’s Audio, Web and Video Conferencing (AWV) component with a CVSS score of 10.0, and CVE-2026-133303 (XML injection) at 9.0; the other three MiCollab flaws score 5.3–5.9. Federal civilian agencies must fix all the entries except CVE-2026-133311 and CVE-2025-41450 by Nov. 28; those two are due Dec. 9. There are no public details yet on how the flaws are being exploited (The Hacker News); CISA’s entries do not say the Mitel flaws were chained (Shattered).

Danfoss link to supermarket attacks. Danfoss does not say its AK-SM 800A flaws were exploited, but The Hacker News reports they were likely used in recent attacks on supermarket chains across Europe; France has attributed the October attacks on its retail sites to Shahid Kaveh (aka Cyber Aveng3rs), an IRGC-affiliated Iranian cyber unit. CISA separately warned of an unpatched critical Danfoss AK-SM 8xxA flaw, CVE-2026-129713 (CVSS 9.8 / 9.3), a stack-based buffer overflow in an unauthenticated CGI handler affecting 5.4.0 and earlier; Danfoss expects a fix in 5.5.x and advises VPN-only remote access and Strict mode until then (The Hacker News).

Mitel’s advisory MISA-2026-0012 lists MiCollab 10.3.0.18 and earlier as affected; customers should apply the patch on 10.3.0.18 or move to a later release — running the 10.3.0.18 build alone is not enough. Shattered could not confirm fixed builds for the Juniper and Xen Orchestra entries from vendor advisories, and warns that Vates advisory VSA-2026-069 covers a different flaw (Shattered).

Action: inventory MiCollab, AK-SM 800A / System Manager 800A, Session Smart Conductor/Router and Xen Orchestra; pull management interfaces off the internet; patch, then hunt for compromise before the fix date.

FIFA confirms hackers accessed “personal and business information”

“FIFA can confirm that it has been the victim of a criminal cyberattack resulting in unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information,” a FIFA spokesperson told Al Jazeera. FIFA condemned the “unlawful acquisition and publication of information, particularly personal data,” and said it is working closely with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement. It did not say how many people or organisations were affected, when the attack took place or what the business information included (Al Jazeera (AP/Reuters)).

A person with knowledge of the matter told Reuters that less than 0.1% of the total data FIFA holds was affected and that no files from the abandoned FIFA Forward Enterprise project — the scrapped plan to sell a stake in the World Cup — were accessed. A FIFA source told The Athletic the intruder gained access by impersonating staff rather than through a systems or technical failure (Al Jazeera (AP/Reuters)). Takeaway: social engineering of staff, not a software flaw, is the reported entry point — review help-desk identity verification and privileged-access approvals.

(Desk record, Nov 17) Optimizely flaw used to plant malware on the sites of Zoom, BBC, CNN, HSBC and 170+ others

An attacker exploited a vulnerability in Optimizely’s web experimentation platform to add malicious JavaScript to customer projects; Optimizely says it fixed the flaw and removed the code. Report URI’s Scott Helme found it served to visitors of 178 organisations on Nov 16. The kit swapped crypto wallet addresses, skimmed cards, stole cookies and screenshots, and on iPhones and iPads delivered the Coruna and DarkSword exploit kits; only 24 affected organizations (15%) had a CSP strict enough to block it (Help Net Security, desk record). Audit third-party scripts served from vendor CDNs.

(Desk record, Nov 10) Microsoft patches exploited Outlook zero-day in 1,093-CVE November release

Microsoft’s November 2026 Patch Tuesday (Nov. 10) addressed 1,093 CVEs — 63 rated Critical, 1,029 Important and one Moderate — including CVE-2026-124194, a Microsoft Outlook spoofing vulnerability that was exploited in the wild as a zero-day. It carries a CVSSv3 score of 6.5 and is rated Moderate; an attacker could exploit it by convincing a target to open a crafted Microsoft Office file. Tenable notes the advisory credits the discovery to a researcher who used Anthropic’s Claude (Tenable).

SOCRadar says a crafted message file makes Outlook connect to an attacker-controlled location without the usual external-content warning, leaking the user’s NTLMv2 hash; the fix ships through Office updates rather than the Windows cumulative update, so Office channels should move first. It reports CISA added the flaw to the KEV catalog on Nov. 10 with a Nov. 24 remediation deadline for federal agencies, and recommends blocking outbound SMB (TCP 445) to the internet and enforcing SMB/LDAP signing and EPA as general hardening (SOCRadar).

Other flaws to prioritize, per Tenable: Active Directory Domain Services RCE CVE-2026-118146 (CVSS 8.8, Critical, “Exploitation More Likely”), Exchange Server RCE CVE-2026-118207 (8.8, Critical, More Likely), Windows Schannel RCE CVE-2026-119952 (8.1, Critical, unauthenticated, More Likely) and SharePoint Server elevation of privilege CVE-2026-120002 (9.9, Important, Less Likely) (Tenable).

Advisory

CISA urges immediate patching of “Java4Shell” (CVE-2026-114938)

A memory-corruption flaw in OpenJDK’s Base64 decoder can let unauthenticated attackers run code on servers hosting Java apps; Oracle and the OpenJDK project fixed it Tuesday. Oracle rates it 8.1 (CVSS 3.1); finder Calif rates it 9.2 (CVSS 4.0). There is no evidence of successful attacks yet, but CISA “strongly urges” organizations to patch now. Calif says it achieved unauthenticated RCE in default configurations of Keycloak, Jira, Confluence, JFrog Artifactory, Jenkins and PingFederate. VulnCheck says the bug only triggers on CPUs with AVX-512 VBMI; ARM64 is not affected. Corretto, Temurin, Microsoft’s OpenJDK, Zulu, SapMachine, Liberica and Red Hat OpenJDK builds are also affected. CISA’s interim advice: inventory JDKs, validate and size-limit Base64 input and limit unauthenticated access (Help Net Security, citing CISA).

Ransomware

New “Citrus” ransomware hides C2 as HTTPS, abuses “Certighost” AD CS flaw

Kaspersky documented a previously undocumented group, Citrus, hitting Russian companies via compromised contractors and stolen VPN/RDP credentials, then escalating through CVE-2026-54121 (“Certighost”), an Active Directory Certificate Services flaw Microsoft patched July 14, 2026, that can yield certificates for domain controllers. It tunnels traffic over port 443 with Xray VLESS/REALITY disguised as Yandex requests, uses LLM-generated scripts, and deploys the Go-based Sn00py payload (.citrus extension).
