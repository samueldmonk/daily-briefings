Top Story

Hackers hijacked AOMEI’s update servers to push malware through Backupper

Attackers compromised the update and notification systems behind AOMEI Backupper, a Windows backup and disk-cloning tool, in supply-chain attacks between April and July 2026, briefly tampering with two configuration files and then restoring them to hide the activity, BleepingComputer reports. Researchers at ManageEngine’s Threat Analysis Lab (METAL), working with Google’s Threat Intelligence Group and Mandiant, attribute the campaign to a threat actor tracked as UNC7017, which used the infrastructure to distribute a previously unknown downloader, CROSSROAD, and a backdoor, SHINYROAD (BleepingComputer, Oct. 19; Google; ManageEngine).

How it worked. The modified files redirected the software to download a malicious ZIP archive — with an altered checksum so it passed verification — and used the notification mechanism to run it with no user interaction. Researchers identified three exposure windows: May 28 (10 minutes), July 16 (6 minutes) and July 20 (5 minutes). SHINYROAD abused legitimate Radmin components to load malicious code and connect to attacker infrastructure; during the July 20 attack Google also saw an attempt to steal Windows NTLM hashes via an invisible popup pointing to an attacker-controlled WebDAV resource. Affected organizations were found in the U.S., Australia, India, Singapore, Spain and Switzerland, and Google warned the attacks were opportunistic (BleepingComputer).

What to do. AOMEI says its CTO is leading an investigation and that it has upgraded its servers, but ManageEngine warns a server-side fix “does not remove the backdoor from machines that received it during an earlier window.” Backupper users should check the published indicators — suspicious scheduled tasks, unexpected executables in AOMEI install directories and connections to infrastructure including 8.210.84[.]56 and hostnames under storage-data[.]com (BleepingComputer).

Patch Priority

New Patch now: Plex Media Server — the “Plex2Shell” chain CVE-2026-96652, -96654, -96655, -96656 lets an attacker with no Plex login run code on servers older than 1.43.3.10861; Plex confirmed “actively and widely exploited” attacks on Oct. 19 and CISA added all four to KEV on Oct. 20. Updating does not remove an existing infection — check for unknown processes and .so/.dll files in the Codecs folder (HOL Guard; CISA). CISA’s due dates for this batch could not be confirmed from the catalog this edition.

Overdue: BeyondTrust Remote Support CVE-2026-102764 (authentication bypass, 26.2.3 and earlier; patch supported versions 24.3.1+, upgrade older ones first) and Fortinet CVE-2025-61624 (FortiOS auto-script path traversal used to deploy PEERSTITCH) — KEV deadline was Monday, Oct. 19, 2026 (1 day overdue). CISA says both were actively exploited for initial access, persistence, privilege escalation and lateral movement (Security Affairs; CISA alert).

AOMEI Backupper users: hunt for CROSSROAD/SHINYROAD indicators now — patching the vendor’s servers does not clean infected endpoints (BleepingComputer).

All other overdue federal deadlines are listed in the KEV section below.

Threat Actor Spotlight

Supply chain

UNC7017 — “ephemeral” tampering of a vendor’s update path

Google and ManageEngine track the AOMEI attacker as UNC7017. Its tradecraft relied on minutes-long edits to vendor-hosted configuration files that were reverted almost immediately, which is why AOMEI initially suggested the content had been modified in transit — an explanation ManageEngine said “the evidence did not support.” ManageEngine first notified AOMEI on June 7 and only caught the malicious files being served live on July 16 after continuous monitoring; Mandiant notified AOMEI on July 17 and the company responded on July 29 that it had taken corrective action (BleepingComputer; Google).

Breaches & Incidents

New Exploited

“Plex2Shell” attacks hijack home Plex servers for crypto mining and proxies

A researcher chained four Plex Media Server bugs into a public no-login exploit, Plex2Shell, on Oct. 16; a honeypot caught in-the-wild exploitation the next morning, installing an XMRig-based Monero miner and a residential-proxy program called myawesomeproxy. Plex patched in build 1.43.3.10861 (announced Sept. 1) and on Oct. 19 confirmed “actively and widely exploited” attacks, telling owners to assume a compromise; CISA added all four CVEs to KEV on Oct. 20 (HOL Guard).

Ransomware

Qilin, Inc and other ransomware crews still exploiting PAN-OS GlobalProtect flaw

Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 says ransomware groups including Qilin and Inc continue to exploit CVE-2026-0257, an authentication bypass in PAN-OS GlobalProtect portals and gateways that lets attackers set up an unauthorized VPN session; it has investigated attacks since early June across industries including finance, healthcare and manufacturing. Palo Alto disclosed and patched the bug on May 13. In one case attackers pivoted from the VPN gateway to vCenter, disabled ESXi security controls and dumped credentials from a domain controller’s virtual disk; ReliaQuest says Settra ransomware operators are exploiting it too (The Register, Oct. 20).

Exploited

Siemens Software Center flaw abused in ClickFix malware attacks

Siemens said on Oct. 19 that attackers exploited CVE-2026-110936, a DLL side-loading flaw in Siemens Software Center, pairing a genuine signed copy of the program with malicious DLLs delivered through ClickFix lures. Siemens rated it CVSS v4 8.4; version 4.0.9.8 fixes it and installs automatically on next launch. Siemens says none of its systems or update channels were breached and links the activity to the Romulus ClickFix platform (SecurityWeek, Oct. 19).

Law enforcement

Eight years for hacker behind $3.3M tax-preparer spearphishing scheme

Nigerian national Chukwuemeka Victor Amachukwu, 40, was sentenced to eight years in prison after pleading guilty to conspiracy to commit computer intrusions and wire fraud; he was extradited from France in August 2025. Prosecutors say he and co-conspirators spearphished tax preparation businesses in New York, Texas and other states from 2019 to 2021, stealing data on at least 800 customers and obtaining about $2.5 million in fraudulent tax refunds plus $819,000 in fraudulent SBA disaster loans. He was ordered to pay $3.6 million in restitution and forfeit nearly $11 million (BleepingComputer, Oct. 20).

Supply chain

Rank Math PRO WordPress plugin downloads trojanized for 11 hours

Some downloads of Rank Math PRO version 3.0.123 from the vendor’s official site between Oct. 15 at 20:02 UTC and Oct. 16 at 07:12 UTC carried malware that plants hidden backdoors, communicates with rank1cdn[.]com and can expose database credentials, API keys and other secrets; the free WordPress.org version was not affected. Rank Math released a clean v3.0.124 and says sites that activated the bad build should be treated as compromised — deactivating or updating does not remove the threat. Recommended steps: restore from a clean backup, rotate all passwords, keys and salts, and block outbound connections to rank1cdn[.]com (BleepingComputer, Oct. 19; Rank Math advisory).

Data breach

Nikkei discloses its third breach in about a month — 1.83 million records

Attackers used stolen credentials to access a Nikkei development system early on Oct. 7, exposing personal-information records stored in the cloud. About 150,000 of the 1.83 million records contained at least one identifier such as name, email, address, phone number or date of birth; the remaining 1.68 million contained no identifiers, and no credit card data was exposed. Nikkei notified Japan’s Personal Information Protection Commission on Oct. 11 and said that as of Oct. 16 it had no evidence of misuse (BleepingComputer, Oct. 19).

AI-driven attacks

Attackers abused AI pen-test tool Cairn to automate a website-hacking spree

A Chinese-speaking, apparently financially motivated actor used the AI-driven penetration-testing tool Cairn to target about 100 sites in the U.S., Australia, Canada, Japan and elsewhere, focusing on e-commerce and payment infrastructure, according to NorForge, which found more than 20 publicly exposed dashboards. Reuters identified victims including John Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins; John Deere said it had no indication sensitive information was accessed, and SharkNinja said the incident was contained to a standalone product-support site. NorForge confirmed at least one web shell install (Dark Reading, Oct. 20).

Healthcare

Paradigm Healthcare breach affects 970,000 Medi-Cal members

The Medi-Cal billing processor told HHS that a breach discovered on Oct. 13, 2025, affected more than 970,000 people; files with names, dates of birth, gender and Medi-Cal member ID numbers may have been accessed between Oct. 8 and Oct. 15, 2025. Notification letters are dated Sept. 14, 2026 — roughly 11 months after detection. No known group has claimed the attack (SecurityWeek, Oct. 20).

Policy

DHS orders federal civilian agencies to remove DeepSeek products

DHS ordered federal civilian agencies to immediately exclude DeepSeek products — including affiliates’ products and third-party services built on DeepSeek technology — from procurement and remove them from agency IT systems, or seek an exception within 60 days; CISA will review exception requests. DHS cites concerns about Chinese government access to DeepSeek infrastructure and potential transfer of sensitive government data. The order, published in the Federal Register on Oct. 20, is not a ban on private or consumer use (BleepingComputer, Oct. 20; Federal Register).

Patch side-effect

October Windows Server updates break Exchange sign-in and mail flow

Microsoft confirmed that October 2026 Windows security updates on Windows Server 2019 and later can stop on-premises Exchange users signing in to Outlook on the web and leave mail stuck in the Outbox; the update caps Endpoint Mapper at 512 simultaneous Endpoint Lookup calls, which heavy Exchange workloads can exceed. Microsoft says the October Exchange security updates are not the cause and should not be uninstalled; a registry workaround (EpmapSavedContextCap) is available pending a fix (BleepingComputer, Oct. 19).

Exploited

SiteGuard WAF flaw exploited to run commands as root or SYSTEM

Attackers are exploiting CVE-2026-106931, an OS command-injection flaw in the statistics feature (enabled by default) of SiteGuard Server Edition, the Japanese host-based web application firewall; the vendor and JPCERT/CC rate it critical and confirm in-the-wild abuse. Affected: Apache httpd, Nginx and IIS editions 2.20 and later before 8.00 Update 5 (FreeBSD: before 7.00 Update 6). The vendor warns patching may erase attack traces — download “Diagnostic information” first (BleepingComputer, Oct. 18).

Incident

Bullhorn takes staffing platform offline after cyberattack

Staffing-software firm Bullhorn, which says it serves more than 10,000 customers, disabled access to its platform to contain a cyber incident behind a system-wide outage; it says the immediate threat is eradicated and is restoring services with CrowdStrike. It has not yet determined whether customer data was exfiltrated; non-SSO users must reset passwords and API passwords need rotation (BleepingComputer, Oct. 19).

AI / keys

OpenCode breach exposed users’ external AI-provider API keys

The open-source AI coding project says a “large-scale distillation attack” hit 1.4% of OpenCode Console users who had linked external providers; some reported up to $1,700 in unauthorized charges and Google Cloud project suspensions. Earliest possible key exposure was Oct. 17, 00:30 UTC; affected users were emailed and should rotate keys (BleepingComputer, Oct. 19).

Supply chain

OVHcloud CLI releases backdoored on GitHub to steal developer secrets

An attacker hijacked a developer’s GitHub account and modified OVHcloud CLI versions 0.11.0 through 0.15.0 to deliver information-stealing malware; CEO Octave Klaba said the compromised release appeared at 14:47 on Oct. 19 and was blocked at 16:21 after a customer report. Versions 0.10.0 and 0.16.0 are clean; GitHub release immutability had been disabled. OpenSourceMalware links the attack to a likely new GlassWorm operation. Anyone who ran an affected binary should treat all secrets on that machine as compromised (BleepingComputer, Oct. 20).

Outage

Revolut outage disrupts banking services worldwide

Revolut said a service disruption since Tuesday morning hit multiple services — its status page flagged 28, including sign-ins, card payments and transfers — while stating customers’ money and data are safe. Reuters reportedly linked it to a “power event” disrupting Google Cloud’s London europe-west2 region; no attack has been reported (BleepingComputer, Oct. 20).

Retail

Ahold Delhaize: cyberattack disrupted services across the U.S. and Europe

In a Monday update, Ahold Delhaize said last week’s incident forced it to take some systems offline, including brand websites and apps, though stores stayed open. Albert Heijn’s site and app are back, with home deliveries expected to resume Friday, Oct. 23; e-commerce at its U.S. brands is expected back “in the coming days.” Stop & Shop employees had reported trouble accessing schedules and pay stubs. The company has filed a police report in the Netherlands and says it has found no evidence so far that sensitive customer, associate or partner data was stolen (BleepingComputer, Oct. 20).

Supply chain

ConnectWise Cloud Backup attack hit Microsoft 365 tenants

ConnectWise shut down customer access to Cloud Backup (formerly SkyKick) after attackers accessed application identities and compromised Microsoft 365 tenants; Huntress counted at least 162 compromised tenants in its environment, with scripted sign-ins traced to Oct. 13–14 from one IP, 144.172.64[.]128. As of an Oct. 18 update, backups and restores were still offline (BleepingComputer).

Breach

One compromised login exposed California students’ records

An unauthorized party used a compromised credential between May 22 and Sept. 18, 2026 to copy California student files from an SFTP site run by Educational Testing Service; data on students enrolled in the past three school years was accessed. No Social Security numbers or financial data were involved; ETS detected the activity Sept. 25 and notifications began Oct. 16 (SecurityWeek).

Extortion

Feds suspect a consulting-firm executive helped run ShinyHunters’ leak site

KrebsOnSecurity reports investigators believe an executive at a U.S. consulting firm helped operate the extortion site shinywire[.]com, active from about May 3 to Oct. 16, 2026 with roughly 100 victims; per the FBI, ShinyHunters has extorted more than $70 million this year. The firm is not named and no charges have been filed (KrebsOnSecurity).

Nation-state

Fortinet KEV flaw tied to “PeerStitch” FortiGate implant in telecom hack

Palo Alto Networks’ Unit 42 says suspected state actor UNC5820 used the FortiJump flaw CVE-2024-47575 and CVE-2025-61624 against a telecom organization, exfiltrating configuration data for more than 500 managed FortiGates and deploying an in-memory ARM64 implant (SecurityWeek).

Patch

F5 ships out-of-band fixes for three critical NGINX flaws

CVE-2026-15176 and CVE-2026-97205 (ngx_http_v3_module) and CVE-2026-93479 (map directive) can let unauthenticated attackers crash NGINX workers on non-default configurations, and possibly execute code when ASLR is disabled or bypassed. Fixes cover NGINX Plus, Open Source, Ingress Controller, Gateway Fabric and Instance Manager; F5 has not flagged exploitation (BleepingComputer, Oct. 19).
