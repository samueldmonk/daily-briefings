Top Story

Fight week in Doha: Stirling one win from the light-heavyweight title picture

Navajo Stirling (11-0) meets former champion Jiří Procházka in Saturday’s main event at ABHA Arena, coming off a knockout of former champion Jan Blachowicz; Procházka returns from his loss to Carlos Ulberg for the vacant title at UFC 327 in April (UFC.com; 1News). 1News reports a win “should cement his status as the No. 1 contender” for the belt held by City Kickboxing teammate Ulberg, whose return from ACL surgery is estimated for early 2027; if that slips further, the UFC would likely create an interim title (1News, Nov. 19).

Odds: Stirling −148 / Procházka +124 (Yahoo Sports); UFC.com’s listing shows Stirling −155 / Procházka +130 (Yahoo Sports; UFC.com, via search, Nov. 19).

Last Saturday: Gane edges Hokit in a record slugfest at MSG; Harrison spoils Nunes' return

Ciryl Gane defended his heavyweight title for the first time with a hard-fought unanimous decision over Josh Hokit at UFC 334 inside Madison Square Garden on Nov 14 (ESPN); all three judges scored it 48-47. Gane landed 237 significant strikes, a record for a UFC heavyweight fight (Yahoo Sports). Hokit, a Contender Series signee, was challenging in just his fifth UFC fight (ESPN).

Co-main: Kayla Harrison ended a rivalry nearly eight years in the making, submitting Amanda Nunes by rear-naked choke early in Round 4 to keep the women's bantamweight title; Nunes was returning from a 3½-year retirement (ESPN). Nunes has since called for a rematch (ESPN headline).

Farewell: Stephen "Wonderboy" Thompson stopped Charles Radtke with a spinning kick in Round 3 and retired (ESPN).
