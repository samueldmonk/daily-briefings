⛨ The Cyber Wire · The Wire

$320 million in bitcoin left a sidechain’s federation wallet and most of it came back — and the briefing corrects its own account of the federal patching clock

A print server is still the most urgent box on the security desk — PaperCut NG/MF is under hands-on human intrusion and carries the only federal deadline that has not elapsed, Monday 14 September, seven days out. Two items are new at 6:35 PM, and the larger one has no CVE attached to it. Self-described white-hat actors moved about 4,000 BTC — roughly $320 million, close to 95% of the reserves backing the Liquid Bitcoin sidechain — out of its federation wallet on 6 September; Blockstream disabled the bridge nodes, pausing the network and its two-way peg. 3,400 BTC ($268m) has been returned and about 598.5 BTC ($47m) kept. The “whitehats” label is the actors’ own, written on-chain, and nothing read confirms it; there is no CVE, no patch and no KEV entry, so it carries no deadline and does not displace PaperCut as the urgent box. Two arithmetic gaps are printed rather than smoothed: 600 against the 598.5 stated, and $52m against the $47m stated. The second new item is the briefing correcting itself: it had said BOD 26-04 replaced BOD 22-01’s uniform three weeks; returns read this run give the old clock as 14 days for post-2021 CVEs and six months for older ones, and the new one as 3, 14 or 60 calendar days across five risk tiers. No deadline on the page moved. The item new at 6:05 PM was the smallest entry on the page: a round-up dated 7 September puts CVE-2026-51693 at CVSS 9.8 in the TOTOLINK T6 router. It goes onto the watch list with no vulnerability class, no affected build and no fixed version — the summary gives none and no vendor advisory was fetched — so an owner cannot yet check a box against it, and the briefing says so instead of implying otherwise. It is not among the ten CVEs the same round-up calls actively exploited, and it carries no deadline. The two items new at 5:38 PM are carried. Broadcom patched CVE-2026-59346 (CVSS 9.3), an integer overflow in the VMXNET3 virtual network adapter of VMware Workstation and Fusion: a malicious actor with local administrative privileges inside a guest VM can execute code on the host — a VM escape, gated behind guest admin rights and a VMXNET3 adapter, affecting 25H2 and 26H1 and fixed in 26H1u1 alongside CVE-2026-59347 (8.1). No return states exploitation in the wild. And CISA, the FBI and HHS put the Medusa ransomware operation past 500 U.S. critical-infrastructure victims — a figure that is cumulative since June 2021 and current as of April 2026, updating a March 2025 advisory that said 300+, not a count of anything that happened today. The 5:05 PM edition’s item was the pair of numbers that lets an administrator check their own box: returns gave the vulnerable SonicWall SMA1000 builds as 12.4.3-03453 and older and 12.5.0-02835 and older under vendor notice SNWLID-2026-0016 — the briefing has carried the fixed hotfixes since its 10:46 edition but never the vulnerable ones, and that SSRF-to-command-injection chain (CVE-2026-83548 → CVE-2026-83549) sits at the top of the group whose federal deadline elapsed on 5 September, tied on CVSS 10.0 with Kestra OSS rather than alone. A conflation stays refused alongside it: a summary calls CVE-2026-32475 an Adobe Commerce and Magento flaw, when it is the Elementor Pro WordPress flaw and the Magento story is StyleSmuggler, which has no CVE at all. The 4:35 PM edition’s Cisco IOS XR item is carried. Tomorrow is Patch Tuesday; a preview return’s “150–300+” size estimate is a projection and is refused.

Futures have been trading again for about half an hour, nobody at the desk has read the new tape, and nothing else on the markets page is new

U.S. stock and bond markets were shut all day for Labor Day and reopen at 9:30 AM ET Tuesday, and every oil contract that was still trading has now halted too. Nothing on the markets page is new at 6:35 PM — the sweep returned only carried material, so no New tag is attached to it — and the only thing that has moved is the clock: the 6:00 PM ET reopen is now about half an hour in the past, the 1:00 PM ET halt is a closed five-hour interval, and no post-reopen level is asserted for any symbol. The new thing at 6:05 PM was that same clock at the moment it crossed: the 6:00 PM ET CME reopen had just happened, so the 1:00 PM ET equity-index halt is a closed five-hour interval rather than a running one, and the ticker at the top of the markets page has started moving again on Tuesday’s trade date. No level is asserted for the reopened tape — the reopen time was verified in an earlier edition and the elapsed arithmetic is this desk’s, but no return read here gives a post-reopen price for any symbol. Regular U.S. trading still resumes 9:30 AM ET Tuesday. The 5:38 PM edition’s item filled a gap the briefing had carried since the halt: it has said since the 1:00 PM ET halt that the ticker at the top shows last matched prices with no live market behind them, but never carried a number for them. A summary now supplies the Sunday-evening futures prints — S&P 500 futures flat at 7,720.0, Nasdaq 100 +0.1% at 29,589.25, Dow −0.3% at 53,292.0 — mixed, thin ahead of the holiday, with investors weighing tighter Fed policy after a stronger-than-expected August jobs report. These are not the prices at the halt: no return gives a level for where the three sat when CME stopped matching at 1:00 PM ET, so none is asserted. Everything else matched something carried — the Brent $97.39 print, the Hormuz restricted-zone warning and the 11 September CPI / 15–16 September FOMC sequence. The 4:35 PM edition’s new items were the tapes that did close: the Stoxx 600 ended just below the flatline, CAC 40 +0.33%, FTSE MIB +0.25%, DAX −0.25%, with oil and gas the standout sector at +1.29%; earlier Kospi +3.09% at the open and Nikkei 225 up nearly 1%. Two crude prints came with them — Brent November +0.88% at $97.13, WTI October +0.92% at $92.32 — both below the $97.93 touch the briefing holds, so no single Brent level is asserted for a thirty-fourth consecutive edition. Two returns disagree on the FTSE 100’s direction, so no FTSE figure is published.

⊘ The Octagon · Tale of the Tape

The same stale champions list came back with the same three wrong belts, and the briefing refused all three again — a fifth straight edition with nothing new

The vacant women’s flyweight title still leads: Natália Silva meets Wang Cong at UFC 332 on 3 October in Salt Lake City, prelims 4 p.m. ET, main card 8 p.m. ET, with Silva −210 (opened −250) and Cong +180 (opened +210) per BetOnline.ag. A fifth consecutive sweep returned nothing new, so the briefing carries no New tag — but two things did happen to what it already carries. For a second consecutive edition a champions-list return arrived with the same three superseded belts on it — Pereira at light heavyweight, Chimaev at middleweight and Shevchenko at women’s flyweight — and all three were refused: the board carries Ulberg, Strickland and vacant, and a list still seating Shevchenko cannot be current for a card built on her giving the belt up. The other eight lines matched exactly. And a third return is the first to use the briefing’s own verb: a UFC 332 preview says she was “forced to vacate the title due to injury” that “will sideline her for up to a year,” matching the about-a-year absence already carried. The count is three returns deep, 2–1 for “stripped” by volume, but the one saying vacated agrees with the promotion’s 5 September announcement, which is the source the briefing follows. The conflict is narrowed, not closed, and it stays printed: the first reference return said Shevchenko “was stripped of the title,” the briefing says vacated, and those are different events. A second return in the 5:05 PM edition repeats “stripped” and adds “due to injury” — agreeing on the cause, still disagreeing on the mechanism. Last completed event 5 September; next 12 September in Glendale.
