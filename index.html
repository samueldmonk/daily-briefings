⛨ The Cyber Wire · The Wire

A fake Lockheed Martin job advert, a Windows kernel zero-day, and five weeks inside defence contractors

Check Point Research has attributed zero-day exploitation of CVE-2026-68820 — an elevation-of-privilege flaw in the Windows AFD.sys driver — to North Korea’s Lazarus Group, running its long-standing Operation Dream Job campaign. Microsoft patched it on August 11; Check Point says the group had been using it since at least early July, roughly five weeks earlier. Targets were defence, aerospace, aviation, drone, robotics and military-technology organisations in France, Germany, Brazil and India. The lure is a job offer: a malicious DLL displays a convincing Lockheed Martin job description while loading MISTPEN, a downloader that talks through the Microsoft Graph API and OneDrive, which then escalates to SYSTEM through the AFD.sys use-after-free and executes the FudModule kernel rootkit. The flaw entered CISA’s KEV catalogue on August 11 with a federal deadline of August 25. Elsewhere on the page: an actor calling himself TheHatman is offering roughly 3.6 million records pulled from corporate Azure and Entra tenants — more than 1.7 million from McDonald’s, about 800,000 from Tata Consultancy Services, 425,000 from Vodafone — including job titles, manager chains and Global Administrator listings, with Hudson Rock calling the data authentic but the access vector unknown; consumer lender Heights Finance is notifying more than 1.2 million people whose Social Security numbers and account details were taken from a third-party cloud platform; ShinyHunters, refused a ransom by Brinks Home, has leaked more than 41GB of files from a claimed 4.9 million Salesforce records taken in a July 13 Entra vishing call; and CISA’s deadline for the actively exploited Ray flaw falls Thursday, a three-day window from Monday’s KEV listing.

▲ The Closing Bell · The Tape

The split widened: the Nasdaq is off 1.34%, the chip index roughly 3.7%, and the Dow is all but flat

As of roughly 1:00 PM ET the Nasdaq Composite was down 1.04%, the Dow 0.16% and the Russell 2000 0.91%, with the S&P 500 off about 0.6% — a second consecutive narrowing after a morning in which the Nasdaq’s loss widened at every check (about 1.2% at 10:30, 1.34% at 10:55, 1.6% at 11:36); Yahoo Finance renders the same session as Nasdaq −1.3%, Dow −0.2%, S&P −0.6%. The recovery is not reaching the chips: Nvidia is down about 2% and Intel about 5%. The damage is concentrated in memory and storage: Western Digital is down about 7% to $500.05, Marvell and Seagate about 8% each, Micron about 5%, with rising long yields cited as the cause rather than anything company-specific — a hard turn from Monday, when Western Digital rose 6.5% on SanDisk’s investor day. The long end made a new high and then reversed: CNBC has the 30-year topping 5.33%, a fresh 19-year high on inflation and deficit concerns, before falling to 5.294%, with the 10-year at 4.712%. The cause behind it is unchanged — President Trump rejected extending the 60-day Iran ceasefire that expired Monday and crude stayed bid, with Brent near a three-week high and US crude topping $85. The money went to defensives: health care led sectors at +1.59%, energy +1.30% (Benzinga has XLE +1.05%) and staples +1.11%.
