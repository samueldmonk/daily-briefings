⛨ The Cyber Wire · The Wire

A fake Lockheed Martin job advert, a Windows kernel zero-day, and five weeks inside defence contractors

Check Point Research has attributed zero-day exploitation of CVE-2026-68820 — an elevation-of-privilege flaw in the Windows AFD.sys driver — to North Korea’s Lazarus Group, running its long-standing Operation Dream Job campaign. Microsoft patched it on August 11; Check Point says the group had been using it since at least early July, roughly five weeks earlier. Targets were defence, aerospace, aviation, drone, robotics and military-technology organisations in France, Germany, Brazil and India. The lure is a job offer: a malicious DLL displays a convincing Lockheed Martin job description while loading MISTPEN, a downloader that talks through the Microsoft Graph API and OneDrive, which then escalates to SYSTEM through the AFD.sys use-after-free and executes the FudModule kernel rootkit. The flaw entered CISA’s KEV catalogue on August 11 with a federal deadline of August 25. Elsewhere on the page: consumer lender Heights Finance is notifying more than 1.2 million people that names, Social Security numbers, financial account details and government ID numbers were taken from a third-party cloud platform — a breach discovered on May 7 and notified only on August 11 — and CISA’s deadline for the actively exploited Ray flaw falls Thursday.

▲ The Closing Bell · The Tape

The split widened: the Nasdaq is off 1.34%, the chip index roughly 3.7%, and the Dow is all but flat

As of roughly 11:36 AM ET the Nasdaq was down 1.6%, the S&P 500 down 0.5% and the Dow lower by about 139 points. The Nasdaq’s loss has widened through the morning — about 1.2% at 10:30, 1.34% at 10:55, 1.6% now — even as the bond market that caused it relented: the 10-year has eased to 4.70% and the 30-year edged down to 5.30% after touching a 19-year high. The concentrated damage is in semiconductors: the Philadelphia semiconductor index fell roughly 3.7% in early trade, with Nvidia, Meta and other large technology names weakening together. The cause is unchanged — President Trump rejected extending the 60-day Iran ceasefire that expired Monday, crude stayed bid with Brent near a three-week high and US crude topping $85, and the 30-year Treasury yield hit a 19-year high earlier, CNBC putting it above 5.31% and Bloomberg near 5.32%. The money went to defensives: health care led sectors at +1.59%, energy +1.30% and staples +1.11%. Home Depot beat on both lines and reaffirmed guidance, then gave the gain back and spent the morning among the Dow’s worst.
