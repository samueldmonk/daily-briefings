The Wire

Security

Two federal remediation deadlines expired at midnight and two more fall today — the Citrix NetScaler flaw that ran out yesterday turns out to have been patched by Citrix in June as a denial-of-service bug and only later understood to allow unauthenticated remote code execution, with webshells already landing on compromised appliances; a seventh check of the KEV catalogue found no batch newer than August 27 but an unrelated search turned up an August 25 addition that none of the seven checks had returned, which is the plainest evidence yet that this board sees the deadlines it can source rather than all of them; new this run, more than 8,300 internet-exposed Gitea servers remain unpatched against an exploited 9.8-rated code-injection flaw that a default setting lets an unauthenticated attacker reach by simply registering an account, with miners as the observed payload; and Berlin’s state government has refused to pay after an intrusion that kept exfiltrating data for a week between the first report and the network being cut, with Rhysida claiming 5.79 terabytes three weeks before a city election — a figure that is the attacker’s and is printed as such, alongside the McKesson and PaperCut items already carried.
