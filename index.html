⛨ The Cyber Wire · The Wire

Four exploited flaws, one Friday deadline: CISA gives federal agencies three days to fix vCenter, SharePoint, Windows IKE and macOS

CISA added four actively exploited vulnerabilities to its KEV catalog on August 18 and ordered federal civilian agencies to remediate all of them by August 21. Three carry a CVSS of 9.8: CVE-2026-33824 (Microsoft IKE Service Extensions, double free), CVE-2026-59310 (Broadcom VMware vCenter, path traversal) and CVE-2026-65400 (Apple macOS Screen Sharing, improper authentication); the fourth, CVE-2026-55040, is a 9.1 SharePoint authentication bypass. The vCenter entry is the one with confirmed victims — German IR firm QUIRSO counted 361 unique victim IP addresses across 47 countries, with attackers first contacting their domains on August 3, five days after Broadcom disclosed the flaw, then establishing persistence through a cron job running reverse_ssh. Broadcom says there is no workaround. The SharePoint flaw is being exploited with Rapid7’s public proof-of-concept, which chains four weaknesses in the JWT validation pipeline to forge a token and impersonate any user including administrators. Elsewhere: consumer lender Heights Finance is notifying more than 1.2 million people whose Social Security numbers, bank routing details and driver’s licence numbers were taken from a third-party cloud platform, and researchers have found more than 50,000 live Stripe merchant API keys sitting in public repositories, GitHub Actions logs and misconfigured servers — Stripe itself was never breached. The separate Ray deadline falls tomorrow. New this run: watchTowr and VulnCheck report active scanning and exploitation attempts against CVE-2026-64849 (CVSS 9.3, unauthenticated SSRF in MLflow before 3.15.0, used to reach cloud metadata endpoints and steal secrets) and CVE-2026-25895 (CVSS 9.5, missing authentication plus path traversal in the FUXA SCADA/HMI package, giving unauthenticated remote code execution). Neither is in KEV, so neither carries a federal deadline.

▲ The Closing Bell · The Tape

The Treasury stepped in at the long end, and the tape turned — while Moderna more than doubled

US stocks rose Wednesday morning after the Treasury Department said it would at least double its “liquidity support” buybacks of longer-dated debt, to at least $4 billion per operation, sending the 30-year yield down nine basis points to 5.20% from Tuesday’s 5.33% — a 19-year high — and after President Trump paused the 50% tariffs on Canadian goods for three days, citing a deal with Mark Carney’s team. Yahoo Finance has the Dow up about 0.4% and the S&P 500 up about 0.5%, with the Nasdaq Composite only edging above the flat line amid continued chip weakness; a second morning read renders it S&P +0.43%, Nasdaq +0.40%, Dow +0.25%. The largest single move is not macro: Moderna and Merck said their individualised mRNA vaccine intismeran, with Keytruda, met the primary endpoint in the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 trial across 1,137 patients with resected stage IIB–IV melanoma — the first positive Phase 3 for an mRNA cancer therapy. CNBC says the stock more than doubled; other reads range from about 90% to more than 120%, so every figure is printed with its source. Marvell rose more than 11% after granting Google a warrant for up to 58.97 million shares at $206.58, about $12.18 billion. Underneath, Korea took Tuesday’s chip rout and made it worse: the KOSPI fell 5%–5.4%, Samsung 7.54%–7.82% and SK Hynix 9.75%–9.93%. Target beat and raised but 40% of its EPS came from a $994 million tariff refund; Lowe’s comps rose just 0.2% and it trimmed guidance. The July 28–29 FOMC minutes land today at 2:00 PM ET — from a meeting that held the range at 3.50%–3.75% over three dissents in favour of a hike. Newly on the movers board this run: Keysight rose 2% on a beat ($3.07 a share against $2.48 expected) and Mercury Systems fell more than 9%.

⊘ The Octagon · Tale of the Tape

Six contracts from five fights — including one for the man who lost

Dana White’s Contender Series returned to the Meta APEX on Tuesday for Week 2 of Season 10, and White handed out six UFC contracts from a five-fight card: Kaik Brito, Trent Miller, Cristian Perez, Alik Lorenz, Roman Gabriel Puga and Taner Trembley. Every winner was signed — and so was one loser, with both men in the Puga–Trembley three-rounder earning deals. Week 1 produced four, so the season has generated ten contracts in a fortnight. Brito, the headliner, is a former Oktagon welterweight champion who took that title in February and arrived 19-6. Elsewhere: Carlos Prates, after watching Islam Makhachev set the all-time Octagon win-streak record at UFC 330, has told the champion “I will bring that belt to Brazil” and named December as his date — Makhachev has called Prates the most deserving next challenger at 170, though nothing is signed. The next card is Saturday in Sacramento, where No. 6 Anthony Hernandez faces No. 10 Gregory Rodrigues at middleweight, Hernandez −166 to −176 across books after opening near −167.
