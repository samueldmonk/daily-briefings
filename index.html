⛨ The Cyber Wire · The Wire

A GitLab flaw went from disclosure to in-the-wild attack in days — and the next federal deadline is tomorrow

watchTowr told The Hacker News on August 21 that CVE-2026-19478 (CVSS 9.4), a code-injection flaw in GitLab reachable through a GraphQL directive, is under active exploitation days after disclosure. It needs no credentials, no user interaction and no unusual configuration; an unauthenticated attacker can delete entire repositories, forge merge records to make it look as though a fix landed when it didn’t, and ban project maintainers. watchTowr reproduced it “within minutes” and then caught exploitation against its honeypots — principal researcher Jake Knott called it “the new reality” of AI-compressed exploitation, where waiting for the next patch cycle is too late. Fixes are in GitLab 19.2.4, 19.1.6, 19.0.8 and 18.11.11. Meanwhile the patch clock is loud: CISA added two actively exploited TrueConf Server flaws on August 20 and set the deadline for CVE-2026-72529 at August 23 — tomorrow, with its companion CVE-2026-72530 due September 3, even though both were fixed back in June. All four flaws from the August 18 batch came due yesterday and are now past due, alongside three from August 11. One correction worth flagging: Microsoft’s bulletin for the maximum-severity Entra ID RCE CVE-2026-69836 (CVSS 10.0) originally read “Exploited: Yes,” but on August 21 Microsoft corrected it to No — the flaw is fully mitigated and needs no customer action. And in the spotlight this run: Hunt.io reconstructed Operation CameraSwarm from a 407 MB working directory the operator left exposed, documenting more than 14,530 Dahua devices compromised in 35 days, backdoor accounts planted on 1,923 cameras and 283 more reached by serial number alone through Dahua’s own cloud relay.

▲ The Closing Bell · The Tape

Friday’s rally trimmed a losing week, but the bond market still wrote the script

US markets are closed for the weekend; the figures here are Friday’s official close, August 21. Stocks rose and cut into a shaky week, though the bond market — the centre of Wall Street’s action lately — stayed jumpy and added pressure. The Dow did the work, up 517.80 points, or 1%, to 53,277.01; the S&P 500 added 33.21 points, or 0.4%, to 7,674.37; the Nasdaq composite rose 113.29, or 0.4%, to 26,180.45; and the Russell 2000 gained 25.44, or 0.9%, to 3,017.87. For the week, all four still fell — the S&P down 1.4%, the Dow 0.8%, the Nasdaq 2.1% and the Russell 1.6%. The catalyst came from Treasury, not the Fed: Secretary Scott Bessent said the department had doubled its long-term debt buybacks from $2 billion to at least $4 billion per operation, and told CNBC it could go higher. It only partly worked — Trading Economics had the 10-year at 4.7% and the 30-year back near 5.25%, nearly erasing Wednesday’s decline. Capital.com’s Daniela Hathorn tied the softer tone to global bond pressure, another rise in oil and uncertainty about the Fed’s next move. Ross Stores rallied on a profit beat and a strong US business-activity report lifted profit hopes broadly. Single names did the rest: Moderna +17% on positive Phase 3 data for its personalised mRNA cancer vaccine with Merck, Robinhood +11.1% and Coinbase +8.5% on a bitcoin surge that carried BTC past $76,900, against Marvell −6% into its August 27 earnings, Teradyne −4% on a Baird downgrade and AppLovin −2.3% after Piper Sandler cut its target to $325 from $385.

Sacramento, tonight: Hernandez rebuilds, Rodrigues climbs

UFC Fight Night: Hernandez vs. Rodrigues is live tonight at the Golden 1 Center in Sacramento — prelims from 5 PM ET, main card at 8 PM ET on Paramount+. Anthony Hernandez is a −185 favourite at Hard Rock Bet with Gregory Rodrigues at +150; DraftKings has it −198 / +164. Hernandez had won eight straight before Sean Strickland stopped him in the third round in February, his first defeat in nearly six years; Rodrigues comes in on three consecutive wins, including a first-round stoppage of Brunno Ferreira at UFC 326 in March, and has taken six of his last seven. The co-feature pairs Reinier de Ridder with Roman Dolidze at light heavyweight. Behind it, the most recent card still resonates: at UFC 330 in Philadelphia on August 15, Islam Makhachev beat Ian Machado Garry by unanimous decision to retain the welterweight title, notching his 17th straight UFC win and setting a new record for consecutive victories inside the Octagon, while Mackenzie Dern banked her first strawweight defence over Gillian Robertson and Edson Barboza left his gloves in the cage after a 16-year UFC career. Four $100,000 Performance bonuses went to Dustin Stoltzfus, Charles Johnson, Jeremiah Wells and Jalin Turner, whose 39-second knockout was the night’s fastest finish. Ahead: Noche UFC in Glendale on September 12 and UFC 331: Van vs. Pantoja 2 in Los Angeles on September 19.
